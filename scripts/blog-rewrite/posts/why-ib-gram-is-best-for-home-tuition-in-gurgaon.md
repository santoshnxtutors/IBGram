## What Are the Four Main Home Tuition Options in Gurgaon?

The four main options are a matching platform that shortlists tutors for you, a coaching centre or institute with fixed batches, a freelance tutor found through word of mouth or a school community, and an online marketplace where you browse tutor profiles and book directly. Each shifts responsibility differently between the family and the provider.

:::steps Four routes to a home tutor

The differences are practical rather than ideological. A matching platform does the searching and shortlisting for you, which saves time but depends on the quality of its vetting. A coaching centre offers structure and peers but a fixed pace. A freelancer offers a personal relationship and flexibility but no safety net. A marketplace offers choice and price transparency but leaves the judging to you.

## When Does a Coaching Centre Make Sense?

A coaching centre makes sense when a student benefits from a structured batch, a fixed timetable and the presence of peers, and when the syllabus is popular enough that the centre has real depth in it. It tends to work well for students who need external discipline and who do not need heavy individual adaptation.

The strengths are real. A centre offers a defined schedule, an environment away from home distractions, peers to compare against and often a bank of practice material. For a student who drifts without a structure, the fixed rhythm of a batch can be exactly what is needed.

The limits are equally real, and they matter more for IB and IGCSE than for a conventional syllabus. Batches move at one pace. IB assessment is criterion-referenced, with internal assessments, extended writing and command terms that reward individual feedback on the student's own work. A batch of fifteen cannot easily provide that. Where a student's problem is a specific gap or a style of answering, a batch may cover the right topic and still miss the student. For a fuller comparison on one subject, [IGCSE maths home tutor versus coaching](/blog/igcse-maths-home-tutor-vs-coaching/) sets out who each suits, and [one-to-one IB coaching: is it worth it](/blog/1-to-1-ib-coaching-in-gurgaon-is-it-worth-it/) offers a decision matrix across group classes, centres and self-study.

A coaching centre is usually the sensible choice when:

- the student is mostly on track and wants structure and practice volume,
- the subject is widely taught and the centre has a strong record in it,
- the student works better outside the house, and
- the family can manage the travel without eating into sleep or activities.

It is usually the weaker choice when the problem is specific to one student, or when the subject is a niche one at HL or in a less common IGCSE syllabus.

## When Is a Word-of-Mouth Freelance Tutor the Right Choice?

A word-of-mouth freelancer is the right choice when a trusted family can describe, from experience, exactly what the tutor did for a student similar to yours, and when you are comfortable managing the arrangement directly. A recommendation from a family you know is genuinely valuable information, and a good freelancer can be excellent.

The advantages are straightforward: a personal relationship, flexibility on time and place and often lower overhead. Many families keep a good freelance tutor for years.

The risks are quieter. A recommendation tells you the tutor suited another family's child at another time, in another subject, perhaps another board. The tutor who was perfect for a CBSE Class 10 student may not know how an IB Internal Assessment is marked. A referral also gives you little recourse if things go wrong: there is no one to ask for a different tutor, no one to cover a long absence and no independent check of qualifications. Because it is personal, ending the arrangement can feel awkward.

### Questions to ask any freelance tutor

- Which syllabus and level have you taught most recently, and can you describe a typical student?
- How do you handle criterion-based marking, command terms or mark schemes?
- What is the arrangement if you are unwell or travelling during a mock period?
- How will you tell me what you worked on and what should happen next?
- What happens if either of us feels the fit is not right after a month?

A freelancer who answers these specifically is probably worth trying. One who answers in generalities is not necessarily poor, but you will be doing the diagnosing yourself.

## What Do Online Marketplaces Offer, and What Do They Leave to You?

Online marketplaces offer a large pool of tutor profiles, visible ratings and the ability to book quickly, but they leave the judging, the curriculum check and the management of the relationship to you. They suit confident, informed buyers who know exactly what they need.

The pool is the main attraction. A student studying a less common subject, such as IB Economics HL or Cambridge IGCSE Computer Science, may find a specialist who is not available locally. Booking is usually fast, and online lessons remove the geography problem entirely.

The cost of that breadth is your time and attention. Profile text is written by the tutor and is not independent evidence. A high rating tells you that students were broadly satisfied, not that the tutor understands the IB Diploma Programme (DP) criteria, and ratings in one syllabus do not transfer to another. Many marketplaces list tutors for every board and every subject, so the filtering falls to you. And because bookings are often one-to-one transactions, continuity depends on the individual tutor staying available.

If you go this way, a short trial lesson and a specific question about the syllabus tell you more than any rating. ## Why Does Curriculum Fit Matter So Much for IB and IGCSE?

Curriculum fit matters because IB and IGCSE are families of programmes, not single syllabuses, and a tutor who is excellent in one corner may be only average in another. A student's problem usually sits in a specific syllabus, level and assessment style.

The IB includes the Primary Years Programme (PYP), the Middle Years Programme (MYP) and the Diploma Programme (DP). MYP subjects are assessed against four criteria (A to D), each scored 0 to 8, with the total out of 32 converting to a 1 to 7 grade. The DP is a two-year course of six subjects, three or four at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL), with Theory of Knowledge (TOK), the Extended Essay (EE) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) alongside.

IGCSE in India means Cambridge IGCSE (Cambridge International Education) or Pearson Edexcel International GCSE, usually a two-year course across Grades 9 and 10. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November; Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers: Core tops out at grade C, while Extended reaches A*.

Those differences are not trivia. A tutor who has taught only Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 may not know the shape of Pearson Edexcel's 4MA1 papers. A tutor strong in DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) may be less comfortable with Applications and Interpretation (AI), which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. Ask any route the same plain question: which syllabus, which level, which recent students of this type? The answer, or the lack of one, tells you most of what you need.

## How Do You Compare Options Without Being Swayed by Marketing?

You compare options by testing each against the same short list of questions and by asking for evidence you can check, not for reassurance. Every route in this guide presents itself well; the useful information comes from specifics.

Use this numbered process as a stand-alone method for any provider:

1. Write down your child's board, programme, subject, level and the specific problem you want solved.
2. Ask each provider how they would diagnose the problem in the first session or two.
3. Ask for the tutor's experience with that exact syllabus and level, not a general subject.
4. Ask what a trial lesson looks like and what you will be able to judge from it.
5. Ask what happens if the fit is wrong: how quickly, and with what process, can you change?
6. Ask how progress will be reported and when you should expect to see evidence.
7. Check the practical details: location, travel, format, schedule and communication.

### What good answers sound like

Good answers are specific and cautious. "We would start with a short diagnostic on algebra and a timed section, then agree a plan for the first four weeks" is an answer. "We will make your child confident and score well" is not. Be especially careful with anyone who offers a guarantee, because no tutor can guarantee a grade, and with anyone who offers to write or edit an Internal Assessment or Extended Essay. Every IB internal assessment is the student's own work, and the IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to give feedback on one draft but does not allow anyone, tutor or parent, to write, rewrite or edit the student's submission.

## Home, Online or Hybrid: Which Format Fits Which Route?

Among home tuition options in Gurgaon, home, online and hybrid lessons all work with every route except the coaching centre, which sets its own format. The choice between them depends on the subject, the student and the household, and a good provider will say when online is the better answer.

## What Safety and Trust Questions Should You Ask Before a Tutor Enters Your Home?

You should ask who the tutor is, how their profile was verified, who else will be in the house during the lesson and how you will reach someone if a problem arises. Home tuition puts an adult in your child's space, and a sensible family treats that with the same care as any other arrangement.

Practical steps are not complicated. Meet or speak with the tutor before the first lesson. Ask what "verified" means for the provider: identity, qualifications, experience or all three. Have a parent or another adult in the home during lessons, particularly with younger students, and let the lesson happen in a shared room with the door open or a clear line of sight. Agree how communication happens, and keep it with parents for younger students. If a child reports discomfort at any point, take it seriously and stop the arrangement.

None of this is a reason to avoid home tuition. It is simply what a careful family does, and a provider who welcomes these questions is a better sign than one who finds them awkward.

## What Should a First Month With Any Tutor Look Like?

The first month with any tutor should look like a diagnostic, a plan and early evidence: a clear picture of where marks are lost, an agreed focus and some visible movement in the error log or timed work. If the first month is only repetition of what the school already did, the arrangement needs a conversation.

:::timeline A reasonable first month with a new tutor
Week 1 | Diagnostic task and a shared error log
Week 2 | One focus topic and a timed section
Week 3 | Review, adjust the plan, set the next target
Week 4 | Check evidence and decide whether to continue
:::

At the end of week four, ask three questions. Can the student explain something they could not before? Has a timed mark moved, even slightly? Does the tutor know what the next four weeks should target? If the answer to all three is no, raise it with the tutor first, then ask for a different match. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the main home tuition options in Gurgaon?

The main options are a matching platform, a coaching centre, a freelance tutor found by referral and an online marketplace. A platform shortlists tutors for you, a centre offers fixed batches, a freelancer is arranged directly and a marketplace lets you browse and book. Each shifts work and risk differently, and the best choice depends on the student, subject and how much of the searching you want to do.

### Is a coaching centre better than a home tutor for IB or IGCSE?

Not in general. A centre suits students who need structure, peers and practice volume and who are broadly on track. A home or online tutor suits students with specific gaps or a niche subject, because feedback can be built around their own work. IB assessment rewards individual feedback on criteria and extended writing, which large batches find hard to give. Many families use both.

### Is a recommended freelance tutor safer than a platform?

A recommendation from a family you trust is valuable, but it is not the same as verification. It tells you the tutor suited one child in one syllabus. A platform adds screening, a process for a different match and a point of contact, though quality depends on the platform. Ask any route what verification means and what happens if the fit is wrong.

### Do online marketplaces work for IB subjects?

They can, particularly for specialist subjects where the best tutor is not local. The work of checking curriculum fit falls to you, and ratings do not transfer between syllabuses. Ask the tutor about the exact syllabus and level, request a trial lesson and look for specific answers instead of general reassurance. Online lessons work especially well for mathematics and sciences with a shared whiteboard.

### Will IB Gram promise a particular grade?

No, and no tutor or platform honestly can. Grades depend on the student's work, the school's teaching, the paper on the day and grade boundaries. Say so early, give specific feedback and ask for a change. With IB Gram, the family can request a different match. With a coaching centre, you can usually change batches. ### Is home tuition better than online tuition?

Neither is better in general. Home tuition suits younger students, households with quiet space and subjects where seeing paper working helps. Online suits mathematics and sciences, specialists who are far away and busy exam periods. Many families combine both across a year. A good provider will tell you when online is the better answer for a particular student, even in the same neighbourhood.

### Do we need a tutor at all?

Not always. If a student is on track, has clear feedback at school and good study habits, extra tuition may add cost and load without much benefit. A tutor helps most with a named gap, a switch of board, a niche subject or the need for specific, actionable feedback. Ask what is missing before deciding, and consider a short diagnostic first.

### How do we check that a tutor really knows the IB or IGCSE syllabus?

Ask for the syllabus, level and recent students of a similar profile, and ask how they handle command terms, mark schemes or criteria. A tutor who knows the syllabus answers with specifics, such as paper structure or criteria names. Use a short trial lesson to see how they respond to your child's own work, which tells you more than any profile.
