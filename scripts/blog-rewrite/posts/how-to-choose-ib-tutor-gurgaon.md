Everything else, including the profile photo, the claimed years of experience and the confident promises, is secondary.

## The Short Version: What a Good Match Looks Like

A good match has four visible features, and you can check all four within the first two weeks.

First, the tutor can say in plain language what your child is currently assessed on. For MYP that means the four criteria; for the DP it means papers, the Internal Assessment (IA) and the weightings. Second, the first session produced a diagnosis with two or three named priorities rather than a general impression. Third, there is homework or practice between sessions that the student can do alone. Fourth, after a few weeks there is something you can look at that was not there before: a corrected attempt, a cleaner method, a criterion that moved.

If one of these is missing at week three, raise it. If two are missing, change the plan or the tutor.

:::steps The five checks in order
Fit | Programme, subject, level, school year
Diagnosis | What is actually going wrong
Method | How sessions are structured
Evidence | What you can see after three weeks
Logistics | Whether the week survives
:::

## Why Choosing an IB Tutor Is Different From Choosing a Tuition Teacher

Most tuition in India is built around a single board, a fixed syllabus and a predictable answer key. IB is not. Marks in the DP come from papers marked against mark schemes and command terms, from an IA that the student must author alone, and from a points system that adds Theory of Knowledge (TOK) and the Extended Essay (EE). In the MYP, every subject is judged against four criteria scored 0 to 8 and converted to a 1 to 7 grade. The PYP has no external exams at all.

That structure changes what a tutor needs to know. A strong subject graduate who has never taught the IB will often over-teach content and under-teach the thing that costs marks: how the question is worded, what the command term demands, how the answer must be set out. A tutor from a coaching background may drill speed on questions that the IB does not ask in that form.

The consequence for parents is practical. Ask the tutor to teach you something small about how your child's programme assesses work, in two minutes, without jargon. Someone who has taught it can do this at once. Someone who has not will talk about hours, results and teaching style instead.

## What Credentials Should You Actually Check?

Check three things: direct IB teaching or tutoring experience at the right level, subject mastery beyond the level taught, and a verifiable identity. Degrees and certificates matter less than whether the tutor can explain how marks are earned in your child's subject. Years of experience are a weak signal on their own, because ten years of repeating a poor method is not experience worth paying for.

Here is how to read each type of claim.

| Claim | What it tells you | What to ask next |
|---|---|---|
| "Taught IB for several years" | Familiarity, possibly | Which programme and which subject, and with which kind of student? |
| "IB examiner or moderator" | Marking insight, if true | Which subject and session? Can they show how they read a script? |
| "Strong degree in the subject" | Content mastery | Have they taught the IB version of the course, not just the subject? |
| "Past students scored well" | Nothing checkable | Ask for the method, not the scores |
| "Trained teacher" | Pedagogy | Do they plan from diagnosis, or from a fixed set of notes? |

Be careful with scores. A tutor who quotes student results is describing students you cannot verify, and results depend far more on the student than on the tutor. A better question is: "Tell me about a student who was stuck at the same stage as my child and what you changed." Listen for a specific change in method, not a happy ending.

On the platform side, IB Gram asks families for board, programme, level, subject, school calendar, location and schedule, and shortlists tutors whose profiles have been verified and who fit. Whichever route you take, ask what verification means in practice: what documents, what conversation, what sample teaching.

## MYP Versus DP: What the Tutor Must Know for Each

The programmes are different jobs. A tutor excellent at one can be ordinary at the other.

**MYP (roughly ages 11 to 16, MYP 1 to 5).** Every subject is assessed on four criteria scored 0 to 8, and the total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through grade boundaries. In Mathematics the criteria are Knowing and understanding, Investigating patterns, Communicating, and Applying mathematics in real-life contexts. A tutor who only teaches procedures will lift criterion A and leave B, C and D flat. Many schools also split MYP 4 and 5 mathematics into standard and extended; extended content prepares a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA). Ask the tutor which track your child is on and how that changes what they teach. There is no single national MYP exam in India, and MYP eAssessment in MYP 5 is optional and decided by each school, so a tutor who promises "board exam preparation" for MYP is describing something that may not exist for your child.

**DP (two years, DP1 and DP2).** Six subjects, three or four at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL), each graded 1 to 7. TOK and the EE add up to 3 points, so the maximum is 45, and 24 is the minimum for the diploma, subject to failing conditions. A DP tutor needs paper-level knowledge: how Paper 1 differs from Paper 2, where the IA fits, what the current subject guide says. In DP sciences, for example, the investigation is the IA at 20% and is marked on four criteria of 6 marks each; in mathematics the exploration is also 20%, marked out of 20.

**PYP (ages 3 to 12).** There are no external exams and no published PYP grades. Support is about concepts, reading, number sense and confidence within units of inquiry. A tutor who brings worksheets and drills is solving a problem the programme does not have.

:::stats What differs by programme
0-8 | MYP score per criterion
1-7 | Grade scale, MYP and DP
45 | Maximum DP points including core
0 | External PYP exams
:::

If your child is moving from MYP 5 into DP1, ask the tutor specifically how they handle that transition. It is the point where capable MYP students most often discover that the habits that earned a 6 do not scale. The longer discussion of timing is in [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/).

## How Do You Know If a Tutor Can Diagnose Before Teaching?

A tutor diagnoses when the first session is mostly questions, short tasks and listening, and ends with a written summary naming two or three specific gaps. If the first hour is a lecture or a worksheet from a fixed pack, there was no diagnosis, however friendly the session felt.

When you are working out how to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon, this is the test that matters most. A real diagnostic has four parts: a conversation about what the student finds hard, a look at two or three pieces of recent marked work, a handful of carefully chosen fresh questions, and the student explaining their own method aloud. The aim is to separate causes that look identical on a report card: a missing prerequisite, careless reading of the command term, poor time use, weak correction habits, anxiety, or a gap in writing rather than in knowledge.

Consider a typical MYP 5 student scoring 4 in criterion C (Communicating) while criterion A is fine. A weak tutor assigns more problems. A good one notices the student can get correct answers but writes steps in an order that hides the reasoning, then builds a routine around structuring a response. Same student, completely different plan.

Ask for the summary in writing, or at least in a message after session one. It should contain a priority, the evidence for it and what the next two weeks will do about it. "Needs more practice" is not a diagnosis. "Loses marks on 'show that' questions by skipping the line that proves the result" is.

## The Trial Lesson: A Script You Can Run

The trial is where most families either learn everything or learn nothing, depending on what they watch. A friendly demo that ends with "he is a smart boy, just needs practice" has told you the tutor is pleasant.

Run the trial this way.

1. Before the session, send one recent marked piece of work and the school's current unit topic. Note whether the tutor reads it before arriving.
2. In the first ten minutes, listen for questions about how the student approached the work, not about marks.
3. Watch for one small teaching moment: the tutor explains a single idea, then immediately hands the student a related question to attempt alone.
4. Notice what happens when the student gets something wrong. Good tutors ask a question that exposes the error; weak ones correct it instantly.
5. In the last five minutes, check that the student states what they will do before the next session.
6. Afterwards, ask your child three things: what became clearer, what did you have to work out yourself, and what are you supposed to do next?
7. Within a day, expect a short message from the tutor with the diagnosis and a proposed plan.

The student's answers matter more than your own impression. A child who says "he explained everything" has been talked at. A child who says "she made me try it first and then showed me where I went wrong" has been taught.

## What Red Flags Should End the Conversation?

Seven behaviours end the conversation or at least pause the booking.

:::bars Red flags, ranked by how quickly I would walk away (illustrative judgement)
Offers to write or fix the IA | 100 | Walk away
Guarantees a grade | 90 | Walk away
Fixed package before seeing any work | 70 | Push back
No questions about the school or calendar | 60 | Push back
Only quotes past student scores | 45 | Probe
Cannot describe a session structure | 40 | Probe
Rankings here are a judgement, not data. Anything touching authorship should end the talk at once.
:::

The first two deserve a longer word.

**Anything touching authorship.** The IB's academic integrity rules are clear that the IA, the EE and other assessed pieces must be the student's own work. A teacher may comment on one draft; nobody may write, rewrite or edit the submission. A tutor who offers to "polish" an IA is offering to put your child's diploma at risk. A good tutor will teach the skill, point at the criterion and ask the student to reread their own paragraph against it. The practical boundary is discussed at length in the [IB Internal Assessment guide](/blog/ib-internal-assessment-guide/).

**Grade guarantees.** No tutor can guarantee a grade. Outcomes depend on the starting point, attendance, independent work, the school's assessment and plain luck on exam day. A tutor who promises a 7 is either inexperienced or selling, and both are reasons to look elsewhere.

The remaining flags are softer. A fixed package before the tutor has seen any work means the plan was designed for the tutor's calendar and not your child's gap. No questions about the school calendar means the tutor will not plan around tests, mocks and the IA deadline. Heavy discounts for paying months upfront shift the risk onto you, which matters in the first term when you do not yet know whether the fit is real.

## Comparing Tutors: A Scoring Table You Can Use

When you have two or three candidates, memory blurs. Score each against the same criteria immediately after the trial.

| Criterion | What a 2 looks like | What a 0 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Programme knowledge | Names criteria or papers without prompting | Speaks generally about "the IB" |
| Diagnosis | Written summary with named priorities | "He just needs practice" |
| Session structure | Describes explain, attempt, feedback, follow-up | "We go through the chapter" |
| Feedback | Marks specific errors and requires a redo | Corrects silently, moves on |
| Integrity stance | Volunteers the authorship boundary | Vague about IA help |
| Calendar awareness | Asks for test, mock and IA dates | Does not ask |
| Honest limits | Says what tutoring cannot do | Promises improvement |

Score each from 0 to 2 and total out of 14. A tutor under 9 is usually not ready for your child, however likeable. Do not let price decide between two tutors scoring within a point of each other; the cheaper one may have a slower plan, and the dearer one may be wasting your money on travel time. ## Home, Online or Hybrid: Choosing the Mode in Gurgaon

Mode is a fit question, not a quality ranking. The tutor's teaching matters more than the delivery channel, but the wrong channel can quietly kill a good plan.

**Home sessions** suit younger students, students who need routine and a physical workspace, and families who value a tutor seeing the child's real working environment. In societies with gate entry, such as those along Golf Course Road, in DLF phases and Sushant Lok, agree in advance how visitors are registered, or the first ten minutes of every session disappear. Weekday evening traffic on the main corridors can turn a tutor's arrival time into a coin toss, so ask honestly how far they travel and what happens in bad weather.

**Online sessions** suit specialists who are not nearby, students with a packed weekday who can use a slot a tutor can only offer remotely, and families abroad or travelling. Good online IB teaching uses a pen tablet or a shared whiteboard, saved work and, for maths, the graphic display calculator (GDC) on screen. A one-way video lecture is a poor imitation. The structure of good online teaching is covered in [online IB tutoring in India](/blog/online-ib-tutoring-india/).

**Hybrid** combines both: a longer in-person session for difficult concepts and shorter online reviews for corrections or timed practice. ## Should You Choose One-to-One, a Group Class or a Coaching Centre?

Pick one-to-one when the student's gap is specific, when the school pace does not match the student, or when the subject depends on a personal diagnosis, as in maths, sciences and IA supervision. Pick a group class when the content is shared and the student only needs structure. Self-study works for the strong, disciplined student who needs resources rather than explanation.

One-to-one is not automatically better. A mediocre one-to-one tutor who talks for the whole hour is worse than a well-run small group where the student attempts questions. What one-to-one gives you is the ability to adjust, which only helps if the tutor uses it. ## How Do You Judge Fees Without Comparing Rupee Figures?

Judge fees by what each session contains and what the tutor does outside it, not by the headline number. Two tutors at the same quoted rate can differ by a factor of two in value once you count preparation, feedback on written work, travel time and how much of the hour is the student working.

Fee drivers include the programme, HL versus SL, the subject, the tutor's depth and track record, whether travel to your home is built in, session length and any packages. Rather than comparing quotes, ask three questions of every tutor. What do you prepare before each session? How do you give feedback on work between sessions, if at all? What happens if the student has to cancel at short notice?

## How Often Should Sessions Happen, and When Should You Start?

Start with the smallest frequency that can address the identified gap, usually one session a week, and review after three or four weeks. Add sessions only when the student is finishing the between-session work and there is a clear reason, such as a mock series or an IA deadline. More hours without more independent practice is the most common way to spend money and learn little.

Timing matters as much as frequency. A student starting in the first term of DP1 has room to build a foundation. A student starting six weeks before DP2 mocks is doing triage. Both can be right decisions, but they need different plans and different expectations. ## What Does Personalised Teaching Look Like in Practice?

Personalised teaching means the plan changes because of what the student did, not because of a label. Three observable signs: the tutor's questions in session two depend on mistakes from session one, the practice set is built from your child's errors and not from a stock pack, and the plan has a review point where the tutor is willing to change course.

For the MYP, that includes planning around the four criteria rather than around chapters. For the DP, it includes the school's assessment calendar, the IA timeline and the HL or SL mix. A tutor who treats every Year 12 student alike and every Year 13 student alike is running a class with one child in it.

Ask how the diagnostic changes the plan after four weeks. A confident answer includes a specific example and a way to re-test. ## How Does Subject Choice Affect the Tutor You Need?

The subject combination changes the tutoring load. A student with three HL sciences and maths needs a very different weekly plan from one with two HL humanities and an HL language. Where a student is still deciding between Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation, or between HL and SL, tutor selection can wait; making the subject choice with the school's DP coordinator comes first.

## What Should Parents Do Between Sessions?

Parents should do less than most expect. Your job is to protect the slot, protect sleep and ask three questions a week: what did you work on, what was hard, what is next. You are not the subject supervisor, and turning into one corrodes the tutor-student relationship.

Concretely, do four things. Keep a shared folder where the student saves each session's work. Check once a fortnight that corrected attempts exist next to the originals. Keep school assessment dates visible on one calendar. Tell the tutor early about any change in school load or health.

Avoid three habits: ask the student to repeat the lesson to you, hunt for marks after each test, or compare progress with friends' children. None of these change learning, and all of them increase pressure. If a student seems to be improving in understanding but the test marks lag, give it two assessment cycles before drawing a conclusion.

## How Do You Know If It Is Working After a Month?

It is working if you can point to three things: the student starts tasks with less prompting, the same type of error appears less often, and there is a corrected attempt that shows the student changed method, not only the answer. Grades often lag behind these signals by a few weeks.

It is not working if sessions are pleasant but nothing in the student's own work has changed, if the tutor does most of the writing, or if the homework is skipped without consequences. Raise it directly. A good tutor welcomes the conversation and adjusts; a defensive one has told you what you need to know.

| Signal | Healthy | Worrying |
|---|---|---|
| Session talk time | Student talks and writes at least half | Tutor talks most of the hour |
| Between-session work | Set, done, reviewed | Rarely set or never reviewed |
| Errors | Named, tracked, shrinking | Corrected silently, repeating |
| Student attitude | Willing to attempt before being shown | Waits for the answer |
| Communication | Short updates with evidence | Only "doing well, needs practice" |

If you see the worrying column twice in a row, ask for a plan reset: a mini diagnostic, a revised priority list and a date to review. If the tutor cannot produce one, switching is reasonable. IB Gram lets families ask for a different match, subject to tutor availability.

## What If the Tutor Is Good But the Student Is Resisting?

Resistance usually has one of four causes: the student was not part of the decision, the sessions feel like punishment, the tutor's style does not fit, or the workload is already too heavy. Fix the cause before changing the tutor.

Involve the student in the trial. Let them say what they want from sessions in their own words. For older DP students, make the plan theirs: they choose the priorities from the diagnostic. For MYP students, a shorter session with a clear goal often works better than a long one. If a student is overloaded, the answer may be fewer sessions, not more, and a conversation with the school about timing of assessments.

Sometimes the honest answer is that the student does not need a tutor right now. Strong students with good school teaching and a clear plan can do very well without one, and a family that waits and watches for a term loses nothing.

## What Should You Share With the Tutor Before the First Session?

Share more than you think necessary, because every item prevents a wasted first lesson. A useful briefing contains the programme and year, the subject and level, the school's calendar for the next term, recent marked work, the current topic, the student's own description of what is hard, what has been tried and what the student enjoys.

Also share the logistics: preferred mode, available days, a quiet space, a device that works if online, and a clear policy for cancellations. Confirm the tutor's stance on academic integrity in writing, in one line. This is the boring part of the process, and it prevents most disputes.

When you contact IB Gram, the same details help: board, programme, level, subject, school calendar, location and schedule. A specific brief produces a shorter, more useful shortlist than a general one.

## What Is a Realistic Timeline for the Decision?

Most families handle this badly because they either decide in a day, under pressure from a bad test result, or drag the process out for weeks while the student's problem grows. A reasonable timeline for how to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon is two weeks from first enquiry to a decision.

1. Day 1: Write down the programme, subject, level, school dates and the three things that worry you.
2. Days 2 to 4: Send the brief; read profiles; shortlist two or three tutors.
3. Days 5 to 8: Run one trial with each, using the script above, and score them on the table.
4. Day 9: Ask your child which one they would prefer to work with and why.
5. Day 10: Choose; agree a four-week start and a review date in writing.
6. Weeks 3 to 4: Check the evidence trail; confirm or reset.

The four-week start is the key move. It turns a large commitment into a small, reviewable one, and it makes it easier for both sides to be honest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon if I know nothing about the IB?

Ask the tutor to explain how your child is assessed, in plain words, in two minutes. A tutor who has taught the programme can do this immediately, naming criteria for MYP or papers and the IA for the DP. You do not need to judge the subject content; judge the clarity, the questions they ask about your child and whether they offer a diagnosis before a package. The school's coordinator can confirm anything you are unsure of.

### How many tutors should I interview before deciding?

Two or three is enough. More than that and the comparison blurs, while fewer than two leaves you with nothing to compare against. Run the same trial script with each, score them on the same table, and ask your child for an opinion. If none of the first three feels right, the problem may be the brief you sent and not the tutors, so tighten it before searching further.

### Is a trial lesson enough to judge a tutor?

No, but it is enough to eliminate poor fits. A trial shows communication, subject command and how the tutor responds to errors. It cannot show whether progress is real. Use it to filter, then judge the chosen tutor on evidence after three or four weeks: corrected attempts, fewer repeated errors and more independent starts. Agree a review date when you begin so neither side feels trapped.

### Should I pick a tutor who works at my child's school?

Not automatically, and check the school's rules first. Some schools discourage or restrict teachers from tutoring their own students, and a conflict of interest around assessment is a real concern. A tutor from outside the school can be more useful because they work for the student, not the school's timetable. Whatever you choose, make sure the arrangement is transparent to the school if it asks.

### How much homework should a tutor set?

Enough to practise what was taught, and no more than the student can finish alongside school. For most students, that means one to three short tasks between weekly sessions. The tutor should check the work next time and use errors to shape the following plan. If no homework is set, the lesson was an isolated event. If heaps are set and never reviewed, the tutor is generating busywork.

### Can a tutor help with the Internal Assessment?

Yes, within limits. A tutor can teach the skills the IA needs, explain the criteria, question the student's reasoning and ask them to reread their own work against a criterion. A tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission. The IB treats the work as the student's alone, and the school checks for similarity. Anyone offering to write or polish an IA is a risk to your child's diploma.

### What if my child and the tutor do not get along?

Raise it early and ask for a different match. Chemistry matters because a student who dreads the session will not do the follow-up work. Give it two or three sessions, since shyness is normal, but do not wait a term. IB Gram lets families request a different tutor match, subject to availability, and a good tutor will not take a change personally.

### Does a more expensive tutor mean a better one?

No. Price tracks experience, subject, level, travel and session length, and only sometimes tracks teaching quality. Judge value by what each session contains and what the tutor does between sessions. A higher rate is justified when it buys diagnosis, written feedback and criterion-level knowledge you can see. Compare tutors on the scoring table first and on price second.

### Is online as good as home tutoring for IB?

For many students, yes, provided the tutor uses a shared whiteboard or pen tablet and the student writes during the session. Online suits specialists who are not nearby and students with tight weekdays. Home suits younger students and those who need routine and a physical workspace. Hybrid often works best across a DP year. The decision is about the student, not about one mode being better.

### How long should I stay with a tutor before deciding it is not working?

Give it four weeks, then review against evidence. By that point you should see a diagnosis, named priorities, corrected attempts and some independent starts. If none of these exist, ask for a reset. If the tutor cannot reset, switch. Do not wait for a test result as proof, because results lag learning and a single test is noisy.
