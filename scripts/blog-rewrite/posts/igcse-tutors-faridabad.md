## What Does a Switch From CBSE or ICSE to IGCSE Actually Change?

A board switch changes how knowledge is tested far more than which knowledge is tested. The common differences a switching student meets are criterion-referenced or command-term marking instead of a fixed answer key, coursework and internal tasks that carry real weight in some subjects, wider reading and independent research, and less emphasis on memorised derivations.

In plain terms, here is what that looks like for a student sitting down to do homework in the first weeks of Grade 9 or Grade 10.

- **The question asks for reasoning, not recall.** "Explain why", "suggest", "describe how" and "calculate" each demand something different. Writing everything you know about the topic earns nothing if it does not answer the verb.
- **Marks are given for steps.** In maths and science calculations, the method is rewarded separately from the answer. A correct answer without working may earn less than a wrong answer with sound method.
- **Data and practical skills are examined.** Science papers ask the student to handle tables, graphs and experimental designs, and some include a practical test or an alternative-to-practical paper.
- **The exam series are different.** Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. Schools decide which series they enter students for.
- **Course structure may differ by subject and tier.** Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers: Core tops out at grade C, Extended reaches A*. Edexcel maths is tiered Foundation or Higher.

Nothing here is a criticism of CBSE or ICSE. The boards are built for different purposes. The point is that a student trained to one pattern needs to be re-trained to another, and it is quicker with someone who knows both.

## IGCSE Tutors in Faridabad: Where the Transition Gaps Show Up First

Across subjects, the first gaps tend to appear in the same four places.

| Gap | What it looks like | Why a switch exposes it |
|---|---|---|
| Command words | Writes a paragraph when "state" needed a phrase, or a phrase when "explain" needed a chain | Earlier boards often reward recall of the textbook line |
| Method marks | Final answer only, few steps | Answer-key marking trained answer-first habits |
| Data skills | Misreads axes, ignores units, cannot plot cleanly | Less data handling in the earlier curriculum |
| Extended writing | One-line answers to multi-mark questions | Short-answer drilling |

A good tutor names which of the four are present, in which subject, and builds the first month around them. A tutor who simply "covers the syllabus" will miss them.

## Maths: The Biggest Adjustment for Many Switchers

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams): Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator); Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier.

For a switching student, the maths gap is rarely about being unable to do the mathematics. It is about the following.

### Showing working that earns method marks

A CBSE student may be used to getting the answer and moving on. At IGCSE, each step is a potential mark. The tutor trains the habit of writing the equation, the substitution and the result on separate lines, even when the arithmetic is easy.

Consider a student solving a simultaneous-equations question. Writing only x = 3, y = 2 scores the final marks if correct, but if one value is wrong, no marks for method are available. Writing the elimination step gives the examiner something to credit.

### The non-calculator paper

Non-calculator papers test fluency in fractions, surds, algebra and number. A student used to relying on a calculator during practice may find the non-calculator paper slow and error-prone. The remedy is a short daily drill by hand, kept for a few minutes, not an hour at the weekend.

### Tier and topic differences

Topics may be sequenced differently from the earlier board. Rather than assume which topics are new, a tutor should lay the 0580 or 4MA1 syllabus beside the student's earlier notes and mark what has been seen, what has been seen differently and what is new. Typical gaps are in statistics and probability presentation, function notation, transformations and vectors, but this differs by school, so check the syllabus rather than guess. Families can also read [IGCSE Maths 0580 and 0606: a parent guide](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/) for the syllabus structure and the Additional Mathematics decision.

### A sample first-month maths plan

1. **Week 1.** Diagnostic on fresh 0580 or 4MA1 questions, covering both calculator and non-calculator styles. Mark by the board's method-mark logic, not by answer alone.
2. **Week 2.** Repair the largest prerequisite gap, often algebraic manipulation or fractions.
3. **Week 3.** Introduce exam-style questions on the current school topic, with worked examples written in full.
4. **Week 4.** First timed set of 30 minutes, debriefed line by line. Start an error log.

## Sciences: Practical Skills and Written Reasoning

Cambridge IGCSE sciences (syllabuses examined from 2023): Paper 1 multiple choice (Core) or Paper 2 multiple choice (Extended), Paper 3 theory (Core) or Paper 4 theory (Extended), and practical assessment through Paper 5 (practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), chosen by the school. Weighting: multiple choice 30%, theory 50%, practical 20%. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with Paper 1 and Paper 2 for each separate science and practical skills assessed inside the written papers.

### What a switching science student usually lacks

**Practical and data handling.** The practical paper or its alternative asks the student to plan, record, tabulate, graph and evaluate. A student who has done fewer practicals, or who did them without writing up measurements and uncertainties, can lose marks in 20% of the assessment before touching the theory.

**Written explanation.** "Explain" questions need chains: cause, mechanism, consequence. A student trained to produce definitions may write a true fact that does not answer the question.

**Calculations with units.** Many IGCSE calculation questions give a mark for the formula, one for substitution and one for the answer with a unit.

### Subject notes

- **Biology 0610** covers a broad list of topics, from classification and cell movement to transport, coordination, inheritance and biotechnology. The adjustment is reading data and writing structured explanations. Pearson Edexcel equivalent: 4BI1.
- **Chemistry 0620** includes stoichiometry (including the mole), electrochemistry, energetics, organic chemistry and chemical analysis. The mole and the practical analysis techniques are the common stumbling blocks. Pearson Edexcel equivalent: 4CH1.
- **Physics 0625** covers motion, forces and energy, thermal physics, waves, electricity and magnetism, nuclear physics and space physics, and expects students to recall and rearrange the equations in the syllabus. Pearson Edexcel equivalent: 4PH1.

A tutor for science at this stage should combine concept teaching with a regular data question and a regular practical-skills question. The subject-level reasons students stall are described in [why students struggle in IGCSE Science](/blog/igcse-science-tutoring-guide/).

## English: Three Different Syllabuses Under One Name

English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses. Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500 has a reading paper, then directed writing and composition or a coursework portfolio. English as a Second Language 0510 and 0511 cover reading and writing plus listening; 0511 counts speaking into the grade, while 0510 reports speaking separately. Literature in English is 0475. Pearson Edexcel offers International GCSE English Language A (4EA1), English Language B (4EB1) and English as a Second Language (4ES1).

For a switching student the first job is to confirm *which* English the school has chosen, because the skills differ.

- A CBSE or ICSE student with strong literature habits may need to learn **directed writing**: writing for a given purpose and audience from a stimulus text.
- A student accustomed to grammar exercises may need to shift to **extended composition** and to explaining the effect of language choices.
- A student who has never been assessed on listening or speaking may need rehearsal for those components in the second-language syllabuses.

Tutoring should therefore start with a written sample, a reading task and a short conversation, and then shape the plan. ## Economics, Business Studies and Computer Science: New Subjects for Many Switchers

Some students meet these subjects for the first time at IGCSE.

**Economics 0455.** Paper 1 is multiple choice (30%) and Paper 2 is structured questions with data (70%). Six topics: the basic economic problem; the allocation of resources; microeconomic decision-makers; government and the macroeconomy; economic development; international trade and globalisation. Pearson Edexcel's 4EC1 has Paper 1 Microeconomics and business economics, and Paper 2 Macroeconomics and the global economy. A student coming from a commerce stream elsewhere may find the diagrams and data response the new part.

**Business Studies 0450.** Paper 1 (short answer and data response) and Paper 2 (case study) are 50% each across six areas, including marketing, operations, financial information and external influences. Calculations include break-even, cash-flow forecasts, profit and ratios. Pearson Edexcel's 4BS1 uses Paper 1 on small businesses and Paper 2 on large businesses.

**Computer Science 0478.** Paper 1 covers computer systems; Paper 2 covers algorithms, programming and logic. The pre-release material was removed, and practice uses a language such as Python, Java or Visual Basic. Edexcel's 4CP0 pairs a written paper with an on-screen practical programming paper in Python.

For a student new to these subjects, early tutoring should build vocabulary, diagrams and case-study reading habits rather than rush past papers. ## How Do You Tell a Real Gap From an Adjustment Wobble?

Most students who switch board have a rough first term. Some of that is simply adjustment, and it settles by itself. Some is a genuine gap that will cost marks all the way to the exam series. The distinction decides whether tutoring is needed.

**Likely an adjustment wobble:** the student's marks dip in the first test, then recover in the second as they learn the style; the student can explain mistakes once shown; the student is engaged and asking questions.

**Likely a real gap:** the same error recurs in test after test; the student cannot explain a correct answer they copied; the student avoids a subject; the marks stay flat despite effort.

A simple test: ask the student to redo a question they got wrong, a week later, without help. If they can, it was adjustment. If they cannot, there is a gap to fix.

### The evidence a parent can gather without being a subject expert

- The first two marked tests, with the examiner's comments if any.
- Two or three questions the student got wrong, and a retry a week later.
- The student's own description of what feels unfamiliar.

Bring these to the first tutor conversation. A tutor who asks for them is working properly; a tutor who sells a package without seeing them is not.

## A Ten-Week Transition Plan for a Switching Student

A realistic plan for a student who joins IGCSE at the start of Grade 9 or early in Grade 10. Adapt it to the school's calendar, and confirm the exam series with the school.

:::timeline Ten weeks to settle into the IGCSE style
Weeks 1-2 | Diagnostic in each tutored subject; map syllabus against earlier notes
Weeks 3-4 | Repair prerequisites; learn command words and mark logic
Weeks 5-6 | Guided exam-style questions; start the error log
Weeks 7-8 | First timed sections; practical or data skills in sciences
Weeks 9-10 | Review against the diagnostic; decide what to continue
:::

### Weeks 1 and 2: diagnose

The tutor sets fresh questions from the actual syllabus, in the board's style, and watches how the student attempts them. The aim is two or three named priorities per subject.

### Weeks 3 and 4: repair and decode

The prerequisite gaps are mended and the student learns to read the question. A good exercise: give a past-paper question and ask the student to rewrite it in their own words, then identify the command word and the number of marks, and state what a full-mark answer would contain before writing it.

### Weeks 5 and 6: guided application

The student attempts exam-style questions with decreasing prompts. Feedback is given against the mark scheme's language. The error log begins here: date, question, mistake type, fix.

### Weeks 7 and 8: timing and practical skills

Short timed sections begin. In science, a practical or data question is set weekly. In English, a timed piece of directed writing is attempted.

### Weeks 9 and 10: review

Part of the original diagnostic is repeated. Compare the evidence. Decide whether to continue at the same pace, reduce, or change focus.

## What if the Switch Happens Late, in Grade 10?

A late switch is harder, because the time is shorter and the exam series is closer. The plan must be triaged.

1. **Confirm the series and the tier** with the school; a Grade 10 student cannot choose the series independently of the school.
2. **Rank subjects by gap and weight**, and tutor the largest gaps first.
3. **Use the board's past papers early**, because the question style is the main adjustment.
4. **Protect sleep and school work.** An overloaded student loses more than extra sessions gain.
5. **Review fortnightly**, not termly.

For a revision structure in the last weeks, see the [six-week IGCSE revision plan](/blog/igcse-revision-six-week-plan/). Note that the February/March Cambridge series exists specifically for India and lets Grade 10 results arrive in time for Indian school admissions to Class 11 and for IB Diploma Programme (DP) or A Level entry in the same academic year, which can compress the calendar more than families expect.

## What a Good Tutor Does Differently for a Switching Student

A generic tutor teaches the syllabus. A tutor who understands the switch does something more specific.

- **Starts with a diagnostic in the board's style**, not the previous board's.
- **Teaches command words explicitly**, with examples from the board's papers.
- **Marks like an examiner**, giving the student the language of the mark scheme.
- **Builds an error log** and reviews it weekly.
- **Introduces practical and data skills early**, in sciences.
- **Manages the workload**, so the student is not overloaded by trying to catch up everywhere at once.
- **Explains the exam series and tiers** and says when a question is for the school to decide.
- **Builds independence**, with fewer prompts each week.

Ask a prospective tutor to describe their first month with a student like yours. The answer should mention a diagnostic, command words and mark schemes. If the answer is "we will cover the syllabus topic by topic", keep looking. ## Mistakes Families Make During a Board Switch

- **Hiring after the first bad test.** One test is noise; two with the same error pattern is a signal.
- **Hiring a tutor from the old board.** A tutor who knows CBSE well but has not handled IGCSE papers may reinforce answer-key habits.
- **Cramming past papers before understanding the style.** Start with the command words and mark schemes.
- **Adding hours instead of fixing the method.** A few focused sessions beat a crowded timetable.
- **Ignoring the school.** The school sets the tier, the series and the coursework rules.
- **Expecting a fast fix.** Habit change takes a few weeks of consistent practice.
- **Not keeping a record.** Without an error log, each session is a one-off.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it hard to switch from CBSE to IGCSE?

It is a real adjustment, but most students manage it within a term with the right support. The content overlaps in places; the difference is in how questions are asked and marked. Expect a dip in early test marks as the student learns command words, method marks and written explanation. If the same errors persist after a few weeks, a tutor can help close the specific gap.

### How long does it take to adjust to IGCSE marking?

For many students, six to ten weeks of practice with exam-style questions and mark-scheme feedback is enough to settle. Faster adjustment happens when the student learns the command words early and receives marked feedback each week. Slower adjustment is usually linked to gaps in basics or to too little practice. Use the first two tests as evidence.

### Do I need a tutor for every IGCSE subject?

No. Tutor the subjects with the largest gaps or the highest stakes, often maths and one or two sciences. A switching student who is comfortable in a subject after the first term may need none. Use the first test results and the student's own view to decide, and review each term. Avoid overloading the timetable.

### Should my child choose Core or Extended?

The school sets the tier, usually after assessing the student, and a tutor does not choose it. Core tops out at grade C and Extended reaches A*, so the tier matters for the ceiling. If you think the entry is wrong, speak to the subject teacher early, with evidence from tests, rather than late in the year.

### Is online tutoring good enough for a switching student?

Yes, if the tutor can see written work live and the student stays focused. For maths and sciences, use a pen tablet or a camera on the page. Some switching students, especially anxious ones, do better with a tutor in the room at first. A hybrid arrangement can start at home and move online as confidence grows.

### What is the difference between Cambridge and Edexcel for tutoring?

The papers, tiers and grading differ: Cambridge IGCSE uses Core and Extended tiers in many subjects and grades most syllabuses A*-G, with some 9-1 versions under different codes, while Pearson Edexcel International GCSE grades 9-1 and uses Foundation and Higher in maths. A tutor should know your board's papers and mark schemes. Confirm the board with the school before you start.

### My child understands in class but loses marks. Why?

Understanding is not the same as showing it on paper. Common causes are missing working, vague explanations, misreading command words and weak timing. Ask the tutor to mark a recent test and classify each lost mark by cause. The fix is usually technique and practice, not more content. Compare a repeat question a week later to see whether it has stuck.

### Can we switch tutor if it is not working?

Yes. Review after four to six sessions: is the error log shrinking, is the student more independent, is feedback specific? If not, ask for a change. IB Gram allows a family to request a different match, subject to tutor availability. Change one thing at a time so you can see what helped.

### When should a Grade 9 student start tutoring?

When a recurring gap appears, which for a switching student is often within the first term. Starting early with a light weekly session is more effective than waiting for Grade 10, when time is tighter and the exam series is closer. If the student is settling in well, wait and watch the first two tests.

### Will IGCSE grades be accepted for Indian university admission?

Requirements vary by university and course. The Association of Indian Universities issues equivalence for international qualifications, so check current requirements with the university or board concerned rather than relying on general advice. If the student is moving to Class 11 in an Indian board or into the IB DP or A Levels, confirm entry requirements with the receiving school.
