For an International Baccalaureate (IB) Middle Years Programme (MYP) student, the IB maths coaching vs private tutor decision comes down to one question: is the problem the same as the class's problem, or is it specific to your child? A coaching class is built to move a group through the syllabus at one pace with shared practice. A private tutor is built to move *one student* through *their* gaps. When your child's gap is broad and ordinary, such as general practice or exam stamina, a good class can be excellent and economical. When the gap is narrow and specific, for instance a student who understands the mathematics but loses most of the marks on communication or real-life application, a group class rarely has the room to find it.

This guide is for parents of MYP 3, 4 and 5 students in Gurgaon and elsewhere, particularly families at schools such as Pathways and Heritage, who are choosing between a group programme and one-to-one help. It compares the two fairly, using the four MYP maths criteria as the lens, and it explains pace, feedback, attention, cost drivers, scheduling and which kind of student suits which. It is written from the mentoring side of IB maths by Ajay Vatsyayan, IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, and it makes no claim that one format wins for everyone. Schools named here are context only.

## How MYP Maths Is Assessed, and Why It Matters for This Choice

The format you choose should match how your child is actually marked. IB Middle Years Programme (MYP) Mathematics is assessed against four criteria: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating, and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts. Each is scored 0-8. The criterion total out of 32 converts to a final 1-7 grade through IB grade boundaries. There is no single national MYP examination in India, so the school's own tasks carry the marking, and MYP eAssessment in MYP 5 (on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios) is optional, with each school deciding whether to offer it.

:::stats MYP Mathematics at a glance
4 | Criteria, A to D
0-8 | Score range per criterion
32 | Maximum criterion total
1-7 | Final MYP grade scale
:::

Why does that matter for coaching versus tutoring? Because a student can sit at the same final grade for very different reasons. Two students both at a 5 might have the following profiles.

| Student | A | B | C | D | What they need |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Student 1 | 7 | 4 | 4 | 4 | Investigation and communication, not more procedure |
| Student 2 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 5 | Stronger knowledge and understanding of core content |

The totals are chosen so the two profiles look similar on a report, and the figures are illustrative, not data about real children. The point is that the same final grade hides different gaps, and a class that teaches both students the same lesson helps one and bores the other. The grade-band mechanics are laid out in [how an MYP maths student moves from a 5 to a 6 or 7](/blog/how-can-an-ib-myp-maths-student-move-from-5-to-6-or-7/).

## What Is IB Maths Coaching, and What Is a Private Tutor?

**IB maths coaching** is a group arrangement in which several students learn together under one teacher, in person or online. It usually has a fixed timetable, a planned run through the syllabus, shared practice material and periodic tests. Size, quality and style vary a great deal between providers.

**A private tutor** is one teacher working with one student, at home or online, with lessons designed around that student's current level, school calendar and assessment tasks. The tutor can change pace and content from one week to the next.

Neither is a quality label. A weak tutor is worse than a good class, and a strong class is better than a poor tutor. The comparison below assumes both are competent, so you can see the structural differences.

## IB Maths Coaching vs Private Tutor: The Structural Differences

| Dimension | Coaching class | Private tutor |
|---|---|---|
| Pace | Set by the group | Set by the student's gaps |
| Content | Planned sequence, often ahead of or beside school | Follows school tasks and the student's weak points |
| Feedback | Mostly on tests and homework, in batches | Immediate, line by line, on the student's own working |
| Attention | Shared | Undivided |
| Peer learning | Yes: seeing other approaches | No, unless the tutor builds it in |
| Structure and routine | Strong, built in | Depends on the tutor and the family |
| Flexibility of schedule | Fixed batch timings | Usually negotiable |
| Cost drivers | Shared cost across the group | Tutor's time, experience and session length |
| Diagnosis | General, by test results | Specific, by criterion and error type |
| Risk | Student gets lost or coasts | Student becomes dependent on the tutor |

Two things stand out in this table. The strengths of coaching are *structure and shared practice*; the strengths of tutoring are *diagnosis and feedback*. Which you need depends on which is missing.

No rupee figures appear on this page, because fees depend on the provider, the tutor's experience, the level, session length, travel and mode, and they change. As a general principle, group coaching spreads a teacher's time across several students, so the cost per student tends to be lower, while one-to-one concentrates a tutor's time on one student, so the cost per session tends to be higher. The right comparison is cost per *useful* hour: an hour in which your child's specific gap is addressed against an hour spent on content they already know. ## When Does a Coaching Class Work Well for MYP Maths?

A coaching class works well when the student can keep the class's pace, wants regular structured practice and does not need extensive individual repair. It is not an inferior option, and for many students it is the right one.

Consider the profile of a student who suits a class:

- Reasonably secure foundations, with a few gaps rather than many.
- Comfortable asking questions in a group, or happy to learn by listening.
- Motivated by peers and a fixed weekly rhythm.
- Needs a routine more than a custom plan.
- Does well on routine problems and wants more mixed practice.

For this student, a class adds three things a lone student may not have: a timetable that creates the habit of study, a bank of practice questions, and the experience of seeing how classmates tackle the same problem. That last point has real educational value in a subject where Criterion B rewards noticing patterns, because hearing another student's route can reveal a pattern you had missed.

### The limits of a class for MYP

A class has trouble with four things in particular.

1. **Criterion-specific weaknesses.** A class paced for the average student cannot spend a week on one student's Criterion C habits.
2. **Unfamiliar problem practice.** Criteria B and D reward investigating and applying in new situations. In a large class, the teacher often has to demonstrate the method for the group, and the student may not get a real chance to struggle with an unseen problem.
3. **Feedback delay.** Written feedback on a test arrives days later, by which time the student has moved on.
4. **Confidence.** A student who is already anxious about maths may stay silent in a group, and the teacher may not notice the gap.

None of these is certain to happen, and a skilled teacher in a small class can reduce them. They are the points to check when you visit.

## When Does a Private Tutor Work Better for MYP Maths?

A private tutor works better when the gap is specific, the student has plateaued, confidence is low, or the school's tasks need individual attention. It is also the better choice when the student needs a different pace, whether faster or slower.

### The student with a specific criterion gap

Consider a student who earns high marks on Criterion A tasks but only middling marks on Criterion C. The student can do the mathematics but explains it badly. Group practice cannot address this, because the improvement comes from writing reasoning, receiving line-by-line feedback and rewriting. A tutor can ask, "Why is this step true?" and wait for the student to write the answer.

### The student who has plateaued

A student who has hovered at the same level for a year usually does not need more of the same. They need a diagnosis of *why*. A tutor can examine the student's actual work and find that the problem is, for example, that they stop investigating after finding the first pattern and never generalise. That is a habit, not a content gap, and it is fixed with targeted practice.

### The student who is behind or ahead

A student who has missed a term, or who is strong and bored, needs pace that a class cannot adjust. A tutor can fill gaps quickly or extend a student with extended-level material. For students in MYP 4 and MYP 5 where schools often split mathematics into standard and extended, with extended covering additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches, a tutor can help the student prepare for that route. See [MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics tutoring in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-myp-4-myp-5-mathematics-tutoring-gurgaon-pathways-heritage/) for the topic map.

### The anxious student

A student who freezes at tests benefits from individual attention, because the tutor can build confidence step by step and use an error log to show that mistakes are patterns, not verdicts. That work is covered in [MYP maths exam confidence](/blog/ajay-vatsyayan-sir-ib-myp-mathematics-exam-confidence-gurgaon/).

In the IB maths coaching vs private tutor comparison, the four criteria give a precise way to compare the formats.

| Criterion | What it rewards | Coaching class | Private tutor |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: Knowing and understanding | Accurate knowledge and correct use of mathematics | Strong: structured coverage and practice | Strong: can target specific content gaps |
| B: Investigating patterns | Exploring, spotting and generalising patterns | Mixed: depends on whether the class has time for open tasks | Strong: tutor can stage investigations and withdraw prompts |
| C: Communicating | Clear mathematical reasoning and notation | Weak to mixed: feedback is delayed and general | Strong: line-by-line feedback and rewriting |
| D: Applying in real-life contexts | Modelling and applying maths to realistic situations | Mixed: tasks are often shared and standardised | Strong: tutor can use the student's own school tasks (without doing them) |

This is a judgement about typical structure, not a verdict on any provider. A good class can include investigation and discussion; a poor tutor can drill procedure. The table says where each format is *naturally* strong.

## Ajay Vatsyayan's View: Match the Format to the Diagnosis

The question Ajay Vatsyayan, IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, starts from is not "class or tutor?" but "what is this student's actual gap?" His approach in IB maths, described in more detail for older students in [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/), is built on a genuine diagnosis of the student's own work, a system for analysing errors so that recurring ones are treated as trainable habits, and exam strategy training with timed practice. That logic applies to MYP too: find the pattern in the student's work first, and only then decide whether the support should be shared or individual.

In practice that means a short diagnostic before any commitment, whichever format a family prefers. The diagnostic uses fresh questions, asks the student to explain choices aloud, and ends in two or three named priorities. If those priorities are broad, a class may be enough. If they are specific, one-to-one is likely to be more efficient.

## How Do You Decide? A Five-Step Method

A short numbered process that works as a standalone answer to "how do I choose between an IB maths coaching class and a private tutor for my MYP child?"

1. **Get the criterion scores.** Ask the school for the child's A to D levels on recent tasks, not only the final grade.
2. **Find the pattern.** Is one criterion much lower than the others, or are all four similar? A single weak criterion points to a tutor; an even profile with general gaps points to either.
3. **Name the problem in one sentence.** For example, "He knows the content but does not write his reasoning", or "She is behind on algebra from last year".
4. **Try the format.** Book a trial in the format you lean towards and judge whether the specific problem was addressed, not whether the session was pleasant.
5. **Review after four to six weeks.** Compare the error log and the next task's criterion scores. Change one variable at a time.

:::steps Choosing between a class and a tutor
Scores | Collect criterion levels A to D
Pattern | One weak criterion or an even profile?
Problem | State the gap in one sentence
Trial | Test the format on that gap
Review | Compare evidence after four to six weeks
:::

## Questions to Ask a Coaching Class Before You Enrol

A fair IB maths coaching vs private tutor comparison comparison means testing the class as hard as you would test a tutor. Ask:

- How many students are in a batch, and does the teacher know each student's work?
- Is the teacher familiar with the MYP criteria, not only the mathematics?
- How is feedback given on student work, and how quickly?
- Do students attempt unfamiliar investigation and real-life tasks, or mainly routine exercises?
- What happens when a student falls behind or is far ahead?
- Can the student attend a trial class first?
- How is progress reported, and against which criteria?
- Does the class align with the school's current unit, or run on its own sequence?

A class that answers these clearly is probably worth a trial. A class that talks only about results from earlier students, with no description of method, deserves caution.

## Questions to Ask a Private Tutor Before You Commit

The same rigour applies to tutors.

- How will you diagnose my child's gap, and what will I see afterwards?
- How do you use the MYP criteria in your lessons?
- What does a session look like? How much of it does my child spend working?
- How do you handle school tasks, and what is the line between teaching a skill and doing the work?
- What do you set between sessions, and how do you review it?
- How will we know after a month whether it is working?
- Can we have a trial session?

A tutor who speaks in terms of criteria, evidence and independent work is describing a method. A tutor who promises a grade is not. ## Academic Integrity: What Neither Format Should Do

MYP tasks and the Personal Project are the student's own work. A class or a tutor may teach skills, explain criteria and give feedback, but must not complete or substantially rewrite assessed work. Where a school has rules about outside help on specific tasks, follow them. A tutor who offers to "just do the investigation" is putting the student at risk. This applies equally to a coaching class that provides model answers to be copied into a school task.

## Can You Use Both?

Yes, and many families do. A class can provide the weekly rhythm and shared practice, while a few one-to-one sessions target a specific criterion or prepare for a particular assessment. The combination works if one adult keeps track of the plan, so that the class and the tutor are not teaching conflicting methods.

A common pattern: class in the first term of the year for structure, a short block of tutoring before an important task or before MYP 5 assessments, and a return to the class afterwards. Another is tutoring for a student who has missed a term, then a return to a class once the gap is closed.

Be cautious about doing both at full intensity. A student who attends school, a class, a tutor and homework in the same week has little time to think, and thinking is what MYP maths rewards. ## Home, Online or Hybrid for the Format You Choose

The delivery mode is a separate decision from the format.

- **Home tutoring** suits students who need someone beside them to start work, and families who value face-to-face feedback. In Gurgaon, gate entry, evening traffic and school finish times affect scheduling, so agree a buffer.
- **Online tutoring or classes** suit organised students, families with busy weekdays and those who need a specialist who is not nearby. A pen tablet or a camera on the page keeps the working visible.
- **Hybrid** can combine the two, for example home sessions for habit-building and online sessions for quick doubt-solving.

## Signs a Coaching Class Is Not Working

Give a class a fair trial, then check for these signs after four to six weeks.

- The student's criterion scores have not moved.
- The student says the lessons are "too fast" or "too slow".
- The student copies solutions without understanding them.
- Homework is completed but the same errors recur.
- The student is quiet in class and does not ask questions.
- Feedback arrives too late to change the next task.

If several of these apply, add a short block of one-to-one help or change the format.

## Signs a Private Tutor Is Not Working

Likewise, check for these signs.

- The tutor does most of the talking and writing.
- The student cannot repeat a method independently a week later.
- The same errors appear in each session.
- There is no record of what was covered and no plan for the next session.
- The tutor offers to complete school tasks.
- The student becomes dependent and will not attempt questions alone.
- There is no review point.

If these apply, ask for a change of approach or a different match. IB Gram allows a family to request a different match, subject to tutor availability.

## MYP Maths and the Road to the Diploma Programme

For students in MYP 4 and MYP 5, the choice of support also affects readiness for the IB Diploma Programme (DP). The DP offers Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA), which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and Mathematics Applications and Interpretation (AI), which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. The school's DP coordinator makes the final placement, but a student who has built strong algebra and clear communication in MYP is better placed for either route.

A class that drills only routine procedure may leave a student surprised by the DP's unfamiliar questions. A tutor who builds conceptual understanding and communication habits can smooth the transition. ## A Worked Illustration: Two MYP 4 Students, Two Choices

These are composites to show reasoning, not accounts of real children.

**Asha** is in MYP 4. Her criterion levels are close together, around the middle of the scale, and her teacher says she needs more practice and exam-style fluency. She is comfortable in groups and enjoys discussing problems. A coaching class with a small batch, regular practice and weekly tests suits her. The parents agree to review after six weeks by looking at her next two criterion scores.

**Kabir** is also in MYP 4. His Criterion A level is high, but his C level is low: he gets answers right and writes almost nothing. He dislikes writing out reasoning and says he "just knows". A class would give him more content he already has. A private tutor who sets short tasks in which he must justify each step, then marks them against the Criterion C descriptors and asks him to rewrite, addresses the real gap. The parents choose a weekly one-to-one session for eight weeks.

The two students share a grade and need opposite things. That is the case for diagnosing before choosing.

## Common Mistakes Families Make in This Decision

These are the usual errors in an IB maths coaching vs private tutor choice.

- **Choosing by price alone.** A cheaper option that does not address the gap is not cheaper.
- **Choosing by the label.** "Coaching" and "tutoring" describe formats, not quality.
- **Skipping the diagnostic.** Without evidence, every provider's pitch sounds plausible.
- **Enrolling for a year.** Commit for a short period, then review.
- **Using the final grade only.** The criterion levels carry the information.
- **Adding hours instead of changing method.** If the same errors persist, more hours will not fix them.
- **Ignoring the child's view.** A student who dislikes a format may not engage with it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a private tutor better than a coaching class for MYP maths?

Not in general. A private tutor is better when the student's gap is specific, such as one weak criterion, a plateau or low confidence. A coaching class is better when the student needs structure, regular practice and peer learning, and has only broad gaps. Diagnose first, using the criterion levels, then choose the format that matches the problem.

### Which is cheaper, coaching or a private tutor?

Group coaching usually costs less per student because the teacher's time is shared, while a private tutor costs more per session because the time is individual. The better comparison is value per useful hour. If a class spends most of its time on content your child already knows, it is poor value however low the fee. Ask what is included.

### Can my child do both a class and a tutor?

Yes, and many families do, for example a class for weekly structure and a short block of tutoring for a specific criterion or task. The risk is overload and conflicting methods. Keep one person responsible for the plan, share notes between the two, and review after a few weeks to see whether the combination is helping or only adding hours.

### How do I know whether my child's problem is specific or general?

Look at the criterion levels on recent tasks. If one criterion is much lower than the others, the problem is specific. If all four are similar and the student needs more practice or exam stamina, it is more general. A short diagnostic with fresh questions and the student explaining their reasoning will give a clearer answer.

### Does a coaching class cover the MYP criteria properly?

It depends on the provider. Some classes are built around the criteria and include investigations and real-life tasks; others focus on procedure and tests. Ask how the class addresses Criteria B, C and D, how feedback is given, and whether the teacher is familiar with MYP marking. A trial class will show you more than a brochure.

### What if my child is shy in a group?

A shy student may stay silent in a class and leave gaps unnoticed. That is not a reason to avoid group learning entirely, but it is a reason to choose a small batch with a teacher who checks individual work, or to add one-to-one sessions to build confidence. A tutor can use an error log to show the student that mistakes are patterns, not verdicts.

### Can a tutor help with MYP investigations and the Personal Project?

A tutor can teach skills such as how to structure an investigation, how to justify steps and how to read a criterion, and can ask questions that push the student's thinking. The work must remain the student's own, and the school sets the rules for help. A tutor who writes or substantially rewrites assessed work puts the student at risk.

### When should an MYP student start extra maths support?

When a recurring gap appears, ideally early in the year, not just before a major assessment. For MYP 4 and 5, starting early helps with the transition to extended topics and the DP choice. A light weekly session is often enough for a student who is mostly on track. Waiting for a crisis leaves less time to repair.

### How long should we try a format before changing?

Four to six weeks is usually enough to see whether the format is working. Look at the error log, the next criterion scores and the student's independence. If the same errors persist, change one variable at a time: the tutor, the format or the frequency. Changing everything at once makes it impossible to know what helped.

### Does the format matter more than the teacher?

The teacher matters more. A strong teacher in either format will beat a weak one. The format determines what is structurally easy: a class makes routine and shared practice easy, a tutor makes diagnosis and feedback easy. Choose the teacher whose method matches your child's gap, and then choose the format that suits the way your family works.
