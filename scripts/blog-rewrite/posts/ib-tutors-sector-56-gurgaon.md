## What Does an MYP Student in Sector 56 Actually Face Each Week?

An MYP student faces a week of continuous assessment, not a single exam. The International Baccalaureate (IB) Middle Years Programme runs for five years (MYP 1 to 5, roughly ages 11 to 16) across eight subject groups, and every subject is assessed against four criteria, each scored 0 to 8. The four criterion scores add to a total out of 32, which converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through IB grade boundaries. That structure means the pressure is spread across every task, every week, not stored up for one hall in May.

:::stats MYP assessment at a glance
8 | Subject groups across MYP 1 to 5
4 | Criteria per subject, A to D
0-8 | Score per criterion
1-7 | Final grade after boundaries
:::

For a family, that structure has three practical effects.

1. **Many small deadlines.** Tasks arrive in different subjects with different criteria, so a week can contain a lab write-up, a language acquisition task, a maths investigation and a humanities source analysis.
2. **Marks tied to communication as much as content.** A student who understands the science can still lose most of a criterion by presenting a method badly.
3. **No single national MYP examination in India.** Schools set their own assessments, and MYP eAssessment in MYP 5 (on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios) is optional, with each school deciding whether to offer it. So the calendar in a Sector 56 household depends on the school, and the first job in any tutoring plan is to get that calendar on one page.

### The projects that sit on top of subjects

Two projects break the normal rhythm. The Community Project comes in MYP 3 or 4, and the Personal Project in MYP 5. They run for months, alongside everything else, and both are the student's own work. A tutor can help a student plan a timeline and think through process, and cannot write or edit the submission. A family that expects the extra project to fit into whatever time is left over usually finds there is none, so it helps to mark project milestones on the family calendar at the start of the year.

### Why the rhythm matters more than the hours

A student in MYP 2 who does two focused sessions a week for a term typically gets more from the arrangement than one who does five sessions in the fortnight before exams. The reason is spacing. Skills that are practised on a regular beat, such as writing up an investigation or structuring a paragraph, are retained and improved. Skills that are practised in a rush are borrowed and returned. ### Three things that decide whether an evening slot works

**Arrival time at home.** A child who reaches home at four o'clock has a different evening from a child whose school bus returns after six. Fix the slot to the later of the two weekdays, not the earlier, because a plan built on the best day fails on the worst.

**Gate entry in societies.** Gated societies typically need a visitor to be registered at the gate. Add the tutor's name to the society's regular visitor list, or send the gate a message before each session, and the problem disappears.

## Which MYP Subjects Benefit Most From a Tutor?

The subjects that benefit most are the ones where the criteria demand a skill that a classroom of thirty cannot fully coach: Mathematics, the sciences, Language Acquisition and the written parts of Individuals and Societies. A tutor works well on a defined skill gap and works poorly as a general homework supervisor.

### Mathematics

MYP Mathematics is assessed on four criteria: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating, D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts, each scored 0 to 8. Schools often split MYP 4 and 5 into standard and extended mathematics, and extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches. A student can be strong on Criterion A and weak on B and D, because investigation and application are different from solving exercises.

A tutor who works on Criterion B teaches the student to find a pattern, state a general rule and justify it. A tutor who works on Criterion D teaches the student to build a model from a real context, choose a degree of accuracy and comment on whether the result makes sense. These are learnable moves, and they are the places where marks move fastest. The criteria themselves are unpacked in the guides to [MYP mathematics criteria A, B, C and D](/blog/ib-criterion-a-b-c-d-explained-myp-mathematics-gurgaon/) and [moving from a 5 to a 6 or 7 in MYP maths](/blog/how-can-an-ib-myp-maths-student-move-from-5-to-6-or-7/).

### Sciences

MYP sciences assess designing and carrying out investigations, processing and evaluating data, and reflecting on the impact of science. The recurring loss is in communication: a results table without units, a graph with unlabelled axes, a method written as a narrative. A tutor can sit with the student and a real write-up and model how the table, the graph and the evaluation should look, then watch the student redo the next one.

### Language Acquisition

For students whose first language is not English, MYP Language Acquisition is taught in phases 1 to 6 rather than as a single course. Progress here depends on regular speaking and writing practice, which is difficult to get at scale in a class. A tutor offers conversation time, feedback on written tasks and a place to try out phrases without an audience. The IB has no subject called ESL, though parents often search for it when they mean this support.

### Individuals and Societies, and Language and Literature

These subjects reward argument and evidence. The common issue is that a student writes everything they know instead of answering the task. A tutor works on planning: reading the task, identifying the criterion, building a paragraph with a claim, evidence and explanation, and checking the wording of the command term. The vocabulary of command terms is covered in the guide to [IB command terms with real examples](/blog/ib-command-terms-explained-with-real-examples/).

### Subjects that rarely need a tutor

Design, Arts, and Physical and Health Education usually do not need weekly support. If a child needs help in one of them, it is typically a one-off conversation about a specific task, not a standing slot. A family who spreads tutoring across five subjects often ends up with diluted sessions and no visible change. It is better to choose one or two.

## How Do You Build a Weekday Rhythm Around School and Activities?

Build the rhythm by fixing the immovable items first (school hours, commute, fixed activities, family meals), then placing one or two tutoring sessions in the weekdays that have the most slack, and protecting at least one evening and one weekend half-day with no academic work at all. A rhythm that has no rest in it fails by midterm, because the child stops cooperating.

1. **List the fixed items.** Write school end time, the bus return, each activity with its day and finish time, and any weekend commitments.
2. **Mark the slack evenings.** The best slot is usually the weekday with the lightest activity load and not necessarily the earliest.
3. **Choose the subject.** Pick the one subject where criterion marks have the most room to rise.
4. **Place the session.** Leave a gap of thirty to forty-five minutes after school for a snack and a break before a session.
5. **Add a short homework block** on one other day, so the work from the session is practised while it is fresh.
6. **Protect rest.** One evening with no screens-for-study and no tutor, and one weekend half-day free.
7. **Review monthly.** Adjust the slot or the subject if the plan is straining.

### Three sample weekly rhythms

The following are illustrations of how a week can be arranged, not templates to copy. Actual times depend on the school and the child.

| Day | Rhythm A: one subject, MYP 2 or 3 | Rhythm B: maths and science, MYP 4 | Rhythm C: MYP 5, project season |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | School, activity | School, maths session | School, light revision |
| Tuesday | Homework, rest | Homework, 20-min recall | Personal Project work |
| Wednesday | Tutor session, 60 min | School, activity | Maths or science session |
| Thursday | Homework, rest | Science session | Personal Project work |
| Friday | Free evening | Free evening | Free evening |
| Saturday | Optional 30-min practice | Short criterion task | Half-day project block |
| Sunday | Plan the week, 15 minutes | Plan the week, 15 minutes | Plan the week, 15 minutes |

### Reading the table

Rhythm A is the lightest: one weekly session, one practice block, two free evenings. It suits a student in the early MYP years who needs a skill built and has a full activity schedule. Rhythm B adds a second subject for a student in MYP 4 who has criteria gaps in both maths and science. Rhythm C is built for MYP 5, when a long project, possible eAssessment preparation and the final-year criteria all collide. In each, one evening is deliberately empty, and that is not laziness. The evening off is what makes the rest sustainable.

## What Does a Good After-School Session Look Like?

The best IB tutors in Sector 56 Gurgaon run sessions that are short, focused and built on the child's own recent work. It does not repeat the lesson from school. The tutor opens with a quick recall question, spends most of the time on one skill using the child's actual task or a close cousin of it, has the child produce something in the session, and ends with a clear small task for the week. Seventy-five minutes is a common length for MYP students, and sixty minutes is enough for younger ones.

### A sample session in MYP Mathematics

:::steps A 60-minute MYP maths session
Recall | Five minutes on last week's rule or method
Skill | Work through one criterion-focused task
Produce | Student writes a short investigation step alone
Review | Check against the criterion descriptors
Set | One small task for the week
:::

Consider a student in MYP 4 who scores well on Criterion A but loses marks on B. The tutor might give a short sequence of numbers or shapes, ask the student to tabulate the first six cases, state the rule in words, express it algebraically, test it on a seventh case and then explain why it works. The point is not the answer to that sequence. It is the routine, because the same routine applies to the next investigation task, and the one after.

### What a session should leave behind

By the end of each session the student should have something in the notebook that was not there before: a worked example in their own handwriting, a corrected paragraph, a model of a table. Sessions that end with the tutor having done all the work leave nothing behind. If your child cannot say what they did in the last session, ask the tutor to change the format.

## What Can an IB Tutor Do Around Assessed Work, and What Can't They?

A tutor can teach the skill behind an assessed task, explain the criteria, question the student's reasoning and respond to the student's own draft with feedback on process. A tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the student's submission. The IB's academic integrity policy treats assessed work as the student's own, and a school can act on work that is not.

For MYP tasks, the boundary looks like this.

| A tutor may | A tutor may not |
|---|---|
| Explain what Criterion D asks for, using a different example | Supply the real-life context and model for the student's task |
| Ask the student to reread a paragraph against the descriptor | Rewrite the paragraph for the student |
| Teach how to set out a results table | Fill the table with data for an investigation |
| Help the student plan a timeline for a project | Write the Personal Project report or product |
| Discuss the student's reasoning about a pattern | State the general rule that the student was meant to find |

Families sometimes ask for more than this, because a deadline is close and the child is stressed. A tutor who agrees is putting the student at risk. A better response to a looming deadline is a short, honest conversation with the school about extension policy and a restructured plan. The full treatment of rules and limits for Diploma internal assessments is in the [IB Internal Assessment guide](/blog/ib-internal-assessment-guide/), and the principle is the same one at the MYP level.

## How Do You Move From MYP to DP Without a Dip?

Plan the move from MYP 5 into DP1 from MYP 4, because the jump in pace, independence and assessment style is where many capable students lose ground. The DP is a two-year course of six subjects (three or four at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL)), with Theory of Knowledge (TOK), the Extended Essay (EE) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) alongside. The MYP trains for this, but unevenly.

A family in Sector 56 whose child is in MYP 4 or 5 benefits from three conversations. First, with the school about which DP subjects are on offer and which choices are open. Second, with the child about what they enjoy and where their strengths lie. Third, with a tutor who can teach across the transition, so the same person is available in MYP 5 and DP1. IB Gram looks for that kind of continuity when it matches. The year of transition is covered in [IB tutors in DLF Phase 5](/blog/ib-tutors-dlf-phase-5-gurgaon/), and the subject-choice question in [IB DP STEM subject choice](/blog/stem-subject-choice-ib-dp/).

### Signs the MYP-to-DP jump is coming for your child

- Criterion scores of 5 or 6 on writing-heavy tasks that fall in the DP's first term.
- Reliance on teacher prompts to start tasks.
- Short answers where longer, structured ones will be required.
- No routine of reviewing marked work.
- In maths, extended content that was understood in class but not practised independently.

The fix is not extra content. It is independence: planning a task without prompts, reviewing marked work against criteria, and writing at length. These are exactly the things a weekly session can train.

## How Do You Handle Siblings, Activities and Exam Weeks?

Households with more than one child, and children with serious activities, need a rhythm that is flexible without being fragile. The rule is to fix the pattern and let the content move.

### Siblings

If two children are on different programmes, one on MYP and one on DP or IGCSE, a tutor can sometimes be matched for both, but the sessions should stay separate, since the needs differ. Place the sessions back to back on one evening when the household allows it, to reduce the number of times a tutor has to pass through the gate. Where a tutor can teach more than one subject at the right level, say so at the start. Otherwise two tutors, each in their own subject, are better than one stretched tutor.

### Sports and performing activities

A child with a daily training schedule has fewer free hours and needs a shorter session. Two forty-five-minute sessions work better than one long one. In match or performance weeks, pause or move sessions and tell the tutor in advance. A student who is physically exhausted retains little, and a cancelled-then-rescheduled session is cheaper than a wasted one.

### Exam and criteria-deadline weeks

In the two weeks before a major assessment, shift the content of sessions to the specific task or exam, bring in recent tests and practise timed answers. In the week after, review the marked work in the next session, because that is when the child is most receptive to feedback. A school's calendar for MYP 5 eAssessment, where offered, sets the countdown for that year.

### Holidays and travel

Online sessions allow continuity during family travel. For longer breaks, a short rest is useful, but a gap of several weeks can undo a term's progress. A single light session a week during a break, or a small recall task, keeps the habit alive.

## What Does a Month-One Review Look Like?

Review the arrangement after four weeks with three questions: is the student doing more of the thinking, is there visible change in the work, and is the rhythm sustainable? A month is long enough to see the first signs and short enough to change course. The scores may not have moved yet, so look at the process first.

| Question | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Is the student doing the thinking? | They explain what they did in the last session | They say the tutor "showed" them the answers |
| Is there change in the work? | Better-structured write-ups, fewer repeated errors | The same comments on every task |
| Is the rhythm sustainable? | The slot is kept, the child is not drained | Frequent cancellations, resistance |
| Is communication clear? | Brief, regular updates with specifics | Silence or vague praise |
| Is the plan connected to school? | The tutor refers to the criteria and school feedback | Sessions are generic revision |

If two or more rows show warning signs, talk to the tutor first. If the issues remain after a further two sessions, ask IB Gram for a different match. The aim is to find a tutor and rhythm that fit, not to endure a poor fit out of politeness.

### What progress looks like

A typical pattern for a student with a criterion gap is that the first change is in process: the student starts writing a plan before starting, labels tables, and rereads the descriptor. The second change is in task scores, often by a point or two on the weakest criterion. The final-grade change lags behind. Families who expect a full grade lift in a month tend to be disappointed, and those who watch the process see the lift arrive.

## What Do Parents in Sector 56 Often Get Wrong?

These are the patterns that most often undermine an otherwise sensible arrangement.

1. **Booking too many subjects.** Three tutors, three evenings, no rest, and no visible change.
2. **Choosing a slot that only works on the best day.** The plan collapses on the busiest weekday.
3. **Treating the tutor as a homework supervisor.** Sessions become an hour of sitting beside a child doing school tasks.
4. **Expecting the tutor to write.** The boundary around assessed work protects the child.
5. **Starting only when marks drop.** Early support in MYP 3 or 4 is cheaper in effort than recovery in MYP 5 or DP1.
6. **Ignoring the school's feedback.** The best sessions use the teacher's comments and criterion scores as the starting point.
7. **Skipping rest.** A child with no empty evening burns out.
8. **Never reviewing.** An arrangement that is not reviewed drifts.

### Which MYP year is the best time to start tutoring?

Start when a skill gap appears, which for many students is MYP 3 or 4, rather than waiting for MYP 5. A tutor can help a student plan a timeline, think through the process and understand the criteria. A tutor cannot write or edit the project, and the work must be the student's own. A motivated child learns more in an hour than a reluctant one learns in three.
