## How Is IGCSE Economics Examined?

Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 has two papers. Paper 1 is multiple choice and carries 30%; Paper 2 is structured questions with data and carries 70%. The syllabus covers six topics: the basic economic problem; the allocation of resources; microeconomic decision-makers; government and the macroeconomy; economic development; and international trade and globalisation.

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics 4EC1 is organised around two papers by content area: Paper 1 covers microeconomics and business economics, and Paper 2 covers macroeconomics and the global economy. The weighting tells a tutor where to spend time. ## What Do Students Find Hard in IGCSE Economics?

The difficulties are consistent across students and schools, and they show up in marked scripts as patterns.

**Definitions without development.** A student writes "inflation is a rise in the general price level" and stops, when the question asked for an explanation of the effects on households.

**Diagrams drawn but not used.** A demand and supply diagram sits on the page with no labels, no shift described and no link to the written answer.

**Ignoring the data.** In data response, the extract or table is the point. Answers that could have been written without reading it earn few marks.

**One-sided answers.** A question asks the student to consider advantages and disadvantages, and only one side appears.

**Confusing similar terms.** A movement along a curve versus a shift of the curve; a change in demand versus a change in quantity demanded; nominal versus real.

**Weak numerical handling.** Calculating percentage changes, price elasticity of demand or inflation rates with unit or rounding errors.

Each of these is trainable, and none of them is fixed by rereading the textbook. A tutor who uses the student's own marked work and gives them timed practice with feedback fixes more in four weeks than reading fixes in four months. ## Teaching Diagrams Properly

Diagrams are where Economics students gain or lose a surprising share of marks. A tutor should treat them as a skill with a method, not as a decoration.

### The Diagram Routine

1. Draw the axes and label them, with units where relevant: price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal.
2. Draw the original curves and label each, including the equilibrium.
3. Show the change as a shift or a movement, with arrows and new labels.
4. Mark the new equilibrium and the changes in price and quantity.
5. Refer to the diagram in the written answer, saying what the shift shows.

Students lose marks for unlabelled axes, curves that do not intersect where they should, and diagrams the written answer never mentions. A simple habit, drawing every diagram in pencil with a ruler and labelling everything before writing a word, removes most of those losses.

### The Core Set of Diagrams

At IGCSE level, the diagrams worth rehearsing until they are automatic include:

- Demand and supply, with shifts and movements, and the effect on equilibrium price and quantity.
- Price elasticity of demand: steep versus shallow demand curves and what they imply for revenue.
- Production possibility curves: scarcity, choice, opportunity cost, shifts from growth.
- The effect of an indirect tax and of a subsidy on supply.
- Price controls, such as a maximum or minimum price, and the resulting shortage or surplus.

Check the current syllabus for exactly which diagrams are expected. A tutor should ask the student to draw each from memory weekly, then explain the economics out loud.

### Movement Versus Shift

This is the single most common source of confusion. A change in the price of the good itself moves the student along the existing curve: a change in quantity demanded or supplied. A change in anything else, such as income, tastes, the price of related goods or costs of production, shifts the whole curve. Students who can state this rule in their sleep stop mislabelling answers. A quick drill: give twelve short scenarios and ask the student to write "movement" or "shift", and which curve.

:::steps A chain of reasoning for any Economics explanation
Point | State the economic effect
Because | Give the mechanism
So | Follow the effect through
Apply | Use the data or context
Weigh | Judge it, if asked to evaluate
:::

## Data Response: Using the Extract

Paper 2 at Cambridge gives structured questions with data. The data might be a table of prices and quantities, a short news extract, a chart of inflation and unemployment, or a country comparison. The marks reward answers that use it.

### A Method for Data Questions

1. Read the question first, so you know what to look for.
2. Read the extract, underlining numbers and key phrases.
3. Answer using at least one specific figure or phrase from the data.
4. Link the data to an economic idea with the right term.
5. Check the command word and match the depth.

A good habit is to write the figure into the first sentence of the answer. "Unemployment fell from 8% to 6%, which suggests that..." is an answer. "Unemployment fell" is half of one.

### A Worked Example in Words

Suppose an extract shows that the price of a mobile data plan fell by 10% and the number of subscribers rose by 25%. A question asks the student to calculate the price elasticity of demand and comment on it. The student divides the percentage change in quantity demanded by the percentage change in price: 25 divided by 10 gives 2.5, ignoring the sign, which means demand is elastic. They then comment: because the percentage rise in quantity exceeds the percentage fall in price, total revenue is likely to rise, which is why a firm facing elastic demand may consider cutting prices. Notice what earned the marks: the formula, the numbers, the label (elastic), and a consequence for the firm.

### Numerical Skills in Economics

A short list of calculations appears regularly: percentage change, price elasticity of demand, inflation and unemployment rates, simple costs and revenue, and sometimes exchange-rate conversions. A tutor should drill these in ten-minute bursts with explicit working and units. Students who fumble arithmetic under time pressure lose marks that conceptual strength cannot recover.

## Writing Developed Answers and Evaluation

The marks in longer structured answers reward development. A one-line answer earns a mark; a developed chain earns several. Teach the student to write in short chains of linked sentences.

For example, a question about why a government might subsidise electric vehicles. A weak answer: "It helps the environment." A developed answer: "A subsidy lowers the cost of production for firms, which shifts supply to the right, so the market price falls and more electric vehicles are bought. This reduces emissions, a positive externality, because consumers who buy the vehicle do not capture the full social benefit." Notice the mechanism and the vocabulary.

### Evaluation Without Waffle

Where a question asks the student to discuss or evaluate, a short, structured evaluation helps. Give both sides, then a judgement with a reason. A useful frame: one benefit with its mechanism, one drawback with its mechanism, and a conclusion that depends on a stated condition, such as the size of the subsidy, the elasticity of demand or the time frame. A student who writes "it depends" must say on what.

| Command word | The answer should | Typical loss |
|---|---|---|
| Define | State the meaning precisely | Vague wording |
| Explain | Give cause and effect in a chain | Describing, not explaining |
| Calculate | Show formula, working and unit | Answer without working |
| Analyse | Break down causes or effects, with links | A single undeveloped point |
| Discuss | Give both sides and a judgement | One-sided list |

## What Does an Economics Home Session Look Like?

A session of about ninety minutes is a reasonable shape, though a tutor may work in sixty. Here is how a good one runs.

1. **Review (10 minutes).** The student's recent marked work or a past-paper question that went wrong.
2. **Diagnose (10 minutes).** The tutor asks the student to explain their answer aloud and finds whether the gap is knowledge, reasoning or presentation.
3. **Teach (25 minutes).** The missing idea, with a diagram built step by step and a real-world example.
4. **Apply (25 minutes).** A fresh question, unaided, followed by marking against the markscheme logic.
5. **Plan (10 minutes).** The next task: a diagram drawn from memory, a data question, or a calculation set.

The parent sees a pattern over weeks: fewer unlabelled diagrams, answers that use the data, and a student who can explain a concept in plain words. If the sessions are only the tutor talking, the student is not practising.

:::stats What parents can expect to see within a few weeks
Diagrams | Labelled and referred to in answers
Data | A figure quoted in each data answer
Chains | Cause and effect written in order
:::

### Early Year

Start with a diagnostic: one data question, one diagram, one longer answer, and a conversation. The tutor finds whether the student's problem is diagram precision, development of answers or vocabulary. Within two or three sessions the student should have a diagram routine and a vocabulary list built from their own errors.

### Mid Year

Rotate through topics while keeping a weekly data-response question, so the skill does not fade between units. Each week ends with a short written answer marked with feedback. The error log records every lost mark with a reason.

### Before and After Mocks

Mixed practice replaces topic practice, and timed written answers replace untimed ones. After the mock, spend a full session on the paper. Where did marks go: knowledge, development, data use, diagrams or time? The tutor and student reset the plan on that evidence. ## Past Papers: How to Use Them

Past papers matter more in Economics than in many subjects because the question style is consistent. Use official papers and markschemes provided through the school or Cambridge's channels, matched to the current syllabus. A useful method is to complete a paper, mark it with the markscheme, and note not only what the right answer was but what the markscheme rewarded: which words, which steps, which diagram elements.

Examiner reports, where available, show the common errors in each series. A tutor can turn them into a checklist of mistakes to avoid. Avoid memorising model answers: questions change, and a memorised answer to a different question loses marks.

## Cambridge or Edexcel: Does the Board Change the Tutoring?

The economic ideas are the same; the paper structure and question style differ. Cambridge's Paper 2 uses structured questions built around data, so practice must include working with extracts. Edexcel splits its papers between microeconomics and business economics, and macroeconomics and the global economy, so the plan should map topics to the paper that examines them.

A tutor who has worked with only one board should say so, and a good one will adjust practice material to the student's board. Ask for the syllabus code at the first meeting. If the school has not yet confirmed which board the student sits, find out before booking a long package.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is IGCSE Economics hard?

It is manageable for most students who practise written answers, because the ideas are accessible and the exam rewards structured reasoning. It feels hard when students rely on definitions, draw careless diagrams or ignore the data. Students who practise explaining cause and effect with feedback generally find it becomes much easier within a term.

### When should my child start Economics tuition?

Start when marked work shows a pattern of lost marks, such as weak diagrams or underdeveloped answers, ideally early in Grade 10 or even in Grade 9. Waiting until mocks leaves little time to build habits. A student who is on target and writing developed answers may not need tutoring at all.

### How many sessions a week does Economics need?

One focused session a week is enough for most students who want support, rising to two in the weeks around mocks and finals. Economics rewards regular written practice with feedback, so a weekly marked answer matters more than extra hours of explanation.

### Should we choose home or online tuition for Economics?

Both work. Home sessions suit students who focus better beside a person and like drawing by hand, while online sessions can work well with a pen tablet and shared whiteboard, and widen the choice of tutors. Many families use a mix. The deciding factor is whether the student actively participates.

### What is the difference between Cambridge 0455 and Edexcel 4EC1?

Both teach the same core ideas but are examined differently. Cambridge 0455 has a multiple-choice paper and a structured paper with data, while Edexcel 4EC1 splits papers between microeconomics and business economics, and macroeconomics and the global economy. Confirm your child's board and code, and use matching past papers.

### How do I help my child with diagrams?

Ask them to draw the core diagrams from memory every week with labelled axes, curves and equilibrium, and to explain each aloud. A pencil and ruler help. Check that every diagram is referred to in the written answer. A tutor can give feedback, but the habit is built by weekly repetition.

### Can my child improve at data response quickly?

Yes, because the method is teachable. Reading the question first, quoting a figure from the extract and linking it to an economic idea is a routine that can improve answers within a few weeks. Weekly practice with marking against the markscheme makes it stick.

### Does my child need to follow the news?

Not for the exam, but real examples help understanding. A student who reads about prices, interest rates or trade can connect them to the syllabus, which makes reasoning easier. Exam answers still need syllabus terms and the extract's data, so news is a support, not a substitute.

### What if my child is not improving after a month?

Ask the tutor what the diagnosis was and which patterns of lost marks have shrunk. A month is enough to see better diagrams or more developed answers even if marks lag. If the answers are vague, ask for a different match. IB Gram lets families request one.
