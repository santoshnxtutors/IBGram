How to prepare for IB Math AA HL comes down to a calendar: a sequence of topics, checkpoints and papers laid across roughly two years, with the right kind of work in each term. Students who treat the course as one long sprint tend to arrive at the DP2 mock with a pile of half-learned units. Students who treat it as a calendar arrive with four or five topics that are properly solid and a clear list of what is not.

This guide is for International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme (DP) students taking Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches at Higher Level (AA HL), and for parents who want to know what a sensible year looks like. It starts from the first week of DP1 and ends at the final exam. It does not try to be the grade-7 tactics guide; for the marking and exam-technique side, read [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/) alongside this one. Here the question is narrower and more practical: what should you be doing this term, and why?

One note on dates. Schools differ in when they start DP1, how they order topics and when they hold mocks. Use the structure below as a frame and ask your maths teacher or DP coordinator for the actual dates.

## What Does IB Math AA HL Ask of You Over Two Years?

IB Math AA HL asks for deep algebraic fluency, comfort with functions and calculus, and the habit of writing complete mathematical arguments, sustained over two years. DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each available at HL and SL: Analysis and Approaches (AA), which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and Applications and Interpretation (AI), which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. HL carries 240 recommended teaching hours, against 150 for SL.

The assessment is straightforward to state. Three written papers and an internal assessment make up the grade:

:::bars Where the AA HL grade comes from
Paper 1 (no calculator) | 30 | 30%
Paper 2 (calculator) | 30 | 30%
Paper 3 (extended problems) | 20 | 20%
Internal assessment (exploration) | 20 | 20%
Exams are 80% of the grade; the exploration is the 20% you control.
:::

Papers 1 and 2 run 120 minutes each for 110 marks. Paper 3 is the extended paper, built around longer, scaffolded problems on unfamiliar contexts. Check the current subject guide for the exact duration and marks of Paper 3, because they are the details most worth confirming with your school.

The syllabus itself falls into five topic areas: number and algebra, functions, geometry and trigonometry, probability and statistics, and calculus. HL adds extra content in each, including complex numbers, proof by induction, vectors in three dimensions and more advanced calculus. What makes the course hard is not any single topic. It is that the exam draws on all five at once, and that questions are written to look unfamiliar. A calendar solves exactly this problem, because it forces you to revisit topics before you forget them.

## How Should You Think About the Two Years as a Whole?

The simplest answer to how to prepare for IB Math AA HL is to divide the course by what each stretch of time is for. Think of the two years as four phases with different jobs: build, connect, rehearse and convert. The build phase is DP1. Connect is the second half of DP1 and the first term of DP2. Rehearse starts after the first DP2 term. Convert is the final run-in to May, where the goal changes from learning to collecting marks.

:::timeline The four phases of the AA HL calendar
DP1 terms 1-2 | Build: core algebra, functions, trigonometry
DP1 term 3 | Connect: calculus, mixed sets, exploration topic
DP2 terms 1-2 | Rehearse: new content, full papers, mock
DP2 final run-in | Convert: timed papers, error log, Paper 3
:::

Two principles hold across all four.

**Revisit before you finish.** A topic you studied in August and never touched again will be 40% gone by the DP1 end-of-year exam, and 70% gone by the DP2 mock. The fix is cheap: a short retrieval session each week on a topic from three to six weeks earlier.

**Switch the work as the date approaches.** Early on, you learn methods. Later, you choose methods. The skill the exam tests is choosing: seeing an unfamiliar question and deciding which tool applies. That skill only develops if mixed-topic practice starts well before the final year.

## What Should You Do in the First Week of DP1?

How to prepare for IB Math AA HL begins in week one with set-up, not content. In the first week of DP1, set up the habits and tools that the next two years depend on: a notebook system, an error log, a calculator routine and a quick diagnostic of what you actually remember from earlier years. The aim is not to learn anything advanced. It is to find out where the foundations are weak while there is still time to fix them quietly.

Here is a numbered start-of-course checklist that works as a stand-alone routine:

1. Get the current subject guide and the formula booklet from your school, and read the assessment section once.
2. Check your calculator. Know which graphic display calculator (GDC) your school expects, and spend an hour on its basic functions before any lesson relies on it.
3. Take a 40-minute diagnostic of Grade 10 algebra: expanding, factorising, fractions, indices, simultaneous equations and basic trigonometry. Mark it strictly.
4. Start an error log, with a column for the question, the mistake, the cause and the fix.
5. Fix a weekly slot for retrieval practice, even if it is only 30 minutes at first.
6. Write down your target grade and the university offers it connects to, so the work has a reason behind it.

The diagnostic matters more than it looks. A DP1 student who scored well in IGCSE Extended often finds that algebra which looked automatic in Grade 10 was supported by familiar question shapes. When the shapes change, the fluency turns out to be narrower than it felt. Students arriving from other boards face their own version of this gap. If that describes you, [the transition from IGCSE mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/) goes through the specific gaps and a bridge plan.

## What Is the Best Order to Study the AA HL Topics?

The best order for AA HL topics is the order that lets each unit use tools from the previous one: algebra first, then functions, then trigonometry, then differential calculus, then integration, with probability and statistics and the vectors and complex-number units placed where your school's timetable puts them. Your school's order may differ, and that is fine. What matters is what you do inside that order.

The table below gives a sensible default and the work each block needs. It is an illustration of how to use any sequence, not an official IB order.

| Block | Typical content | What must be automatic before moving on | Common early trap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Number and algebra | Sequences and series, exponents and logarithms, binomial theorem, proof | Rearranging, laws of logs, series formulae | Skipping the "show that" proofs because they feel optional |
| 2. Functions | Domain and range, composites, inverses, transformations, polynomial and rational functions | Sketching from an equation, reading features from a graph | Treating graphs as pictures instead of information |
| 3. Geometry and trigonometry | Radians, identities, equations, triangle methods, circular functions | Exact values, identity manipulation without notes | Relying on a calculator for values Paper 1 needs exactly |
| 4. Calculus (differential) | Limits, differentiation rules, applications, optimisation | Chain, product and quotient rules without hesitation | Learning rules but not what a derivative means |
| 5. Calculus (integral) | Integration methods, definite integrals, area, volume, differential equations | Spotting which method a function needs | Substitution done mentally, working skipped |
| 6. Vectors and complex numbers | Lines, planes, products, Cartesian and polar forms | Moving between forms, geometric interpretation | Memorising results without the geometry |
| 7. Probability and statistics | Distributions, conditional probability, hypothesis testing | Choosing the right model, stating assumptions | Calculator output without interpretation |

Two comments on that table. First, calculus is the longest thread. It touches functions, trigonometry, algebra and most of Paper 3, so any weakness in blocks 1 to 3 shows up there. Second, vectors and complex numbers are self-contained, which makes them excellent places to bank secure marks. A student who commits properly to them often finds them more predictable than calculus.

If your main question is which individual topics repay the most practice and what typical errors look like inside each, [the topic-by-topic AA HL strategy](/blog/how-to-score-a-7-in-ib-maths-aa-hl-step-by-step-strategy/) is the dedicated guide. This post stays with the calendar.

## What Should DP1 Term 1 Look Like?

DP1 term 1 should look like careful building: new content at a steady pace, algebra practised until it is automatic, and the first error log entries. The temptation in the first term is to feel relaxed because the content seems familiar. That feeling is the problem.

In a typical term 1, you will meet number and algebra and the start of functions. The practical routine looks like this:

- **After each lesson:** redo two or three of the class questions without looking, within 24 hours.
- **Weekly:** one 30-minute set of mixed algebra, drawn from earlier lessons, so nothing sits unused.
- **Fortnightly:** one question you cannot solve at first sight, worked on for at least 20 minutes before looking at help.
- **End of term:** a timed 50-minute section on the term's content, marked against the scheme, with every lost mark entered in the error log.

The unfamiliar question is the habit that separates students later. Spending 20 minutes stuck is uncomfortable and also the most direct training for Paper 3. Students who look at the solution after two minutes train themselves to recognise answers, not to find them.

Consider a student who scores 78% on every class test in term 1 and decides the course is going well. On inspection, the tests were all single-topic, taken the week after teaching. The same student, given a mixed set three weeks later, scores 55%. Nothing has gone wrong in the lessons. The calendar simply had no mixed practice in it. That is why the weekly mixed slot is part of term 1 and not something to add later.

## What Should DP1 Term 2 Add?

DP1 term 2 should add trigonometry and differential calculus to the algebra and functions already built, and start introducing past-paper style questions in short, untimed form. It is also the term where the first real divergence between students appears, because calculus punishes weak algebra.

Three additions make term 2 different from term 1.

**Past questions by topic, not full papers.** Take the topic you have just finished and work through past exam questions on it. Study the markscheme for each one: which lines carry method marks, where follow-through applies and how the answer is expected to be presented. Official markschemes are among the most useful documents in the course, because they show exactly where marks live. Past papers, markschemes and subject reports come through the school or the IB's official channels.

**A note on notation.** Maths notation is not decoration in this course. Use correct equals signs, brackets and domain statements. Writing "f(x) = 2x, so 2x = 4 = x = 2" is the kind of line that loses marks while the answer is right.

**GDC fluency begins.** Paper 2 and Paper 3 require a calculator, and the efficient use of one is a skill. Learn to graph, solve numerically and read intersections, and learn when not to use it. The calculator is a tool for checking and for tasks no human should do by hand, not a replacement for reasoning. For technique, see [the GDC advantage in IB Math AA HL](/blog/the-gdc-advantage-in-ib-dp-mathematics-aa-hl/), which covers the solver, graph analysis and when the calculator can hurt.

At the end of term 2, take a cumulative timed paper section covering everything so far. This is the first real signal about the state of the build, and it arrives while there is still plenty of time to act on it.

## What Should DP1 Term 3 and the Summer Do?

DP1 term 3 and the summer break should do three jobs: finish the first calculus block, take the DP1 final exam with a proper analysis afterwards, and start the exploration. This is the most important handover point in the whole calendar, because the work you do here sets up everything in DP2.

### Treat the DP1 final exam as the first mock

Most schools run an end-of-year exam in DP1. Treat it as a rehearsal, whatever the school's weighting is. After it, do not just note the percentage. Sort every lost mark into one of four causes: knowledge gap, method choice, arithmetic or notation, and time. The proportions tell you what to fix over the summer. A student losing most marks to arithmetic needs a different summer from one losing marks to knowledge gaps.

### Start the exploration while there is room to think

The mathematics exploration (IA) is the same task for AA and AI, SL and HL: 20% of the final grade, 12 to 20 pages, marked out of 20 on five criteria: A Presentation (4), B Mathematical communication (4), C Personal engagement (3), D Reflection (3) and E Use of mathematics (6). HL work is judged against a higher standard on criterion E. The teacher can comment on one draft, and the work must be the student's own.

The summer after DP1 is the right moment to choose a topic, because you have a year of mathematics behind you and a free week to read. A good topic comes from something you noticed, a pattern or a question the course left open, and is narrow enough to do properly in 12 to 20 pages. Poor topics are the grand ones ("the mathematics of music") that produce shallow description. If you want a guided look at what a strong exploration involves, the [complete guide to IB Internal Assessment with examples](/blog/complete-guide-to-ib-internal-assessment-ia-with-examples/) works through concrete cases.

Remember that every IB internal assessment is the student's own work. The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to give feedback on one draft; it does not allow anyone, tutor or parent, to write, rewrite or edit the student's submission. A tutor may teach the underlying skill, question the reasoning and ask the student to reread their own paragraph against a criterion.

### Use the summer for the weakest block

If the end-of-year analysis shows a weak block, spend four to six weeks on it, not on everything. A tidy summer plan has three parts: a repair block on the weakest unit, a weekly mixed set and a light reading task for the exploration. Ten to twelve hours a week is more than enough. A student who spends the whole break on full past papers will usually burn out by August.

## How Do You Run the First Term of DP2?

The first term of DP2 should finish new content in the topics still ahead, produce a first draft of the exploration, and begin the shift from learning methods to choosing them. It is the busiest term of the calendar, because university applications and the core components also land here.

:::steps The DP2 term 1 workflow
Content | Finish integration, vectors, statistics, complex numbers
Mixed sets | Weekly sets across all topics
Exploration | First draft submitted by school deadline
Applications | Keep a fixed weekly maths slot
:::

The most common failure in this term is losing the weekly mixed set to other deadlines. The exploration, the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge and applications all compete for the same evenings. The defence is a fixed, short slot that does not move: three sessions of 60 to 90 minutes, always at the same time. When schedule pressure rises, shorten the session, but keep the slot.

For students applying to universities, the exact interplay between applications, predicted grades and maths performance is covered in [IB university preparation: subject choices, predicted grades and timelines](/blog/ib-university-preparation-guide/). The short version is that the DP2 mock usually carries the most weight in a school's prediction, which makes the next term more important than it appears.

## What Should DP2 Term 2 and the Mock Exam Look Like?

DP2 term 2 should be built around full, timed papers, marked strictly against the official scheme, with the mock exam as the main checkpoint. Each school sets its own mock dates, usually several months before May, so ask for the dates early and build backwards.

A workable pattern for the weeks leading to the mock:

| Weeks before mock | Main work | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 8 to 6 | Topic-by-topic past questions on every unit, weakest first | Updated error log |
| 5 to 4 | One full paper a week, alternating Paper 1 and Paper 2 | Marked papers with cause tags |
| 3 to 2 | Paper 3 style problems, one a week, untimed then timed | A list of recurring Paper 3 gaps |
| Final week | Light review, formulae, sleep, no new material | A calm student |

After the mock, spend a full session on analysis before doing any more practice. A mock result of 62% is a fact; the useful information is how the other 38% was lost. In most scripts, a large share of lost marks falls into a short list: working skipped, an arithmetic slip in a non-calculator paper, an interpretation line left out in a calculator paper, and a Paper 3 part abandoned early. Each of these is fixable in weeks, which is why the mock is a diagnostic and not a verdict.

[IB Math AA HL paper preparation](/blog/ib-math-aa-hl-paper-preparation-gurgaon/) goes into the three papers one by one, with question styles, timing and drills for each. Use it from this term onwards.

## How Do You Build Up to Paper 3?

You build up to Paper 3 by starting early, working on one extended unfamiliar problem a week, and teaching yourself to attempt every part even when you are stuck. Paper 3 is 20% of the grade and the least like anything else in the course, so it needs months, not weeks.

Here is the build-up that works inside the calendar:

1. **DP1 term 3:** begin by simply reading Paper 3 style questions, working only the first part, to see how scaffolding is written.
2. **DP2 term 1:** work one extended problem every two weeks, untimed, writing out every step.
3. **DP2 term 2:** one problem a week, first untimed and then within the time the part's marks suggest.
4. **Final run-in:** a timed Paper 3 every second week, marked against the scheme, with a note of every part abandoned.

Two habits matter more than any content knowledge here. First, read the whole problem before starting; later parts often reveal what the first part is for. Second, if part (c) depends on part (b) and you could not do (b), state an assumed value and carry on. Follow-through marks exist for precisely this situation, and students who stop dead give up marks they had already earned in method.

## How Do You Keep a Calendar Honest With an Error Log?

You keep a calendar honest by tying every checkpoint to a written error log, because an error log turns the vague feeling of "I need more revision" into a short, specific list of fixes. Without it, calendars drift: the dates stay, but the work stops targeting real weaknesses.

A log needs four columns and a rule. The columns are the question, what went wrong, why it went wrong and what you will do differently. The rule is that you review it weekly and delete an entry only when the same type of question has been answered correctly twice, at least a week apart.

| Question | What went wrong | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Integration by parts, Paper 1 | Sign error in the second step | Rushed, no line written | Always write the u, v, du, dv line |
| Vectors, line and plane intersection | Used the wrong direction vector | Did not sketch | Sketch first, label vectors |
| Statistics, hypothesis test | Conclusion without context | Stopped at the p-value | Finish with a sentence in the problem's context |
| Paper 3, part (d) | Left blank | Stuck on part (c) | State an assumption and continue |

Over two years, a log becomes the best revision document you own, because every line in it is a mark you personally lost. The general system is set out in [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/); in the calendar context, what matters is when you review it. Schedule a 20-minute log review every Sunday and a longer one after every mock and every timed paper.

## What Does the Final Run-In to May Look Like?

The final run-in to May should be short, structured and mostly about timed practice: roughly the last ten to twelve weeks, with the weeks shifting from topic revision to full papers and then to light consolidation. Exam sessions run in May and November, and schools in India overwhelmingly enter candidates for the May session. May results are released in early July.

A sensible allocation across the final stretch:

- **Weeks 12 to 8:** finish a complete pass over every topic using past questions, weakest first, and rebuild the formula sheet from memory.
- **Weeks 7 to 4:** two full papers a week, alternating papers, each marked strictly. Paper 3 weekly.
- **Weeks 3 to 1:** one paper every two or three days, with analysis, then tapering. Rehearse the paper-day routine, including calculator checks.
- **The last 48 hours:** no new questions. Review the error log, the formula list and your timing plan.

Students often ask whether they should do more at the end. The honest answer is that the last two weeks reward consistency far more than intensity. A twelve-hour cram the night before does less than a good sleep. [How toppers study in the last 30 days before IB exams](/blog/ib-exam-strategy-how-toppers-study-in-the-last-30-days/) is the dedicated guide to that stretch.

## How Many Hours a Week Does AA HL Preparation Take?

AA HL preparation takes roughly 8 to 10 hours a week outside lessons at a sustainable pace, lighter in the first term of DP1 and heavier from the start of the final year. Those hours must fit alongside five other subjects, the core and, for many students, applications, so they need to be planned, not hoped for.

:::bars Typical weekly maths hours across the calendar (illustrative)
DP1 term 1 | 40 | About 4 hours
DP1 terms 2 and 3 | 60 | About 6 hours
DP2 terms 1 and 2 | 80 | About 8 hours
Final 10 weeks | 100 | About 10 hours
These are planning guides, not IB requirements. Adjust to your other subjects.
:::

The bars are a judgement, not an official figure, and your own numbers will differ. What matters is the shape: lighter early, heavier late, with a regular weekly rhythm. Five sessions of 90 minutes are better than one seven-hour Sunday. If you can only manage seven hours in DP2, protect the quality of the sessions: timed work, strict marking, error log review.

## Where Does a Tutor Fit Into the Two-Year Calendar?

A tutor fits into the calendar at the points where independent work is least effective: the DP1 diagnostic, the summer repair, the Paper 3 build-up and the post-mock analysis. Outside those points, many students manage perfectly well on their own and do not need weekly lessons all the way through.

A tutor helps when there is a named problem that the student cannot diagnose alone, or when feedback at school is too general to act on. Ask yourself whether a tutor would provide feedback specific enough to act on, such as "you lose method marks because you substitute mentally in integration by parts", which is a fix, as opposed to "revise calculus", which is a comment. If the answer is yes, the support is probably worth considering. If a student is steadily improving, with an error log that is getting shorter, extra lessons may add cost without adding much.

On timing, the most valuable windows are early. A short diagnostic in the first term of DP1 costs little and prevents months of drift. The next is the summer between DP1 and DP2, when a focused block can repair a weak topic before the pressure arrives. After that, support is most useful in the six to eight weeks around the mock. ## What Mistakes Break an AA HL Calendar?

The mistakes that break an AA HL calendar are almost all about timing, not talent: starting mixed practice too late, leaving the exploration to the last month, treating Paper 3 as a late addition and revising by re-reading notes. Each of them looks reasonable at the time.

| Mistake | How it shows up | The calendar fix |
|---|---|---|
| Only single-topic practice | High class marks, weak mock | Add a weekly mixed set from term 1 |
| Exploration left late | Rushed IA, thin reflection | Choose the topic in the DP1 summer, draft in DP2 term 1 |
| Paper 3 left to the end | Blank final parts in the mock | One extended problem every fortnight from DP2 term 1 |
| Revising by re-reading | Feels productive, retains little | Retrieval and past questions instead |
| No analysis after tests | Same errors repeat | Tag every lost mark with a cause |
| Dropping the weekly slot under pressure | Gradual decay | Shorten sessions but keep the slot |

If you remember one thing about how to prepare for IB Math AA HL, make it this: every one of these mistakes is a calendar problem, and a calendar problem is solved by moving a date. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should you start preparing for IB Math AA HL?

You should start in the first week of DP1, and ideally in the summer before it. Use the early weeks for a foundation diagnostic, an error log and a calculator check. Starting early does not mean heavy work: thirty minutes of retrieval a week in the first term is enough to prevent the common DP2 scramble. Students who begin in the final year can still improve, but they have far fewer options.

### How many hours a week do you need for AA HL?

Plan on about 8 to 10 hours a week outside lessons at full pace, with less in the first term of DP1 and more in the final ten weeks. Quality counts for more than total volume. Five sessions of ninety minutes with timed work and strict marking outperform a long, unfocused weekend. Scale the plan to your other subjects and commitments.

### Should you finish the syllabus before starting past papers?

No, begin with topic-by-topic past questions as soon as a unit is complete. Full timed papers come later, once most of the content is covered, typically in DP2. Doing questions by topic early shows you how marks are awarded and where your understanding is thin, while there is still time to fix it. Waiting for the full syllabus compresses all that learning into a few frantic months.

### When should you start the exploration?

Choose a topic in the summer after DP1 and aim for a first draft early in DP2 term 1. The exploration is 12 to 20 pages, marked on five criteria out of 20, and a teacher can comment on one draft only. Starting early gives time for a narrow topic and honest reflection. A rushed exploration usually loses marks on personal engagement and reflection.

### Is a tutor necessary to do well in AA HL?

No, many students do well without one. A tutor helps most when a student has a named gap they cannot diagnose alone, or when school feedback is too general to act on. If the error log is shrinking and mock marks are rising, extra lessons may not add much. No tutor can guarantee a grade, so judge any support by the evidence it produces.

### What is the best time to hire a tutor for AA HL?

The most useful windows are the first term of DP1, the summer between DP1 and DP2, and the six to eight weeks around the mock. A short early diagnostic is cheap and prevents drift. The summer repair block fixes a weak topic before pressure builds. The mock window sharpens exam technique. Outside these points, support is often optional.

### How do you balance AA HL with six other commitments?

Fix a short, regular weekly maths slot and protect it before other deadlines arrive. Three sessions of 60 to 90 minutes that never move are more reliable than ambitious plans that collapse. When the exploration, Extended Essay and applications collide in DP2, shorten sessions instead of skipping them. Keep a single calendar for every deadline so conflicts are visible early.

### What should you do if you fall behind the calendar?

Identify which block you are behind on and why, then cut scope instead of rushing everything. Fix the foundations of the weakest unit first, keep the weekly mixed set going and ask your teacher what the school will assess next. Falling behind by a few weeks in DP1 is common and recoverable. Falling behind silently for a term is what causes trouble.

### Do you need a graphic display calculator from day one?

You need to know your calculator well before Paper 2 and Paper 3 practice begins, so start in DP1. Check which model your school expects, learn graphing, numerical solving and basic statistics, and practise deciding when not to use it. Paper 1 is a non-calculator paper, so manual fluency remains essential. The calculator supports reasoning; it does not replace it.

### How should the DP2 mock fit into your plan?

Treat the DP2 mock as a diagnostic first and a prediction input second. Schools usually weigh it heavily when forming predicted grades, but the useful part is the analysis afterwards: which marks were lost and why. Spend a full session sorting errors by cause, then rebuild the next eight weeks around the biggest categories. The mock is a map, not a final verdict.

### Can you recover from a weak DP1 year?

Yes, if the weak year is diagnosed honestly and the summer is used well. Many students finish DP1 with scattered gaps in algebra or functions. A focused repair block, a weekly mixed set and an early start on timed papers can change the DP2 mock considerably. It takes consistent work, and the improvement shows in the evidence a school uses for predictions, not in promises.
