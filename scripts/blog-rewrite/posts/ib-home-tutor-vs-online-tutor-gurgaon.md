## The Short Answer: Decide by the Student, Not by the Format

A mode is a delivery method. The thing that changes grades is the quality of the diagnosis, the feedback loop and the student's own practice between sessions. A mediocre tutor in a living room is still a mediocre tutor, and a strong specialist on a stable video link is still a strong specialist. So the IB home tutor vs online tutor in Gurgaon decision is mostly about removing friction: whichever mode lets this particular student turn up, concentrate, show their working and get corrected quickly is the right one.

Four situations make the choice easy.

- **Choose home** when the student is in MYP 1-3, loses focus on screens, writes slowly and needs the tutor to see the page, or when the school week is so full that a fixed weekday slot at the dining table is the only slot that survives.
- **Choose online** when the subject needs a rare specialist (HL Physics, HL Chemistry, HL Computer Science, a particular language), when the family is travelling or relocating, when the home is noisy at the only free hour, or when most of the work is short, targeted correction.
- **Choose a mix** when the student is a DP student with both a long-running weekly subject and an exam-season need for extra short sessions. The practical set-up is in the hybrid guide linked above.
- **Choose neither yet** when the student has not looked at their own last three assessments. Mode will not fix a student who has not diagnosed what is going wrong.

The rest of this post is the evidence behind those four lines, so you can check your own case against it.

## What Each Mode Actually Changes in an IB Lesson

Strip away marketing and each mode changes five concrete things: how the tutor sees the student's work, how quickly feedback lands, how much time is lost in transit, how many tutors you can choose from, and what the student does with their eyes and hands.

**How the tutor sees the work.** At home, the tutor sits beside a notebook and watches pen strokes: a sign flipped, a bracket dropped, a diagram drawn before the reasoning. That is genuinely valuable for MYP Criterion C (communicating) and for DP Paper 1 style working. Online, a pen tablet or a phone propped over the page gets you most of the way, but only if the family sets it up properly. A student typing answers into a shared document is not being taught the same skill.

**How quickly feedback lands.** Home lessons are fixed events; the next feedback arrives at the next visit. Online lets a student photograph a stuck question on Thursday night and get a ten-minute correction, which is often where the real learning happens. Whether the tutor offers that is a policy matter, not a mode matter, so ask.

**Time lost in transit.** A home tutor travelling across Gurgaon on a weekday evening absorbs real travel time and risk of delay. Everyone who has driven Golf Course Road or the Sohna Road stretch at six o'clock knows the problem. That cost shows up as lateness, shortened sessions or a higher quote. Online removes it for both sides.

**The size of the tutor pool.** Home restricts you to tutors willing to reach your society; online opens the whole country. For common subjects like MYP Mathematics or DP English this barely matters. For a subject with few specialists it can matter a great deal.

**Eyes and hands.** A student on a laptop is one tab away from a game. A student at the dining table with a printed past paper has fewer exits. Younger students and students with attention difficulties often feel this difference strongly; many older students do not.

:::stats Where the two modes differ in practice
5 | Real differences: seeing work, feedback speed, transit, tutor pool, distraction
0 | Differences in the IB syllabus or the marking
:::

The second line is the important one. IB assessment does not know or care how you were taught. Paper 1 and Paper 2 are sat in an exam hall either way.

## How to Score Your Own Case: Eight Questions

Use this like a checklist, not a calculator. For each question, note which side the answer leans towards. If six or more lean the same way, start there. If it is close to even, start with the mode that is easier to set up this week and review after a month.

1. **How old is the student?** MYP 1-3 leans home. MYP 4-5 is neutral. DP leans online or either.
2. **Which subject?** Mathematics and sciences with heavy diagrams lean home for beginners and online for specialists. Humanities and language subjects lean online, because feedback is on written work that can be shared. See the table in the next section.
3. **How well does the student concentrate on a screen?** Honest answer, not hoped-for answer. Distractible leans home.
4. **Is there a quiet, lit table at the lesson hour?** If the only free hour is when a sibling is practising an instrument in the next room, lean online with headphones in a different room, or shift the hour.
5. **How scarce is the specialist you need?** If the subject-level combination has few good tutors near you, lean online.
6. **How many hours of the week are already scheduled?** A student returning from school at five, with sport and homework, benefits from an online session that starts at seven-thirty without any hosting at home.
7. **Is the family travelling, or likely to relocate?** Online keeps continuity with the same tutor. Switching a home tutor for a move means starting again.
8. **Does the student need physical materials?** Past papers on paper, a formula booklet, coloured pens for annotating an English text: home makes this easy; online needs scanned copies.

:::steps How to apply the eight questions
Score | Mark each answer as home, online or neutral
Tally | Six or more on one side decides it
Trial | Start with a demo in the leaning mode
Review | Check after four weeks and adjust
:::

In an IB home tutor vs online tutor in Gurgaon comparison, a family that scores a clear four-four split should not agonise. Pick the mode that is cheaper to try, book a demo, and decide with evidence from the first month.

## Which IB Subjects Suit Home and Which Suit Online?

The table below is a judgement, not a rule. It reflects how the subject is usually taught and assessed, and it assumes a student of average organisation and a decent internet connection. Individual students will break it.

| Subject / component | Leans | Why |
|---|---|---|
| MYP Mathematics (MYP 1-3) | Home | Younger students need handwriting watched; Criterion C working is physical |
| MYP Mathematics (MYP 4-5) | Either | Extended content is abstract; a pen tablet works well online |
| DP Mathematics AA HL | Online or home | Specialist matters more than location; GDC on screen works well online |
| DP Mathematics AI HL / SL | Online or home | Technology-heavy; screen-sharing a GDC emulator or spreadsheet is natural |
| Mathematics Internal Assessment (IA) | Online | Feedback on drafts is document-based; sessions can be shorter and more frequent |
| Physics / Chemistry HL | Online | Few strong specialists; diagrams and derivations transfer fine to a tablet |
| Biology HL / SL | Either | Diagram-heavy, but photos of labelled diagrams work online |
| Sciences IA | Online | Data analysis and write-up feedback happens on shared documents |
| Economics / Business | Online | Essay and data-response feedback is text-based; tutor pool is wider |
| English A / English B | Online or home | Written feedback travels well; spoken practice (Individual Oral) works well either way |
| Computer Science | Online | Code on a shared screen is easier than on paper |
| Extended Essay (EE) and Theory of Knowledge (TOK) | Online | Supervision sessions are conversational; documents are digital |
| PYP, MYP 1-2 any subject | Home | Short attention spans, tactile resources, parents often present |

Three points about the table.

First, "online" for the sciences and mathematics only holds if the student writes on a surface the tutor can see live. A tutor looking at a face while the student writes silently on paper off-camera cannot catch the error as it happens, and that is the entire advantage of live teaching.

Second, the IAs are the easiest place to use online sessions because the work is already a document. Remember the academic integrity rule: a tutor may teach the skill, ask questions and point at a criterion, but the student writes every word. That is true in either mode.

Third, for DP English the choice often turns on the Individual Oral. Rehearsing a fifteen-minute spoken performance works fine over video, but some students settle faster when the tutor is in the same room because they are practising speaking to a person, not to a camera.

## Is a Home Tutor Better for Younger IB Students?

Yes, for most students in MYP 1-3 and anyone in PYP. Younger students tend to learn better with a person in the room because they read the tutor's face and hands, they cannot yet manage a screen and a worksheet at once, and they find it harder to say "I did not get that" into a camera. A home tutor can also pick up non-verbal cues: a student rubbing their eyes, hiding a wrong answer with a palm, or going quiet when a question gets hard.

The balance changes around MYP 4. By then the student has the independence to manage a laptop, a pen tablet and a worksheet, and the content (extended mathematics, sciences with real derivations, Individual and Societies essays) is better served by a specialist than by whoever lives within six kilometres. For MYP Mathematics specifically, the criteria (A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating, D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts) reward visible reasoning, so the student must be taught to write it down in a way the tutor can examine, in either mode. The [IB MYP tutor page](/ib-myp-tutor-in-gurgaon/) describes how that support is arranged.

A practical test for a parent: if your child cannot yet sit through a thirty-minute video lesson at school without being redirected, a ninety-minute online session at home will be harder still, and a home tutor is the safer start.

## Is an Online Tutor Better for DP Students?

Often, but only when the student is organised enough to treat it as a real appointment. A DP student has six subjects, a Theory of Knowledge (TOK) essay, an Extended Essay (EE), Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) commitments and Internal Assessments (IAs) in most subjects. Time is the scarcest resource. An online session removes travel for the tutor and any hosting for the family, and it makes sessions of forty-five minutes viable, where a home visit rarely makes sense for less than an hour.

Online also solves the specialist problem. A DP2 Physics HL student in Gurgaon who needs help with the Additional Higher Level (AHL) topics may find only one or two strong tutors within a reasonable drive, and both may be booked on weekday evenings. Online widens the pool to those who can teach the exact course.

The risk is passivity. The student who watches the tutor solve a problem on screen and nods is not learning. Good online teaching keeps the student writing, with the tutor watching. A session where the student speaks for less than a third of the time is a lecture, and a lecture is the one thing a student can get from a recorded video for free. The pen-tablet and whiteboard set-up that prevents this is described in the online tutoring guide.

Choose a home tutor in DP when the student has a long-standing weakness in handwriting or organisation, lives close to a strong local tutor, or has proven they cannot stay on task in front of a screen. That is a legitimate choice, not an old-fashioned one.

## Home vs Online by Student Profile

Subjects tell you part of the story. The student tells you the rest. Five profiles come up repeatedly.

:::bars Typical lean by student profile (illustrative, higher = more online)
Self-driven DP2 student | 80 | Online
Anxious MYP 4-5 student | 40 | Either
Distractible younger student | 15 | Home
Student with a rare HL subject | 85 | Online
Student far behind in basics | 30 | Home first
Illustrative leanings from tutoring practice, not research data. Individual students vary.
:::

**The self-driven DP2 student.** Someone with a clear problem list, a past-paper habit and the discipline to attend. Online is efficient: the session is a surgical review of two or three papers' worth of errors, and travel adds nothing.

**The anxious MYP 4-5 student.** Anxiety often lives in a specific place: the blank page of an investigation, or timed assessment conditions. A tutor who is physically calm and present helps some students; others find it easier to ask for help from a distance. Try the home mode for the first two sessions, then ask the student, not the parent, which they prefer.

**The distractible younger student.** Home, with the phone out of the room. A screen opens too many doors for a student who has not yet built the habit of staying with a task.

**The student with a rare HL subject.** Online is almost always the right call. The tutor's subject depth outweighs everything else. Weigh the logistics only after the specialism is confirmed.

**The student far behind in basics.** This student needs patient, slow repair of fundamentals, frequent re-explanation and a tutor who can see small errors in real time. Home often starts better. After the foundations are rebuilt, many switch to online for efficiency. This is a natural handover point between modes, and it can be a deliberate part of the plan rather than an accident.

## Where Online Quietly Fails

Families rarely hear this part, so here it is plainly.

**A weak connection.** One that drops every ten minutes breaks the thread of a derivation. If the home Wi-Fi is unreliable at the lesson hour, fix it or choose home. A wired connection or a router in the same room solves most problems in practice.

**Poor writing set-up.** A tutor who cannot see the student writing a mathematical argument is teaching blind. If the family cannot arrange a tablet, a phone stand or a webcam angled at the page, the session degrades to talk. That is a limit, not a feature.

**Shared screens that become passive.** The tutor writes, the student watches. This looks productive and is not. Insist that the student does at least half the writing.

**A student who treats it as optional.** Online lessons are easier to skip, easier to join late and easier to half-attend. Fixed times, a fixed place and a parent who knows when it starts keep it real.

**Spoken language practice with a poor microphone.** For the Individual Oral in English or a Language Acquisition subject, audio quality matters. Test the sound before you commit.

## Where a Home Tutor Quietly Fails

Equally, home has weaknesses a brochure will never admit.

**Distance becomes the selection criterion.** The family picks the tutor who can reach the society, not the tutor who best fits the subject. A good test: if you had to teach this subject yourself, would you want this person? If the honest answer depends on the commute, you are choosing by location.

**Weekday traffic.** A tutor scheduled for six-thirty in the evening, crossing Gurgaon, can be late often enough to damage the habit. Ask the tutor how they travel, and watch the first month.

**Gate entry and logistics.** Large societies have visitor registration, security checks and lift access. Tell the guard in advance and share the tutor's details so the first ten minutes of each session are not lost at the gate.

**Cancellations.** A tutor who has travelled across the city and found nobody home is rightly unhappy. A student who arrives home late from an activity leaves the tutor waiting. Agree a cancellation window in advance.

**No flexibility in an emergency.** If a test is on Monday and the student panics on Saturday night, a home tutor is hard to book at short notice. Online is easier for those short corrections, which is why a mix can serve better than either mode alone.

## A Worked Example: Three Students, Three Different Answers

These are illustrative scenarios, not case histories.

**Consider a student in MYP 3, Gurgaon, strong in numeracy but slow in writing.** Her school report shows Criterion A at the top of the range and Criterion C pulling the grade down. She writes her answers in a rush and leaves out the reasoning that earns marks. A home tutor sitting at her table can interrupt mid-line, say "tell me why you moved that term across", and make the missing sentence appear on the page. After two months of home sessions the writing habit has built and the family can consider moving to fortnightly online check-ins.

**Consider a DP1 student in HL Chemistry who has just moved schools.** The new school's pace is faster and he is behind in organic chemistry. The only tutors available nearby are generalist science teachers; one specialist teaches in another part of the city. Online is the right call: the specialist works through two problems per session with the student writing on a tablet and sharing the screen, and the student sends a photograph of any stuck question between sessions. The saved commute is spent on a past-paper section.

**Consider a DP2 student in Mathematics AA HL, three months before the May exams.** She has the knowledge but loses marks on Paper 1 without a calculator. Her tutor visits at home on Saturday for a ninety-minute timed Paper 1 section, because the exam conditions (paper, no screen, a quiet room) are easier to reproduce at a table. On Tuesday and Thursday evenings she has thirty-minute online sessions to go through the errors. This is a mix, and it is a mix because each task has a different best setting.

The pattern across the three is that the mode followed the task. None of the families chose a mode first and then forced the task into it.

## What a Demo Session Should Show You in Each Mode

A demo costs the family little and tells it much. In either mode, watch for the same signals, because they are signals about the tutor and the fit, not about the mode.

1. The tutor asks about the school's calendar, the current unit and recent assessment feedback before teaching anything.
2. The tutor gives the student a question to try before explaining, and watches how the student starts.
3. The student speaks, writes and makes errors during the session. A demo where the tutor speaks for forty minutes shows you a lecture.
4. The tutor names two or three specific priorities at the end, in plain language.
5. The tutor says what would change the plan, for example "if the next criterion score does not move, we will adjust".

For a home demo, add: did the tutor arrive on time, and did the first ten minutes settle quickly? For an online demo, add: could the tutor see the student's writing live, and did the audio and video hold up? These are the extra checks that only each mode adds.

If the demo goes badly, say so. A fair provider will offer a different tutor. ## Can You Switch Modes Mid-Year Without Losing Progress?

Yes. Switching is easy if the tutor keeps a written record of the student's current priorities, recent errors and next topics, because that record moves with the student regardless of mode. The loss comes from changing tutors, not from changing delivery.

A clean switch has three parts. First, hand over a one-page summary: where the student is, what the last three sessions covered, what the next two should cover. Second, run the first session in the new mode as a short, focused review rather than a new topic, so you can see whether the set-up works. Third, give it two weeks before you judge. Students need time to adjust to a tablet or to a person at the table.

The commonest healthy switches are from home to online after a student has rebuilt foundations, and from online to home before a high-stakes exam block when the family wants timed papers in a physical room. Pre-exam Papers 1 and 2 are sat on paper, so some families deliberately reintroduce paper practice with a tutor in the room in the last weeks. The [last 30 days before IB exams](/blog/ib-exam-strategy-how-toppers-study-in-the-last-30-days/) guide gives a plan for that stretch.

## How Does School Timing Change the Choice?

School calendars matter more than parents expect. Most IB schools in India run an academic year from about April to March and enter candidates for the May exam session, so DP2 students are under heavy pressure from the start of the new year through to the exams, with mocks set by the school several months before May. In that window, the logistical advantages of online grow: a shorter session can be slotted between school hours and sport, and weekends are already crowded.

Some typical pressure points where the mode question changes:

- **The first weeks of DP1.** The student is adjusting, and a home tutor builds a relationship. If the school is far from the home, online may be more reliable on weekdays.
- **Before a school's internal examinations.** Short online sessions for specific topics work well.
- **DP2 mock season.** Timed paper practice at a table, with a tutor present or at least a parent supervising, mirrors the real hall.
- **The IA drafting window.** Online, document-based sessions are efficient; the teacher can comment on one draft, so a tutor's role is to teach the skill and ask questions, not to edit.
- **The final weeks before May.** Many families mix: online for quick error clinics, home for full timed papers.

## Common Mistakes When Choosing Between the Two

These are the errors seen most often when families weigh IB home tutor vs online tutor in Gurgaon.

**Choosing by prestige.** Some families assume a tutor in the room is more serious; others assume online is more modern. Neither is evidence of quality.

**Choosing by the parent's convenience.** A parent who works from home may prefer a tutor at the table, but if the student does better online, convenience should lose.

**Skipping the trial.** A demo takes one session and removes a lot of guessing. Do it in the mode you lean towards, then, if it is close, try the other for one session.

**Ignoring the physical set-up.** At home, a student with the television audible from the next room is no better off than an online student with notifications on. Fix the environment first.

**Treating the choice as permanent.** The best plans move with the student. A fixed mode for two years is a tradition, not a strategy.

**Asking the tutor which mode they prefer.** A tutor who works only at home will say home is better; one who works only online will say the opposite. Ask what the student's work needs and listen for specifics.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a home tutor or an online tutor better for IB students in Gurgaon?

Neither is better in general; each suits different students. Home suits younger students, easily distracted students and anyone who needs handwriting watched live. Online suits DP students who need a rare specialist, families with tight schedules and short targeted sessions. The right choice depends on age, subject, attention and logistics, which is why the eight-question score above is more useful than a general answer. Start with the mode your score leans towards, review after four weeks and switch if the evidence says so.

### Do online IB lessons work as well as in-person ones?

They can, if the set-up is right. The tutor must be able to see the student writing live, the connection must hold, and the student must do at least half of the writing. When those three conditions are met, online and in-person sessions can produce similar learning. When they are not, online degrades into a lecture. The test is simple: in the demo, did the tutor catch a mistake as it happened? If yes, the set-up works.

### Which IB subjects are best taught online?

Subjects with few specialists and document-based feedback do especially well online: HL Physics, HL Chemistry, Computer Science, Economics, the Extended Essay and the Internal Assessments. Mathematics works well too if a pen tablet is used. Younger students in MYP Mathematics and any PYP learner usually do better with a tutor in the room. The subject table earlier in this post gives a lean for each, and individual students can differ from it.

### My child gets distracted easily. Should I avoid online?

Not necessarily, but start with a home tutor if the distraction is severe. A screen offers a game, a chat and a video one click away. If you try online, keep the device in a shared room, close other tabs, use a tablet for writing instead of typing, and stay nearby for the first sessions. If after three or four sessions the student is still drifting, switch to home. This is a fit issue, not a failing.

### Is home tutoring in Gurgaon affected by traffic and gated societies?

Yes, both. Weekday evening traffic can make tutors late or shorten sessions, and large societies often require visitor registration at the gate. Share the tutor's name and number with security in advance, agree a cancellation window and ask how the tutor travels. If lateness becomes a pattern, move the slot to a weekend, pick a nearer tutor or switch to online for weekday sessions.

### Can we start with one mode and switch later?

Yes, and many families do. The loss comes from changing tutors, not from changing delivery. Ask the tutor to keep a written record of priorities and recent errors so the handover is clean, run a short review in the new mode first and give it two weeks before judging. The most common switches are home to online once foundations are rebuilt, and online to home before timed paper practice.

### What if my child says they prefer online but their marks are not improving?

Preference and progress are different things, so look at the evidence. Ask whether the student is writing during sessions, whether corrected errors stop recurring, and whether homework is done. If the answers are no, try one or two in-person sessions as an experiment, or change the session structure. The problem may be passivity, not the mode. If neither helps after a month, a different tutor may fit better, and you can ask for a different match.

### Do I need to be at home during an online lesson?

For younger students, yes, at least nearby. For older students a parent does not need to sit in, but should know the lesson time, check that the student is at the table with the device in a shared space, and ask afterwards what was covered. A short chat at the end of a session, such as "what will you do before next time?", does more good than hovering.

### Is a hybrid arrangement worth the complexity?

Often, for DP students in the last year. A weekly home session for timed papers, plus short online clinics for specific errors, uses each mode for what it does best. For a younger student with one subject, the added complexity is rarely worth it. The practical set-up is in the hybrid planning guide linked above.

### How do I know if the tutor is a good fit after the first month?

Look for three things: the student can explain what they are working on and why, recent errors have changed in kind (not just count), and the tutor can show you a record of priorities, progress and next steps. If these are missing after four weeks, raise it with the tutor first. If nothing changes, ask for a different match rather than waiting for the term to end.
