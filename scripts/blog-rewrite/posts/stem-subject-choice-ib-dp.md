IB DP STEM subject choice comes down to three questions asked in a fixed order: which degree you might apply for, what the universities on that list publish as their requirements, and which combination of Higher Level (HL) and Standard Level (SL) subjects you can sustain for two years while still scoring well. Most families start with the second question, or with a rumour about which subject "looks best", and end up with a combination that is hard to defend.

This guide is for students in the final year of the Middle Years Programme (MYP) or the first weeks of the Diploma Programme (DP) who are choosing, or considering changing, their maths and science subjects, and for the parents advising them. It covers the four common degree pathways (engineering, medicine and health, computer science and data, and pure science), the Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) versus Applications and Interpretation (AI) decision, how to check what a university actually asks for, the combinations people regret, and how a tutor fits into the decision. It does not rank subjects by prestige, because that is not how admissions work.

One caution before anything else. Admissions requirements change from year to year and differ by country and by university, so treat every example here as a pattern to verify, never as a rule. The sources that count are the course pages of the universities you may apply to and your school's DP coordinator.

## The three questions that decide an IB DP STEM subject choice

A sound IB DP STEM subject choice is built from three layers, and each one can overrule the one below it.

The first layer is the **degree**. "Engineering" and "medicine" are not equal as signals. Many engineering courses expect HL maths and physics; many medical courses expect HL chemistry and often a second science. "Computer science" splits depending on whether the course is theory-heavy or applied. A student who says "something in STEM" has not yet made the decision.

The second layer is the **evidence** from universities. Requirements are published per course, sometimes per year, and sometimes with a footnote that changes the meaning. The only way to know is to read them.

The third layer is **capacity**. Three hard HL subjects, a language, a humanities subject, the Extended Essay (EE), Theory of Knowledge (TOK) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) is a heavy load for two years. A combination that looks ideal on paper but produces 5s is usually worse for the student than a slightly different one that produces 6s and 7s.

:::steps The order for choosing STEM subjects
Degree | Shortlist two or three courses, not one
Evidence | Read each course's published requirements
Strengths | Check marks in MYP maths and science
Load | Count the hours a week each HL needs
Decide | Pick, then agree a review point in DP1
:::

## How the DP structure shapes the choice

The DP asks for six subjects, one from each of six groups (with the arts group replaceable by a second subject from groups 2 to 4), three or four at HL and the rest at SL. HL carries 240 recommended teaching hours and SL 150. Each subject is graded 1 to 7, and TOK and the EE together add up to 3 more points, so the maximum is 45. The minimum for the diploma is 24 points, subject to the IB's failing conditions.

Two consequences follow for STEM students. First, you have only three or four HL slots, and a student who wants HL maths, HL physics and HL chemistry has used all three before a language, a humanities subject or a third science is considered. Second, every subject outside STEM is still a graded subject. A 4 in English or a humanities subject costs the same number of points as a 4 in physics. Students who treat the non-STEM groups as an afterthought lose points they could have kept.

If you want to see how the core components sit around subject work, the [IB DP core planning timeline](/blog/ib-dp-core-planning-timeline/) lays out TOK, the EE and CAS across the two years.

## Mathematics first: AA or AI, HL or SL?

Maths is the subject that most often decides the rest of a STEM combination, because it is the one that is hardest to change later and the one that the largest number of degree pathways refer to.

DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each at HL and SL. Mathematics Analysis and Approaches is algebraic and proof-heavy, with an emphasis on calculus, functions and rigorous reasoning. Mathematics Applications and Interpretation is modelling and statistics-heavy, with technology used throughout. Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and the student, and the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement.

| Question | Points to AA | Points to AI |
|---|---|---|
| Does the target course list calculus or "advanced mathematics" as required? | Yes | No |
| Is the student fluent and happy with algebraic manipulation by hand? | Yes | Not necessarily |
| Is the student interested in statistics, data and modelling? | Less so | Yes |
| Is the degree in engineering, physics, mathematics or computer science theory? | Usually AA HL | Rarely enough on its own |
| Is the degree in economics, psychology, data science or social science? | Depends on university | Often acceptable, check carefully |
| Is the student's MYP extended mathematics grade strong? | Supports AA | Neutral |

The decision is covered in depth in [IB Maths AA vs AI: which one should you choose](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/). The point to take from the table is that AI HL is a legitimate, demanding course, not a soft option, and AA SL is a very different offer from AA HL. A student who picks AI HL because it feels easier is often surprised by how much modelling, statistics and technology it asks for.

### Maths SL: when it is the right call

Maths SL is a perfectly reasonable choice for a student whose degree does not need advanced maths, such as many biology, medicine or health courses, and whose HL slots are better used on sciences. The mistake is not choosing SL; the mistake is choosing it without checking the course page. Some medicine and life-science courses accept SL maths. A few engineering, physics and computer science courses will not.

Students aiming for the most mathematical courses can find the way the HL AA course is assessed explained in the [guide to scoring a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/). Reading the structure of Paper 1, Paper 2, Paper 3 and the internal assessment before choosing is a good way to test whether the course suits you.

## Pathway 1: engineering

Engineering degrees nearly always depend on strong mathematics and physics. The usual pattern, which you should verify, is Mathematics AA at HL and Physics at HL, with a third subject chosen for breadth or because the course lists it. Chemistry is sometimes listed for chemical, materials or biomedical engineering.

**The common combination.** Maths AA HL, Physics HL, and either Chemistry SL or Computer Science SL, with English, a second language and a humanities subject (Economics, Business Management, Geography or similar) at SL or HL. This gives the course-required subjects at HL and keeps the load manageable.

**Where students go wrong.** A student wants engineering but takes Maths AI HL because friends said it was easier. Some courses accept it with a note; many do not. Another goes the opposite way, taking all three of Maths AA HL, Physics HL and Chemistry HL without the time to do all three well, and finds the IA deadlines colliding in DP2.

**The test.** Search for the engineering course pages of three universities you might realistically apply to. Note the maths requirement, the physics requirement and whether they list a preferred third subject. If all three agree on Maths AA HL and Physics HL, that is the answer. If they diverge, the student has more flexibility than they thought.

For students who have chosen Physics at HL, the guides to [what to look for in an IB Physics HL tutor](/blog/ib-physics-hl-tutor-in-gurgaon-how-to-choose-the-right-one/) and the [IB Physics HL study plan](/blog/ib-physics-hl-most-important-topics-study-plan/) show what the course demands once the choice is made.

## Pathway 2: medicine, dentistry, veterinary and health sciences

For medical and health courses, Chemistry is the subject most often named as required, and Biology the subject most often named second. A combination of HL Chemistry and HL Biology is common among applicants, with Maths at SL or AI and the rest of the diploma balanced across languages and humanities.

Entry rules differ greatly between countries. Some courses specify two sciences at HL, some accept one HL and one SL, and some include an admissions test or interview weighting that matters as much as the subject grades. A few ask for specific minimum scores at HL, so the question is not only which subject but what score.

**The common combination.** Chemistry HL, Biology HL, Maths AI or AA at SL (or HL if the student is strong), English, a second language and a humanities subject.

**Where students go wrong.** Taking Physics HL "to keep options open" and dropping Biology, then discovering the courses they wanted listed Biology. Or choosing Chemistry HL without having done well in MYP chemistry, and meeting the calculation load of moles and energetics late. If a student is considering this, the guide to [IB Chemistry HL versus SL support](/blog/ib-chemistry-tutor-in-gurgaon-hl-vs-sl-support-guide/) explains what each level involves, and [why students struggle in IB Chemistry](/blog/ib-chemistry-why-students-struggle-how-to-fix-it/) explains where marks are lost.

Biology at HL has its own character: a large volume of content and a heavy emphasis on data-based questions and extended responses. DP Biology is organised into four themes (A Unity and diversity, B Form and function, C Interaction and interdependence, D Continuity and change) studied across four levels of organisation: molecules, cells, organisms and ecosystems. Students who prefer reasoning to recall sometimes find it harder than expected.

## Pathway 3: computer science, data science and software

This pathway is the least standardised. Some computer science degrees ask for HL mathematics and treat computing as a bonus. Others, especially those with a strong theory and algorithms component, want Mathematics AA HL. Data science and analytics courses often accept AI HL and value statistics, but verify.

**The common combination.** Maths AA HL or AI HL, Computer Science at HL or SL, a science (Physics is a frequent partner because of the problem-solving style), and the standard language and humanities subjects.

**Where students go wrong.** Assuming that taking Computer Science is enough. For many universities the maths grade carries more weight than the computing grade, because computing is not offered in every school and admissions teams cannot assume it. Another trap is choosing Maths AI HL for a theory-heavy course because "it is applied, like computing", only to find the course lists AA.

If the school does not offer IB Computer Science, or if the student needs help in it, [IB Computer Science tutoring in Gurgaon](/ib-computer-science-tutor-in-gurgaon/) is the relevant service page.

## Pathway 4: pure science, mathematics and research degrees

Physics, chemistry, mathematics, biochemistry, geology and similar degrees usually expect HL in the relevant subject plus HL maths, or HL in two of the three core sciences and Maths at least at SL. The more mathematical the degree, the more it leans on AA HL.

For physics and mathematics degrees, Maths AA HL and Physics HL are close to universal. For chemistry, Chemistry HL with Maths at HL or SL and either Physics or Biology is common. For biological sciences, Biology HL and Chemistry HL are the usual pair, with maths at least at SL.

Students aiming at research-led universities should also think about the IA and the EE. The science IA is the scientific investigation, worth 20%, marked on four criteria of 6 marks each: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion and Evaluation, with a maximum of 3,000 words. Students may investigate in groups of up to three, but each writes an individual report. A student who enjoys that kind of independent inquiry is better suited to the HL sciences than one who prefers problem sets.

## What university requirements actually look like, and how to check them

"HL maths" is not a single requirement. The same phrase can hide four different meanings.

| What the course page says | What it can mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "Mathematics HL" | Either AA or AI HL, or AA HL only | Read the footnote or admissions FAQ |
| "A science at HL" | Any of Physics, Chemistry, Biology | Check if one is named as preferred |
| "Chemistry and one other science" | HL in one, SL acceptable in the other | Confirm the level for each |
| "Typical offer 38 points with 6,6,5 at HL" | Subject grades are part of the offer | Treat the HL grades as the target |
| "Prior study of physics preferred" | Not required, but helpful | Weigh against capacity |

Four sources, in order of reliability, are the course page on the university's own site, the admissions office's FAQ or email reply, the DP coordinator's experience with past applicants to that institution, and then everything else (forums, agents, relatives). Never rely on a table on a forum that is more than a year old.

### Indian universities and the AIU

Many Gurgaon students apply to Indian universities as well as abroad. Equivalence for Indian university admission is issued by the Association of Indian Universities (AIU), and requirements differ by university and by course. Families should confirm with the institution rather than rely on general advice. ### Entrance exams and the DP

Students who intend to sit separate entrance exams, such as JEE for engineering, should think about how the DP subjects align with the entrance syllabus. The DP does not cover everything such exams test, so the combination matters, and so does a plan for the gap. ## Combinations people regret, and why

Regret in an IB DP STEM subject choice tends to follow a small number of patterns. Seeing them in advance is worth more than any ranking.

**The prestige stack.** Three HL STEM subjects chosen because they look strong. The student does well in two and struggles in the third, or the combination leaves too little time for the EE and IA. Outcome: a 5 or a 4 in a subject the student did not need at HL.

**The "keep every door open" combination.** HL maths, HL physics, HL chemistry and HL biology are not possible, since only three or four HL slots exist. Students who try to cover every pathway end up covering none well. A better rule is to cover the two most likely pathways and be honest about the rest.

**The easy-subject downgrade.** Choosing AI HL or an SL subject only to protect the score, then finding that the intended course needs the harder option. A related pattern is dropping to AA SL without realising that many courses want HL.

**The late switch.** Changing level or subject in DP1 is possible in many schools but gets harder every week, because the course moves on. A student who realises in November that they need AA HL rather than AI HL is making a bigger leap than one who switches in the first two weeks. Ask your coordinator for the school's deadline.

**The overload.** Taking a demanding HL subject while also carrying a heavy sports or arts commitment and a demanding CAS project. Capacity is real.

**The preference ignored.** Choosing a subject for parents' reasons when the student dislikes it. Two years of working at something you dislike is long, and the grade often shows it.

### A regret test

Before finalising, ask the student to imagine reporting results in July of DP2 and being disappointed in one subject. Which one is it likely to be? If the answer is obvious, discuss it now. If the student says "none", ask which subject they would most want to drop if they could. That subject is the one to examine.

## Is it better to choose Maths AA HL or Physics HL first if I can only keep one?

Keep the one the target degree names first, and if both are named, keep Maths AA HL, because it is the harder subject to add later and the one most STEM courses reference. Physics can sometimes be taken at SL while still meeting entry rules for an engineering course that asks for "physics" without a level. That is rarer than families assume, so verify it.

A student who would keep neither at HL, because the target is medicine, should be guided by Chemistry and Biology instead. The question "which one" only makes sense once the degree is chosen, which is why the degree comes first in the order above.

## Can you take HL Maths, HL Physics and HL Chemistry together?

Yes, many students do, but the load is high. Those three subjects together carry 720 recommended teaching hours at HL over two years, before the language, humanities, TOK, EE and CAS. Families should only plan this combination if the student's MYP results in both maths and science were consistently strong, if the school's timetable and IA schedule allow it, and if the student has a realistic plan for managing deadlines. For a degree that names all three, it may be necessary; for one that names two, the third is often better at SL.

## What do MYP results tell you about STEM subject choice?

MYP grades are useful evidence, though not a verdict. A student who consistently scores 6 or 7 in extended mathematics and sciences is generally well placed for HL. A student with 4s and 5s in the same subjects should treat HL as a decision that needs support or a different route, not as something that will sort itself out.

Read the criteria, not just the grade. A student with strong Criterion A (Knowing and understanding) and weak Criteria B, C and D (Investigating patterns, Communicating, Applying mathematics in real-life contexts) can do routine work but may struggle with the open-ended problems that DP HL assessments use. If the weakness is in communicating, the fix is a habit, and the gap is small. If it is in applying, the fix takes longer. A school's recommendation about extended or standard mathematics in MYP 5 is also a useful signal about AA readiness.

## A worked example: three students, three combinations

These are illustrations, not real families.

**Rohan wants mechanical engineering.** He has strong MYP extended maths and likes physics. He checks four engineering courses and all list Maths and Physics at HL. He chooses Maths AA HL, Physics HL, Chemistry SL, English and a language at SL, and Economics at SL. Chemistry at SL keeps the load manageable and still gives him a science for breadth.

**Meera wants medicine.** She enjoys biology and chemistry and finds maths adequate but not exciting. Her target courses list Chemistry and Biology. She chooses Chemistry HL, Biology HL, Maths AI SL, and English, a language and Psychology at SL. She keeps Physics for an optional self-study if she has time, but does not take it for the diploma.

**Kabir wants computer science and is unsure.** His shortlist includes one theory-heavy course and one applied course. The first lists Maths HL, the second does not specify the level. He chooses Maths AA HL, Computer Science HL, Physics SL and the usual non-STEM subjects. This keeps both options open without taking a fourth science. He agrees a checkpoint with his coordinator at the end of the first term to confirm the plan.

None of these is a universal answer for IB DP STEM subject choice. Each is the result of checking requirements, then fitting capacity.

## When to change your mind in DP1

The first weeks of DP1 are the cheapest time to change a subject or level, and many schools set a formal deadline. A student who finds that their HL subject is far beyond their current level, or that their target course has changed, should speak to the coordinator immediately.

Signs that a change is worth discussing: scores in the first unit tests far below the level needed for the target course, a need for help that is much larger than a tutor can address in the available time, or a shift in intended degree. Signs that it is not: one bad test, difficulty adjusting to the new style (common in the first month), and a temporary dip caused by workload.

A tutor can help distinguish between "this is hard and needs support" and "this is the wrong fit". A good tutor will say so when the evidence points to a level change. When a student needs help with the bigger question of timing, [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/) covers how to decide.

## How a tutor fits into the subject choice

Tutors do not choose subjects, and any tutor who tries to is overreaching. What a tutor can do is give evidence. A short diagnostic before the final decision can show whether a student's algebra is strong enough for AA HL, whether their chemistry calculations are fluent enough for Chemistry HL, or whether their reading and writing in the sciences suits the extended responses. That evidence goes into the family's conversation with the school coordinator.

After the choice, a tutor works on the specific HL demands. For physics and chemistry, that means [IB Physics HL](/blog/ib-physics-hl-tutor-gurgaon/), [IB Chemistry HL](/blog/ib-chemistry-hl-tutor-gurgaon/) and [IB Biology HL](/blog/ib-biology-hl-tutor-gurgaon/) support, each with different paper structures. ## Subject choice and the rest of the diploma

A STEM student's non-STEM subjects matter. Group 1 (language and literature) and group 2 (language acquisition) are graded, and they carry the same points. English A Language and Literature or English A Literature requires analysis and essay writing that some STEM students underestimate. Students whose first language is not English take English B, assessed through Paper 1 (a writing task), Paper 2 (listening and reading) and the individual oral.

Group 3 offers several options that pair well with STEM. Economics, with its units on microeconomics, macroeconomics and the global economy, suits students aiming at data science, finance or engineering management. Group 6 can be used for an arts subject or a second subject from groups 2 to 4, such as another science.

Think about the Extended Essay as well. The EE is an independent research essay of up to 4,000 words and roughly 40 hours of work, supervised by a teacher at the student's own school. A STEM student often writes it in a science, maths or computer science subject, and the choice of subject for the EE ties to the subject combination. The guide to [writing an IB Extended Essay](/blog/how-to-write-a-perfect-ib-extended-essay-ee/) covers the process.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which IB subjects do I need for engineering?

Most engineering courses expect Mathematics and Physics, often at HL, and many specify Mathematics Analysis and Approaches. Chemistry is sometimes required for chemical and materials engineering. Requirements vary by university and by country, so check the course pages of the institutions you may apply to and confirm with your DP coordinator. A typical safe combination is Maths AA HL and Physics HL plus a third subject chosen for breadth.

### Which IB subjects do I need for medicine?

Medical and health courses usually require Chemistry, often at HL, and many also ask for Biology. Some accept a different second science, and some courses state specific minimum grades at HL. Maths is often accepted at SL. Because rules differ widely between countries, read each course's published requirements and check admissions test and interview expectations, which can matter as much as the grades.

### Is Maths AI HL accepted for engineering and computer science?

Sometimes, but not reliably. Some courses accept AI HL, some accept it only with a note, and many specify AA. If the course is mathematically intense, assume AA HL is expected until the course page says otherwise. If you are unsure, email the admissions office with the exact question, because the answer can change the choice between AA and AI.

### Can I do three sciences in the IB Diploma?

Usually not: most schools cap it at two. The six subjects must include one from each of groups 1 to 5, so a student gets one science from group 4 and can take a second by using the sixth slot instead of an arts subject. A third would mean dropping a language, humanities or maths subject, which the diploma does not allow. Check your school's timetable for how classes are offered.

### Should I take HL Physics if I want to do medicine?

Usually not unless the course asks for it. Chemistry and Biology are the subjects most often named for medicine, so Physics HL is more likely to use a slot you would better spend on those. If you love physics or want to keep engineering open as a second option, take it at SL. Check each target course's requirements first.

### Is it too late to change subjects after the first term of DP1?

It gets harder with every week, and each school has its own deadline. Many schools allow changes in the first few weeks and become stricter afterwards. If a change looks likely, speak to your coordinator immediately. A student who is struggling for a specific, fixable reason may be better supported than moved. A tutor's diagnostic can help you decide which situation you are in.

### Do universities prefer HL subjects?

Universities usually specify the subjects and levels they need for a course, and many also set a total points requirement. HL signals greater depth in the subject, but a strong SL grade in a subject the course does not require may matter less than a strong HL grade in one it does. Predicted grades and the overall score both feed into offers. Read each course's requirements for what counts.

### What if my school does not offer the combination I want?

Ask the coordinator about alternatives, such as an online course provider the school recognises, or a different subject that still satisfies the course. Some students consider a school change, which is a major decision to weigh against the disruption. Avoid dropping a required subject without confirming the effect on your applications first.

### Can a tutor help me decide between HL and SL?

A tutor can give evidence, not the decision. A short diagnostic session shows how comfortable the student is with the content and the style of questions at each level. That information goes into a conversation with the school coordinator and the family. The final choice depends on the course requirements, the student's capacity and the school's timetable.

### How many HL subjects should a STEM student take?

Three is the minimum, four is the maximum, and the right number depends on the target course and the student's load. Four HL subjects increase workload significantly, and many students are better served by three HL plus three SL with strong grades. Take a fourth only when a course requires it or when the student has clear capacity.
