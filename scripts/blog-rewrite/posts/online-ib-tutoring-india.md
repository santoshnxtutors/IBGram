Online IB tutoring in India works when the lesson is built around a shared writing surface, a tutor who has taught the specific International Baccalaureate (IB) syllabus, and a fixed structure that ends with work the student must do alone. It fails when the "lesson" is two people talking over video while the student nods. The technology is not what separates the two outcomes. The session design is.

This guide is for parents and students in Gurgaon, Delhi, Mumbai, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Kolkata, a Tier 2 city with no IB specialist within 40 minutes, or a family living abroad whose child follows an Indian IB school calendar. It covers the equipment that matters (and the equipment that does not), a minute-by-minute session blueprint, how each Diploma Programme (DP) and Middle Years Programme (MYP) subject behaves on screen, the awkward realities of power cuts, bandwidth and time zones, and the cases where we would tell you to skip online and book a tutor in the room.

If you are still deciding between a tutor at your door and a tutor on a screen, read the subject-and-student scoring table in [IB home tutor vs online tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-home-tutor-vs-online-tutor-gurgaon/) alongside this page. This one assumes you have chosen online, or are seriously considering it, and want to know how to make it work.

## What Is Online IB Tutoring, and How Is It Different From a Recorded Course?

Online IB tutoring is a live, scheduled lesson between one tutor and one student (occasionally two) on a video call with a shared digital whiteboard, where the tutor can see the student's working appear in real time and respond to it. The word that matters is *live*. A recorded course, a coaching app with a video library, or a pre-set worksheet portal is something else.

The difference is easy to feel in the first ten minutes. In a recorded lecture, a student who misreads the question watches the instructor answer the correct question. In a live lesson, the tutor sees the misreading on the board as it happens and asks "read me the command term in that question." That interruption is the product. Everything else, the pen tablet, the screen sharing, the folder of past papers, exists to make that interruption possible and fast.

It also differs from a school classroom. A school teacher is running a plan for twenty or thirty students. A tutor in an online lesson is running a plan for one, built from that student's marked scripts, their Internal Assessment (IA) drafts and the specific marks they lost last month. That is why a well-run online hour can cover more ground than it appears to, and why a badly run one is such a waste: there is nowhere for a weak plan to hide.

## Why Families Choose Online IB Tutoring in India

Four reasons come up repeatedly for choosing online IB tutoring in India, and they are not equally good reasons.

**Access to a specialist.** IB is taught in a limited number of schools, and the pool of tutors who know the markschemes, the command terms and the criterion language is thin in most cities. A family in Indore or Coimbatore may have no one within reach who has taught Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) Higher Level (HL). Online removes the postcode from the search. This is the strongest reason.

**Time recovered from travel.** In Gurgaon, Delhi, Mumbai and Bangalore, a 60-minute lesson that needs a 45-minute commute each way is a poor use of a Class 11 evening. Online hands that time back to the student, which in a heavy DP1 week can mean the difference between finishing the lesson's practice that night or not at all.

**Scheduling flexibility.** Tutors working across time zones can offer slots that a local tutor cannot, including very early or late sessions for students abroad. Rescheduling a lesson also costs less when nobody is travelling.

**Continuity.** A student who moves city, changes school within the IB system, or spends a term abroad can keep the same tutor. For DP2 students in particular, changing tutors four months before the May exams is a real cost.

The weaker reason is "it is cheaper." Fee differences between online and home sessions exist, but they depend on the tutor, the level and the subject, not on the medium alone. The drivers are set out in [what affects IB tutor fees in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-tutor-fees-in-gurgaon-what-affects-the-cost/), and the same logic applies across Indian cities. Choose online for access and time, not on a hunch about price.

## The Kit That Actually Matters

Parents often ask whether they need to buy anything. The honest answer is: one inexpensive item, and a few settings.

| Item | Needed? | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop or desktop with a webcam | Yes | A phone screen is too small to read a graph or a worked solution. A tablet is acceptable for English or Economics but poor for maths. |
| Pen tablet or tablet with stylus | Yes for maths and sciences | Typing mathematics is slow and discourages the working-out the markscheme rewards. Handwriting on the shared board is the single biggest quality lever. |
| Second device (phone) on a stand | Strongly recommended | Points at the student's paper so the tutor sees handwritten working, graph sketches and diagrams. |
| Wired earphones or a headset | Recommended | Cuts echo and background noise from a household that is still running. |
| Graphic display calculator (GDC) | Yes for maths | The tutor needs to see what the student presses. See the GDC section below. |
| Wi-Fi with a mobile hotspot as backup | Yes | The backup is what saves the lesson on the evening the router fails. |
| Ring light, fancy microphone, dedicated "studio" | No | Not worth buying. A desk lamp and a quiet corner are enough. |

Two settings are worth ten minutes the day before the first session. First, agree which platform the lesson runs on and test screen sharing and the whiteboard with the tutor, ideally in a short trial. Second, make sure the pen tablet's pressure and palm rejection work. A tablet that registers the heel of the hand as scribbles makes the student give up and type, and the benefit disappears.

### The pen tablet question

A basic drawing tablet connected to a laptop is usually enough. Many students already have a tablet and stylus for school notes and can use it as a second whiteboard surface. What matters is that handwriting appears on the shared canvas within a fraction of a second, that the tutor can write on it too, and that the board saves. Ask the tutor to export the board at the end of each lesson. A folder of dated board images is the closest thing online tutoring has to the exercise book a student carries home.

## The Shared Whiteboard: Where the Teaching Actually Happens

The whiteboard replaces both the paper on the desk and the tutor's pen. A good lesson uses it in three distinct ways.

**The tutor writes, the student watches.** Used sparingly: to model a method, to show how a markscheme line is laid out, to demonstrate where a "show that" argument ought to start. Anything beyond ten minutes of this and the student is spectating.

**The student writes, the tutor watches.** This is the core of the hour. The student attempts a question on the shared board while talking through each step. The tutor stays quiet until a mistake appears, then asks a question rather than supplying the fix: "What does the second line assume about the sign of x?"

**The board is annotated with the markscheme.** After an attempt, the tutor overlays the method marks, accuracy marks and the notation the examiner expects, and the student sees where each mark sat. This is difficult to do on a video call without a board and trivial with one, which is why the shared board is the main advantage over a casual video chat.

For subjects that are not maths-heavy, the shared board still earns its keep. In English or Economics, a student can paste a paragraph on to the board and the tutor can highlight claims that lack evidence, evaluation that is only asserted, or an analysis that restates the quotation. Paper-based marking is rarely this immediate.

## The GDC on Screen: Teaching Calculator Skills Online

In IB Mathematics, the graphic display calculator (GDC) is not an optional extra. Paper 2 and Paper 3 of Analysis and Approaches HL require it, and Applications and Interpretation (AI) uses technology throughout the course. A tutor who cannot see what the student is pressing cannot fix a slow or wrong workflow.

There are three practical set-ups, and a good tutor will insist on one of them:

1. **Handheld GDC under the second camera.** The phone on a stand is angled so the tutor sees the calculator screen and the student's fingers. Simple, but screens can glare.
2. **Emulator on the laptop, shared on screen.** Most major GDC makers provide software emulators that mirror the handheld's menus. The student's screen share shows exactly which menu path they took. This is the cleanest option for demonstrating techniques.
3. **Both, switched by task.** Emulator for learning a new technique, handheld for timed practice, because the exam is sat on the handheld and keystroke muscle memory matters.

The reason this matters is that most GDC marks are lost to *slowness* and to *unreadable output*, not to ignorance. A student who finds the intersection of two curves in 20 seconds and writes down "intersection at x = 2.31 from GDC" with a sketch earns the marks. One who spends three minutes wrestling with window settings and then writes only a number does not. For the specific techniques worth drilling, see [the GDC advantage in IB DP Mathematics AA HL](/blog/the-gdc-advantage-in-ib-dp-mathematics-aa-hl/).

## A Session Blueprint: What 60 Minutes Should Look Like

A tutor who cannot describe the structure of a typical lesson is improvising. This is the blueprint we recommend families ask about, whichever platform is used.

:::timeline A well-structured 60-minute online IB lesson
0-5 min | Warm-up: one retrieval question from last week's topic
5-15 min | Review of homework errors, with the student explaining each
15-40 min | New material or past-paper practice, student writing on the board
40-52 min | Timed exam-style question under real conditions
52-60 min | Marking, a short error-log entry, and tomorrow's task
:::

A few notes on why this order works.

The **warm-up** makes the student retrieve something from memory without notes. Retrieval is a stronger memory aid than rereading, and starting with it also tells the tutor, honestly, what has stuck.

The **homework review** is where the real diagnosis happens. The tutor does not simply mark the page. The student is asked to explain two or three wrong answers aloud. Students who cannot explain why an answer was wrong have usually copied a method, and that is the cue to go back to the idea.

The **new material or practice block** is the longest block but should still be mostly the student's hand on the board. If the tutor has been speaking for fifteen consecutive minutes, something has gone wrong.

The **timed question** is non-negotiable. Under real conditions, silently, with the camera on the student's paper. IB marks are earned under time pressure, and a student who only ever works at their own pace online will be surprised by the paper.

The **close** is five minutes that most students and tutors skip. It produces a short error-log entry (what went wrong, why, and what the fix is) and a single piece of homework, small enough to be completed. A tutor who sets forty questions of homework is not being generous. They are making the next session's review impossible.

For a 90-minute session, the sensible change is a longer practice block and a second timed section, not a longer lecture. Longer sessions suit exam preparation, and shorter ones, 40 to 45 minutes, suit younger MYP students whose attention on a screen fades sooner.

## How Each Subject Behaves Online

Not every IB subject translates to a screen equally. The honest summary is below, followed by detail.

| Subject | Online fit | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics AA / AI (HL and SL) | Very good with a pen tablet | Handwriting on the board is essential; GDC demo needs a camera or emulator |
| Physics, Chemistry | Good | Diagrams and derivations suit the board; practical skills for the IA need teacher-led lab work at school |
| Biology | Good | Diagrams and data analysis translate well; models are easier with a shared image library |
| Economics, Business | Very good | Diagrams are quick to draw; evaluation writing benefits from live annotation |
| English A / English B | Good | Text annotation works well; oral practice needs a stable audio connection |
| Languages (Hindi, French, Spanish) | Good to mixed | Speaking is fine online; pronunciation feedback needs good audio |
| MYP Mathematics (MYP 4-5) | Good with parental set-up | Younger students need a more structured session and less screen time per block |
| Visual Arts, Design, Music | Limited | Practical craft and performance are hard to coach through a screen; theory and portfolio review work |
| CAS and TOK | Good for guidance | Reflection conversations and essay planning work well |

**Mathematics** is the subject online suits best, with one condition: the student writes. Students who type, or who watch a tutor write, learn markedly less than students who work every step on the board. For the broader subject structure and how to build marks, the flagship guide [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/) lays out the system we expect an online tutor to follow.

**Sciences** work on screen for theory, calculations and data interpretation. The caution is the IA. The DP science internal assessment is an investigation the student carries out and reports individually, and the practical work happens in the school laboratory. An online tutor can help a student shape a research question, think about uncertainty, and read a criterion, but cannot supervise the practical. They also must not write or edit the report. The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher feedback on one draft, and a tutor's proper role is to teach the skill and ask questions, never to produce the text.

**Economics and Business** are among the easiest subjects to move online, because diagrams are quick to draw and the core skill is written evaluation, which is easy to annotate live.

**English and languages** work well, with an audio caveat. Individual oral practice depends on a clear connection. A student rehearsing a 15-minute Individual Oral with a voice that breaks every thirty seconds is practising the wrong thing.

**MYP subjects** are worth a separate mention. MYP students are younger, the criteria language (Criteria A to D, each scored 0 to 8) is unfamiliar to families, and parents are rightly more involved. For MYP maths in particular, see [the global guide to choosing an online IB maths tutor for MYP students](/blog/how-to-choose-online-ib-maths-tutor-myp-students-global-guide/) and the page for [MYP online tutors across India](/india/ib-myp-online-tutors/).

## A Worked Example: One Lesson on a Calculus Question

An abstract blueprint is less convincing than a real hour, so consider a typical DP2 student working on an optimisation question in Analysis and Approaches HL.

The tutor sets the question on the shared board and asks the student to write the first line only. The student writes the objective function but forgets to state the constraint that links the two variables. The tutor does not mention the missing constraint. She asks: "How many unknowns does your function have, and how many equations?" The student sees two unknowns and one equation, and writes the constraint herself.

She differentiates, sets the derivative to zero, and arrives at a critical point. The tutor asks one more question: "How do you know it is a maximum and not a minimum?" The student has been skipping this step in homework because "the answer looked sensible." The tutor points to the method mark the markscheme reserves for the justification, and the student adds a second-derivative test.

Then comes a short timed question of the same type. The student does it in six minutes, working on the board, and the tutor marks it against a markscheme line by line. The two marks lost, one for the missing justification and one for an unrounded final value, go into the error log. Next week's warm-up will be a question that needs exactly that justification.

None of this required special technology beyond the shared board. It required a tutor who knew where the marks sit and who resisted the urge to explain. That is the difference you are paying for, and it is available online as readily as in person.

## When Is Online IB Tutoring the Wrong Choice?

Online IB tutoring is the wrong choice when the student cannot hold attention on a screen for an hour, when the home has no quiet corner or reliable connection, when the subject depends on hands-on practical coaching, or when the student is young enough to need an adult in the room. In those cases, in-person lessons will usually produce more learning per hour.

Look at the four situations more closely.

**The student drifts on screens.** Some students, particularly younger MYP students, have spent so many hours on screens for school that a tutor on a laptop reads as "more of the same." If a trial lesson shows the student checking another tab, stifling yawns, or answering in monosyllables, the fault is not always the tutor. A tutor in the room often changes the energy.

**The home environment cannot support it.** A shared room, a family television in the next room, a younger sibling's classes, or a connection that stutters every evening will undermine online lessons quickly. Fixing the room is cheaper than changing the tutor, but if it cannot be fixed, choose in-person.

**The learning needs are physical.** Visual Arts portfolios, Design technology builds, music performance and laboratory technique are hard to coach online. Theory around them, such as reflection, research or evaluation, can work well, but the craft needs the room.

**The student needs a very structured adult presence.** A student with significant attention difficulties, or one rebuilding confidence after a bad term, sometimes responds better to a tutor who is physically there, who can see the tapping foot and the clenched jaw. This is a judgement for the family and the tutor to make together after a trial.

## Time Zones, Power Cuts and Bandwidth: The Practical Plan

These three are the real reasons online lessons fail, and no guide that skips them is worth much.

### Families in India

The usual enemies are evening power cuts in some areas, a weak connection at the peak hour of 7 to 10 pm when the whole building is online, and monsoon-season outages. The workable plan has four parts. Keep a mobile hotspot on a second network ready before the lesson starts. Charge the laptop and tablet fully, so a short power cut does not end the session. Agree with the tutor that a dropped lesson resumes for the remaining time, or that the missed minutes are made up. And lower the video quality before lowering the audio, because a lesson without video is usable and one without audio is not.

### Families abroad

A student at an Indian IB school who is temporarily abroad, or a family that follows the Indian school calendar from overseas, has a different problem. Indian Standard Time is UTC+5:30 with no daylight saving, so the gap to the UK, UAE, Singapore, the USA and Australia shifts with their clock changes twice a year. When a lesson slot is agreed in October, check whether it will still suit both sides in April. Pages for families overseas are at [IB tutors for the USA](/usa/), [the UK](/uk/), [the UAE](/uae/) and [Singapore](/singapore/).

### Tutors travelling or in other cities

A tutor in a different time zone from the student is manageable but should be agreed in advance. If the time zone changes mean the student is being taught at 6 am before school, that is not a sustainable arrangement however good the tutor is. Sleep is part of the plan.

### A quick resilience checklist

1. Test the platform, whiteboard and screen sharing with the tutor in a trial lesson.
2. Place the router in a spot with a clear signal to the study desk, or use a wired connection.
3. Keep a charged hotspot device within reach.
4. Agree what happens if the lesson drops: resume, reschedule, or make up the minutes.
5. Share the board export folder with the tutor so notes survive any device problem.

## How to Test an Online IB Tutor Before You Commit

A trial lesson is the best test, and a good tutor expects it. What follows is a checklist for what to observe, not just whether the student "liked" the tutor.

:::steps Judging a trial lesson
Setup | Whiteboard, audio and screen sharing work first time
Questions | Tutor asks more than they explain
Marks | Tutor refers to markschemes and command terms
Close | Error log and one clear task for next time
:::

During the trial, watch for these signals.

- **Who writes on the board?** If the tutor does most of the writing, the student is spectating.
- **What does the tutor ask?** Good tutors ask the student to explain. Weak ones ask "do you understand?"
- **Do they mention marks, not just topics?** An IB tutor should talk about method marks, command terms, and criteria without being prompted.
- **Is there a diagnostic?** The first sessions should include some way of finding out what the student actually knows, such as a short paper, a past script, or a marked IA draft.
- **Is the close concrete?** A named piece of homework and a short error-log entry.
- **Does the student leave knowing something they did not know?** The most honest test of all.

Many students find the first session a little awkward on any platform, so judge across two or three trial lessons if the budget allows, and not on first impressions alone. The wider checklist for credentials, red flags and board knowledge sits in [how to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/how-to-choose-ib-tutor-gurgaon/), which applies equally to tutors outside the city.

## Setting Up the Home for Online Lessons

The room matters more than the router. A few simple arrangements make a visible difference.

A **fixed place** with a table at the right height, good light on the paper, and the second camera on a stand. The student should not be working from a bed.

A **no-interruption rule** for the hour: phone on silent, notifications off on the laptop, a door that closes. Younger students may need a parent nearby, but within earshot, not looking over a shoulder.

A **paper stock**: a notebook for each subject, a pen the student likes, a ruler, and a clean copy of any past paper being used. Online lessons do not mean paperless lessons. The exam is on paper, so practice should also be on paper at least part of the time.

A **file system**: a folder per subject, containing the board exports, marked papers and the error log. This sounds fussy and saves a great deal of time in DP2, when a tutor needs to find a script from October to prove a point in March.

For MYP students, a parent should be in the building and aware of when the lesson is, and should look at the board export afterwards. For DP students, the expectation shifts: the student runs the lesson, and the parent receives a short summary every few weeks.

## The Parent's Role During Online Lessons

Parents are rightly curious and sometimes anxious about what is happening on the screen. A balanced approach looks like this.

Be present at the start of the first lesson to confirm the technology works, then step back. A parent in the corner of the room for every session changes the student's behaviour, usually for the worse in older students.

Ask for a **short written summary** after each block of sessions. A good tutor can write three lines: what was covered, what the student found hard, and what the next step is. If the tutor cannot, that is information.

Look at the **board exports** once a fortnight. You do not need to understand the mathematics to see whether the student is writing full working, and whether the same sorts of marks are recurring in the error log.

Do not attend to the **mood of the lesson** as a verdict. A good lesson is often uncomfortable, because the student is being asked to do things they cannot yet do. A student who says "it was hard today" is usually having a better lesson than one who says "it was fine."

## How Online Tutoring Fits the IB Calendar

A tutoring plan that ignores the school calendar wastes the hours. The general shape across a DP year is below, and the specifics depend on the school. Check dates with the school's DP coordinator.

:::timeline Typical intensity of online support across the DP
DP1 Term 1 | Foundations, gap repair, building habits
DP1 Term 2-3 | Core topics, first IA planning
DP2 Term 1 | IA completion, Paper practice begins
DP2 Term 2 | Mock preparation and error-log work
Pre-May | Timed papers, final revision, light touch
:::

In DP1, a typical pattern is one session a week per subject that needs support. In DP2, many families increase to two sessions for the hardest subject in the three months before the school mocks, then taper in the weeks before May. The question of when to start is covered in [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/), and for students whose weekdays are full, [weekend IB tutoring](/blog/weekend-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/) shows how to arrange blocks that do not burn the student out.

Online sessions make the high-intensity months easier to manage, because an extra 45-minute lesson on a Tuesday night does not need a journey. That is one of the clearest practical gains. It is also a risk: it is easy to over-schedule when scheduling is frictionless. A student with eight hours of tutoring a week and no time to practise alone is being taught rather than helped.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is online IB tutoring as effective as in-person tutoring?

Online IB tutoring can be as effective when the tutor is IB-trained, the student writes on a shared board and the lesson has a fixed structure. It is less effective for young or easily distracted students, and for practical subjects. Effectiveness depends on lesson design far more than on the medium. A trial lesson shows quickly which side a particular student falls on.

### What equipment does my child need for online IB lessons?

A laptop with a webcam, a pen tablet or tablet with stylus for maths and sciences, a phone on a stand to show handwritten work, and a stable connection with a mobile hotspot backup. For maths, the student also needs the graphic display calculator or an emulator. Expensive extras such as ring lights or studio microphones are unnecessary.

### Can online tutors help with the Internal Assessment and Extended Essay?

Yes, within the IB's academic integrity rules. A tutor can help a student understand the criteria, choose and narrow a topic, plan the structure and question their reasoning. A tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission. The school's teacher or supervisor gives feedback on one draft, and the work must remain the student's own. Be wary of anyone who offers more.

### How long should an online IB lesson be?

For DP students, 60 to 90 minutes works well, with 90 reserved for exam preparation phases. For MYP students, 40 to 60 minutes is usually enough because attention on a screen fades sooner. One long lesson is rarely better than two shorter ones in the same week, so split the time if the student tires.

### Does online IB tutoring work for MYP students?

It works for MYP when a parent sets up the room, the sessions are shorter, and the tutor knows the criteria (A to D, each scored 0 to 8). It is less suited to MYP 1 to 3 students who struggle to sit still. Many families start online for MYP 4 and 5, when the content is more exam-oriented.

### What happens if the internet or power fails mid-lesson?

A prepared family resumes on a mobile hotspot within a few minutes and the tutor extends or reschedules the lesson. Agree the rule beforehand, charge the devices, and keep the board export folder shared so notes are not lost. If outages in your area are frequent at the lesson hour, move the slot rather than fight it.

### Can my child take lessons from abroad while following an Indian IB school?

Yes. The practical issues are the time zone, which shifts with daylight saving changes in the student's country, and making sure the tutor plans around the school's own mock and May exam dates. Agree a slot that works in both winter and summer, and confirm the plan with the school if the student will be away for long.

### How do I know the online tutor is genuinely qualified for IB?

Ask which programme, subject and level they have taught, and ask them to describe a past-paper question's markscheme in their own words. IB Gram shortlists tutors whose profiles are verified and fit the family's query, and a demo or trial session can be arranged before committing, subject to tutor availability. Still run your own check during the trial.

### What if my child makes no progress after a month online?

Review the lessons before blaming the medium. Check that the student writes on the board, that there is a diagnostic and an error log, and that the homework is small enough to finish. If the structure is right and nothing improves, ask for a different tutor match. If the structure is wrong, fix that first. Progress in IB is rarely visible in four weeks, but process should be.

### Can we combine online and home lessons?

Yes, and many families do, and online IB tutoring in India often sits alongside a home tutor for exactly this reason. A common pattern is home lessons during term for the subject that needs close attention, and online sessions for extra subjects, exam weeks or holidays. The set-up each mode needs, and how to switch midyear, is covered in the hybrid guide linked earlier in this post.
