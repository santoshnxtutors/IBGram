## Which IGCSE Maths Syllabus Is Your Child Actually Taking?

Before you speak to any tutor, confirm the exact syllabus code, the tier and the exam series with the school. IGCSE mathematics is not one course: Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580, Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606 and Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) differ in structure, content and the papers a student sits. A tutor who is strong on one can be weak on another.

For Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams), Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), and Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Core tops out at grade C and Extended reaches A*. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier and grades on the 9 to 1 scale.

| Syllabus | Board | Structure | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics 0580 Core | Cambridge | Paper 1 (non-calculator), Paper 3 (calculator) | Grades cap at C |
| Mathematics 0580 Extended | Cambridge | Paper 2 (non-calculator), Paper 4 (calculator) | Reaches A* |
| Additional Mathematics 0606 | Cambridge | Two written papers, calculator allowed, no tiers | Graded A* to E; usually taken alongside 0580 Extended |
| Mathematics A 4MA1 | Pearson Edexcel | Foundation or Higher, two calculator papers per tier | Grades 9 to 1 |

Parents sometimes also meet 0607, another Cambridge mathematics syllabus with its own content and assessment. If your child's school uses it, ask the tutor specifically about it, and confirm the details against the current syllabus from the school.

### Why the code matters to the tutor

The code decides the papers, and the papers decide the preparation. A student on 0580 Extended needs a tutor who can prepare for a non-calculator paper and a calculator paper, with a stretch of topics that reaches A*. A student on 0606 needs a tutor comfortable with functions, logarithms and exponentials, coordinate geometry of the circle, trigonometry, series, vectors, differentiation and integration. A student on 4MA1 Higher needs a tutor who knows the Edexcel question style. The parent guide to [IGCSE Maths 0580 and 0606](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/) explains the two Cambridge courses in more detail.

### Core or Extended: a tier question that shapes everything

A student entered for Core cannot score above a C, however well they do. A student entered for Extended who is not ready can be demoralised by papers that are out of reach. The school makes the entry decision, and a parent can ask how it was reached and whether it can be reviewed. A strong tutor will have a view on the tier after a short diagnostic and will say so honestly, including when they think a student is better served by securing a strong result at one tier than overreaching at the other.

## What Qualities Separate a Strong IGCSE Maths Tutor in Gurugram?

A strong IGCSE maths tutor in Gurugram shows six qualities: syllabus fluency, a diagnostic habit, an ability to teach method and not only answers, disciplined past-paper practice, honest communication and reliable logistics. Qualifications and years of experience can help, but they are weak signals on their own, and none of them replaces a trial session.

1. **Syllabus fluency.** They can tell you what each paper covers, which topics carry the most marks, and where students on your child's tier typically lose them.
2. **A diagnostic habit.** They want to see marked work before proposing a plan.
3. **Teaching of method.** They get the student to do the thinking, and they explain why a step is valid.
4. **Past-paper discipline.** They use papers and mark schemes at the right stage and analyse errors.
5. **Honest communication.** They report what is and is not improving, in plain words.
6. **Reliability.** They arrive on time, keep the slot and plan around the school calendar.

### Quality one: can they speak the syllabus?

Ask: "What are the first topics you would check for a Grade 10 student on 0580 Extended, and why?" A strong answer names specifics: algebraic manipulation and rearranging, number and standard form, ratio and proportion, trigonometry including non-right-angled triangles, and the habit of showing method on the non-calculator paper. A weak answer says "everything" or "we go chapter by chapter." The topic list for each paper is on the official syllabus, and a tutor who has taught the course will not need to look it up.

### Quality two: do they diagnose before they teach?

A strong tutor starts with a short, honest diagnostic. That means looking at the last two school tests, a past-paper section done under time, and a conversation about what the student finds hard. They are trying to sort errors into four causes: gaps in earlier content (often algebra or fractions from Grade 8), problems reading and setting up questions, calculation and calculator habits, and tier or timing mismatch. A tutor who starts teaching Chapter 1 on day one, without any of this, is following a script and not your child.

### Quality three: do they teach method or hand over answers?

In a session, watch who holds the pen. The pattern to look for is: tutor models once, student does the next one alone, tutor watches and asks "why did you do that step?", student corrects. The pattern to avoid is a tutor who does the question while the student nods. The old test for any lesson is whether the student can do a similar question alone a few days later. Ajay Vatsyayan's approach puts concept first: the student should know what a formula represents and when to use it, so that an unfamiliar question can be adapted to, and the tutor identifies whether a mistake is conceptual, a calculation slip, a misreading or a wrong method, since each needs a different response.

### Quality four: how do they use past papers?

Past papers are valuable only when used at the right stage and analysed. A strong tutor introduces topic-level exam questions early, moves to mixed-topic sections, and then to full timed papers in the final months. After each paper, the student and tutor mark with the official mark scheme, record each lost mark by cause in an error log, and redo the wrong questions. The detailed method is in the guide to [IGCSE maths past papers](/blog/why-past-papers-are-key-to-igcse-maths-success/). A tutor who "gives lots of papers" without marking and analysis is adding volume and not skill.

### Quality five: do they communicate honestly?

You should hear from a good tutor, in a few lines every two weeks, what the student has worked on, what is improving and what is not. A tutor who only reports that the student is "doing fine" is not helping you decide anything. Ask at the start how and how often you will get updates, and whether you can see the error log.

### Quality six: are they reliable?

Reliability is boring and decisive. A tutor who cancels often, arrives late or cannot commit to a regular slot will undermine the arrangement, however good the teaching. In Gurugram, weekday evening traffic and society gate entry affect home sessions, so ask how the tutor plans for them, and whether online sessions are the fallback.

## What Should a Strong Tutor Do in the First Month?

In the first month, a strong IGCSE maths tutor diagnoses, sets a small number of targets, starts an error log, fixes one or two high-yield habits, and gives you evidence of change in the student's process, even if the grade has not moved yet. A month is short, so expect early signs and not transformation.

:::timeline The first four weeks with a new maths tutor
Week 1 | Diagnostic: marked work, a timed section, a conversation
Week 2 | Targets agreed; error log begins; first habit fixed
Week 3 | Topic repair plus mixed practice; first timed section
Week 4 | Review: error-log trends, process change, next plan
A typical pattern; the pace depends on the student's starting point and the exam date.
:::

### Week one: diagnose

The tutor looks at two recent marked tests, sets a timed section from a past paper of the right tier, and talks to the student about how they approach questions. They also check the calculator: whether the student can use fraction, standard-form and statistics functions correctly. By the end of the week they should be able to tell you the three most important things to fix, in plain language, with evidence from the student's work.

### Week two: targets and the error log

The tutor sets two or three targets, such as "write the working for every non-calculator question" or "stop losing marks on rearranging formulae". They begin an error log with columns for question, topic, cause (gap, reading, calculation, method, timing, careless) and the fix. This is the same instrument that helps students in IB mathematics, laid out in the guide to [scoring a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/), and it works at IGCSE level just as well.

### Week three: repair and mix

Topic repair begins on the largest gaps, usually algebra, then proportion, then geometry. The tutor adds mixed practice, since the exam does not announce the topic. A first timed section tests whether habits hold under a clock.

### Week four: review with evidence

The tutor shares the error-log trend, and shows what has changed in the student's process: working shown, fewer repeated mistakes, better setup of word problems. They propose a plan for the next four to six weeks, adjusted to the exam series. If nothing has changed, they say so and explain what they will do differently.

### What a parent should see by the end of the month

- A short written summary: strengths, gaps and the main causes of lost marks.
- An error log with at least a few entries and visible fixes.
- A student who can say what they have been working on and why.
- At least one timed section marked against the official scheme.
- A clear plan for the next phase.

If you see none of these, raise it with the tutor first, and ask IB Gram for a different match if the answer is unconvincing.

Match the tutor's strength to the student's main problem rather than looking for one all-rounder. Four profiles cover most families, and each calls for a different emphasis. The profiles below are illustrations of common patterns, not real students.

| Panics in exams | Anxiety, timing, blanking | Graded timed practice, a start-anywhere routine, calm structure |

### A gaps profile in detail

Consider a student who scored comfortably in Grade 8 and now finds Grade 9 algebra confusing. The tutor's first job is to find where the chain broke. It is often at fractions, negative numbers or the laws of indices, topics that students were once taught and never secured. A good tutor goes back to that point, rebuilds it with short exercises and returns quickly to the current syllabus. A tutor who keeps pushing forward on the current topic while the foundation is cracked is wasting the student's time.

### An A* profile in detail

A student aiming for the top grade on Extended usually knows the content and needs to stop losing marks at the hard end. Typical issues are slow setup of multi-step problems, unreliable accuracy on the non-calculator paper, and gaps on the less-practised topics such as functions, transformations of graphs and vectors. A strong tutor here supplies difficult, unfamiliar questions, teaches routines for starting a problem you cannot see through, and uses full papers under time. ## What Questions Should You Ask a Tutor Before You Commit?

Ask questions that force specifics, because specifics are hard to fake. You are looking for concrete, checkable answers, not reassurance.

1. **Which syllabus codes and tiers have you taught recently?** Listen for 0580 Core, 0580 Extended, 0606 or 4MA1, and for the exam series.
2. **How will you find out where my child is losing marks?** The answer should include marked work and a timed section.
3. **What would the first month look like?** Look for a diagnostic, targets, an error log and a review.
4. **How do you use past papers?** Look for staging, mark schemes and error analysis.
5. **How do you handle the non-calculator paper?** Expect mention of working, exact values and mental fluency drills.
6. **How will I know it is working?** Expect a plan for updates and a review point.
7. **What happens if it is not working?** A good tutor welcomes this question.

## What Are the Warning Signs of a Poor Fit?

A poor fit shows up early, and the cost of ignoring it is weeks of low-value sessions. These are the signs to act on.

- **Promises of a grade.** No tutor can guarantee a grade.
- **No diagnostic.** Teaching starts at Chapter 1 regardless of the student's marked work.
- **Answers handed over.** The student copies solutions and cannot repeat them.
- **Volume without analysis.** Many papers, no marking or error log.
- **Confusion about the syllabus.** Mixing 0580 and 0606 content, or not knowing the tier structure.
- **Poor reliability.** Frequent cancellations or lateness.
- **Vague updates.** "He is doing well" with no specifics.
- **The child dreads sessions.** Some discomfort is normal, but persistent dread signals a style mismatch.

If you see two or more, talk to the tutor, and if nothing changes within a couple of sessions, ask IB Gram for a different match. Changing a tutor is a normal part of finding a fit and not a failure.

## How Does a Tutor Handle the Calculator and Non-Calculator Papers?

A strong tutor treats the non-calculator and calculator papers as two different skills, because they reward different habits. On Cambridge 0580, Core students sit a non-calculator paper (Paper 1) and a calculator paper (Paper 3), and Extended students sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Edexcel 4MA1 has two calculator papers per tier, so the balance differs.

### The non-calculator paper

The non-calculator paper demands fluent arithmetic and algebra. Typical losses are in fractions, percentages, standard form, surds where relevant, and algebraic manipulation done mentally. A tutor should run short daily drills on these, insist on written working and teach exact answers (fractions or surds) where the question allows. A student who relies on the calculator for everything arrives at this paper unprepared.

### The calculator paper

The calculator paper rewards efficient use of the calculator and careful reading. Typical losses are rounding too early, incorrect mode (degrees and radians), mis-keyed brackets, and not checking the answer for sense. A tutor should teach the student to use the calculator for what it does well and to keep full-precision values until the final answer. Accuracy conventions matter: check the paper's instructions on the degree of accuracy, and use them.

### Habits for both

- Write the method line even when the calculation is easy.
- Keep units and labels.
- Check that the answer is reasonable.
- Read the question twice and underline the command word.
- Leave a short check for the last minutes.

## What About Additional Mathematics 0606 and Students Heading for the IB?

If your child is taking Additional Mathematics 0606 or planning to take IB Mathematics Analysis and Approaches at Higher Level, the tutor needs to be able to carry the student across a bigger jump than 0580 alone. Additional Mathematics has no tiers, two written papers with a calculator allowed, and grades A* to E. Its content includes functions, quadratic functions, factors of polynomials, equations, inequalities and graphs, simultaneous equations, logarithmic and exponential functions, straight-line graphs, coordinate geometry of the circle, circular measure, trigonometry, permutations and combinations, series, vectors in two dimensions, differentiation and integration.

For these students, the tutor's value is in the early parts of calculus and the habit of rigorous reasoning. A student who learns to differentiate and integrate cleanly at 0606 enters IB or A Level with a head start. A tutor who has also taught IB or A Level mathematics can see the destination and teach with it in mind. Pearson Edexcel's nearest equivalent to 0606 is International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics (4PM1).

### Choosing between sequences of courses

Some students take 0580 Extended in Grade 9 and 0606 in Grade 10, others take both in parallel. The school decides, and a tutor should work with the school's plan, not against it. If your child has a choice, ask the tutor for a candid view of the workload, and see the parent guide to [IGCSE Maths 0580 and 0606](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/).

## Ajay Vatsyayan's Approach to IGCSE Maths

Ajay Vatsyayan is IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, and his approach to IGCSE maths is built on the same principles as his IB work: understand before you drill, solve problems, correct errors at their cause and use past papers in stages.

**Concept first.** The student should know what a formula represents and when it applies. Once the concept is clear, practice builds fluency, accuracy and speed. Concept-first teaching pays off when a question does not look like the one in the book.

**Problem solving as a routine.** Read the information, identify what is required, select a method, carry out the calculation, and check the answer. Repeated often enough, this becomes a habit that a student can run on an unfamiliar question.

**Guided practice that fades.** Students start with questions that reinforce the concept, then move to harder and less familiar ones, with the tutor's guidance reduced over time. The final exam is independent work, so the practice should end that way.

**Error correction by cause.** A mistake may be conceptual, a calculation slip, a misreading or a wrong method. Each needs a different response, and an error log makes the pattern visible.

**Past papers in stages.** Topic questions first, then mixed sections, then timed papers, each marked against the official scheme.

### Is there a single best IGCSE maths tutor in Gurugram?

No. The best tutor depends on your child's syllabus, tier, main weakness, learning style and schedule. A tutor who is excellent for an A* candidate on Extended may be a poor fit for a student rebuilding algebra. Judge on fit and evidence of progress, and be cautious of anyone who claims to be best for every student.

### What is the difference between 0580 Core and Extended for a tutor?

Core tops out at grade C, and Extended reaches A*. Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), and Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). A tutor needs to prepare differently for each: Core focuses on securing standard content and accuracy, and Extended adds harder topics and multi-step reasoning.

### Does my child need a tutor who knows Edexcel 4MA1 specifically?

Yes, if the school enters your child for Pearson Edexcel. International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier, and its question style differs from Cambridge. A tutor who has only taught Cambridge may handle the content well but need time to learn the Edexcel papers. Ask which past papers they use.

### When should we start with a tutor?

Start when a gap appears, not when marks fall. Grade 9 is a good time to repair foundations, since the course is cumulative and Grade 10 is crowded. If you are already in Grade 10, begin now with a diagnostic and a focused plan. The earlier months are for repair, and the final months for full timed papers.

### How long before we see results?

Expect signs in process within four weeks: working shown, an error log in use, fewer repeated slips. Test scores often move after six to eight weeks, and final grades lag further behind. A tutor who promises results in two weeks is overselling. If there is no change in process after a month, review the arrangement.

### Is home tuition better than coaching for IGCSE maths?

Neither is better in general. Home tuition suits students who need individual pacing and attention, and coaching suits students who thrive in groups and want a structured programme. Some families combine them. Consider your child's personality, level and goals, and weigh value rather than price alone. The comparison guide linked above sets out the trade-offs.

### Can online tutoring work for IGCSE maths?

Yes, if the student can write and show working clearly, with a tablet and stylus or a webcam on paper, and if the tutor uses a shared whiteboard. Online works well when the best-matched tutor is far away or when evenings are tight. Check that the tutor can see the student's handwriting, because working is where marks are lost.

### What if my child does not get on with the tutor?

Say so early and ask IB Gram for a different match. Be specific about what is not working: pace, style, communication, subject depth or logistics. A change after a trial or a month is normal. Rapport matters in maths, because a child who is afraid to ask a question will not learn from the answer.

### Should my child take Additional Mathematics 0606?

It depends on their plans and the school's guidance. 0606 is useful for students heading towards IB Mathematics AA HL or A Level Mathematics, and it is usually taken alongside 0580 Extended. It adds workload, so the school and a candid conversation with a tutor are the best sources of advice. Ask what the student is aiming for after Grade 10.
