## What Does the First Year of IGCSE Actually Involve?

The first year of IGCSE is the first half of a two-year course, usually Grade 9 and Grade 10 (Class 9 and Class 10 in Indian terms), in which students cover most of the syllabus content for each subject and build the skills the final papers test. In most schools there is no external exam in Class 9. The marks that count come from the series entered at the end of the course, and Class 9 is the time to learn the content properly while the stakes are low.

That sounds relaxing, and it is the trap. A student who treats Class 9 as a rehearsal discovers in Class 10 that half the syllabus was met once, briefly, and never consolidated. The Class 9 school exams exist to prevent that, but they are written by the school and often test recall of recent chapters, which is a weaker signal than the board's papers will be.

Three features set IGCSE apart from the CBSE or ICSE experience that many South Delhi students come from:

- **Papers reward application, not recall.** Questions often present data, a diagram or an unfamiliar situation and ask the student to use a familiar idea on it.
- **Command words decide the marks.** "State", "describe", "explain" and "evaluate" are different instructions that ask for different depths of answer.
- **Tiers and options exist.** Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers, and some have a choice between coursework and a written paper, or between a practical test and an alternative-to-practical paper.

For the exam series structure, entries and paper types across the whole course, [IGCSE exam preparation](/blog/igcse-exam-preparation-guide/) is the reference. What follows is how those facts apply in Class 9.

:::timeline The two-year IGCSE shape
Class 9 term 1 | Settle subjects, learn habits, find gaps
Class 9 term 2 | Cover content, first internal exams
Class 9 end | Decide tiers and options, summer consolidation
Class 10 | Finish content, mocks, entry series
Exact dates and entries are set by the school; ask the coordinator for the calendar.
:::

## Which Decisions Should You Make Early in Class 9?

Four decisions shape Class 10, and each is easier to make well in the first term than in the second year. The school makes the final decision on entries, so treat these as conversations to start, not choices to impose.

**1. The tier for each subject.** In Cambridge IGCSE, many subjects have Core and Extended tiers. Core tops out at grade C, and Extended reaches A*. Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered as Foundation or Higher. A student placed on the lower tier cannot reach the top grades however well they perform, and a student placed on the higher tier without secure foundations loses marks across the whole paper. The decision is usually made by the school on the basis of Class 9 performance and teacher judgement.

**2. The subject mix.** Students typically take several subjects, with a core of mathematics, English and sciences, and options such as Economics, Business Studies, Computer Science or a language. The mix matters for what comes after: the IB Diploma Programme (DP), Cambridge International AS and A Levels, or a move to CBSE or ISC in Class 11 all have different expectations.

**3. Single, coordinated or separate sciences.** Some schools offer separate Physics, Chemistry and Biology, others a combined or coordinated science course. The first is a heavier workload that prepares better for HL sciences in the IB. Ask which your school offers and what it means for what you can study later.

**4. Which exam series the school will enter.** Cambridge runs February/March (offered only in India), May/June and October/November series, and Pearson Edexcel runs January and May/June. The February/March series exists so that Grade 10 results arrive in time for Class 11 admissions and for IB DP or A Level entry in the same academic year. Schools decide which series to enter, and a student cannot choose independently of the school.

A short list of questions to take to the school coordinator in the first term:

1. Which tier will my child be placed in for mathematics and the sciences, and when is that decided?
2. What is the criterion for moving between tiers?
3. Which series will the school enter for Class 10?
4. For subjects with a coursework or practical option, which option does the school use?
5. What happens after Class 10 at this school, and what grades or subjects does it expect?

The answers turn vague worry into a calendar.

## How Do You Choose Between Core and Extended in Class 9?

You choose between Core and Extended by looking at where the student's foundation actually is, what the next stage needs, and what the school's calendar allows. Extended is the right default for a student aiming at A-level style study, the IB DP at higher levels, or a quantitative degree, and Core is a legitimate choice for a student who needs a secure, achievable course and whose plans do not require the top grades in that subject.

Think of it as a trade-off between ceiling and security. Core caps at grade C but asks for less breadth. Extended has no cap but includes content, such as more demanding algebra, functions and trigonometry in mathematics, that a student with gaps will find punishing.

A practical test in Class 9: ask your child to work through a handful of questions from the first half of Extended mathematics without help, and write down which they could not start. If most of them are blocked by a missing Grade 8 skill (fractions, algebraic manipulation, linear equations), the first step is to repair that skill, and tier is a conversation for later. If the blockers are advanced ideas that have simply not been taught yet, the student is on track for Extended.

For mathematics specifically, Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 has Core papers 1 (non-calculator) and 3 (calculator) and Extended papers 2 (non-calculator) and 4 (calculator). A student heading for IB Mathematics Analysis and Approaches at HL, or A Level Mathematics, is normally expected to take Extended, and often Additional Mathematics 0606 as well. The two syllabuses and how to support a child at home are explained in [the IGCSE Maths 0580 and 0606 parent guide](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/).

## Which Subjects Do South Delhi Students Most Often Need Help With?

The subjects that most often prompt a tutor search are mathematics, the sciences and, for students switching from another board, English. The pattern is consistent across Delhi: mathematics because the syllabus is cumulative and gaps compound, sciences because of command words, calculations and practical skills, and English because the writing tasks differ from what Indian boards trained.

**Mathematics.** Year one is where algebra, number and the early geometry are consolidated. A student who is shaky on negative numbers, fractions or manipulating equations will struggle with every later topic. The most useful tutoring in Class 9 maths is early and diagnostic: find the gap, fix it, and build accuracy habits. The ten errors that cost marks most often are set out in [top 10 mistakes in IGCSE maths](/blog/top-10-mistakes-in-igcse-maths/), and the root-cause view is in [why students struggle in IGCSE Mathematics](/blog/igcse-mathematics-tutoring-guide/).

**Sciences.** Cambridge IGCSE sciences are examined with multiple choice (30%), theory (50%) and practical assessment (20%), with the practical through a practical test or an alternative-to-practical paper that the school chooses. Class 9 is the time to learn the equations the syllabus expects students to recall and rearrange (Physics 0625 is explicit about this), to practise handling data in tables and graphs, and to build the vocabulary that the command words demand. The common difficulties are in [why students struggle in IGCSE Science](/blog/igcse-science-tutoring-guide/). Students aiming for separate sciences at the IB later benefit most from a firm start.

**English.** English at IGCSE is several syllabuses, not one: First Language English, English as a Second Language and Literature in English are different courses with different assessments. A student switching from CBSE or ICSE often needs help with directed writing, writing for audience and purpose, and in some syllabuses, speaking and listening. The skills behind this are in [IGCSE English communication skills](/blog/igcse-english-communication-skills/).

**Economics, Business Studies and Computer Science.** These are less often a tutoring need in Class 9, since many schools introduce them gently. They become a need in Class 10, when the data-response, case-study and pseudocode papers are close.

## What Habits Should a Class 9 Student Build in the First Term?

The habits that matter in the first term are small, repeatable and unglamorous. They are also the ones that separate students who coast and then scramble from students who arrive in Class 10 with a stable base.

**A single place for corrected work.** Every test, every worksheet and every homework piece that comes back marked goes into one folder or notebook with the corrections done in a different colour. The point is not neatness. It is that by Class 10, the student has a record of what went wrong.

**An error log from week one.** A short table in the back of each subject notebook: the question, what happened, the cause and the fix. Students who start this in Class 9 have a revision list ready before revision begins.

**A weekly retrieval session.** Once a week, thirty minutes, closed book: write down everything remembered about last week's topics, then check. Gaps found this way are the cheapest gaps to fix.

**Command-word reading.** Underline the command word in every question, in every subject, for the whole year. By Class 10 it is automatic.

**Showing working in maths.** Write the setup line and the substitution even when the answer is obvious. This is a habit that takes a term to build and saves marks for two years.

**A fixed study slot.** Consistency matters more than length. Forty-five focused minutes at the same time each weekday beats a weekend marathon that burns out by Monday.

**Reading beyond the textbook.** In English and humanities especially, regular reading of newspapers, articles and fiction builds the vocabulary and sense of structure that essay papers reward.

| Habit | What it protects against | Time per week |
|---|---|---|
| Corrections folder | Repeating the same errors | Part of homework |
| Error log | Not knowing which errors are yours | About 20 minutes |
| Retrieval session | Content fading before Class 10 | 30 minutes |
| Command-word underlining | Answering the wrong question | None, built into work |
| Working in maths | Losing method marks | None, built into work |

## How Does Moving From CBSE, ICSE or Another Board Change Class 9?

A student moving into IGCSE from CBSE, ICSE or a state board in Class 8 or Class 9 meets a different style of assessment before meeting any new content. The common differences are criterion-referenced or command-term marking instead of a fixed answer key, coursework and practical skills that carry real weight, wider reading and independent research, and less emphasis on memorised derivations.

The practical effects in year one:

- Students who were rewarded for writing everything they knew find that a "state" question wants one precise sentence, and an "explain" question wants a reason, not a list.
- Students who relied on memorised worked examples find that IGCSE questions present the same idea in an unfamiliar setting.
- Students used to long written answers in sciences find multiple-choice and data questions that need quick, accurate reading.

The remedy is not more content. It is practice with the format: short questions in the style of the board, marked strictly against the mark scheme. A tutor who understands both boards can help close the gaps quickly. ## When Does a Class 9 Student Actually Need a Tutor?

A Class 9 student needs a tutor when there is a specific, identifiable gap that school help is not closing, when a board switch has left foundations uneven, or when an early tier decision depends on securing a subject quickly. A student who is keeping up, enjoys the work and understands why marks are lost does not need one, and a tutor added for reassurance often adds cost without adding learning.

Signs that outside help is worth considering:

- **Marks are falling in a cumulative subject** (maths, chemistry) while the student says "I understand it in class".
- **The student can follow a worked example but cannot start a fresh question.** That is a transfer problem, and it responds well to guided practice.
- **The same kind of error appears on every test.** A pattern that the student cannot see is a good use of a tutor's eye.
- **A board switch is recent** and the first internal exams have been a shock.
- **The tier decision is close** and a short, targeted push could change which paper the student is entered for.
- **The student is quietly avoiding a subject.** Avoidance is information.

Signs that it is too early or unnecessary:

- The student is meeting expectations, and the main worry is a vague feeling that "everyone has a tutor".
- The school offers regular support sessions and the student has not used them.
- The first internal exam has not yet happened. Wait for one data point.

A tutor in Class 9 should usually begin with a diagnostic, work at one session a week, and agree a review date after three or four lessons. ## What Should an IGCSE Tutor in South Delhi Be Able to Do?

An IGCSE tutor in South Delhi should know the exact syllabus the student is studying, not an approximation of it, and should be able to show how they will find and close the student's gaps. IGCSE tutors in South Delhi are not interchangeable with general subject tutors, because the board's papers, command words and mark schemes are distinctive.

A short checklist for the first conversation:

1. **Which syllabus code and tier?** They should ask, and know the difference between Cambridge 0580 Extended and Edexcel 4MA1 Higher, or between the Cambridge and Edexcel sciences.
2. **How will you diagnose?** A short task on the student's own recent work, not a generic quiz.
3. **What does a typical session look like?** Evidence from the student's work, one priority, a fresh attempt and a plan for between sessions.
4. **How will progress be shown?** A trail of attempt, feedback, corrected attempt and later transfer question, not a verbal assurance.
5. **What will you not do?** A good tutor will not write assessed coursework, and will say so.
6. **When would you tell us to stop?** A tutor who can name the conditions under which the lessons should reduce is thinking about the student's independence.

## How Do Home and Online Lessons Compare for South Delhi Families?

For South Delhi families, home lessons suit students who work best face to face and have a quiet space, and online lessons suit students whose best tutor is not nearby or whose weekday evenings are too tight for travel. Many families use a mix.

When you compare IGCSE tutors in South Delhi, consider the practical side in neighbourhoods such as Saket, Vasant Vihar and Greater Kailash. In older residential colonies and apartment complexes, the question is usually less about distance and more about the logistics of a weekday evening: how long a tutor is realistically on the road after the working day, whether the building has a visitor entry procedure that needs arranging in advance, and whether there is a table and a quiet room at the time of the session. These are small things that decide whether a session starts on time.

Home lessons work well when:

- The student benefits from a tutor who watches the pencil, not the screen, which matters in mathematics where the working is the evidence.
- There is a quiet table and a predictable slot.
- A parent wants to be nearby, without sitting in the room.

Online lessons work well when:

- The best specialist for the subject is elsewhere in Delhi NCR or in another city.
- Weekday evenings are crowded with school, sport and activities.
- The student is comfortable with a pen tablet or shared whiteboard, so working is visible.

Sciences with a practical component need a particular note. A practical paper tests how to handle data, plan an investigation and evaluate results. Most of this can be taught on paper and on screen, and a physical lab is not required for the written practical papers. ## A Sample Weekly Rhythm for a Class 9 Student

A realistic week for a Class 9 student with one tutor session looks quieter than most parents expect. The point is that the tutor session sits inside a week of independent work, not instead of it.

| Day | What happens | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | School; homework; corrections from last test | 45 minutes |
| Tuesday | School; subject practice from the error log | 45 minutes |
| Wednesday | Tutor session (maths or science) | 60 minutes |
| Thursday | Follow-up task from the tutor session | 40 minutes |
| Friday | Light reading or a retrieval session | 30 minutes |
| Saturday | Catch-up, sport, rest; a small mixed set if needed | 45 minutes |
| Sunday | Plan the week; update the error log | 20 minutes |

Adjust to the school timetable. The shape matters: one tutor session, one follow-up, one retrieval session, one planning slot. If the student has sport or activities on weekday evenings, move sessions to a weekend morning or an online slot rather than stacking more hours onto tired days.

## What Does a Good Tutor Do in the First Month With a Class 9 Student?

A good tutor spends the first month finding out what the student actually knows, repairing the foundations that block progress and starting the habits above. They do not rush through the next chapter.

A typical first month:

:::steps A useful first month
Diagnose | Short task on the student's own work
Prioritise | Two or three gaps, written down
Repair | Foundations and accuracy habits
Review | Compare with the first diagnostic
:::

In week one, the tutor sets a diagnostic and reads the student's recent work. In week two, they teach the most important missing prerequisite and set a short follow-up. In week three they mix the repaired skill into questions that look like the board's. In week four they repeat part of the diagnostic and show the family what has changed, and what the next priority is.

The family should see by the end of the month: a short written summary of the gaps, evidence of at least one gap closing, and a clear proposal for the next four weeks, including whether the frequency should stay, reduce or increase. If none of that is visible, ask for a conversation. If it remains absent, ask for a different match.

## A Numbered Plan for the First 100 Days of Class 9

This plan is deliberately plain. It can be run by a family without a tutor, and a tutor can slot into it.

1. **Week 1:** Collect the syllabus code for each subject from the school, and the school's calendar for internal exams.
2. **Week 2:** Start a corrections folder and an error log in every subject.
3. **Weeks 3 to 4:** Ask the school for the tier policy, the series the school will enter and the practical option for sciences.
4. **Weeks 5 to 8:** Run one weekly retrieval session and note which topics fade.
5. **Weeks 9 to 10:** After the first internal exams, list every lost mark by cause, not by subject.
6. **Weeks 11 to 12:** Decide whether a tutor is needed for one subject, and brief them with the list from step 5.
7. **Weeks 13 to 14:** Review the habits: which are working, which have been dropped, and why.

## Mistakes South Delhi Families Make in Class 9

**Waiting until Class 10 to take it seriously.** The marks that count are in Class 10, but the skills that produce them are built in Class 9.

**Buying a tutor for every subject at once.** More hours do not mean more learning. Start with the subject where the gap is clearest.

**Choosing the tier by ambition alone.** An Extended entry without secure foundations loses marks across the paper. A Core entry for a capable student caps the grade. Decide on evidence.

**Treating practical papers as an afterthought.** In the sciences, the practical component is a fifth of the grade, and the skills are learnable in Class 9.

**Letting a tutor do the homework.** Students who submit tutor-written work learn nothing and may breach school rules. A tutor should teach and review, not substitute.

**Ignoring English.** Strong subject knowledge written in unclear English loses marks in every paper.

**Starting online without testing the setup.** A tablet, a stable connection and a saved whiteboard matter more than the platform.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should my child start IGCSE tuition in South Delhi?

Start when there is a clear need, not by default. The best moments are the first weeks of Class 9 if there has been a board switch, or after the first internal exam if marks are not reflecting understanding. A diagnostic first session shows whether regular lessons are needed. If your child is meeting expectations, wait and review after one more assessment.

### Is Class 9 too early for an IGCSE tutor?

Not if the aim is to build foundations. Class 9 is often the best time for tutoring, because there is no external exam pressure and gaps are cheaper to fix. The risk is over-booking: one weekly session with independent follow-up is usually enough. A tutor who proposes several sessions a week for a Class 9 student without a diagnostic is selling hours, not planning.

### How do we choose between Core and Extended?

Look at the student's foundation, the next stage and the school's policy. Extended suits students aiming at IB DP higher levels, A Levels or quantitative degrees, and requires secure Grade 8 skills. Core caps at grade C and suits students who need a secure course. The school makes the entry decision, so ask early what evidence it uses and when it decides.

### Do South Delhi students need a tutor for IGCSE English?

Many do not, but students switching from CBSE or ICSE often benefit from help with writing for audience and purpose, and with command-word reading. IGCSE English covers First Language English, English as a Second Language and Literature, each with different assessments, so confirm which one your child takes. A short diagnostic on a piece of writing shows whether a tutor is needed.

### Should lessons be at home or online in South Delhi?

Choose by the student and the subject. Home lessons suit students who work best face to face and have a quiet space. Online lessons suit families with tight weekday evenings or a specialist elsewhere, and work well when a pen tablet makes working visible. A hybrid plan, with longer in-person sessions and shorter online follow-ups, suits many students.

### Can a tutor help with the IGCSE science practical papers?

Yes, to a point. The practical papers test planning, handling data, graph work and evaluation, which can be taught on paper and on screen. A tutor cannot replace the school's lab sessions, and the school chooses between the practical test and the alternative-to-practical paper. Ask which one your child sits and practise that format.

### How many hours a week should a Class 9 student study outside school?

It depends on the school load and the student, but the useful pattern is a short daily slot and a weekly retrieval session, not long weekend blocks. Many Class 9 students manage well with about forty-five focused minutes on weekdays, plus a tutor session where needed. If the student is exhausted, reduce, do not add.

### What if my child's first IGCSE internal exam goes badly?

Treat it as data. List every lost mark by cause: gaps in content, command-word misreads, missing working or time. A pattern usually appears. Share it with the subject teacher, fix the cheapest causes first and consider a tutor if the same gap persists. One poor exam in Class 9 does not decide Class 10.

### Will tuition promise my child a particular IGCSE grade?

No. No tutor can guarantee a grade, and any promise of one should be treated with caution. Outcomes depend on the starting point, attendance, independent practice and the school's assessment.
