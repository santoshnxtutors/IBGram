An online IB maths tutor for MYP students is a good choice when the best-matched specialist does not live near you, and a poor choice when the family treats "online" as a single product. Quality varies more across online tutors than across any other format, and the differences are mostly invisible on a website: whether the tutor knows the Middle Years Programme (MYP) assessment criteria, whether the student does the thinking during the lesson, and whether the schedule survives a time-zone gap and a school calendar that does not match the tutor's.

This guide is written for parents of MYP students living anywhere: Gurgaon and other Indian cities, the Gulf, Singapore, the United Kingdom, North America or Australasia. ## What Is Different About MYP Maths, and Why It Matters Online?

MYP Mathematics is assessed against four criteria, each scored 0 to 8: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating, and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts. The criterion total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through the International Baccalaureate (IB) grade boundaries. There is no single national MYP examination in India or most other countries; schools set their own assessments, and MYP eAssessment in MYP 5 is optional, with each school deciding whether to offer it.

:::stats MYP Mathematics at a glance
4 | Criteria, A to D
0-8 | Score range per criterion
1-7 | Final MYP grade scale
:::

That structure changes what a good tutor does. A tutor who teaches general school maths very well can still miss the point of MYP, because a student can solve every equation and still lose marks on investigating a pattern, writing a justification or applying mathematics to a real situation. Online, this gap is easier to hide: a polished screen-share of worked examples looks like teaching while the student watches.

The check, then, is whether the tutor works with criteria and not just chapters. For the criteria themselves, [MYP mathematics criteria A, B, C and D explained](/blog/ib-criterion-a-b-c-d-explained-myp-mathematics-gurgaon/) and [the parent version of the criteria](/blog/ib-myp-maths-criteria-a-b-c-d-explained-for-parents/) are the places to start. For how the approach differs from CBSE maths, see [IB MYP maths vs CBSE maths teaching approach](/blog/ib-myp-maths-vs-cbse-maths-teaching-approach/).

## When Is Online the Right Choice for MYP Maths?

An online IB maths tutor for MYP students suits families when they want a specialist more than proximity, when the student is comfortable on a screen, when schedules are tight, or when the family lives where no local specialist exists. It is also the natural choice for families who move between countries during the MYP years, because the tutor can follow the student.

It suits MYP less well in three situations. A younger MYP 1 or 2 student with a short attention span may do better with someone physically beside them. A student who is easily distracted by a device may need home sessions. And a student whose difficulty is mainly organisational, with lost notebooks and unfinished homework, may need the physical presence of a person to restore routine. ## Step One: Check the Tutor Understands MYP

The first filter is not a degree or a number of years. It is whether the tutor can explain the MYP in their own words.

1. Ask: "How do you work with Criteria A, B, C and D in a normal session?"
2. Listen for specifics: investigations for B, written justification for C, real-life tasks for D.
3. Ask for an example of how they would turn a routine textbook question into a criterion-based task.
4. Ask which MYP year the tutor has most experience with, and how they handle the extended topics in MYP 4 and 5.
5. Ask how they work with the student's school, for example reading the task sheet or rubric the teacher issued.

A tutor who says only "I have taught IB students" has not answered. A tutor who describes what a student does in an investigation, and how they would coach the written explanation, has.

### A Quick Test With a Real Question

Give the tutor a simple linear equation the student solves easily, such as 2x + 7 = 19, and ask how they would extend it for MYP. A good answer moves toward a context: a taxi fare, a phone plan, a savings target. The student has to form the equation from the situation, solve it, interpret the answer and write the reasoning. A weaker answer just supplies more equations.

## Step Two: Check the Online Setup

Online maths needs more than a video call. The tools decide whether the student can show and receive mathematical thinking.

| Tool | What it should do | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Pen tablet or touch screen | Let the tutor and student write equations naturally | Typing maths is slow and discourages working |
| Shared whiteboard | Let both write on the same page | The student must contribute, not watch |
| Graphing tool | Plot and annotate graphs live | Criterion B and D tasks use graphs |
| Screen sharing | Show worksheets or school tasks | Lets the tutor see the real assignment |
| Saved work | Keep the board after the session | The student can revise and the parent can see progress |
| Stable audio | Clear voice both ways | Explanations carry reasoning |

Ask for a short test session to check these, before any package. Most failures are mundane: a student writing with a mouse, a connection that drops, a whiteboard the student cannot edit.

### The Student's Side

The student needs a quiet space, a charged device, a pen tablet or a camera angled to show handwritten work, paper and pencil for working, and headphones if the room is noisy. A parent who can look at the setup for two minutes before the first session prevents most problems. If the student has to share a device with siblings, fix that before starting.

:::steps What a well-run online MYP maths session looks like
Review | The student's last piece of work
Target | One specific difficulty
Concept | Revisit with the student writing
Apply | An unfamiliar problem, unaided
Express | The student explains and rewrites
:::

## Step Three: Handle Time Zones Honestly

Time zones are the practical constraint that decides whether an otherwise good match survives. A tutor in India working with a family in the Gulf has a small gap, a family in London a large one, and a family in North America a very large one.

The table shows indicative offsets from India Standard Time (IST, UTC+5:30). Daylight saving shifts some of these by an hour during the year, so always confirm the exact times with a calendar tool.

| Family location | Offset against IST | Practical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Dubai and the UAE | About 1.5 hours behind IST | Easy overlap on weekday evenings |
| Singapore | About 2.5 hours ahead of IST | Tutor evenings are the student's late evenings; weekends may be better |
| London and the UK | About 4.5 to 5.5 hours behind IST | Student's after-school slot is the tutor's late evening |
| US East Coast | About 9.5 to 10.5 hours behind IST | Student's morning or weekend slots fit the tutor's evening |
| Sydney and Melbourne | About 4.5 to 5.5 hours ahead of IST | Student's evening is the tutor's afternoon |

Offsets are approximate and change with daylight saving.

### Rules That Keep a Schedule Alive

- **Choose a slot you can hold for months.** A time that works for three weeks and collapses is worse than a modest, reliable one.
- **Avoid the student's most tired hour.** A late-night session after a long school day teaches little.
- **Use weekends deliberately.** For large gaps, one longer weekend block plus a short mid-week check can beat two late weekday sessions.
- **Set a cancellation and rescheduling rule in writing.** Time zones make last-minute changes more costly for both sides.
- **Name the time zone in every message.** "6 pm" is ambiguous; "6 pm Dubai time" is not.

For long gaps, a plan with one weekly live session and a short written feedback exchange, in which the student uploads a photo of the week's work and the tutor replies with comments, can add value without more live hours.

## Step Four: Match the Tutor's Calendar to the School's

MYP schools do not share one calendar. A tutor who plans a unit of work around the wrong term will teach the wrong thing at the wrong time.

Many Indian schools run roughly April to March. Schools in the northern hemisphere, including most in the UK, Europe, the Gulf and North America, typically start in August or September and run to June or July. Schools in the southern hemisphere, such as Australia and New Zealand, usually start in late January or February and finish in December. Some international schools in Asia follow a northern-hemisphere pattern, others vary, so confirm with the school.

:::timeline Why school calendars matter for an online tutor (typical patterns)
April to March | Many schools in India
August or September to June | Many schools in the UK, Europe, North America and the Gulf
Late January to December | Schools in Australia and New Zealand
Exact dates | Always confirm with your child's school
:::

### What This Changes in Practice

A family in Australia may want a tutor to prepare a student for an end-of-year assessment in November while the tutor's Indian students are mid-year. A family in the UK may need support before a December assessment when the tutor's other students are in a quieter phase. The tutor should plan around the student's own school calendar, ask for the school's task dates, and adjust. Ask the tutor how they handle students whose terms differ from the majority. A good tutor keeps a calendar per student.

If the student moves between schools or countries mid-year, an online tutor offers continuity that a local tutor cannot. Share the new school's calendar as soon as it is known.

## Step Five: Verify the Tutor Before You Pay

Verifying an online IB maths tutor for MYP students is the step families skip because a website feels reassuring. Do not skip it, and do not rely on a single signal.

1. **Ask for a short call before anything else.** Ten minutes of conversation reveals whether the tutor explains clearly and listens.
2. **Check the MYP knowledge** using the questions in Step One.
3. **Ask for a sample of how feedback is given**, such as an anonymised example of a comment on a student's work.
4. **Ask about their own working setup**: tablet, whiteboard tool, backup connection.
5. **Check references in a way that respects privacy.** A tutor can describe the type of student they help; do not ask for another family's details.
6. **Arrange a trial lesson** with the student present, and judge it using the checklist below.
7. **Agree terms in writing**: session length, frequency, cancellation, how progress is reviewed.

Verification includes honesty about limits. A tutor who promises a particular level is promising something outside their control. A tutor who says "I can diagnose this in two sessions and tell you what I think is realistic" is more credible.

## How to Read a Trial Lesson

For an online IB maths tutor for MYP students, a trial lesson is the best evidence a parent will get. Watch these things.

- **Who is doing the mathematics?** If the tutor does most of the work while the student watches, the lesson is a lecture.
- **Does the tutor ask questions?** Real questions, not "do you understand?"
- **Does the tutor find a misconception?** A good tutor identifies a specific error and works on it.
- **Is there an unfamiliar problem?** MYP rewards application, so a lesson with only routine questions is thin.
- **Does the student explain their reasoning?** Criterion C depends on it.
- **Is there a clear next step?** A task for the week, with a purpose.

Afterwards, ask your child three questions: what became clearer, what did you have to think through yourself, and what are you supposed to do next? Their answers tell you more than whether the tutor was friendly.

### Red Flags in a Trial

- The tutor speaks for most of the session.
- Every question is a routine one from the textbook.
- The tutor cannot say which criterion a task addresses.
- The whiteboard is used only by the tutor.
- The tutor offers to do the student's school assessment task or write their investigation.
- A package is pushed before the trial ends.

The last two matter most. MYP assessed work must be the student's own, and a tutor who offers to write it is putting the student at risk of a malpractice finding.

## Ajay Vatsyayan's Approach: Concept, Application, Expression

Ajay Vatsyayan's approach to MYP maths is described through three stages: Concept, Application, Expression. Concept means understanding the mathematics, not just the procedure. Application means using it in unfamiliar and real-world situations. Expression means communicating the reasoning clearly in writing and in speech.

The framework suits online teaching because it prevents a lesson from turning into a digital homework session. An online lesson might begin with a conceptual review, move to a contextual problem the student has not seen, have the student build and solve it on a shared board, and end with the student explaining their reasoning and improving the written presentation. The student is using mathematics, not receiving it.

:::figure signature-ajay:::

Three features matter for a parent evaluating any tutor against this model. The tutor asks "why" regularly: why this method, why this value is reasonable, why a pattern continues. The tutor diagnoses before teaching, so the session targets a real gap. And the tutor never promises a level. Online tutoring can support improvement when the weakness is correctly identified, but the outcome depends on engagement, consistency, school assessment and starting point.

## What About MYP 5 and the eAssessment?

MYP 5 students may sit MYP eAssessment, which combines on-screen examinations and ePortfolios. It is optional, and each school decides whether to offer it. If the school does, an online tutor can be well placed to prepare a student, because practising on screen is the format of the exam itself. A tutor should be familiar with the requirements and ask the student to practise on a screen, with the kind of on-screen tools the school expects. See [MYP 5 mathematics eAssessment preparation](/blog/ib-myp-5-mathematics-eassessment-preparation-pathways-heritage-gurgaon/) for what that preparation involves.

If the school does not offer eAssessment, the tutor's work in MYP 5 centres on the school's own assessments and on preparing the student for the Diploma Programme (DP), where maths becomes AA or AI at Standard or Higher Level. Schools often split MYP 4 and 5 maths into standard and extended; extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches, so the tutor should know which track the student is on.

## Online Tutor, Coaching Centre or Group Class?

Families abroad often ask whether to use an online group class or an online private tutor. A group class is cheaper per hour and gives some peer interaction, but a single tutor cannot adapt to each student's criteria gaps. A private tutor costs more per hour and gives diagnosis and feedback. For a quick, honest comparison, [IB maths coaching vs private tutor for MYP students](/blog/ib-maths-coaching-vs-private-tutor-myp-student/) lays out who each suits.

As a rule, a student who is broadly on track and needs extra practice may do well in a group. A student with a specific pattern of lost marks, or who is working toward MYP 5 and DP readiness, usually benefits from one-to-one attention.

## Budgeting Without Surprises

Fees for an online IB maths tutor for MYP students vary widely and there is no universal rate. Experience, MYP expertise, session length, preparation and feedback all move the price, and a specialist working online can still charge a premium, since travel is only one part of a tutor's cost. Prices cannot be compared meaningfully until sessions, length and what is included are the same.

## Common Mistakes Families Make When Choosing Online

**Choosing on the website.** A polished site says nothing about how the tutor teaches.

**Skipping the trial.** One lesson with the student present is worth more than any testimonial.

**Ignoring time zones until week three.** The schedule that seemed fine becomes a nightly strain.

**Hiring a general maths tutor.** MYP criteria are not optional extras; a tutor who does not understand them teaches the wrong thing well.

**Accepting a lecture format.** If the student is passive, learning is thin whatever the tutor knows.

**Letting the tutor do the assessed work.** It puts the student at risk, and it removes the learning.

**Overbooking.** A student with six commitments and four tutors does not improve; they tire.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can online tutoring work for MYP maths?

Yes, when the tutor knows the MYP criteria and the student does the thinking during the lesson. A pen tablet, a shared whiteboard and saved work make the format effective. It works less well for very young students with short attention spans or students who are easily distracted by a device.

### How do I know the tutor really understands MYP?

Ask how they work with Criteria A, B, C and D in a normal session and listen for specifics: investigations, written justification, real-life tasks. A tutor who says only that they have taught IB students has not answered. A short trial lesson shows whether the explanation matches the practice.

### What tools does an online maths lesson need?

A pen tablet or touch screen, a shared whiteboard, a graphing tool, screen sharing, stable audio and a way to save the board afterwards. Typing maths is slow and discourages working. Test the setup in a short trial session so connection and writing problems appear before any package begins.

### How do I handle a large time-zone difference?

Pick a slot you can hold for months, avoid the student's most tired hour and consider one longer weekend block plus a short written feedback exchange. State the time zone in every message, and agree in writing how rescheduling works. Offsets change with daylight saving, so check dates when clocks change.

### What if my child's school year runs on a different calendar from the tutor's?

Share the school's term dates and task deadlines at the start, and ask the tutor how they plan around them. Schools in India, the northern hemisphere and the southern hemisphere start at different times. A good tutor keeps a calendar for each student and plans units around the student's school, not their own.

### Is online cheaper than home tuition?

Not automatically. Online removes travel, but a specialist's preparation, feedback and expertise cost the same whether the lesson is online or in person, so a specialist may command a premium. Compare what the fee includes, such as written feedback and task review, and cost per useful hour, not just the headline rate.

### Is a group class better than one-to-one for MYP maths?

A group class is cheaper and offers peer interaction, but a single teacher cannot adapt to each student's criteria gaps. A student who is broadly on track may do well in a group; a student with specific lost marks or preparing for the DP usually benefits from one-to-one attention.

### Can a tutor help with MYP eAssessment?

Where the school offers MYP eAssessment in MYP 5, an online tutor can prepare the student by practising on screen. The assessment is optional and each school decides whether to offer it, so ask the school first. If the school does not offer it, tutoring focuses on school assessments and DP readiness.

### What if my child is not improving after a month?

Ask the tutor what the diagnosis was and which patterns of lost marks have shrunk. A month is long enough to see process improvements, such as clearer written reasoning, even if grades lag. If the answers are vague, ask for a different match. IB Gram lets families request one.

### Can the tutor do my child's investigation or assessed task?

No. MYP assessed work must be the student's own, and a tutor who offers to write it puts the student at risk. A tutor can teach the underlying skills, ask questions that sharpen thinking and give feedback on understanding. Treat any offer to do the work as a reason to look elsewhere.
