## What does a good MYP maths tutor need to understand?

A good MYP maths tutor needs to understand how the programme assesses mathematics, not only the mathematics itself. MYP Mathematics is assessed against four criteria, A to D, each scored 0 to 8. The criterion total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through IB grade boundaries. The criteria are A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts.

:::stats MYP Mathematics at a glance
4 | Criteria, A to D
0-8 | Score range per criterion
32 | Maximum criterion total
1-7 | Final MYP grade scale
:::

This matters for tutor choice because three of the four criteria reward things a standard textbook course barely touches: investigating a pattern and generalising it, communicating reasoning in correct notation, and applying mathematics to an unfamiliar real-life situation. A tutor who is excellent at getting a student through a worksheet may leave criteria B, C and D untouched, and the student's final grade will show it.

Schools often split MYP 4 and 5 mathematics into standard and extended; the extended course covers extra topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA). Some MYP schools offer the optional MYP eAssessment in MYP 5, with on-screen examinations; each school decides whether to offer it. There is no single national MYP examination in India. A tutor must know which of these applies to your child, because what to practise differs.

For a full explanation of each criterion, with what top-band work looks like, see [MYP mathematics criteria A, B, C and D](/blog/ib-criterion-a-b-c-d-explained-myp-mathematics-gurgaon/), and for how to read a report card, [MYP maths criteria explained for parents](/blog/ib-myp-maths-criteria-a-b-c-d-explained-for-parents/).

## What are the four kinds of tutor available?

The MYP maths tutor options in Gurgaon are four: a school teacher who tutors privately, a coaching centre or batch, a specialist one-to-one MYP tutor, and an online platform or marketplace. They differ in who teaches, how personal the pace is, how well they know the MYP criteria and how reliably they show up.

| Option | Who teaches | Pace | Typical strength | Typical weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher tutoring privately | A current or former classroom teacher | Often follows the school's sequence | Knows the school's assessment style | May be limited by their own school's habits; availability varies |
| Coaching centre | Rotating faculty in a batch | Set by the batch | Structure, peers, a fixed timetable | Little room for one student's gaps; criteria B to D often thin |
| Specialist MYP tutor, one-to-one | A tutor who works across MYP and DP maths | Set by the student's gaps | Diagnostic teaching, criterion focus | Quality varies by tutor; needs checking |
| Online platform | A tutor matched or listed on the platform | Varies | Wide choice; flexible timing | Variable vetting; the shared-writing set-up matters |

Among MYP maths tutor options in Gurgaon, each of these can be excellent or poor. The category tells you about the structure, not the quality. A coaching centre with a strong MYP team can beat a weak private tutor; a specialist who has no MYP experience can be worse than a classroom teacher. The scorecard later in this guide exists so you judge the person, not the label.

## What should you expect from a teacher who tutors privately?

Expect a person who knows the school's expectations and assessment tasks, but check that they can teach beyond them. A classroom teacher knows how tasks are set and how a particular school's department marks. That is valuable. The risks are that they may teach to their own school's pattern, that their time is limited by their school workload, and that a teacher privately tutoring students from the same school may face a conflict that the school will have views on. Ask what the school's policy is on private tutoring by staff before you proceed.

A good private teacher-tutor will still diagnose before teaching. If a teacher starts with the next chapter and does not look at the child's last criterion scores, you are buying extended class time and not tutoring.

## What does a coaching centre offer for MYP maths?

A coaching centre offers structure, a timetable, and a peer group. For a student who benefits from routine and from working alongside others, these are real advantages. A centre can also keep a child on a steady schedule, which some families find hard to do at home.

The weaknesses are structural. A batch moves at the pace of the batch, so one student's gaps in algebra or in written reasoning get little attention. MYP criteria B (investigating patterns) and D (real-life application) are hard to teach to a group in the same way as a worksheet topic. If a centre's teaching is chiefly "worked example then similar questions", the student is getting more of what school already provides. ## What does a specialist MYP maths tutor do differently?

A specialist MYP tutor diagnoses the student first, works against the criteria and sets the pace by the student's gaps. The best of them can show you what they found in the first sessions, how they will address it and how they will know it worked.

What to look for:

- **A criterion-by-criterion view.** The tutor can say which of A to D is holding the student back and why, using recent school work.
- **Reasoning over repetition.** Sessions include investigating a pattern, justifying a generalisation and explaining a method, not only practising a type of question.
- **Real-life tasks.** The student meets modelling problems of the Criterion D kind and learns a routine for starting unfamiliar ones.
- **A withdrawal of support.** Prompts reduce over weeks so the student becomes independent.
- **Continuity into DP.** For MYP 4 and 5 students, the tutor knows what DP AA or AI will ask, so the work builds towards it.

For a parent, the practical test is: can the tutor describe the next four weeks in specific terms, and will they show you evidence after them? A tutor who can do this is working from a plan. ## What about online platforms for MYP maths?

An online platform widens your choice and can solve a scheduling problem, but the quality of the tutor and the lesson set-up matter more than the platform's name. Online works well when the tutor and student share a writing surface and the student writes the working. It works badly when the tutor talks over slides and the student watches.

Check three things. First, how the platform vets tutors: ask what is verified and how. Second, whether you can have a trial session and ask for a different match. Third, whether the tutor knows the MYP criteria, not just the DP syllabus. Many tutors on large platforms teach DP and IGCSE mathematics; fewer have worked on the MYP, where the criteria differ.

## How do you score MYP maths tutor options in Gurgaon?

To compare MYP maths tutor options in Gurgaon fairly, score each candidate against the same list of criteria, weight the criteria by what your child needs, and compare totals. The aim is not to produce a mathematically perfect ranking; it is to make the trade-offs visible so that a charming but thin candidate does not win by default.

Use a scale from 0 to 5 for each row, and a weight from 1 to 3 for how much it matters to your child.

| Criterion | What to look for | Weight (1-3) | Score (0-5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| MYP criteria knowledge | Can explain A, B, C, D and how a final grade is formed | | |
| Diagnostic method | Starts with fresh questions and recent work | | |
| Reasoning and investigation | Teaches patterns, generalising, justification | | |
| Real-life application | Handles Criterion D-style tasks | | |
| Communication | Insists on notation, working and explanation | | |
| Independence | Withdraws prompts over time | | |
| Continuity | Knows what DP AA/AI will ask | | |
| Logistics | Reliable schedule, travel or online set-up | | |
| Honesty | No guaranteed grades; clear on limits | | |
| Review | Fixed progress point, willingness to change | | |

Multiply weight by score and sum. The weights are the interesting part. A student in MYP 2 might weight logistics and communication higher; an MYP 5 student with an eye on DP might weight continuity and Criterion D. Fill in the weights before you meet any tutor, so the conversation does not change the grid.

### A worked example

Consider a parent comparing three candidates for a student in MYP 4 who loses most marks on Criterion B. She weights "Reasoning and investigation" and "Diagnostic method" at 3, "MYP criteria knowledge" at 3, "Logistics" at 2 and the rest at 1.

- **A private teacher-tutor** scores high on criteria knowledge and logistics (close to the school, available on weekday evenings) but moderate on reasoning, since sessions follow the school's worksheets.
- **A coaching centre** scores high on logistics and structure, low on diagnostics and on investigation.
- **A specialist one-to-one tutor**, met for a trial, starts with an unfamiliar pattern question, asks the student to explain her thinking and writes a short summary of two priorities. She scores high on diagnostics and reasoning, a little lower on logistics because of travel, which could be solved by moving two sessions online.

The weighted totals favour the specialist, but the point is not the number. It is that the parent can say why: the child's loss is in Criterion B, and only one candidate worked on it in the trial. This scenario is illustrative, not drawn from a real family.

## What should a trial lesson show?

A trial lesson should show how the tutor thinks with your child, not how well they present. A good one has a small piece of real teaching followed by a piece of real work from the student, then a plan.

Watch for these signs:

1. **The tutor starts with a question,** not a lecture. They find out what the student does with an unfamiliar problem.
2. **A mistake is met with curiosity.** The tutor asks why the student chose a step, instead of supplying the correct one at once.
3. **The student writes most of the lines.** If the tutor writes, the student is watching.
4. **The task touches reasoning,** for example "describe the pattern, then explain why it must continue", not just calculation.
5. **There is a closing summary** the student can say back: what became clearer, and what to do before the next session.

After the trial, ask your child three questions. What became clearer? What did you have to work out yourself? What are you doing before next time? The answers tell you more than the tutor's confidence. A demo or trial can be arranged through IB Gram before you commit.

## Does a tutor from my child's school help?

Sometimes, but it is not a requirement. A tutor who knows the school's task style and expectations can help a student prepare for assessed tasks in the way the school sets them. The same tutor may be less useful if the student's difficulty is outside that pattern, for example weak algebra from earlier years. If the student is likely to stay in the same school into DP, a tutor who can follow the student across the MYP-to-DP transition adds continuity; the question of choosing one tutor who can carry a student from MYP 4 into DP is covered in [how to choose an IB maths tutor for a Pathways student](/blog/how-to-choose-ib-maths-tutor-pathways-student-gurgaon/).

## What are the red flags?

The main red flags are promised grades, no diagnostic, and a style of teaching that only repeats school. Others to watch for:

- **A guaranteed result.** No tutor can control a student's grade. Outcomes depend on the starting level, attendance, practice and school assessment.
- **Only routine questions.** The student never meets an unfamiliar problem or a real-life task.
- **A calculator-first or answer-first habit,** where the working and reasoning are an afterthought.
- **Memorised mark-scheme solutions** in place of method.
- **No review point.** If nobody can say when progress will be assessed, nobody is measuring it.
- **A tutor who does the work.** For any assessed task, a tutor may teach and question but must not complete the student's work.
- **Dismissing the MYP criteria,** or treating MYP maths as a lighter version of DP.

A few of these in a good tutor can be fixed by a conversation. A cluster of them is a reason to look again.

## When should you switch tutor?

Switch when the evidence, not the mood, says the arrangement is not working. After four to six weeks, repeat a short task from the first diagnostic. Check three things: whether the student starts problems more independently, whether the same errors repeat less, and whether the student can explain a method in their own words. If there is no movement on any of the three, ask the tutor what will change. If the answer is vague, ask for a different match.

Do not switch on the strength of one bad week. Students have variable weeks, and a switch resets the diagnostic. Equally, do not stay for a term out of politeness. A fair rule is: one honest conversation, one adjusted plan, one more review.

Week 1 | Diagnostic and a named priority
Weeks 2-3 | First repair, one independent task a week
Weeks 4-5 | Mixed tasks; criterion-style practice
Week 6 | Repeat the diagnostic; continue, adjust or switch
:::

## Do MYP students even need a tutor?

Not always. If a student understands lessons, completes tasks, receives feedback they can use and meets criteria steadily, a tutor adds little. Tutoring is worth considering when a criterion consistently lags, when the student has gaps from earlier years that the class cannot repair, when confidence has dropped, or when an MYP 4 or 5 student wants a stronger base before DP.

Two things to try first. Ask the teacher which criterion holds the student's grade back and what a next-level piece of work would look like. And look at the student's recent assessed work with them: where were marks lost, and in which criterion? ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who is the best MYP maths tutor in Gurgaon?

There is no single best tutor, only the best fit for a particular student. The right choice depends on which criterion is holding the grade back, the child's year, the preferred mode and the schedule. Use a scorecard, run a trial with each candidate on the same task, and judge by how the tutor diagnoses and teaches, not by claims or school names.

### Is a school teacher a good choice for private MYP maths tuition?

It can be, particularly for knowledge of how tasks are set and marked. The limits are availability, a tendency to follow the school's sequence and possible school policies on private tutoring by staff. Check the policy, and test whether the teacher diagnoses and teaches beyond the class pattern. A teacher-tutor who only repeats the next chapter adds little.

### Are coaching centres good for MYP maths?

They suit students who need structure and gain from a peer group. They are weaker for individual gaps, and criteria B and D (investigating patterns, real-life application) are hard to teach in a batch. If you try one, ask how they teach investigation and application, and watch whether your child is working or only listening.

### How do I check whether a tutor knows the MYP?

Ask them to explain the four criteria and how a criterion total becomes a final grade, then give a Criterion B-style task and see how they teach it. A tutor who knows only the DP syllabus or textbook topics will struggle on criteria B to D. Ask for an example of how they would help with a real-life modelling task.

### How many sessions a week does an MYP maths student need?

Start with one focused session a week and a short independent task between sessions. Two a week can help during a short intervention or before an assessment. More hours without follow-up rarely solve the problem. Review after three or four weeks using the student's work, and adjust the frequency to the evidence.

### Can a tutor help with MYP assessed tasks?

A tutor can teach the skills, question the student's reasoning and help the student understand the criteria. A tutor must not complete or rewrite the assessed work, which has to be the student's own. If a tutor offers to "finish" a task, treat it as a red flag. Follow the school's instructions on what help is allowed.

### Is online tutoring good enough for MYP maths?

Yes, if the tutor and student share a writing surface and the student writes the working. Online suits students who need a specialist who is not nearby and families with tight schedules. It does less well when the tutor lectures over slides. Test the set-up first, and ask for a trial. Some students do best with a hybrid.

### What does a fair trial lesson look like?

A short piece of teaching, then real work from the student, then a plan. The tutor should ask questions, respond to mistakes with curiosity, and leave the student writing most of the working. Afterwards, ask your child what became clearer, what they worked out themselves and what they will do before the next session.

### Will a tutor promise a better MYP grade?

A responsible tutor will not. No tutor can guarantee a grade, because outcomes depend on the student's starting level, attendance, practice and the school's assessment. A good tutor defines a process, shows evidence of progress and changes the plan when evidence demands it. Be cautious about promises of a specific result.
