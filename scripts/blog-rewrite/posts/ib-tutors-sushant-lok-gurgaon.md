## What Makes Tutoring in Sushant Lok Different From Other Parts of Gurgaon?

Tutoring in Sushant Lok is different mainly because of the housing mix and the household structure it tends to produce. Sushant Lok is broadly known for a large share of independent builder floors and houses, alongside group housing in parts of the three phases. That mix means some families have a street-level door and a tutor who simply walks in, while others have a gate, a visitor registration and a lift.

None of this is a barrier to home tuition. It is a set of small logistics that decide whether the arrangement runs smoothly in week six, when everyone is tired. Three differences are worth thinking about before the first lesson.

**Entry and timing.** A tutor arriving at an independent floor needs the door answered and a place to put a bag. A tutor arriving at a society may need to be registered with the gate in advance, and the guard needs to know the expected time. Families who sort this out before lesson one avoid the awkward first ten minutes at the gate.

**Shared space.** Independent floors often mean multi-generational households, a home office on the same floor, or a sibling sharing a room. Societies can mean flats with fewer rooms but more consistent noise control. Neither is better. What matters is whether there is a stable, quiet place for ninety minutes.

**Household rhythm.** Many Sushant Lok families have both parents working and more than one school-age child. A tutoring slot that fits one child's schedule can collide with another's activity. That is where planning pays off, and it is the main subject of this guide.

## Independent Floor or Society: Which Setup Suits Home IB Tuition Better?

Neither setup is better, but each suits a different kind of lesson. An independent floor tends to offer more flexibility in room choice and scheduling; a society flat tends to offer more predictability in noise and access once the visitor process is arranged. Choose by looking at the practical points in the table below.

| Practical point | Independent floor | Society flat |
|---|---|---|
| Tutor arrival | Door or intercom; usually quick | Gate registration, sometimes advance approval |
| Lesson space | Often a spare room or study | Often a bedroom or dining table |
| Noise | Street, neighbours, construction nearby | Corridor, lift, common-area events |
| Scheduling flexibility | Easy to shift on short notice | May need advance notice to the gate |
| Sibling overlap | Possible if separate rooms exist | Harder in small flats; stagger sessions |
| Backup for power or internet | Depends on the building | Often shared backup, check Wi-Fi |

### What to arrange before the first lesson

A short list saves real friction.

1. Tell the tutor which gate or entrance to use and what to say at the guard post.
2. Decide the lesson room and confirm a table with enough surface for papers and a laptop or tablet.
3. Check that the room has a door that closes and lighting that does not depend on one lamp.
4. Test the Wi-Fi in that room, even for a home lesson, because past papers and the graphic display calculator (GDC) emulator are often on screen.
5. Agree who will be home. A parent present in the next room is helpful; a parent hovering at the table is not.
6. Fix the start time and the grace period, so the first lesson does not set the habit of drifting.

Societies sometimes restrict visitors at certain hours, and rules vary by building and by management, so ask your resident welfare association or society office. That is a local fact only your building can confirm.

### A scenario worth recognising

Consider a typical DP1 student in an independent floor. The only quiet room is a spare bedroom that doubles as the guest room, and the lesson time overlaps with a grandparent's evening television. Nobody is at fault; the household is simply full. The fix is not a better tutor but a better time: shifting the session an hour earlier or moving the lesson to a weekend morning. It is a small adjustment that determines whether the next six months feel organised or tense.

## How Should You Set Up a Study Space for IB Home Tuition?

Whichever IB tutors in Sushant Lok you end up with, the lesson is only as good as the corner it happens in. A good IB study space is one stable place with a table, light, a closed door and everything the lesson needs within reach. It does not need to be a dedicated study; it needs to be the same place every time, because a student concentrates faster in a place that already means work.

The checklist differs a little by subject and by programme, but the core is the same.

- **A table, not a bed.** A desk or dining table with a chair that lets the student sit upright for ninety minutes.
- **Light on the page.** A desk lamp on the writing side avoids shadows; natural light helps but fades in the evening.
- **A flat surface for papers.** IB past papers are large, and a student working a Paper 3 or a data-based question needs room to spread them.
- **A pen tablet or whiteboard for maths and sciences.** Even in a home lesson, a small whiteboard lets the tutor and student work side by side.
- **A charged laptop or tablet.** For past-paper PDFs, simulations and GDC software.
- **A place for the phone, away from the table.** One of the strongest predictors of a good session is the phone being elsewhere.

### The noise question, honestly

Noise is the most common complaint about home tuition in busy areas. Construction, a neighbour's generator, a pressure cooker at six in the evening: these are real, and a tutor who says they do not matter is not being honest. Plan around them. If the house is noisy at a predictable hour, move the lesson. If it is noisy unpredictably, consider a hybrid arrangement where some sessions are online with headphones, which can cut a good deal of background sound.

## How Do You Plan Tutoring When There Are Two or More Children?

You plan tutoring for siblings by separating three questions: who needs what, who can share a tutor and who must not share a time slot. The answers are different for every household, and the common mistake is to treat the children's needs as one block.

Sibling combinations in Sushant Lok households often look like an MYP student and a DP student, two MYP students a year or two apart, or an IGCSE student and a DP student. Each case has its own logic.

| Household | Typical need | What usually works |
|---|---|---|
| MYP student plus a DP student | Different programmes, different tutors | Separate tutors, back-to-back slots, one room used in sequence |
| Two MYP students | Possibly the same subject at different levels | One tutor visiting twice in an evening, separate sessions |
| IGCSE student plus a DP student | Different boards, different calendars | Separate subject tutors; align with the older child's mocks |
| Twins in the same programme | Same subjects, different strengths | Separate diagnostics; shared slot only if levels match |

### Why sharing a session is rarely a good idea

Two siblings in the same session sounds efficient and rarely is. Tutoring works because the tutor responds to one student's errors in real time. A second student in the room halves the attention, and a younger child often becomes either a spectator or a distraction. There are exceptions: two students at the same level working on the same unit can benefit from a joint session for a short revision block. As a default, plan separate sessions.

### Scheduling around the older child's year

When a family has a DP2 student, the household calendar quietly bends around that child's mocks and May exams. Exam sessions run in May and November; schools in India overwhelmingly enter candidates for the May session, and each school sets its own DP2 mock dates, usually several months before May. Younger siblings' tutoring should be planned around those weeks, not scheduled over them. If a younger child's MYP unit test falls in the same fortnight, shift that child's session instead of the DP2 student's.

## How Do You Plan Several DP Subjects in One Household?

You plan several DP subjects by ranking them by risk, not by weight, and putting tutoring where the gap between current performance and the target is largest. A student with six subjects does not need six tutors. Most need one or two, chosen carefully, plus a plan for the rest.

The International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme is a two-year course (DP1 and DP2) of six subjects from six groups, three or four at Higher Level (HL, 240 recommended teaching hours) and the rest at Standard Level (SL, 150 hours). Each subject is graded 1 to 7. Theory of Knowledge (TOK) and the Extended Essay (EE) combine for up to 3 more points, so the maximum is 45; 24 points is the minimum for the diploma, subject to the IB's failing conditions. Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) must also be completed.

Because grades are subject-based, the useful question for a household is: which of the six subjects carries the most risk, and does any of it connect to a university offer? A student aiming at engineering has Mathematics and Physics at HL as the pressure points. A student aiming at medicine has Chemistry and Biology. Everything else can usually be managed with school support and self-study.

### A simple risk ranking

Rank each subject on three things: how far the current mark is from the target, how much the subject matters to the university route and how much of the problem is content versus technique. Where a subject is far from the target, matters for the offer and the cause is specific and fixable, tutoring pays. Where a subject is near the target, a tutor adds cost without much change.

| Subject situation | Tutoring priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| HL subject named in an offer, marks two grades below target | High | Direct impact on the outcome |
| HL subject, marks one grade below, cause unclear | Medium | Start with a diagnostic |
| SL subject on target | Low | Self-study and school support |
| Core component (EE or IA) at risk | Medium | A skills tutor, within integrity rules |
| Subject needing only exam technique | Short block | A few focused sessions before mocks |

The detail on how subject choice, predicted grades and offers connect is in [IB university preparation: subject choices, predicted grades and timelines](/blog/ib-university-preparation-guide/), which is the planning companion to this page.

### Can one tutor cover several subjects?

Sometimes. A tutor strong in Mathematics may also cover Physics, and one tutor can sometimes cover two sciences. But a single tutor covering HL in three subjects is unlikely to be strong in all of them. If a family is offered that, ask how the tutor knows each subject at that level and what evidence there is. ## What About MYP Students in Sushant Lok?

MYP students in Sushant Lok are best supported by short, regular home sessions focused on one or two subjects, usually mathematics and a science or language, with the aim of understanding the criteria as well as the content. The Middle Years Programme (MYP) is a five-year programme (MYP 1 to 5, roughly ages 11 to 16) across eight subject groups.

Every MYP subject is assessed against four criteria (A to D), each scored 0 to 8; the criterion total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through IB grade boundaries. The Personal Project comes in MYP 5 and the Community Project in MYP 3 or 4. MYP eAssessment in MYP 5, on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios, is optional, and each school decides whether to offer it. There is no single national MYP examination in India.

For MYP mathematics, the criteria are A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts. Schools often split MYP 4 and 5 mathematics into standard and extended mathematics; extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches.

### Why MYP students suit short home sessions

A younger student often concentrates well for 60 minutes and less well for 90. A shorter, regular session at home, with a parent nearby and no travel, tends to work better than a long evening class. The aim is steady criterion practice: investigations, communication of reasoning, and real-life application, which is where many MYP marks are lost.

## When Is Online Better Than Home, Even in the Same Neighbourhood?

Online tutoring is better than home when the best-fit tutor is not nearby, when travel time would eat the slot, when the house is too noisy at the only available time, or when the student is in a mock-heavy period and needs flexibility. Living in Sushant Lok does not mean every lesson should be at home.

Online works particularly well for mathematics and the sciences, where a shared whiteboard and a pen tablet replicate the desk well. It also removes the guest-registration step in societies, which suits families who prefer not to set up gate access for a tutor who visits twice a week. The cost is some of the physical presence: a student who drifts online is harder to catch, and a tutor cannot glance at a page of working unless a camera is set up to show it.

| Situation | Better choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Needs a specific specialist who lives elsewhere | Online | Fit matters more than distance |
| Younger MYP student who needs structure | Home | Presence and routine help |
| Mock season with tight evenings | Online or hybrid | Saves travel and allows short sessions |
| Noisy house at the only free time | Online with headphones | Controls background sound |
| Family travelling or a student abroad for a term | Online | Continuity |
| Practical or lab-linked support | Home or hybrid | Physical materials help |

For families who want both, a hybrid year is common: home lessons in the regular term and online sessions around exams or travel. The mechanics of switching are covered in the hybrid guide linked above, and [online IB tutoring in India](/blog/online-ib-tutoring-india/) explains how online sessions are run well. Many Sushant Lok households end up with exactly this mix, and it is a perfectly sensible outcome.

## How Does the Weekday Calendar Work for Sushant Lok Families?

The weekday calendar works best when it is built around the child's real day: school hours, activities and the time they actually have energy. Evening slots are popular and therefore tight, and Gurgaon's weekday evening traffic can stretch travel for tutors visiting several homes, which is one reason a tutor may offer fewer evening slots than a family expects.

A practical way to think about the week is to count energy, not hours. A DP student who has finished school, a sport and a snack by 5:30 may do their best work at 6:30; the same student at 9:30 may not. Pick the slot where the student is alert and the household is calm, and let the tutor's availability adjust around it. When a family is flexible about days, the number of good tutor matches rises.

Monday | School, then a 90-minute HL subject session

Saturday | Timed paper practice, then review with the tutor
Sunday | Error log review and light revision, no tutor needed
:::

This is an illustration of a rhythm, not a required plan. The principle is that tutoring supports the week, and independent work does the rest. ## What Should You Look for in a Tutor Who Will Work in Your Home?

You should look for subject depth at the right level, clear communication with parents, and a professional approach to the household: punctual, respectful of the space and able to explain what the lesson is for. Verified profiles are the baseline; the trial lesson is where fit shows.

A good first lesson, whether at home or online, has a recognisable shape. The tutor asks what the student is working on, sets a short task to see how the student thinks, marks it with the student, and ends with one or two specific next steps. A student should leave knowing what to do before the next session. A parent should be able to ask, "What did you work on, and what should happen next?" and get a clear answer.

### Questions that tell you something

- What topic would you start with for my child, and why?
- How will you know after four weeks whether this is working?
- How do you handle criterion-referenced marking, and which criteria does my child lose marks on?
- What do you do if my child is stuck on a question for several minutes?
- How do you keep parents informed without taking over the lesson?

### Red flags worth taking seriously

Be wary of any tutor who guarantees a grade, who offers to write or edit an Internal Assessment or Extended Essay, or who cannot explain what a mark scheme rewards. Every IB internal assessment is the student's own work. The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to give feedback on one draft; it does not allow anyone, tutor or parent, to write, rewrite or edit the student's submission. A tutor may teach the underlying skill and ask the student to reread their own paragraph against a criterion, and that is the boundary. Ask about it at the first meeting. ## What Are the Most Common Mistakes Sushant Lok Families Make?

Families searching for IB tutors in Sushant Lok tend to trip over the same few things. The most common mistakes are logistical, not academic: overloading a school night, ignoring the household's noise and space limits, booking a tutor for every subject at once and delaying a diagnostic until after the first bad result. Each is easy to avoid once it is named.

| Mistake | How it plays out | A simpler way |
|---|---|---|
| Too many subjects at once | The student is stretched, nothing improves | Start with the highest-risk subject |
| Home lessons in the noisiest room | Attention falls in week two | Pick the quiet room or move the time |
| Sibling sessions overlapping | One child waits, the other is distracted | Stagger the slots |
| Waiting for the mock to act | Too late to fix the cause | Diagnose in the first term |
| No gate or entry plan in a society | The tutor is late, the lesson is short | Arrange visitor access beforehand |
| Treating the tutor as the whole plan | The student does no independent work | Agree what happens between sessions |

One of these deserves more attention than it usually gets: expecting the tutor to replace the student's own practice. A weekly tutor session is typically one to two hours; the rest of the week belongs to the student. A tutor can direct that work, set the error log and mark the timed papers, but cannot do it. Households that agree this from the start tend to have calmer, more productive arrangements.

Usually through the society's visitor process, which varies by building. ### Can two siblings share one tutor?

They can share the same tutor in separate sessions, but joint sessions rarely work well. Tutoring depends on responding to one student's errors in real time, so a second child halves the attention. Two students at the same level on the same unit can occasionally share a short revision block. As a default, stagger back-to-back slots, with the older child's mock periods given priority.

### How many DP subjects should we get tutoring for?

Usually one or two, not six. Choose the subjects where the gap to the target is largest and where an offer or the diploma itself depends on the result, typically an HL subject. For mathematics and the sciences, often yes, with a pen tablet, a shared whiteboard and good audio. A hybrid arrangement, home in regular weeks and online around exams, suits many families. A demo session helps test which format fits.

### Can a tutor help with the Extended Essay or Internal Assessment?

A tutor can teach research skills, structure and how to read a criterion, but cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission. Every IB internal assessment is the student's own work, and a teacher may give feedback on one draft. Any tutor who offers to write or polish it for you is a risk to the diploma. Ask about the boundary at the first meeting.

### When should a MYP 5 student start tutoring for DP?

Often in the term before DP1 begins, especially in mathematics, where the jump to DP is real. A short bridge block can fix algebra gaps and introduce the style of DP questions.
