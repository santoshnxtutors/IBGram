## Why do English and humanities need a different kind of IGCSE tutor?

English and humanities need a different tutor because the marks come from the quality of an answer, not from a single correct result. A maths tutor can check an answer against a key; an English or Economics tutor must read a response and judge how well it argues, structures and uses evidence, which takes a trained eye and honest, specific feedback.

Three habits separate a good writing-subject tutor from a general one.

- **They mark real writing.** The student writes, the tutor reads the whole piece and returns specific comments: this paragraph repeats the point, this sentence claims more than the evidence shows, this introduction takes 80 words to say nothing.
- **They teach structure as a tool.** A paragraph that makes a point, supports it, explains the support and links back to the question is a unit the student can reuse across subjects.
- **They hold back from rewriting.** A tutor who rewrites a student's essay produces a better essay and a student who has learned nothing. The skill is in getting the student to do the second draft.

For most families looking at IGCSE tutors in Vasant Kunj, the practical question is whether a student needs this help at all. If a student reads widely, writes fluently and is scoring at target, an extra tutor adds load. If the student writes short, safe answers, runs out of ideas in the second paragraph, or loses marks for "lack of development" with no idea what that means, a tutor can help a great deal. ## What are the IGCSE English syllabuses, and which one is my child on?

IGCSE English is not one subject. It is several syllabuses with different papers, and the first job of any tutor is to find out which one the student is sitting. Cambridge offers First Language English 0500, English as a Second Language 0510 and 0511, and Literature in English 0475, while Pearson Edexcel offers International GCSE English Language A (4EA1), English Language B (4EB1) and English as a Second Language (4ES1).

Confirm the code with the school before hiring anyone, because a tutor preparing a student for the wrong paper wastes months.

| Syllabus | Who it is for | What the papers test |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge First Language English 0500 | Students whose strongest language is English | A reading paper, then directed writing and composition, or a coursework portfolio, depending on the option the school chooses |
| Cambridge English as a Second Language 0510 / 0511 | Students learning English as an additional language | Reading and writing plus listening; 0511 counts speaking into the grade, 0510 reports speaking separately |
| Cambridge Literature in English 0475 | Students studying set texts | Responses to prose, poetry and drama |
| Pearson Edexcel English Language A (4EA1) and B (4EB1) | First-language English under Edexcel | Reading and writing tasks in the Edexcel format |
| Pearson Edexcel English as a Second Language (4ES1) | Additional-language English under Edexcel | Skills assessed under the Edexcel specification |

Two practical notes follow from the table.

First, First Language and Second Language are different tasks. A Vasant Kunj student who speaks English at home but whose school has entered them for Second Language is not being "held back"; the syllabus simply has a different shape, including listening and a speaking component for 0511. A tutor must teach to that shape. Equally, a student on First Language who has strong spoken English but weak formal writing needs help with register, structure and precision, not with vocabulary lists.

Second, Literature is a separate subject, not "English plus books". It asks for close reading, an argument about how a writer creates meaning and the ability to quote economically. A student can score well in First Language and still struggle in Literature, and the reverse.

## How does a tutor help with IGCSE First Language English?

A tutor helps with First Language English by teaching the student to read a text for what the question wants, to write directed pieces for a specific audience and purpose, and to compose narrative or descriptive writing with control, not just effort. The work is mostly on short, repeated tasks with marked feedback, because writing improves through revision, not through instruction alone.

Here is what that looks like in practice, task by task.

### Reading: answering the question that was asked

A common loss in reading papers is a competent answer to a question the examiner did not ask. The student explains what a passage is about when the question asked how the writer creates a mood, or lifts a phrase without commenting on its effect. A tutor trains a three-step habit: underline the verb in the question, find two pieces of evidence that match it, and say what each one does. Three weeks of this changes marks more than a month of extra reading.

### Directed writing: audience, purpose, register

Directed writing asks the student to use material from a reading text in a new form, such as a speech, a letter, a report or an article. Students often copy phrases from the source instead of transforming them, or write in a single flat register for every task. A tutor teaches the difference between a speech to a school assembly and a letter to a newspaper editor, and gives practice in lifting an idea, not a sentence.

### Composition: descriptive and narrative writing

Composition rewards control more than ambition. A student who attempts a sprawling plot with six characters often loses marks for structure and ending. A tutor teaches the shape of a short piece: one setting, one change, a deliberate ending, and sentence variety that serves the mood. A weekly timed piece, marked against the syllabus descriptors, is worth more than occasional long ones.

### Coursework option

Some schools choose the coursework portfolio. In that case the work must be the student's own, and a tutor teaches the skills and asks questions, but does not write or edit the submission. The school confirms what is permitted.

## How does a tutor help with English as a Second Language?

A tutor for English as a Second Language works on the four skills the syllabus assesses: reading, writing, listening and, for 0511, speaking. Because these students are building accuracy and range, the tutor spends more time on sentence-level control, vocabulary in context and listening practice than a First Language tutor would.

The value of one-to-one here is obvious: a tutor can hear a student speak, notice the habitual errors, and correct them gently in real time. In a classroom of thirty, the quiet student's errors are rarely heard.

What a good session may contain:

- **A short reading with questions,** with the tutor asking the student to find the evidence in the text and say why it answers the question.
- **A writing task of 150 to 250 words,** marked for accuracy, range and organisation, with three corrections the student must apply in a rewrite.
- **A listening extract,** with the student predicting what will be asked before hearing it.
- **Speaking practice,** if the syllabus counts it, with the tutor playing the examiner and then giving specific feedback on fluency, vocabulary and structure.

A caution for families: the IB has no subject called ESL, and parents sometimes confuse the IGCSE second-language syllabuses with IB English B. If your child may move to the IB Diploma Programme (DP) after IGCSE, the relevant DP subject for a student whose first language is not English is English B at Standard Level (SL) or Higher Level (HL), or English ab initio for genuine beginners. A tutor who knows both can help the student see the bridge.

## How does a tutor help with Literature in English?

A tutor helps with Literature in English by teaching close reading, argument and economical quotation, using the student's own set texts. The student learns to say not just what happens, but how the writer makes it happen and why it matters, which is the core of every high-scoring literature answer.

A typical pattern in Grade 10: the student knows the plot well and retells it. A tutor moves them from "this happens" to "the writer shows this by choosing this word, which suggests this, and that links to the theme of". The sentence structure is a scaffold; the thinking inside it is the student's.

Practical steps a tutor often uses:

1. **Annotate a short extract** together, the student leading, marking word choices, patterns and shifts.
2. **Write one analytical paragraph** from the annotations, using a point, evidence, comment shape.
3. **Compare with a stronger version,** from a past marking exemplar or from the tutor's model, and name the differences.
4. **Rewrite** the paragraph, applying only two changes.
5. **Repeat** with a different extract next week.

This rhythm builds the habit of reading closely, which is the skill examiners reward. Tutors should not give the student ready-made essays on the set texts to memorise; examiners can tell, and the student has learned nothing transferable.

## How do IGCSE Economics, Business Studies, History and Geography reward answers?

These humanities and social-science subjects reward answers that use the right terms, show the chain of reasoning and apply it to the case in the question. A student who writes everything they know about a topic usually scores lower than one who writes the specific chain the question asks for.

The subject structures are worth knowing, because the tutor must teach to them.

- **Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455.** Paper 1 is multiple choice (30 percent) and Paper 2 is structured questions with data (70 percent), across six topics: the basic economic problem; the allocation of resources; microeconomic decision-makers; government and the macroeconomy; economic development; and international trade and globalisation. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) has Paper 1 on microeconomics and business economics and Paper 2 on macroeconomics and the global economy.
- **Cambridge IGCSE Business Studies 0450.** Paper 1 (short answer and data response) and Paper 2 (case study) are 50 percent each, across six areas from understanding business activity to external influences. Calculations include break-even, cash-flow forecasts, profit and ratios. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Business (4BS1) has Paper 1 on investigating small businesses and Paper 2 on large businesses.
- **History and Geography.** The syllabus codes, papers and coursework options differ by board and by school. Ask the school for the exact specification, and check the current syllabus before a tutor plans anything.

What a tutor does with these subjects:

| Skill | What goes wrong | What a tutor trains |
|---|---|---|
| Defining and using terms | Everyday words used for technical ones | A short glossary the student can use in sentences |
| Chains of reasoning | Jumps from cause to conclusion | "Because, which leads to, so" written out in full |
| Using the data | Quoting numbers without comment | Describe the figure, then say what it implies |
| Diagrams (Economics) | Drawn but not explained | Label, shift, then narrate in two sentences |
| Evaluation | A list of "on the other hand" points | One judgement, with a reason, at the end |
| Time in the paper | Long answers to low-mark parts | Marks-to-minutes ratio practised under timing |

## What does a good week of IGCSE English or humanities tutoring look like?

A good week has one piece of timed writing done before the session, a marked review in the session, one targeted skill drill, and a short task for the days between. The student writes, the tutor reads, and the next piece applies one or two named changes, so progress is visible.

Here is a sample weekly rhythm for a Grade 10 student in First Language English, which a tutor can adapt for Economics, Literature or any essay subject.

:::timeline A weekly rhythm for a writing subject
Before the session | Student completes one timed piece
Minutes 0-20 | Tutor returns marked piece, student names two fixes
Minutes 20-45 | Skill drill: audience, evidence or paragraph shape
Minutes 45-60 | Student rewrites one paragraph with the fixes
After the session | One short task, due before the next meeting
:::

Notice what is absent: the tutor does not talk for most of the session, and the student is not handed a model answer to copy. The marked piece is the teaching material. A student who brings nothing to the session gets a lecture, which is the weaker use of an expensive hour. Agree up front that the student writes between sessions.

If your child's school sets frequent assignments already, the tutor's sessions can work on the assignments in progress, so the two efforts reinforce each other and the student is not doing double work.

## How do you judge an IGCSE English or humanities tutor in a trial?

You judge a tutor in a trial by giving them a piece of your child's real writing and watching what they do with it. A tutor who reads it closely, finds one or two specific patterns and teaches one fix is showing you the real work. A tutor who praises it broadly, or rewrites it, is not.

Use this trial plan, which takes about an hour.

1. **Send a recent piece in advance,** with the teacher's comments if there are any.
2. **Watch how the tutor opens.** Do they ask what the student found hard before commenting? A tutor who starts with questions is collecting evidence.
3. **Ask the tutor to name the two biggest patterns** in the writing, in plain language the student understands.
4. **Have the student rewrite one paragraph** in the session, using one of those fixes, while the tutor stays quiet.
5. **Ask for a plan:** what will be worked on over four weeks, how marked work comes back, and when the review is.
6. **Ask the student afterwards** whether the feedback made sense. A student who says "she told me what to fix and how" is describing good teaching.

## What are the red flags for a writing-subject tutor?

The red flags are a promise of a grade, a tutor who rewrites the student's work, a supply of model essays to memorise, no marked work returned between sessions, and no questions about which syllabus the student is on. Any one is a reason to pause.

- **Memorised essays.** Examiners reward answers to the question set. A pre-prepared essay on a "likely" topic often fails to answer the question and signals a tutor teaching to a shortcut.
- **Rewriting for the student.** The student's improvement is capped by how much of the thinking they do.
- **Coursework help beyond skills.** Where the school uses a coursework portfolio, the work must be the student's own.
- **No syllabus awareness.** A tutor who does not ask whether the student is on 0500, 0510, 0511 or 4EA1 does not know what they are preparing for.
- **Grade guarantees.** No tutor can guarantee a grade.
- **No written feedback.** If the student never sees a marked piece, the tutoring is conversation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a tutor for IGCSE English if my child already speaks English at home?

Not necessarily, but fluent speech does not guarantee good exam writing. IGCSE English rewards structure, audience awareness and precise language, which differ from conversation. If your child writes fluently and scores at target, a tutor is optional. If they lose marks for development or structure, a tutor focused on marked writing can help. Ask the subject teacher what they see.

### What is the difference between First Language English and English as a Second Language?

First Language English is for students whose strongest language is English and tests reading, directed writing and composition, or a coursework portfolio. English as a Second Language is for students learning English as an additional language and includes listening, with 0511 counting speaking into the grade. The two are different syllabuses with different tasks, and the school decides which a student sits.

### Can a tutor help with IGCSE Literature set texts?

Yes, by teaching close reading, argument and economical quotation using the student's own set texts. A good tutor does not hand over ready-made essays to memorise; examiners reward answers that respond to the question set. Expect annotation work, one analytical paragraph written by the student, comparison with a stronger version and a rewrite.

### Is online tutoring good enough for essay subjects?

Yes, for most students, and often more convenient than for diagram-heavy subjects. A shared document lets the tutor mark and comment in real time. The caution is handwriting: IGCSE papers are handwritten, so include some timed handwritten practice, either at home or on camera, so the student's speed does not suffer on exam day.

### How many sessions a week does an English or humanities student need?

Most students do well with one marked session a week, with a short piece of writing done beforehand. Two sessions can help in the weeks before mocks, but only if the student is writing between meetings. The value comes from the marked-writing loop, not from the number of hours, so ask the tutor how marked work comes back.

### Does the tutor write or fix coursework?

No. Where a school uses a coursework portfolio, the work must be the student's own, and a tutor teaches the skills and asks questions but does not write or edit the submission. The school sets what is permitted. A tutor who offers to write or heavily rewrite coursework is putting the student at risk and should not be hired.

### Should my child have one tutor for English and Economics, or separate tutors?

Separate specialists are usually better for subjects with different content, but a strong writing tutor can sometimes cover both English and a humanities subject if they know the syllabus. Ask the tutor to describe the paper structure of each and to show how they would mark a sample answer. If the answers are vague for one subject, hire separately.

### What if my child's English improves but the grade does not?

Check whether the improvement is on the skills the paper rewards. Better vocabulary or longer answers may not move marks if the answers still miss the question or lack development. Ask the tutor to mark a timed paper against the syllabus descriptors and show where marks are being lost. If the pattern has not changed after about eight weeks of real work, change the plan or the tutor. No tutor can guarantee a grade.

### Is it too late to start in Grade 10?

It is not too late, but the kind of help changes. Early in Grade 10 there is time to fix habits; closer to the exam, the work is timed practice and error review. Starting a few months before mocks gives a reasonable runway. The earlier a pattern is fixed, the more subjects benefit, so if the problem is visible now, start now.
