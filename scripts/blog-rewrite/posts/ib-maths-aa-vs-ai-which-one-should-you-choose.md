IB Maths AA vs AI is a choice between two different kinds of mathematics, not between a hard course and an easy one. Analysis and Approaches (AA) is built around algebra, functions, proof and calculus, and rewards a student who likes to argue from first principles. Applications and Interpretation (AI) is built around modelling, statistics and technology, and rewards a student who likes to take messy real data and say something defensible about it. Both are offered at Higher Level (HL) and Standard Level (SL) in the International Baccalaureate (IB) Diploma Programme (DP).

This guide is for DP1 students choosing subjects, for Grade 10 and MYP 5 students planning ahead, and for parents trying to understand why the school coordinator keeps asking about degree plans. It sets out what each course actually asks of a student, how the papers differ, how HL and SL change the picture, what universities tend to expect, and how to test yourself honestly before you commit. It also covers the awkward cases: strong at maths but hates proofs, weak at algebra but good with data, undecided about a degree. The overall system for scoring a top grade once you have chosen AA HL lives in [the guide to scoring a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/); this post helps you decide whether that is your course in the first place.

## What Is the Difference Between IB Maths AA and AI?

AA and AI cover the same five broad areas (number and algebra, functions, geometry and trigonometry, probability and statistics, calculus) but at different angles. AA treats mathematics as a structure to be understood and proved: you derive results, manipulate expressions by hand and work with exact values. AI treats mathematics as a toolkit for describing situations: you build models, fit data, test hypotheses and rely on a graphic display calculator (GDC) throughout.

The IB's own framing is that AA is for students who enjoy developing mathematical arguments and solving abstract problems, and AI is for students who want to see mathematics used to describe the world. Both are rigorous. Neither is a "lite" version of the other. The DP Mathematics subject guide treats them as peers, and a student who earns a 7 in either has demonstrated serious mathematical ability of a different flavour.

The simplest way to feel the difference is to look at the verb in a typical question. AA questions often say *show that*, *prove*, *find the exact value* or *hence determine*. AI questions often say *estimate*, *interpret*, *comment on the suitability of the model* or *use your GDC to find*. The first set asks you to produce a chain of reasoning. The second asks you to choose a tool, run it and read the output sensibly.

## Which Is Harder in IB Maths AA vs AI?

Neither is harder in general, and the question hides the real issue. AA is harder for students who find algebraic manipulation, abstraction and long chains of reasoning draining. AI is harder for students who dislike ambiguity, because AI questions often have more than one defensible answer and expect you to justify the one you chose. Difficulty is a property of the match between course and student, not of the course alone.

There is one honest asymmetry. AA HL has the highest ceiling of abstraction in the DP mathematics offer: proof by induction, complex numbers, Maclaurin series and extended Paper 3 problems that ask you to build a mathematical argument in unfamiliar territory. That makes it the demanding option for students who do not enjoy that kind of thinking. AI HL has a different demanding edge: a wide syllabus that includes statistics and modelling topics, heavy technology use, and questions in which the mathematics is straightforward but interpreting it correctly is the entire mark.

A student who picks AI because a friend said it was easier often discovers in the first term that "easier" meant "less algebra", and that statistics, regression, hypothesis testing and written interpretation are a different workload, not a smaller one. A student who picks AA because it sounds more serious often discovers that grinding through integration techniques at 9 pm is not a pastime they can sustain for two years. The better question is the one in the old advice, sharpened: **which kind of mathematical difficulty can you tolerate for two years?**

## What Does Each Course Actually Test?

The structure of the papers tells you more about the course than any description. Treat the table below as a map and confirm the exact timings and mark totals in the current subject guide, because the IB revises guides on a cycle.

|---|---|---|

| Calculator | Paper 1 without; Paper 2 with GDC | GDC expected on all papers |

AI HL follows the same overall pattern of three papers and an exploration, but its papers assume technology throughout, and the content weight shifts towards statistics and modelling.

## AA vs AI at Higher Level and Standard Level

Four combinations exist, and most confusion comes from comparing them across the wrong axis. The choice is really two choices: course (AA or AI) and level (HL or SL). They interact.

**AA HL** is the most demanding and the most commonly asked-for by mathematics-heavy degrees. It suits a student who is already comfortable with Extended-level work in IGCSE or MYP extended mathematics and who enjoys algebra. It takes up a large share of a student's study week.

**AA SL** keeps the analytical flavour with a lighter syllabus: no complex numbers, no proof by induction, no Paper 3. It suits a student who likes the algebraic style but whose degree plans do not need advanced mathematics.

**AI HL** is not a halfway house. It is a demanding course with real statistical depth and a heavy technology skill set. It suits a student who wants serious mathematics but pointed at data, economics, social science or life science applications.

**AI SL** is the course designed for students who need mathematics as a supporting tool, not as a subject they will go deep in. It is also the course most often chosen by students who find algebra a source of anxiety, which is a legitimate reason as long as the degree plan allows it.

A frequent and mistaken ranking in the IB Maths AA vs AI debate puts them in a single ladder: AI SL, AI HL, AA SL, AA HL. That ladder is wrong. AI HL carries a heavier syllabus than AA SL, and students who treat AA SL as "above" AI HL end up in a course that is lighter than the one that would have suited them.

For the HL versus SL decision itself, the practical test is workload. HL has 240 recommended teaching hours against 150 for SL, and a student taking three or four HL subjects should look at the total, not the subject in isolation. A student with HL Physics, HL Chemistry and HL Economics, who also chooses AA HL, is accepting a week that leaves very little space for the Extended Essay (EE), Theory of Knowledge (TOK) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS).

## Which Degrees Expect AA, and Which Accept AI?

University expectations are the single strongest reason to choose one course over the other, and the one most often ignored until too late. The honest answer is that it varies by university and by degree, and the only reliable source is the admissions page of the specific course.

As a working pattern, which you must verify for each target:

- **Mathematics, physics, engineering, computer science at quantitatively demanding universities:** AA HL is usually required or strongly preferred. Some courses will state AA HL explicitly and not accept AI HL.
- **Economics:** varies widely. Some programmes specify AA at a given level, others accept either. Quantitative economics tends to lean towards AA.
- **Business, management, finance:** often accept either, with a level requirement. Check the course page.
- **Psychology, sociology, geography, life sciences, medicine-adjacent degrees:** AI is often a natural fit, and many accept either course. Statistics-heavy training in these fields suits AI's content.
- **Architecture, design, humanities:** mathematics is rarely central, and either course at SL may be sufficient where a mathematics subject is asked for at all.

In India, a student planning to apply to Indian universities should keep in mind that the Association of Indian Universities issues equivalence for international qualifications and that requirements differ by university and by course. The IB's own guidance, and the school coordinator, should be the first checks. A student who is also thinking about JEE Advanced should read [JEE Advanced mathematics preparation](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-jee-advanced-mathematics-ajay-vatsyayan/), where the overlap with AA HL, and the gaps, are discussed in more detail.

## A Student Profile Test You Can Finish in One Evening

Questionnaires about personality are weak guides. A better test is to give yourself two short pieces of actual work and notice which one you finish with energy.

**Task one (about 40 minutes).** Take a topic you have met in Grade 10 or MYP 5, such as quadratics or sequences, and write a short argument that a general result is true. For example, show that the sum of the first *n* odd numbers is *n*². Do not look it up. Work through a few cases, spot the pattern, then try to explain why it must hold.

**Task two (about 40 minutes).** Take a small data set of your own: your last ten school test scores against hours studied, or the daily temperature and ice-cream sales of any published table. Sketch the scatter graph, estimate a line of best fit by eye, and write four sentences about what it does and does not tell you, including one reason the pattern could be misleading.

Now answer honestly:

- Which task did you lose track of time in?
- Which one made you want to do a second example?
- Which one felt like homework?

A student who lights up at task one is likely to enjoy AA. A student who lights up at task two is likely to enjoy AI. A student who enjoyed neither should speak to the maths teacher about the SL option in whichever course the degree plan allows, and should not choose HL out of pride.

The point of the test is not to produce a verdict. It is to move the conversation from "which is better" to "which do I actually want to spend two years doing".

## Student Profiles: Six Typical Cases

These are illustrative composites, not real students.

**The confident algebraist heading for engineering.** Scores well in Extended IGCSE or MYP extended maths, enjoys manipulation, wants an engineering degree. AA HL is the clear choice. The main risk is underestimating the workload when combined with HL physics.

**The strong all-rounder with no fixed degree.** Good at maths, good at everything else, undecided. AA HL keeps the most doors open, because it satisfies the widest range of requirements. The cost is workload. If the student already carries two HL sciences, AA SL or AI HL may be the more sustainable pairing.

**The data-minded student heading for economics or psychology.** Comfortable with graphs and statistics, finds proof tiresome. AI HL or AI SL, depending on the course requirement. Check the economics course page carefully, because some will ask for AA.

**The student who fears algebra but is capable.** Past marks are good but confidence is low, usually because of a shaky Grade 9 foundation. AI can feel like a relief, but the better first move is often to repair the algebra, because a degree plan may require AA later. A diagnostic with a tutor can show whether the fear is a gap or a preference.

**The student drawn to AI because it "looks easier".** Needs a frank conversation. If the degree plan does not need AA and the student prefers applied work, AI is a good choice. If the plan does need AA, choosing AI now stores up a problem.

**The student who wants to keep every option open.** The usual answer is AA, and the usual cost is a heavier schedule. Keeping options open is only worthwhile if the options are real.

## What Does the Internal Assessment Look Like in Each Course?

The mathematics exploration is the same task for both courses, at both levels, so the IA should not drive your choice, but it does shape your two years. It is worth 20% of the final grade, runs to 12 to 20 pages and is marked out of 20 on five criteria: Presentation (4), Mathematical communication (4), Personal engagement (3), Reflection (3) and Use of mathematics (6). HL work is judged against a higher standard on the Use of mathematics criterion.

What differs is the natural territory. AA students tend to choose explorations in pure mathematics: number theory, geometry, calculus applications, probability puzzles with a rigorous treatment. AI students tend to choose explorations built on data and models: sports statistics, population growth, queueing, sound or light measurements. Both can score well. Both can fail by being mathematically thin.

Two things to know about the IA, whichever course you choose. First, it must be the student's own work. A teacher may comment on one draft, and a tutor may teach skills and ask questions but must not write or rewrite any part of it. Second, the topic should be something you can do real mathematics with at the level you take. An AI HL student who explores a statistical topic using only descriptive statistics has chosen a topic that cannot reach the top of the Use of mathematics criterion.

## How Technology Changes the Daily Experience

The GDC is a bigger part of AI than of AA, and students underestimate how much that shapes the course.

In AI, the calculator is a primary tool throughout: regression, distributions, hypothesis tests, solving equations numerically, financial functions. Marks are lost by selecting the wrong menu, mis-entering data or reporting a number without context. The skill is operational fluency plus judgement about what the output means.

In AA, the calculator is allowed on one paper at SL and on two at HL, and absent on Paper 1, which is why hand fluency carries so much weight. Marks are lost by algebraic slips and missing working. The GDC is useful, but the student who relies on it for everything will struggle on the paper where it is not allowed.

A practical consequence: a student who enjoys figuring out a calculator and building small simulations will feel at home in AI. A student who prefers pencil and paper, and likes the clean feeling of an exact answer, will feel at home in AA. If you are choosing AA HL and want to use the calculator well on the papers where it is allowed, [the GDC advantage in IB DP Mathematics AA HL](/blog/the-gdc-advantage-in-ib-dp-mathematics-aa-hl/) covers the techniques worth drilling.

## Coming From IGCSE or MYP: How Your Background Points You

The course you are leaving is a useful clue, though not a rule.

A student coming from Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 Extended, particularly one who also took Additional Mathematics 0606, has already met functions, logarithms, differentiation, integration and vectors, which is the content AA HL builds on. A student who took the Core tier, or Extended without Additional Mathematics, will find AA HL a steeper first term, and AI SL or AA SL may be the more realistic starting point. A student from Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) at Higher tier is in a similar position to the 0580 Extended student; those who took Further Pure Mathematics (4PM1) have a head start on AA HL.

From MYP, the picture depends on whether the school offers standard and extended mathematics in MYP 4 and 5. Extended mathematics covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics AA. A student who took standard mathematics and enjoyed it can still do well in AA, but usually needs a bridging effort over the summer.

The detailed gap analysis and a summer bridge plan live in [from IGCSE mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/). The short version: a good grade in your previous course tells you that you can do mathematics, not which kind you will enjoy.

## Can You Switch From AI to AA, or the Other Way?

:::timeline A sensible decision calendar
Grade 10 or MYP 5 | Build the degree requirement table
Before DP1 | Run the two-task test, talk to the coordinator
DP1 weeks 1-3 | Trial period: choice stays open
End of DP1 term 1 | Typical point to confirm HL or SL
Check your school's own switching deadline; schools differ.
:::

Sometimes, and only early. Schools differ in their policies, and the DP coordinator makes the final decision on placement. In many schools a switch is feasible in the first weeks of DP1, before much of the syllabus has been covered, and becomes progressively harder as the term goes on and as the first internal tests produce marks that will be used for predicted grades.

A switch from AA to AI is generally easier to organise than the reverse, because the content AA students have studied overlaps with AI's, whereas a student moving from AI to AA misses the proof and calculus foundation. A student who moves from AI to AA late in the first term typically has a real gap to close and a short window in which to close it.

The practical advice is to treat the first three weeks of DP1 as a trial period, not a lock-in. Attend the first lessons with the choice open, complete the first problem sets honestly and compare how each type of problem feels. If you are already sure that the choice is wrong, speak to the coordinator immediately. Delay makes the cost higher.

Switching level, from HL to SL within a course, is usually easier than switching course, and schools often treat it as a normal decision to be taken by the end of the first term or at a mid-year review. Confirm your school's deadline.

## How a Tutor Can Help With the Decision, and With the Course Once It Is Made

A tutor is not required to choose between AA and AI, and many families make the decision well with the coordinator and the maths teacher. A tutor becomes useful in three situations.

The first is when the decision is genuinely unclear: the student has strong marks but mixed feelings and the degree plan is open. A diagnostic session and a taster of each course's problem style can answer questions that talking cannot.

The second is when the student has chosen and is struggling. The shape of the struggle is different in each course. In AA, it is usually algebraic fluency and unfamiliar-problem reasoning. In AI, it is usually interpretation, tool use and writing. The root causes of struggle across both courses, and the MYP-to-DP jump, are covered in [why students struggle in IB Mathematics](/blog/ib-mathematics-tutoring-guide/).

The third is when a student wants to move between courses and needs a bridge plan.

Once a course is chosen, the match matters: an AA HL tutor needs depth in proof, complex numbers and calculus and experience of Paper 3, as described in [IB Math AA HL tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-math-aa-hl-tutor-gurgaon-guide/), while an AI HL tutor needs modelling and statistics skills, heavy GDC fluency and familiarity with the exploration, as set out in [IB Math AI HL tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-math-ai-hl-tutor-gurgaon-guide/). A tutor who is strong in one is not automatically strong in the other, and a family should ask which course the tutor has taught recently.

## A Step-by-Step Process for Choosing Between AA and AI

Use this list on its own as a quick decision routine.

1. **List the degrees and universities you are seriously considering,** at least three, including one you are unsure about.
2. **Check each course page for the maths requirement,** noting the course (AA or AI) and the level (HL or SL), and write down the date you checked.
3. **Mark the most demanding requirement you might realistically apply to.** That is your minimum.
4. **Do the two-task test above,** one proof-style and one data-style, and note which you preferred.
5. **Look at your total workload.** Count your HL subjects, the EE, TOK and CAS, and ask whether the course at that level is sustainable.
6. **Speak to the maths teacher and the DP coordinator,** and bring your table and the results of your test.
7. **Decide, then give it three weeks** before you consider changing, unless something is clearly wrong.

## Mistakes That Lead to a Poor Choice

**Choosing by reputation.** AA has a reputation for being "the serious one" and AI for being "the easy one". Both reputations are wrong in ways that cost students marks and confidence.

**Choosing by friends.** A friend's choice reflects their degree plan and their strengths, not yours.

**Choosing before looking at a single university page.** This is the most expensive mistake, because a requirement discovered in DP2 cannot be fixed.

**Taking HL to "keep options open" without checking workload.** An overloaded HL week produces rushed IA and weak EE, which cost more than the option was worth.

**Assuming AI means no algebra.** AI uses algebra constantly. The difference is the purpose and the proportion.

**Ignoring the preference.** A student who dislikes proof and picks AA for a degree that does not require it is making two years harder than necessary.

**Treating the decision as final at 15.** Degree plans change. A choice that satisfies the widest range of plausible requirements at a sustainable workload is better than a choice optimised for one plan at the edge of what the student can manage.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is IB Maths AI easier than AA?

No, they are different, and each is harder for a different kind of student. AI has less abstract algebra and no proof at the core, but it has a heavier statistics and modelling load and expects confident technology use and written interpretation. AA has more algebra, proof and calculus technique. The better question is which style of difficulty suits you, and which your target degrees accept.

### Can I do engineering with IB Maths AI?

It depends on the university and course, and for many engineering degrees the answer is no or "only with extra conditions". Engineering courses at quantitatively demanding universities usually ask for AA HL, and some name it explicitly. Check each course's admissions page, and ask the DP coordinator. If engineering is a serious possibility, AA HL is the safer choice.

### Is AA HL required for economics?

Not always. Some economics programmes accept either AA or AI at a stated level, while others, particularly quantitative or mathematical economics, ask for AA. Policies differ even within one university across different economics degrees. Build a table of the specific courses you are considering and record the requirement for each, with the date you checked.

### Which is better for computer science, AA or AI?

For mathematically demanding computer science degrees, AA HL is usually the stronger preparation and is often what admissions pages name. For applied or data-oriented programmes, either may be accepted. Look at the course module list: if it includes discrete mathematics, proof and algorithms, AA's style matches. If it emphasises data and statistics, AI's does.

### Should I take Maths at HL or SL?

Take HL if your degree asks for it or recommends it, if maths is among your strongest subjects, and if your total workload allows it. Take SL if mathematics is a supporting subject for your plans and you need space for other HL subjects, the Extended Essay and CAS. HL carries 240 recommended teaching hours against 150 for SL, so the difference in weekly work is real.

### Can I switch from AI to AA after DP1 starts?

Sometimes, in the first weeks, depending on the school. The DP coordinator makes the decision, and the feasibility drops as the term progresses because the syllabus has moved on and internal marks begin to count towards predictions. Moving from AI to AA means closing a content gap in proof and calculus. If you are unsure, raise it in the first weeks, not after the first test.

### Does choosing AI hurt my university applications?

Not if the courses you apply to accept it. AI is a respected IB course and is a fit for many degrees in the sciences, social sciences, business and health. It harms an application only when a degree specifically requires AA, or when the level is below the stated requirement. This is why the checking step matters more than the choice itself.

### I am good at maths but dislike proofs. What should I choose?

Consider AI HL or AA SL, depending on your degree plans. AA HL has proof by induction and extended reasoning questions that a student who dislikes them will find a grind. If your degree plan needs AA HL, a dislike of proof is worth testing: some students find they enjoy it once it is taught well. If your plan does not, AI HL offers serious mathematics without that emphasis.

### Is it a problem if I took Core IGCSE Mathematics?

It makes AA HL a steep first term, and many students in that position choose AI SL, AI HL or AA SL, depending on the plan. A bridge programme over the summer can close some of the gap. The first step is a diagnostic to see which topics are missing, then a decision on whether the gap can be closed before DP1 content outpaces you.

### Can a tutor help me decide, or only teach after I have chosen?

A tutor can do both. A diagnostic session with a maths mentor, using problems from each course, shows which style you handle well and where your gaps are, which is useful evidence for the conversation with your coordinator. After the decision, a tutor with recent experience in your course can support the work. Ask which course the tutor has taught recently and at which level.

### My child wants to keep every option open. What do you advise?

Check the options first, because "every option" is rarely every option. Identify the three or four degrees that are realistic, find their requirements, and choose the course and level that satisfies the most demanding of them at a workload your child can sustain. AA HL satisfies the widest range of requirements, but only a student who can carry it well benefits from that breadth.
