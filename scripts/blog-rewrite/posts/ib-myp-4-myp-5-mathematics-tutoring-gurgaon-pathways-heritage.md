MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics tutoring has one job that matters more than any other: to turn a student who can follow a method into a student who can choose one. In the Middle Years Programme (MYP), the last two years of mathematics stop asking "can you calculate this?" and start asking "what is going on here, what mathematics fits it and how do you show your reasoning?" Students who were comfortable in MYP 3 often wobble at exactly this point, and parents usually respond by adding more worksheets.

This guide is for parents and students in Gurgaon (Gurugram) with an MYP 4 or MYP 5 child, including families from schools such as Pathways and Heritage. It explains what is really different about the two years, how standard and extended mathematics compare, a topic map you can check against your child's school plan, how the preparation for the Diploma Programme (DP) Mathematics choice between Analysis and Approaches (AA) and Applications and Interpretation (AI) starts early, and what good tutoring looks like in practice. It also says plainly when tutoring is not needed.

One note on schools. Pathways, Heritage and others are named as context only. Each school sets its own unit plans, assessment dates and standard and extended placement, so the school's mathematics teacher is always the authority on what your child is studying.

## What Changes in MYP 4 and MYP 5 Mathematics?

What changes in MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics is the kind of question, not the amount of content. Topics start to connect, tasks arrive in unfamiliar contexts, and the student is expected to explain reasoning instead of presenting an answer. A child who did well by learning one procedure per chapter often finds the later years harder without having done anything wrong.

The International Baccalaureate (IB) MYP is a five-year programme (MYP 1 to 5, roughly ages 11 to 16) across eight subject groups. Every subject is assessed against four criteria (A to D), each scored 0 to 8; the criterion total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through IB grade boundaries. For mathematics, the criteria are A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts.

:::stats MYP Mathematics at a glance
4 | Criteria, A to D
0-8 | Score range per criterion
32 | Maximum criterion total
1-7 | Final MYP grade scale
:::

Three shifts explain most of the wobble.

**Topics stop living in separate boxes.** A function question may need algebra, a graph and a sentence of interpretation. A student who learned each skill alone has to learn to combine them.

**Contexts get less friendly.** Early MYP problems announce their topic. Later ones describe a situation, such as a phone plan, a garden or a data set, and leave the choice of mathematics to the student.

**Explaining becomes part of the mark.** Criterion C rewards organised, clearly justified work. A correct answer with no reasoning earns less than it feels it should, and parents are often surprised that the report shows it.

The deeper treatment of how each criterion is marked is in [the guide to MYP mathematics criteria A, B, C and D](/blog/ib-criterion-a-b-c-d-explained-myp-mathematics-gurgaon/), and the report-reading side is covered in [MYP maths criteria explained for parents](/blog/ib-myp-maths-criteria-a-b-c-d-explained-for-parents/). This page keeps to the two things the criteria guides do not: standard versus extended, and the road towards DP.

## What Is the Difference Between Standard and Extended MYP Mathematics?

Standard and extended MYP mathematics differ mainly in the amount of additional content and the depth of problem solving: schools often split MYP 4 and MYP 5 into standard and extended mathematics, and extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches. The criteria and the 1 to 7 scale stay the same. What changes is the range of material and how far the questions stretch.

Not every school offers both, and the way a school decides who takes which varies. Ask the maths department how placement works, whether a student can move between the two and what the extended course adds. The comparison below is a general guide, not a statement about any particular school.

| Feature | Standard mathematics | Extended mathematics |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Core MYP topics for the year | Core topics plus additional topics |
| Pace | Steady, with time to consolidate | Faster, with fewer review lessons |
| Problem style | Mostly single-concept and short chains | More multi-step and abstract problems |
| Algebra load | Moderate | Heavy; algebra is the main prerequisite for DP AA |
| Best suited to | Students building confidence and fluency | Students comfortable with abstraction and pace |
| DP link | Supports AI and some AA routes | Designed to prepare for DP AA |
| Main risk | Gaps that surface later in DP | Overload if the foundations are not solid |

### Which one is right for a given child?

The honest answer depends on evidence, not ambition. A child who finds algebra enjoyable, does not mind uncertainty and keeps up with the pace is a natural candidate for extended. A child who understands well but needs time to consolidate may do better, and score better, in standard mathematics, and then reach DP with stronger foundations than a child who was stretched too thin in extended.

Consider two typical profiles. The first student finishes MYP 3 with solid marks, plays two sports and takes three weeks to feel secure on any new algebra topic. Extended would double the pace and halve the review. The second student finishes MYP 3 early with every worksheet and asks for harder problems. Standard would leave that student under-challenged. Neither is better in the abstract; the point is to match the course to the child. The school's mathematics teacher and DP coordinator make the final placement.

If you are weighing a move between the two, the practical test is a short diagnostic: can the student handle unfamiliar multi-step algebra without a template? That one skill predicts comfort in extended more reliably than last term's percentage.

## What Is the MYP 4 and MYP 5 Topic Map?

The MYP 4 and MYP 5 topic map is a way of seeing the two years as a set of connected strands: number, algebra, graphs and functions, geometry and measurement, statistics and probability, with investigations and applications running through all of them. The IB does not publish one national list of MYP mathematics units for India, so your school's unit planner is the authority; use the map below to see how the strands connect and where tutoring tends to matter.

| Strand | Typical MYP 4 focus | Typical MYP 5 extension | Where students commonly struggle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number | Percentages, ratio, indices, standard form | Surds, financial applications, accuracy and bounds | Switching between forms, rounding too early |
| Algebra | Expressions, linear and simultaneous equations, factorising | Quadratics, rearranging, algebraic fractions | Sign errors, losing a step, skipped brackets |
| Graphs and functions | Linear graphs, gradient, simple functions | Quadratic and other functions, transformations | Reading a graph as a picture, not a relationship |
| Geometry and measurement | Area, volume, Pythagoras, similarity | Trigonometry, transformations, circles | Choosing the right formula, units |
| Statistics and probability | Averages, spread, simple probability | Cumulative frequency, combined events, interpretation | Computing without saying what it means |
| Investigations | Patterns, rules for sequences | Generalising and justifying | Guessing a rule and not testing it |

Use this as a conversation starter with the teacher, not as a syllabus. A school may teach trigonometry in MYP 4, or place statistics later. What the map shows is that algebra threads through almost everything, which is why weak algebra is the single most common cause of a stalled MYP 4 or MYP 5 year.

### Algebra is the spine, so test it first

If a student is unsure where to start, test algebra. Ask them to solve a two-step equation, expand and simplify two brackets, rearrange a formula for a different subject, and solve a pair of simultaneous equations, without a worked example in front of them. Mistakes show where the thread is thin. Many students who are described as "weak at graphs" or "weak at geometry" are in fact losing marks to algebra inside those topics.

## How Does MYP Maths Prepare Students for DP AA or AI?

MYP maths prepares students for DP mainly through algebraic fluency, comfort with functions and the habit of explaining reasoning, and the choice between DP Analysis and Approaches and Applications and Interpretation starts to form during MYP 4 and MYP 5. DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each available at Higher Level (HL) and Standard Level (SL): AA, which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and AI, which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout.

Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and the student, and the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement. The decision is made at the end of MYP 5 or the start of DP1, so the signals worth noticing appear earlier.

| Signal in MYP 4 or MYP 5 | Points towards | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Enjoys manipulating algebra and spotting structure | AA | AA rewards algebraic fluency and proof |
| Comfortable with extended maths at pace | AA | Extended content overlaps AA's starting point |
| Prefers real data, graphs and models to symbol work | AI | AI is built on modelling and statistics |
| Likes using technology to explore | AI | Technology is used throughout AI |
| Strong on Criterion D, weaker on pure algebra | AI, or AA with support | Application strength with an algebra gap |
| Undecided about university direction | Ask the coordinator | Requirements differ by course |

This table is a prompt for a conversation, not a prediction. Plenty of students who prefer data and modelling thrive in AA, and plenty who love algebra do well in AI. What helps most is checking the target degree's requirements early, because some courses name a specific DP mathematics course. ### What a student should be able to do before DP1

Whichever course comes next, a student leaving MYP 5 is better placed if they can do five things without a template: rearrange formulas, solve quadratics by more than one method, interpret a graph in a sentence, work with indices and surds accurately and explain a multi-step solution in clear lines. These are skills, not topics, which is why they transfer. ## What Should MYP 4 and MYP 5 Mathematics Tutoring Actually Cover?

MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics tutoring should cover five things: a diagnostic of where marks are really being lost, repair of any foundation gaps in algebra, practice on unfamiliar and multi-step problems, work on explaining reasoning and a link to the next stage, whether that is extended maths or DP. It should not be a second round of the school's worksheets.

A useful way to see this is as a progression. Ajay Vatsyayan, IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, describes the approach as concept, pattern, application and expression: understand the idea, recognise the pattern, apply it in an unfamiliar situation and then express the reasoning clearly. The order matters. A student who is rushed to application before the concept is secure memorises templates, and a student who never reaches expression loses Criterion C marks even when the mathematics is right.

:::steps A concept-first sequence for a topic
Concept | What the idea means, in the student's words
Pattern | Spot the relationship across examples
Application | Use it in an unfamiliar context
Expression | Write the reasoning so a reader follows
:::

### A typical session, honestly described

A typical session runs 60 to 90 minutes. The first ten minutes review the previous week's work: one problem the student redoes without help. The middle block works on the current topic through a mix of representations, such as an equation, a graph, a table and a short context. The tutor prompts more than explains, and when the student is stuck, asks "what do you know already?" before offering anything. The last fifteen minutes go on one unfamiliar problem, tackled aloud so the tutor can hear the reasoning, and on a one-line note of what to practise before next time.

What parents should expect to hear afterwards is specific: "We worked on rearranging formulas; the sign error appeared twice and she caught it herself the second time." That is evidence. "She is doing well" is not.

## How Does a Diagnostic Work for an MYP Maths Student?

A diagnostic works by giving the student a short, mixed set of problems without help and sorting every lost mark into a cause, so that the plan targets the real problem instead of the general topic. It usually takes one session, and it is the most valuable hour in the whole arrangement.

A good diagnostic looks at six things:

1. **Foundations:** algebra, fractions, indices and number accuracy, because they sit under everything.
2. **Conceptual understanding:** can the student explain why a method works, not only run it?
3. **Pattern investigation:** can they find a rule from examples and test it?
4. **Communication:** can they lay out a solution a reader could follow?
5. **Application:** can they choose the mathematics for an unfamiliar context?
6. **Exam habits:** timing, reading the question, checking and presentation.

The profile that results is rarely "weak at maths". It is usually something narrower: strong concepts and poor presentation, or neat work and shaky algebra, or fast calculation and little reasoning. Each calls for a different plan. ## What Does Criterion B Look Like in MYP 4 and MYP 5?

Criterion B, investigating patterns, asks students to explore examples, spot a rule, test it and justify why it holds, and in MYP 4 and MYP 5 the expectations move from describing a pattern to generalising and justifying it. Many students who are strong at calculation find Criterion B uncomfortable, because it removes the safety of a given method.

Take a typical investigation: tiles arranged in growing patterns, and the question asks for the number of tiles in the 50th pattern and a general rule. A student who only counts fails to generalise. A student who tabulates results, notices the difference between terms and writes a rule has done the first half. The second half is to test the rule on a case not used to build it and to explain why it works, ideally with a diagram or an algebraic argument. That last step is what lifts the work into the top band.

Tutoring helps most by slowing the student down at the point where they would jump to a rule. The tutor's useful questions are "how do you know?" and "what would break your rule?" The full skill set is covered in [IB MYP mathematics reasoning skills](/blog/ib-myp-mathematics-problem-solving-reasoning-skills/), which treats Criterion B by year and is not specific to Gurgaon.

## What Does Criterion D Look Like, and How Do You Prepare for It?

Criterion D asks students to apply mathematics in real-life contexts: to choose a model, work with it, interpret the result and judge whether it makes sense. Its difficulty is not the calculation but the decision about which mathematics to use and what the answer means.

Practising Criterion D means practising translation. Take a context such as comparing two mobile plans with different fixed charges and per-minute rates. The student has to set up expressions for each plan, decide what a break-even point means, and say which plan suits a particular usage pattern. Most of the marks sit in the setting up and the interpretation. A student who is given the equations and asked only to solve them has not practised Criterion D.

Good preparation uses a small set of contexts again and again, with variation: change the numbers, change the question, ask what happens if an assumption changes. That is how a student learns to see the mathematics beneath the story. ## How Should MYP 5 Students Handle the Optional eAssessment?

MYP 5 students should treat the optional eAssessment as a format to be familiar with, not a reason to change how they learn mathematics. MYP eAssessment in MYP 5, made up of on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios, is optional, and each school decides whether to offer it. There is no single national MYP examination in India, so many students never sit it.

If your child's school offers it, the useful preparation is practical: timed practice in the on-screen format, reading questions carefully, structured responses and a routine for checking. The underlying mathematics still decides most of the outcome. A student who is secure in algebra, functions and reasoning will adapt to a screen quickly, while a student who relies on memorised templates may be unsettled by any new format. ## Which Plan Fits MYP 4 and Which Fits MYP 5?

The plans differ because the years do. MYP 4 is for connecting topics and settling the algebra foundations; MYP 5 is for independence, long problems and preparing for the next stage. The same tutor can carry a student across both, which is often the right design.

:::timeline MYP 4 to DP1 in broad strokes
MYP 4 term 1 | Diagnostic and algebra repair
MYP 4 terms 2-3 | Functions, geometry links and investigations
MYP 5 term 1 | Longer problems, unfamiliar contexts
MYP 5 terms 2-3 | Timed practice, the AA or AI conversation
Before DP1 | A short bridge block on algebra and functions
:::

### MYP 4 priorities

In MYP 4, the priorities are to make algebra automatic, to build the habit of writing reasoning and to begin the conversation about standard or extended. Short, regular sessions work well: one hour a week, with a short piece of independent work between sessions. A student who arrives in MYP 5 with secure algebra and a habit of explaining has done most of the work.

### MYP 5 priorities

In MYP 5, the priorities shift to independence. The student should be choosing the method, not waiting for a prompt. Work moves to longer problems, unfamiliar contexts and timed sections, and the tutor's prompts become fewer. If the school offers eAssessment, a few weeks of format practice are added. In the second half of the year, the DP preparation begins, and the subject of AA or AI moves from background to a live question. ## When Does a Student Not Need Tutoring?

A student does not need tutoring when marks are steady, the student can explain their own working and the school gives specific feedback on criteria. Tutoring costs money and evenings, and a student who is on track gains little from extra sessions.

Signs that support is probably unnecessary: the student's last two reports show consistent criterion marks, they can redo a missed question after reading the teacher's comment, and they report that they understand the lessons. In that case, a short diagnostic before MYP 5 or before DP1 may be enough, and ongoing weekly sessions may be better saved for a later pressure point.

Signs that support may help: a drop in marks that the family cannot explain, a pattern of losing marks on one criterion, a gap surfacing after a change of school or board, or a student who is clearly stretched in extended maths. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should MYP maths tutoring start?

Start when you can name a specific problem, ideally early in MYP 4, not after a poor report. A short diagnostic in the first term of MYP 4 shows whether algebra foundations are solid and whether the student can handle unfamiliar problems. If everything is on track, wait and review at the end of the term. Waiting until the final term of MYP 5 leaves little time to repair gaps before DP.

### Is extended mathematics necessary for DP AA?

Not strictly, but it is the usual preparation. Extended MYP mathematics covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches. A student who took standard mathematics can still do well in AA, and often needs a bridge block on algebra and functions before DP1. The school's DP coordinator makes the final placement, so ask them early what they expect.

### How do I know if my child is struggling with concepts or exam technique?

A short diagnostic shows it. Give mixed, unfamiliar problems without help and ask the student to explain their working aloud. If the explanation is shaky, the issue is concepts. If the explanation is fine but marks are lost to presentation, rounding or timing, the issue is technique. Many students have a mix, which is why the plan should target the largest cause first.

### Will tutoring raise my child's final MYP grade?

It may help, but no tutor can guarantee a grade. The final 1 to 7 grade comes from the criterion total out of 32 through IB grade boundaries, and it reflects the student's work and the school's assessment. What tutoring can do is remove specific gaps, improve how reasoning is written and build independence. Judge it by evidence after four to six weeks, not by promises.

### Does my child need a tutor who knows our school?

Not necessarily. Pathways, Heritage and other schools set their own unit plans and tasks, and a tutor who knows the MYP criteria well can adapt to any of them. What matters more is that the tutor understands criterion-referenced marking and can work from the child's actual school tasks and feedback. Ask the tutor how they would use your child's recent assessments in the first sessions.

### Can online lessons work for MYP 4 and MYP 5 maths?

Yes, particularly with a pen tablet and shared whiteboard, which let the tutor see working as it is written. Online suits students whose best-fit tutor is not nearby and busy assessment periods. Younger students who need structure may do better at home, and many families use a hybrid. A demo session is a good way to test which format your child concentrates in.

### What should we do about the AA versus AI decision now?

Treat it as information gathering, not a decision. Note which kind of work your child enjoys and does well: algebra and proof-style reasoning point towards AA, data, modelling and technology towards AI. Check the target degree's requirements if there is one, and ask the school coordinator when the choice is made. The decision is usually finalised at the end of MYP 5 or the start of DP1.

### How many hours a week is reasonable?

For most MYP 4 and MYP 5 students, one tutoring session of 60 to 90 minutes a week plus about two short independent sessions is plenty. More is not automatically better, and a student who is already stretched by other commitments may gain more from a lighter, consistent routine. Review the load every month and reduce it if the student is tired.

### Can the tutor help with the Personal Project?

A tutor can help a student plan time and understand criteria, but cannot write or edit the work. The Personal Project comes in MYP 5 and is the student's own work. If the project has a mathematical angle, a tutor can help the student think through the mathematics and check reasoning, within the school's rules on support. Ask the school what help is permitted.

### What if my child does not get on with the tutor?

Say so early and ask for a different match. A poor fit is common and nobody's fault. Give the tutor two or three sessions with specific feedback about pace or style. If your child still leaves confused or tense, a change is the right decision. Staying in an arrangement that is not working for months is more costly than switching.
