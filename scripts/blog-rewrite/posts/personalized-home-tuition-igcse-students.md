Personalised home tuition for IGCSE students is tuition in which the plan is built from evidence about the individual student and rewritten when new evidence arrives. Being taught one-to-one, at home, by a kind tutor is a format. It becomes personalised only when someone has worked out what this particular student is losing marks on, and the next lesson is different because of it.

This post is for parents of students in Grades 9 and 10 following Cambridge IGCSE (Cambridge International Education) or Pearson Edexcel International GCSE (IGCSE), and for students who are weighing whether a home tutor is worth it. It explains what happens in the first week, how a tutor decides between the Core and Extended tiers, how the subject mix is chosen when a student has seven or eight subjects and limited hours, and how a good tutor adjusts from week to week. It leaves out the six-week revision timetable (see [the six-week IGCSE revision plan](/blog/igcse-revision-six-week-plan/)) and the do-it-yourself version of personalisation (see [personalised learning in IGCSE](/blog/personalized-learning-igcse-education/)). The focus here is the *service*: what you should see a tutor do, and how to tell if they are not.

Nothing here is a promise of a grade, and no tutor can guarantee one. What good personalisation changes is how fast the real causes of lost marks are found and removed.

## What Does "Personalised" Really Mean in IGCSE Tuition?

Personalised means that four things about the plan are specific to the student: the starting point, the target, the constraints and the pace. If a tutor cannot tell you what each of those is for your child, the tuition is generic whatever it is called.

- **Starting point.** Not "Grade 9, Cambridge Maths", but "solid on number and graphs, loses marks on algebraic manipulation, reads word problems too fast".
- **Target.** A grade in each subject that is realistic for the tier and the school's expectations, with the next school or programme in view, such as the IB Diploma Programme (DP), A Levels or a move to Class 11 at an Indian board.
- **Constraints.** School timetable, other subjects, sports, travel, and how much the student can absorb on a weekday evening.
- **Pace.** How fast the student consolidates, which is usually different for different subjects.

Many families expect that personalisation is about learning styles, with some students visual and others auditory. The research on matching teaching to styles is not convincing, and a good tutor does not build a plan on it. What does matter is far more mundane: what the student already knows, what they misread, and what they avoid.

## How Does a Good IGCSE Home Tutor Diagnose a Student?

A good tutor diagnoses with the student's own work, not with a chat. The most reliable inputs are a recent school test or mock, a past paper section done under mild time pressure with the tutor watching, and a short conversation in which the student explains how they solved a problem.

A typical first week might use three short pieces of evidence.

1. **A marked paper from school.** The tutor sorts every lost mark into knowledge (did not know it), method (knew it but set it up wrongly), accuracy (slips) and communication (answer right, expression poor).
2. **A timed section of a past paper**, fifteen to twenty-five minutes, with the tutor silent. Hesitation shows what a marked script cannot.
3. **A five-minute explanation.** The student teaches the tutor one topic they believe they know. Gaps in understanding show up quickly.

:::steps The first-week diagnostic
Collect | Latest school test and mock papers
Sort | Each lost mark by cause, not by topic
Observe | A timed section with the tutor silent
Explain | Student teaches one topic back
Plan | Written priorities and a first month
:::

The output should be something you can read: a short note of three to five priorities, each tied to evidence ("loses about a third of the available marks in structured questions on rearranging formulae, because the setup is correct and the final step is dropped"). If you receive only reassurance, ask for the note.

### What a diagnostic is not

It is not a long entrance test designed to impress. It is not a general "assessment" whose results are filed and forgotten. And it is not a way to decide the tutor's hours. The purpose is to shape the first four weeks, and it should be repeated lightly each month.

## Core or Extended, Foundation or Higher: Who Decides and How?

The school decides the entry tier, but the tutor can give useful evidence and the family can ask informed questions. Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers: Core tops out at grade C, Extended reaches A*. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher.

For Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams), Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator); Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Cambridge IGCSE sciences have a similar pattern: Paper 1 multiple choice (Core) or Paper 2 multiple choice (Extended), and Paper 3 theory (Core) or Paper 4 theory (Extended).

:::stats What the tier changes
C | Highest grade from Core papers
A* | Reachable only through Extended
2 | Calculator and non-calculator papers in 0580 per tier
:::

A tier decision is not about ambition alone. A student placed in Core cannot earn above a C however well they perform, and a student placed in Extended without the algebra may find both papers punishing. The honest tutor reasoning looks like this:

| Evidence | Points towards | Caution |
|---|---|---|
| Consistent school marks in the A range on Extended-style questions | Extended | Check non-calculator accuracy |
| Strong on concepts but weak on algebra and speed | Discuss with school; Extended may still be right with targeted repair | Do not drop the tier to protect confidence without a reason |
| Marks stuck near the pass line on topics that Core covers well | Core may suit, and a good C beats a poor Extended result | Check the next school's entry requirement first |
| Student wants IB DP Mathematics AA HL later | Extended, usually with Additional Mathematics | Ask the school about its own pathway |

The tutor should never move the tier themselves; the school enters the student. What a tutor can do is tell you honestly, after four to six weeks of work, whether the student's evidence matches the tier, and then support a conversation with the school. A tutor who always says Extended, or always says Core, is not looking at your child. For the maths-specific version of this decision and the Additional Mathematics question, see [IGCSE Maths 0580 and 0606 for parents](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/).

## How Is the Subject Mix Decided?

A student with seven or eight IGCSE subjects cannot be tutored in all of them, and should not be. A tutor and family decide the subject mix by asking where tutoring hours will move marks the most.

Three criteria help.

**Where the gap is repairable.** A subject where marks are lost to a specific skill gap (rearranging formulae, balancing equations, writing for an audience) is a better candidate than one where the student is simply uninterested.

**Where the subject compounds.** Mathematics underpins the sciences and Economics. A weak algebra base harms Physics calculations and Chemistry stoichiometry as well. Repairing it once improves several subjects.

**Where the next step demands it.** A student aiming for the IB Diploma in Sciences needs sound Maths and a science. A student aiming for Economics at DP needs data-handling and extended writing.

| Student profile | Typical priority | Often left to school alone |
|---|---|---|
| Heading to IB DP Maths AA HL | Extended Maths, Additional Maths, one science | Subjects with steady school marks |
| Strong on science, weak on writing | English or Economics essay skills, then science papers | Maths if marks are secure |
| Even performance, tired by volume | Two subjects only, rotated by term | Everything else |
| Late joiner from another board | Foundations in Maths and English first | Subjects the old board covered well |

It is usual for the mix to change during the year. A student may start with Mathematics and Physics, drop Physics for a term when it stabilises, and add English before the mocks. The plan has to be reviewed, not just continued. ## What Does a Personalised Week Look Like?

A personalised week has a fixed shape and a variable content. The shape stays the same so the student knows what to expect; the content shifts according to what the last week revealed.

:::timeline One tutoring week, Maths and Science
Monday | Review last week's error log, 10 minutes
Midweek lesson | One new idea, then supervised practice
Between lessons | 3 short sets, set by the tutor
Weekend | One timed section, marked by tutor next lesson
:::

A typical ninety-minute lesson might be split like this.

1. **Ten minutes of retrieval**: the tutor asks three questions from last week's topics without notes.
2. **Thirty minutes of teaching on the target gap**, built from the diagnostic and last week's errors.
3. **Thirty minutes of supervised practice** in which the student writes full working and the tutor watches and does not hint unless asked.
4. **Fifteen minutes of marking and error-logging** against the mark scheme, so the student sees what earned marks.
5. **Five minutes on the plan**: what to practise before next time and why.

The *adjustment* happens in step two. If last week's practice shows the student now handles one type of question, the lesson does not repeat it. If a new error appeared, it replaces something else. A tutor who teaches the same chapter order each week regardless is following a syllabus, not the student.

### How a tutor adjusts when something unexpected happens

- **A poor school test.** The tutor looks at the paper before changing anything. One bad score may be a bad day or a topic not yet taught.
- **A topic the school has just covered badly.** The lesson pivots to consolidate it, then returns to the plan.
- **A student who is tired or anxious.** Shorter lessons, retrieval over new content, and honest conversation. Pushing harder on an exhausted student rarely pays.
- **A student who suddenly finds a subject easy.** Move the time elsewhere.

## Why Do Some Home Tutors Fail to Personalise?

Because personalisation is work, and the easy alternatives look similar from outside. The common failure patterns are worth recognising.

| Pattern | What it looks like | What a parent notices |
|---|---|---|
| Homework helper | The tutor completes the school's homework with the student | Homework is done; marks do not move |
| Chapter pusher | Tutor follows the textbook in order | Weak areas are skipped; strong ones repeated |
| Past-paper dumper | Paper after paper, little review | Scores vary; the same errors recur |
| Pleaser | Tutor praises and avoids hard feedback | Student enjoys lessons; confidence outruns results |
| Style matcher | Plans around "visual learner" labels | Nice slides; nothing measured |

None of these requires a bad person. They are the default behaviours of an under-prepared tutor, and a family can test for them in the trial lesson by asking: "What will you do differently next week if this lesson goes well? And if it goes badly?" A tutor with a real plan answers specifically.

## What Does Home Tuition Add That School and Coaching Do Not?

Personalised home tuition for IGCSE students adds attention to one student's actual errors, at a pace set by that student. School classes move at the class's pace. Coaching centres teach groups. Neither can watch your child solve a problem and stop the moment the setup goes wrong.

It does not add everything. A student who needs peer discussion, laboratory equipment or live debate gets those at school. Practical papers in the sciences, assessed through Paper 5 (practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), depend on school laboratory access and on whether the school chose the practical or the alternative, and a home tutor can prepare the student for the written style but cannot replace the laboratory. A tutor should say so plainly.

## How Do Cambridge and Edexcel Change the Plan?

They change it in paper structure, tiering and timing, and a tutor must know which board the student sits. Cambridge IGCSE grades most syllabuses A*-G, and some syllabuses have 9-1 versions under different codes. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE grades 9-1. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June.

The February/March series exists specifically for India and lets Grade 10 results arrive in time for Indian school admissions to Class 11 and for IB DP or A Level entry in the same academic year. Schools decide which series they enter students for; a student cannot choose a series independently of the school. Ask the school which series applies before a tutor builds the calendar.

Practical consequences for personalisation:

- A tutor working back from a February/March series has a shorter DP-style run-up, so revision starts earlier in the academic year.
- Cambridge Mathematics 0580 and Edexcel 4MA1 have different paper structures; do not use one board's papers to prepare for the other without adjustment.
- Additional Mathematics (Cambridge 0606) has no tiers, two written papers with a calculator allowed, and is graded A*-E; it is usually taken alongside 0580 Extended by students heading for IB Mathematics AA HL or A Level Mathematics. Pearson Edexcel's nearest equivalent is International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics (4PM1).

If your child is likely to continue to the IB, this planning interacts with the transition; the maths side is covered in [from IGCSE Mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/).

## What Is a Realistic First Month With a Personal IGCSE Tutor?

A realistic first month of personalised home tuition for IGCSE students produces clarity, not a jump in grades. By the end of four weeks you should have a diagnosis, a written plan, early signs of fewer repeated errors and a decision about whether the fit is right.

| Week | What should happen | What you should see |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Diagnostic from school work and a timed section | A written note of priorities |
| 2 | First targeted teaching and supervised practice | Student explaining a topic back correctly |
| 3 | First timed section marked against the scheme | An error log with categories |
| 4 | Review: what moved, what did not | Plan adjusted; a recommendation on pace and subject mix |

If at week four you have no error log, no note, and the lessons look like homework help, ask for a change. IB Gram lets families request a different match, subject to tutor availability.

## Illustrative Scenarios

These are typical patterns, not real students.

**A Grade 9 student who is "good at maths" but loses marks.** The diagnostic shows correct methods but dropped signs and unstated steps. The plan is not more content; it is a habit of writing every line, checked by the tutor, and a weekly timed section. Marks recover because the errors, not the knowledge, were the problem.

**A Grade 10 student with six weeks to the series and uneven subjects.** The tutor does not try to cover everything. Two subjects get most of the hours, the rest follow a light retrieval routine at home, and the weekly adjustment follows the mock results.

**A student who moved from CBSE in Grade 9.** The gap is in command words and extended writing, not content. The tutor spends the first month on how IGCSE questions are worded and marked, and on the habit of showing working.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is personalised home tuition for IGCSE students?

It is one-to-one tuition at home in which the plan is built from the student's own marked work and changed as new evidence appears. The format is home-based, but the personalisation is in the diagnosis and the weekly adjustment.

If a tutor teaches the same chapters in the same order to every student, the tuition is individual but not personalised.

### How do I know if my child needs a tutor at all?

Your child probably needs a tutor if marks are lost repeatedly for reasons neither the student nor the school has identified, or if one subject is blocking others.

If grades are steady, the student understands their errors and school feedback is useful, a tutor may add cost without adding much. A free consultation can help you decide without commitment.

### Should my child be in Core or Extended?

The school decides, but your child's evidence should guide the discussion. Core grades top out at C; Extended reaches A*.

A tutor can give a view after a few weeks of work. A student heading for IB Mathematics AA HL or A Level Mathematics is usually better in Extended with Additional Mathematics, subject to the school's advice.

### How many subjects should a home tutor cover?

Usually one or two at a time. Tutoring spread across five subjects is thin everywhere and exhausting for the student.

Choose subjects by where marks are repairable, where the subject compounds (Maths into the sciences) and what the next stage demands. Review the mix each term.

### Is home tuition better than online tuition for IGCSE?

Neither is better in general. Home lessons suit students who work well on paper with a tutor beside them, while online lessons save travel and work for families abroad.

Many families combine them. Practical science is a limit on both, because the practical test or alternative to practical depends on school laboratory work.

### My child has had a tutor for a month and nothing has changed. What should I do?

Ask for the error log and the written plan. A month is enough to see whether lost marks are being sorted and addressed, even if the grade has not moved.

If neither exists, request a different match. Poor fit is common and does not mean your child cannot improve.

### How is IGCSE tuition different from CBSE or ICSE tuition?

IGCSE marks depend heavily on command words, shown working and application, with less emphasis on memorised answers.

A student switching boards often needs a month on how questions are worded and marked before content work pays off. See the board-switch advice in our [IGCSE guides](/blog/igcse-exam-preparation-guide/).

### Can a tutor help with IGCSE practical papers at home?

A tutor can teach the written skills for Paper 5 or Paper 6: planning an investigation, handling data, drawing graphs and evaluating methods.

A tutor cannot replace laboratory practice. Which paper a student sits is chosen by the school, so ask the school first.

### Will the tutor follow my child's school or their own plan?

A good tutor does both: the school calendar sets deadlines and topics, while the tutor's plan decides emphasis and method.

Share the school's term plan and test dates at the start. Where the school covers a topic poorly, the tutor may consolidate it before the next test.

### How soon should I expect to see results?

Expect clarity within two to four weeks and measurable changes in error patterns within a term. Grade changes depend on the starting point, the effort between lessons and the exam series.

No tutor can guarantee a grade. Judge the early signs: a written diagnosis, fewer repeated mistakes and a student who can explain their own errors.
