In MYP 1 and 2 the job is usually to build number sense, algebra confidence and the habit of explaining thinking. ## What Does an IB MYP Maths Tutor in Gurgaon Actually Do?

An IB MYP maths tutor teaches the mathematics the school is covering, and trains the four ways the MYP assesses it. In MYP Mathematics every student is marked against four criteria: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts. Each criterion is scored 0 to 8, the criterion total out of 32 converts to a final grade from 1 to 7 through IB grade boundaries, and teachers award levels on tasks through the year. A tutor therefore has two jobs at once: strengthening the concept, and showing the student how that concept appears in an investigation, a written explanation or a real-world task.

That second job is what separates specialist MYP support from a general maths tutor. A student can be fluent with equations and still lose marks on Criterion B because the task asks for a conjecture and a generalisation, or on Criterion C because the reasoning cannot be followed. A good tutor listens for which criterion is costing the student most, then builds a plan around that criterion.

:::stats MYP Mathematics at a glance
4 | Criteria, A to D
0-8 | Score range per criterion
32 | Maximum criterion total
1-7 | Final MYP grade scale
:::

A word about what a tutor does not do. There is no single national MYP examination in India. Assessment is mostly school-set tasks and tests, marked by the teacher against the criteria, with standardisation inside the school. In MYP 5, MYP eAssessment (on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios) is optional, and each school decides whether to offer it. So a tutor should ask the school's own task descriptions, rubrics and teacher comments, not assume a fixed external exam. For the on-screen format, see [MYP 5 mathematics eAssessment preparation](/blog/ib-myp-5-mathematics-eassessment-preparation-pathways-heritage-gurgaon/).

## How Does MYP Maths Support Change from Year 1 to Year 5?

Support changes because the programme changes. MYP is five years, roughly ages 11 to 16, across eight subject groups. Maths in the earlier years is more exploratory and concept-led; by MYP 4 and 5, many schools split students into standard and extended mathematics, and the work begins to look ahead to DP. Schools sequence topics differently, so the table is a typical shape, not a syllabus. Always check your school's scope and sequence.

| MYP year | Typical focus of support | What a tutor watches for |
|---|---|---|
| MYP 1 | Number sense, fractions, early algebra, explaining thinking | Gaps from primary years; fear of "word" questions |
| MYP 2 | Equations, ratio, geometry, first patterns | Procedure without understanding; weak written reasoning |
| MYP 3 | Linear relationships, statistics, patterns and generalising | Criterion B struggle; first modelling tasks |
| MYP 4 | Algebra depth, trigonometry, standard vs extended decision | Pace jump; extended readiness |
| MYP 5 | Consolidation, assessment readiness, DP runway | Exam conditions; stamina; AA or AI direction |

Treat the table as a conversation starter with the school. A student in MYP 2 whose school has already introduced quadratic ideas is not "ahead" or "behind"; the school's order differs. A tutor should ask what has been taught and what is coming, then support the next unit.

:::timeline MYP maths support across the five years

MYP 5 | Assessment readiness, DP runway
:::

## What Does an MYP 1 and MYP 2 Maths Student Need?

MYP 1 and 2 students need a secure base and a good habit of explaining their reasoning. Many children arrive from primary years with uneven fluency: confident with whole numbers, shaky with fractions, unsure about negative numbers, and uncomfortable when a question is wrapped in a story. In these years the tutor's job is less about acceleration and more about making the basics automatic and the language of mathematics familiar.

Typical sessions look like short, active work: a concept explained with a picture or a number line, a handful of questions, then the student explaining a solution aloud or in writing. The goal is not speed. It is a student who can say why a method works, which will matter in every later year. Criterion C, communicating, begins here: a child who writes "I did 3 times 4" has said less than one who writes "there are 3 groups of 4, so 12".

Parents of younger students often ask whether tutoring is necessary at all. Often it is not. A child who is keeping up, enjoys maths and talks about it at home may need nothing beyond curiosity. A tutor helps when a child avoids maths, has had a bad run of tests or is losing confidence. Early support, kept light, can prevent the larger gap that appears in MYP 3 and 4. If the issue is the difference between MYP and a board your child came from, the post on [IB MYP maths vs CBSE maths](/blog/ib-myp-maths-vs-cbse-maths-teaching-approach/) explains the difference in teaching approach.

## What Changes in MYP 3?

MYP 3 is where patterns and generalising come to the front. Students are asked to look at a sequence or a set of results, say what they notice, propose a rule and test it. This is Criterion B, and it is often the criterion students find strangest, because there is no obvious method to apply. A student who is used to being told how to solve a problem may stall when asked to explore.

A tutor helps by teaching the investigation routine: record results in a table, look at differences and ratios, state a conjecture in words, test it on new cases, write the rule in symbols and say where it might fail. The student learns that not knowing the answer at the start is normal. For the reasoning skills in detail, see [IB MYP Mathematics reasoning skills](/blog/ib-myp-mathematics-problem-solving-reasoning-skills/).

MYP 3 is also the first year many students meet extended real-life tasks, which test Criterion D. These ask a student to build a mathematical model of a situation, such as a phone plan, a garden design or a sports data set, solve it and judge whether the answer makes sense. The mathematics may be modest, but the interpretation is new. A tutor should practise reading a context, naming what is given and what is asked and checking an answer against reality.

Some schools place the Community Project in MYP 3 or 4. It is not a maths task, but it demands time and planning, and a tutoring schedule should leave room for it.

## How Do Standard and Extended Mathematics Differ in MYP 4 and 5?

Standard and extended mathematics are the two pathways many schools offer in MYP 4 and 5. Extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA), and standard covers the core course. Schools often decide the placement, usually on the basis of performance, teacher recommendation and the family's intentions, and the arrangement differs from school to school.

The practical meaning for a student:

- **Standard** gives a solid foundation with a lighter load, and it suits students for whom maths is one subject among several or who are heading towards a DP course such as Applications and Interpretation (AI) or a humanities-leaning route.
- **Extended** adds depth in algebra, functions and trigonometry, and moves faster. It suits students who enjoy symbolic work and who may take DP Mathematics AA, particularly at Higher Level (HL).

Neither is better in general, and the choice is one to make with the school. A tutor can help in two ways. First, by assessing readiness honestly: can the student handle the extended pace, or would they be better served by mastering standard? Second, by bridging gaps if a student moves into extended late. Do not push a student into extended solely for status; a struggling extended student often loses confidence, and a confident standard student has a good DP runway. The details of the MYP 4 and 5 topic map and the DP choice are in [MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics tutoring](/blog/ib-myp-4-myp-5-mathematics-tutoring-gurgaon-pathways-heritage/).

### What about the jump to DP?

DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each available at HL and SL: AA, which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and AI, which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and the student, and the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement. The comparison is in [IB Maths AA vs AI](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/). A tutor who knows both MYP and DP can plan MYP 4 and 5 so that the student arrives at DP1 with the right tools; the flagship guide on [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/) shows what the destination demands.

## What Does MYP 5 Maths Support Look Like?

MYP 5 support is about consolidation and readiness. By this year a student has met most of the content, and what matters is performance under assessment conditions: clear written reasoning, accurate working, the ability to handle an unfamiliar problem and the stamina to complete a long task. Where the school offers MYP eAssessment, the student also needs comfort with on-screen tools and the format. Where it does not, the same skills apply to the school's own end-of-year assessments.

A typical MYP 5 plan has four strands. A diagnostic against the four criteria, based on the student's recent tasks and teacher comments. Targeted repair on the one or two criteria where levels are lowest. Timed practice on unfamiliar problems, with marking against the criterion descriptors. And a gradual introduction to DP-style questions, so that the first DP1 test is not a shock.

This is also the year of the Personal Project, and students vary widely in how much time it takes. A tutor should not take over the project, and should not schedule heavy sessions at its deadline. The related plan for lifting a grade is in [how to move from a 5 to a 7 in MYP maths](/blog/how-can-an-ib-myp-maths-student-move-from-5-to-6-or-7/), which explains the criterion totals behind each final grade and which criterion moves fastest.

## How Do Criterion Levels Turn Into a Final MYP Grade?

The final grade is a conversion, and families who understand it ask better questions. Each criterion is scored 0 to 8, so the four sum to a maximum of 32. The school then converts the total to a final 1 to 7 grade using IB grade boundaries, and the exact boundaries are published by the IB for the programme, not set by the tutor or the family. A tutor cannot change the conversion, but they can show where a student's marks are being lost.

The useful insight is that the criteria are uneven in how easy they are to move. A student who is weak on Criterion C (communicating) can often gain several levels in weeks by learning to lay out reasoning, because the content is already understood. A student weak on Criterion B (investigating patterns) usually needs longer, because it is a way of thinking. Criterion D needs exposure to contexts. Criterion A responds to ordinary practice and concept repair.

:::bars Which criterion tends to move fastest (a judgement, not an official figure)
C Communicating | 85 | often quickest
A Knowing and understanding | 70 | steady with practice
D Real-life applications | 55 | needs exposure to contexts
B Investigating patterns | 45 | a habit of mind, slower
These are typical tendencies from tutoring experience; every student differs, so diagnose first.
:::

## How Does a Tutor Work With a Student From Pathways or Heritage?

A tutor works with a student from any IB school by starting with that school's own materials. Schools such as Pathways and Heritage are named here as context because many Gurgaon families ask about them; this guide does not say what either school teaches or assesses and implies no relationship with either. What a tutor does is ask for the unit plan, the task sheets, the rubrics and the teacher's comments, and then build support around those.

A few practical habits make this work. Bring the latest marked task, with the teacher's criterion levels, to the first session. Share the school's term calendar, so that the tutor can see when summative tasks fall. Tell the tutor which topics are coming. And ask the tutor to align terminology with the school: if the school calls a skill "generalising" and the tutor calls it "finding a rule", the student gets mixed signals.

Support should complement the school, not compete with it. If the teacher uses a particular way of setting out an investigation, the tutor should reinforce it. If the student receives feedback, the tutor should help them act on it. A tutor who undermines the teacher confuses the child.

## How Does Ajay Vatsyayan Approach MYP Maths?

Ajay Vatsyayan is IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, and his academic focus covers MYP Mathematics, including MYP standard and extended, and DP Mathematics AA and AI. His teaching philosophy is built around conceptual clarity: the student is encouraged to understand the mathematical idea first, so that different versions of a problem can be solved without a new formula each time.

Four stages describe the approach in a session. Concept, where the student identifies relationships and patterns. Application, where the concept is used in unfamiliar and real-life situations. Expression, where the student communicates the reasoning clearly. And analysis of errors, so that each mistake is traced to a cause: misunderstanding, wrong method, calculation, misreading, incomplete reasoning, uninterpreted answer or time. The session is not a race through questions; it begins with the concept, checks that the student really understands it, and then moves to practice.

Before any plan is made, there is a diagnosis. A low test score does not necessarily mean a whole chapter is weak: sometimes it is one missing concept, sometimes an application issue, sometimes a student who knows the mathematics but cannot communicate it, and sometimes a student who needs confidence with unfamiliar questions. Progress is reviewed regularly against concept understanding, problem solving, pattern investigation, real-life application, communication, accuracy, time management and confidence.

## What Should a Good MYP Maths Session Look Like?

A good session has a shape the parent can recognise. It starts with the student's real work: a marked task, a homework problem or a topic currently in class. The tutor asks questions to find out what the student understands, not only what they got right. Then comes a short explanation or demonstration, followed by the student doing something: solving, explaining, writing. The tutor marks with the criterion in mind and names the cause of any lost level. The session closes with one or two tasks for the week and a note of what to bring next time.

The numbered sequence below is useful to hold in mind, and to check against what you see.

1. **Start from the student's own work**, such as the last task or the current unit.
2. **Check the concept**: ask the student to explain, not just calculate.
3. **Practise on a fresh problem**, with the student writing and the tutor watching.
4. **Mark against the criteria** and name the cause of each gap.
5. **Set one or two specific tasks** for the week, not a pile of worksheets.
6. **Review at the next session**, before starting anything new.

Warning signs include a session in which the tutor does most of the writing, a plan that is the same for every student and a stack of unmarked worksheets. A tutor should also be comfortable saying what cannot be fixed in a session and what depends on practice at home.

## When Is a Tutor Not Needed?

A tutor is not needed when the student is keeping up, understands the concepts and enjoys the subject. A tutor is also not the answer when the real issue is workload, sleep or a difficulty with the school's teaching that a conversation with the teacher could solve. A tutor helps most when there is a specific, diagnosable gap, when confidence has fallen, when a transition is coming or when a student wants challenge beyond the class.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does my child need an IB MYP maths tutor in Gurgaon?

Not necessarily. If your child understands the concepts, completes tasks with reasonable confidence and enjoys maths, a tutor may add little. A tutor helps when there is a specific gap, a drop in confidence, a transition such as MYP 4 to extended or MYP 5 to DP, or a wish for challenge. A trial session will show whether the help is useful.

### Is MYP maths tutoring different from regular maths tutoring?

Yes, because MYP assessment is criterion-based, not a single exam. A tutor has to teach the concepts and also train investigation, communication and real-life application. A general maths tutor may explain the syllabus well but not know how a task is marked against criteria A to D. Ask a tutor how they would work on Criterion B or D.

### At what age or year should we start?

Start when there is a need, not at a fixed year. MYP 1 and 2 support is usually light and aimed at confidence and fluency, MYP 3 and 4 support addresses patterns, modelling and the standard-or-extended decision, and MYP 5 focuses on readiness. Starting early is useful if a child is avoiding maths, but it is rarely necessary otherwise.

### Should my child take standard or extended mathematics?

Follow the school's guidance and your child's profile. Extended adds topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics AA, while standard covers the core course. Extended suits students who enjoy symbolic work and may take AA at DP; standard suits students who prefer a steadier pace or are heading towards AI. The school usually decides placement, so ask the teacher for evidence.

### Is online tutoring good enough for MYP maths?

Yes, if both sides can write. A shared digital whiteboard or a tablet with a stylus makes online sessions effective, and photographed written work can be reviewed afterwards. Younger students who lose focus on screens may do better with a home tutor. Many families use a hybrid. Try a format for a few weeks and change it if it is not working.

### What if my child studies at Pathways or Heritage?

The tutor should work from the school's own task sheets, rubrics and teacher comments. This guide names those schools as context only and states nothing about what they teach or assess. Bring the latest marked task and the term calendar to the first session, and ask the tutor to match the school's terminology and layout.

### Will a tutor improve my child's final MYP grade?

A tutor can help a student understand concepts, communicate better and tackle unfamiliar tasks, which usually shows in criterion levels over time. No tutor can guarantee a grade. The final grade comes from the school's criterion levels converted through IB boundaries, and it depends on the student's practice between sessions as well as the tutoring.

### How long before we see a difference?

Communication and layout habits can improve within a few weeks, because the mathematics is already understood. Pattern investigation and real-life modelling usually take longer, since they are ways of thinking. Review after a month: the error pattern, the student's own explanations and the next task's criterion levels should show whether the approach is working. If they do not, ask for a different plan or match.

### How is MYP 5 different, and is eAssessment compulsory?

MYP 5 is about consolidation and readiness for DP. MYP eAssessment, on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios, is optional, and each school decides whether to offer it, so ask your school what applies to your child. Whether or not it is offered, the skills are the same: clear reasoning, accuracy and handling unfamiliar problems under time pressure.

### What if the first tutor is not a good fit?

Say so early and ask for a different match. IB Gram lets families ask for a different match and can arrange a demo session before committing. Before switching, check whether the issue is the tutor, the schedule or something else such as workload. A good tutor will welcome a review conversation after the first month.
