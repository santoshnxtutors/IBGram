There is no single number for IB maths private tutor fees in India, and any page that gives you one is quoting a tutor, not a market. Fees move with the programme (Middle Years Programme or Diploma Programme), the level (Higher or Standard), the tutor's depth, the city, whether the lesson is at home or online, and how much unpaid work the tutor does between sessions. This guide explains each of those levers so you can read any quote, from any tutor, and know what you are being asked to pay for.

It is written for parents of International Baccalaureate (IB) students in MYP 3 to 5 and Diploma Programme (DP) Years 1 and 2, in Gurgaon, Delhi, Noida, Mumbai, Bengaluru or anywhere else in India, and for families abroad who are comparing Indian-based tutors with local ones. It does not list rupee amounts. Prices in this market are negotiated one family at a time, change every year, and are easy to misquote. What lasts is the method: what to compare, what to ask, and what a good hour of IB maths looks like when you sit in on it.

Two things before we start. A higher fee is not evidence of better teaching, and a lower fee is not evidence of a bargain. And the first question is never "what does it cost?" but "what exactly is my child missing, and which kind of tutor fixes that?" The cost question only makes sense after that one.

## Why Is There No Standard Fee for IB Maths Tutoring?

There is no standard fee because nobody sets one. The International Baccalaureate does not regulate private tutoring, there is no association rate card, and tutors range from a university student who took IB maths three years ago to a former head of department who now teaches a few families. Each prices according to what they can offer and how many hours they have free.

The market is also split into very different products that share one name. "IB maths tutor" can mean a homework-helper who sits with the textbook, a retired teacher who runs a structured weekly programme, an examiner-style marker who does nothing but timed papers, or a mentor who diagnoses, plans, teaches, sets work and reviews it. These are not the same service at different prices. They are different services.

That is why comparing two quotes line by line usually misleads. Two tutors can both say "one hour, one-to-one, IB maths" and be selling different things. The comparison that works is by deliverable: what the student will leave each session able to do, what happens between sessions, and what the family will see at the end of a term.

For the Gurgaon view, where fee drivers are discussed with local detail, see [what affects IB tutor fees in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-tutor-fees-in-gurgaon-what-affects-the-cost/). This page takes the India-wide, maths-only view.

## The Ten Levers That Move an IB Maths Tutor's Fee

Every quote for IB maths private tutor fees in India is the sum of roughly ten decisions. Knowing them lets you ask a tutor which ones apply to your child, instead of arguing about the total.

:::bars What usually drives the fee (illustrative weight of influence, not data)
Programme and level (MYP, SL, HL) | 85 | Large
Tutor's IB maths depth | 90 | Largest
Preparation and feedback outside the hour | 70 | Large
Home visit vs online | 55 | Moderate
City and neighbourhood | 50 | Moderate
Capacity and exam-season demand | 40 | Seasonal
This is a judgement about how much each lever tends to matter, not a statistic.
:::

### 1. Programme and level

MYP maths, DP Analysis and Approaches (AA) Standard Level, and AA Higher Level are three different jobs. MYP is assessed on four criteria, A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating and D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts, each scored 0 to 8. A tutor has to understand how a task is marked, not only how a quadratic is solved. DP Mathematics at HL carries 240 recommended teaching hours against 150 for SL, and AA HL in particular has proof, complex numbers and extended calculus that few tutors can teach well. Fewer capable tutors means higher fees, simply because supply is thin.

### 2. The tutor's real IB maths depth

This is the largest lever and the hardest to see from a profile. A tutor who has taught IB for years knows which mistakes cost marks, how a markscheme gives follow-through credit, and what a Paper 3 question is probing. A tutor who knows only school maths will cover the content but miss the assessment. You pay for the first kind of knowledge because it saves the student time.

### 3. Preparation and feedback outside the session

A tutor who walks in cold charges for one hour. A tutor who read last week's script, built a problem set around the exact error pattern and will mark the homework before the next session is doing perhaps two hours of work for one billed hour. Some build this into the hourly rate, some bill it separately, some do not do it at all. Whether it exists matters more than how it is billed.

### 4. Home visit versus online

Home tuition adds the tutor's travel time, which is unpaid time they could have used for another lesson. Online removes the travel but adds the need for a pen tablet and a graphics display calculator (GDC) shown on screen if the tutor is to be effective. Neither is cheaper by default. The online route is discussed in [how online IB tutoring works in India](/blog/online-ib-tutoring-india/).

### 5. City and neighbourhood

Rates in a city with many IB schools and many international families, such as Gurgaon, Mumbai and Bengaluru, tend to sit above those in a city with a handful of IB schools. Inside a city, gated societies far from the tutor's base add travel cost. Online lessons flatten that difference, which is why many families now hire a specialist from another city.

### 6. Session length and frequency

A 90-minute block is often priced slightly better per hour than a 60-minute one, because setup and review are shared. Twice-weekly lessons usually cost more in total but need less repeated revision. Weekly lessons cost less in total but have a longer gap during which the student forgets. The right rhythm depends on the student's stage, and [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/) covers timing in detail.

### 7. Tutor capacity

A tutor who is nearly full has no reason to discount and may raise rates for new students. A tutor building a practice may price lower. Neither tells you about quality. Ask how many students they take at a time; very high numbers can mean thin attention, very low numbers can mean a real specialist with limited slots.

### 8. Exam proximity

Demand spikes in the months before DP2 mock examinations and the May session. Tutors are fully booked, and new students often pay more or cannot get slots at all. Starting in DP1 or early DP2 costs less per useful hour because there is no scramble.

### 9. Package or pay-per-session

Packages of a month or a term usually reduce the per-hour figure and commit the tutor to a plan. Pay-as-you-go keeps flexibility. Be cautious of large prepayments with no refund terms, a point we return to below.

### 10. Group versus one-to-one

Small-group classes cost each family less but divide the attention. For a student whose problem is specific, one-to-one corrects it faster. For a student who mainly needs structure and company, a group can be enough. See [whether one-to-one IB coaching is worth it](/blog/1-to-1-ib-coaching-in-gurgaon-is-it-worth-it/) for the decision matrix.

## What Does an Hour of IB Maths Tutoring Actually Include?

In any discussion of IB maths private tutor fees in India, a paid hour should include the live teaching, and a good arrangement also includes the work around it: reading the student's recent school work, choosing what to teach, setting practice, marking it, and giving written or spoken feedback. If a tutor bills for the live hour alone and does none of the rest, that is fine, but you are paying for less than the word "tutoring" implies, and the fee should reflect that.

Here is what the unseen work looks like for one student over a week. The tutor reviews the most recent school test or timed paper and sorts the lost marks into causes. They pick the one or two causes that will repay the most effort. They write or select six to ten questions that target those causes and some unfamiliar ones that test transfer. After the session they mark what the student attempted, note what was done well and what recurred, and carry that into next week's plan. For a DP student this might take 40 to 90 minutes. For an MYP student working on an investigation, a similar amount.

:::steps What sits behind one billed hour
Diagnose | Read recent work, sort the lost marks
Plan | Choose the one or two gaps to close
Teach | The live session, mostly the student working
Set | Targeted problems for the week
Review | Mark and feed back before the next lesson
:::

None of this is visible in a rate quote, which is why the single most useful question you can ask is: "What do you do between sessions?" A clear, specific answer is worth a lot. A vague one is a signal.

## How Do IB Maths Private Tutor Fees in India Differ by Programme and Level?

They differ because each programme and level asks the tutor to do a different job, and the fee follows the scarcity of the skill and the preparation each stage needs. HL costs more to supply than SL, and an exam-term plan costs more than a steady DP1 rhythm.

The table below does not show prices. It shows the type of work involved at each stage, which is the real reason fees differ. Read it as: the more of the right-hand column a tutor must actually perform, the more of their time one student takes up.

| Stage | What the tutor must know | Typical extra work | Why it affects the fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| MYP 3 | Foundations; criterion language; building reasoning habits | Reviewing investigation write-ups | Many tutors can teach content, fewer can coach criteria B and C |
| MYP 4 to 5 | Standard versus extended pathway; bridge to DP | Unfamiliar problems; eAssessment practice if the school enters students | A bridge year; wrong habits now cost more later |
| DP Maths AI SL | Modelling, statistics, GDC fluency | GDC technique; IA guidance | Technology teaching is a distinct skill |
| DP Maths AA SL | Algebra, functions, calculus, proof basics | Timed paper practice | A solid core subject; many tutors qualified |
| DP Maths AA HL | Proof, complex numbers, vectors, extended calculus | Paper 3 style questions; markscheme fluency | Fewest qualified tutors; most preparation per session |
| DP2 exam term | All of the above plus pacing under time | Mock analysis; error log; paper strategy | Peak demand, high stakes, little slack |

## Why Do IB Maths Tutor Fees Differ Between Indian Cities?

Fees differ between Indian cities mainly because of local supply and demand: how many IB schools sit nearby, how many families use private tutors, how many qualified IB maths tutors live there, and what it costs the tutor to travel. Cities with large international school clusters generally have higher rates and more specialists; smaller IB cities have fewer tutors, which can push rates up for HL specialists even where the cost of living is lower.

When families search for IB maths tutor fees across India, Gurgaon and the wider Delhi NCR region have a dense mix of IB schools and families who expect tutors to come to the home. Mumbai and Bengaluru have similar ecosystems. In Pune, Hyderabad, Chennai and Ahmedabad there is a steady IB base but a thinner pool of HL maths specialists, so families there often look beyond their city.

Two practical consequences. First, if you live in a city with few HL maths tutors, you are not limited to the local market: an online specialist from elsewhere may be both better matched and comparable in cost once travel is removed. Second, a city-based rate comparison tells you little unless the tutors compared teach the same programme at the same level with the same preparation. City pages on IB Gram, such as [IB tutors in Gurugram](/ib-tutors/gurugram/math-aa-hl/) and [IB tutors in Delhi](/ib-tutors/delhi/myp/), describe how matching works locally without implying a price.

For Indian families abroad, the same logic holds. A tutor based in India and teaching online can be a sound option for a student in the Gulf, Singapore or the UK, provided time zones work and the tutor knows the student's school calendar. Whether the fee compares well with local options is a question of what is included, not the headline number.

## Home Visit, Online or Hybrid: How Mode Changes the Cost

Mode changes the cost structure rather than simply the amount. A home visit includes travel time that the tutor cannot use for another student. An online lesson avoids travel but needs the right kit on both sides. A hybrid plan can combine them across the year.

| Factor | Home tutoring | Online tutoring | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tutor travel | Built into the fee | None | Partly |
| Equipment needed | Paper, whiteboard | Pen tablet, shared whiteboard, GDC on screen | Both |
| Access to specialists | Limited to who can travel | Any city | Mixed |
| Best for | Younger students, hands-on guidance | Older DP students, exam practice | Students whose needs shift |
| Typical weakness | Gate entry, traffic, rescheduling | Distraction, weak internet, no shared handwriting | Needs planning |

A DP student doing timed paper practice often does well online, because the tutor can see the screen work and the paper in real time. An MYP student who needs a patient, physical presence may do better at home. Neither is cheaper for the same quality of tutor. ## What Are You Really Paying For? Four Examples

Abstract lists can mislead, so here are four illustrative situations. They are composites, not real students.

**A MYP 4 student who "gets it in class" but loses marks on criterion C.** The student's answers are correct but terse, and the teacher writes "show your reasoning" on every test. What helps is a tutor who understands the communication criterion and can coach written explanation. A general maths tutor will keep drilling procedures, which the student already has. The fee is for the criterion knowledge.

**A DP1 AA HL student who coasted through IGCSE Extended.** The first term feels fine, then complex numbers and proof arrive and confidence drops. This student needs a tutor who can rebuild reasoning habits without spoon-feeding. The fee is for depth and for the tutor's ability to teach slowly without lowering standards. [From IGCSE mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/) covers this transition.

**A DP2 student four months before the May session, working at a low level.** Time is short, so the tutor has to triage: which topics repay the most marks, which question types are being dropped, how to sit a paper. The fee reflects demand at that point in the year and the tutor's ability to run a tight plan. This is where an examiner-minded specialist earns their rate, and where beginners cost extra in lost time.

**A confident student who wants a little structure.** Weekly sessions with a good generalist tutor may be all that is needed. Paying for a premium specialist here is possible but probably not necessary. The honest answer from a good tutor is sometimes "you do not need me twice a week."

If you are weighing a premium tier in particular, the economics are examined in [why premium MYP maths tutoring is priced as it is](/blog/why-ib-myp-maths-tutor-charges-5000-per-hour/) and [why Gurgaon parents choose premium private tutors for IB mathematics](/blog/why-gurgaon-parents-choose-premium-private-tutors-ib-mathematics/). ## Does a Higher Fee Mean a Better IB Maths Tutor?

No. A higher fee means the tutor believes their time is worth more and the market has agreed, but it does not tell you whether the teaching suits your child. Price correlates loosely with experience and scarcity. It correlates poorly with how well a particular tutor explains, listens and adapts.

There are three common ways a high fee can mislead. The first is branding: a polished website, a long list of schools, confident language. None of this is the lesson. The second is capacity: a tutor with a waiting list can price high because they can, even if the quality is ordinary. The third is credentials that do not match the need: an excellent university maths lecturer may be wrong for an MYP investigation, because the skill required is assessment fluency and patient questioning, not advanced mathematics.

The reverse also holds. A low fee can hide a weak plan, or a tutor with solid knowledge who has not yet learned to price. The only reliable signal is what happens in a trial lesson and over the following four to six sessions.

> A fee tells you what the tutor charges. A trial lesson tells you what the tutor does.

## How to Compare Two Quotes Fairly

When you have two quotes in front of you, put them on the same footing before comparing. The steps below work for any city and any level.

1. Write down the student's exact need in one sentence: programme, level, what is going wrong, and the date that matters (a test, a mock, the May session).
2. Ask each tutor for the same information: their IB maths background, what they would do in the first month, and how they will measure progress.
3. Ask what is included in the fee: preparation, marking, messaging between sessions, session notes, make-up lessons.
4. Ask about format: home, online or hybrid, and what equipment each requires.
5. Ask the cancellation, rescheduling and refund terms, and whether there is a trial lesson.
6. Compare the total plan for four weeks, not the hourly figure, and note what the family receives in return.
7. Attend or listen in on a trial lesson and judge the interaction, not the introduction.

At step 6, a tutor who charges more per hour but needs fewer sessions to fix the issue can cost less overall than a cheaper tutor who needs many more. This is why the hourly number is the weakest figure to compare. [Budgeting across the full two-year DP](/blog/private-ib-tutor-cost-in-gurgaon-full-fee-breakdown/) is covered separately, with hours per subject and when intensity should peak.

## Questions to Ask Before You Pay

Use these in the first conversation. They are designed so that a good tutor answers easily and a weak one hesitates.

:::stats Before you pay, get answers on these
5 | Things the fee should state clearly
1 | Trial lesson before any large commitment
4 | Weeks to judge whether the plan is working
:::

- **What does the hourly fee include?** The answer should name preparation, marking and messaging, or say clearly that they are not included.
- **How do you diagnose my child's gaps?** Listen for a method: a baseline paper, a review of recent scripts, an error classification. "I will see how they go" is thin.
- **How do you teach to the assessment?** For MYP, ask about criteria A to D. For DP, ask about paper types, markschemes and the Internal Assessment (IA).
- **What will I see after four weeks?** Specific signs such as fewer repeated errors, a cleaner write-up, or faster paper timing are better than "improvement".
- **How many students do you take, and how do you handle exam season?** You want to know your child will not be dropped in April.
- **What is your policy on missed and rescheduled lessons?** It should be written, simple and fair to both sides.
- **Do you write or edit the IA?** The correct answer is no. The IB's integrity rules allow a teacher feedback on one draft; they do not allow a tutor to write or rewrite a student's submission. A tutor who offers to "help with the IA" in any other sense is a risk to your child.
- **Can we begin with a trial?** A confident tutor will agree.

## Red Flags in IB Maths Tutor Pricing

Some patterns in how a tutor prices or sells should make you pause. None is proof of a problem alone, but two or three together usually are.

| Red flag | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Large prepayment with no refund terms | You carry all the risk | Ask for a monthly or per-session start |
| Guarantee of a grade | No tutor controls the exam | Walk away; no honest tutor guarantees a grade |
| Fee quoted without asking about the student | One price for everyone means no diagnosis | Ask what the quote assumes |
| Vague answers about the IB | May be a general maths tutor | Ask for a criterion or paper-level example |
| Pressure to commit before a trial | Selling, not matching | Insist on the trial |
| Offers to write or fix the IA or EE | Academic malpractice risk | Decline; the work must be the student's own |
| Testimonials but no description of method | Marketing in place of teaching | Ask what a typical session looks like |
| Very low fee and very high promises | The numbers do not add up | Check the plan, not the promise |

## When a Specialist Is Worth It and When a Generalist Is Enough

A specialist is worth the higher fee when the problem is specific to the IB: unfamiliar-problem handling, criterion-based communication, Paper 3 style questions, or an IA that needs structured guidance without being written for the student. A generalist is enough when the issue is a content gap, such as algebra fluency or trigonometry basics, that any competent maths teacher can close.

Here is a simple way to sort it. If your child's school reports say "needs to show reasoning" or "struggles with unfamiliar problems", a specialist helps. If the reports say "weak in algebra", a good generalist may be enough for the first few weeks, and you can add specialist support later. If the student is already scoring well and wants polish, a lighter, cheaper arrangement may be better than a full programme.

Many families run a two-stage plan: a generalist or a short specialist diagnostic first, then regular lessons from the right person once the gap is clear. This often costs less overall than starting with the most expensive option and discovering the mismatch later.

:::timeline A sensible tutoring plan for an IB maths student
Week 1 | Free consultation, then a diagnostic or trial lesson
Weeks 2 to 4 | Weekly sessions aimed at the one or two biggest gaps
Week 5 | Review: what changed, what did not, adjust the plan
Weeks 6 to 12 | Settled rhythm with fortnightly check-ins
Before each exam | Increase intensity only if the review supports it
:::

## What Should You Expect From the First Month?

You should expect clarity, not transformation. After four weeks a good arrangement shows a named list of the student's recurring errors, a plan for them, some evidence that the student is attempting harder questions alone, and a parent who understands what is being worked on. Marks may move a little or not at all in a single month, and a tutor who promises otherwise is selling.

What the first month should include in a typical IB maths case:

- A baseline: a short paper or a set of questions that exposes the real level, not a friendly warm-up.
- A written error log, in the student's own words, which grows over the weeks.
- At least one session where the student explains a problem aloud and the tutor asks "why".
- Practice that includes unfamiliar questions, not only variations of the textbook example.
- A short note to the parent after each lesson, or at least each week.

If none of this is visible after four to six sessions, raise it. A good tutor welcomes the question. If the answer is evasive, it is reasonable to ask for a different match. IB Gram, for example, lets a family request a different tutor if the fit is wrong, subject to tutor availability. ## How Much Tutoring Does an IB Maths Student Actually Need?

Most students need less than parents fear and more than students admit. One session a week handles a manageable gap. Two a week suits a student who is behind, a DP2 student approaching mocks, or a student who learns by doing a lot of questions with immediate feedback. Three or more per week is rarely sensible except in a short exam-season sprint, because the student needs time to practise alone between sessions and tutoring should not replace that.

Think in terms of hours of independent work per hour of tutoring. If the tutor sets work and the student does none of it, the tutor's hour is being wasted. A rough guide that many mentors use is that each lesson should generate at least as much student work as the lesson itself, done alone and returned for review. A student who does this will usually need fewer paid hours than one who relies on the session for all the thinking.

Over the course of the DP, intensity follows the calendar. DP1 benefits from steady, moderate support while foundations are laid and subject choices are tested. DP2 raises it in the autumn term for the IA and again before mocks and the May session. [IB DP core planning](/blog/ib-dp-core-planning-timeline/) shows how maths support fits alongside the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge and Creativity, Activity, Service.

DP1 term 3 | 45 | Steady
DP2 term 1 | 60 | Higher (IA)
DP2 term 2 | 85 | Peak (mocks)

## Is an IB Maths Tutor Worth the Money?

An IB maths tutor is worth the money when the tutoring solves a specific, identified problem faster than the student could alone, and the family can see progress against that problem. It is not worth it as general insurance, as a substitute for the student's own practice, or when the student is already on track.

A useful test is to ask, after a month, three things. Can I say in one sentence what we are working on? Has the student done more independent work than before? Does the student say the sessions are useful? If the answer to all three is yes, the fee is probably justified whatever the number. If the answer to two or more is no, the arrangement should change, not just continue.

There are also benefits that do not show as a grade within a term: a student who starts attempting unfamiliar problems instead of freezing, who writes reasoning without prompting, who plans revision. These carry into later years and into other subjects. They are real but they are not guaranteed, and no tutor can guarantee a grade.

Equally, some families do not need a tutor. A student with a strong teacher, good habits and steady results may be better served by past papers and a study group. Spending money to feel safe is a choice, but it is a different choice from spending money to fix a problem.

## How Do You Handle Fees Fairly With a Tutor?

Handle fees by agreeing the terms in writing before the first paid lesson: the rate or package, session length, what is included, cancellation rules, and how either side can end the arrangement. A fair tutor will welcome this, and it protects both sides.

A few practical habits help. Pay in short cycles, such as monthly, so that you can leave if the fit is poor. Agree how many make-up sessions are allowed if the student is ill or the school has a trip. Agree how exam-season changes work: some tutors add sessions at a different rate in the final weeks, and it is better to know in advance. If you pay through a platform that matches you with a tutor, ask what the platform's role is and what happens if you want to change tutor.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much should I pay for an IB maths private tutor in India?

There is no fixed figure, and the right amount is whatever matches the value delivered for your child's specific need. Rates vary with programme, level, tutor depth, city and mode. Compare plans for a four-week period, not hourly numbers, and judge by what is included: diagnosis, preparation, marking and feedback. A fee that looks high can be cheaper if it fixes the problem in fewer sessions.

### Why are IB maths tutors more expensive than regular maths tutors?

They are usually more expensive because IB maths needs assessment knowledge as well as subject knowledge, and fewer tutors have both. MYP is marked on four criteria, DP papers reward method and reasoning, and HL content is demanding. Specialists also tend to spend unseen hours on preparation and marking. That said, a regular tutor can be the right choice for a simple content gap.

### Is a higher hourly rate always a sign of a better tutor?

No. A higher rate reflects the tutor's pricing, experience and demand, not the quality of the match. Branding, long waiting lists and impressive profiles can raise fees without improving teaching. A trial lesson and the first four to six sessions tell you far more. Look for diagnosis, clear explanation, questions that make the student think, and a plan you can describe back.

### Should I choose home tuition or online for IB maths?

Choose by the student, not by the fee. Online suits older DP students, timed paper practice and families without a nearby specialist; home suits younger students who need a patient physical presence. Both can be excellent. Online needs a pen tablet, a shared whiteboard and the graphics display calculator visible on screen. Many families use a hybrid across the year.

### Do IB maths tutor fees go up before exams?

Often, in practice, yes, because demand peaks before DP2 mocks and the May session, and tutors with limited capacity fill up. Some add exam-season sessions at a different arrangement. The best protection is to start earlier, in DP1 or early DP2, and to agree in advance how extra sessions will be handled. Leaving it until a few weeks before the exam usually costs more and achieves less.

### Can I pay per session instead of a package?

Yes, and it is a sensible way to start. Per-session payment keeps you free to change tutor if the fit is poor. Packages often reduce the per-hour cost and commit the tutor to a plan, which suits families who have already had a successful trial. Avoid large prepayments with no refund terms, and ask what happens to unused sessions.

### What if my child shows no progress after a month?

Raise it with the tutor straight away, because one month is long enough to see whether the plan is working. Ask what the diagnosis was, what has been worked on, and what the evidence of change is. If the answers are unclear, ask for a changed plan or a different tutor. Marks may lag, but clearer errors, better working and more independent attempts should appear.

### Can a tutor promise a good IB maths grade?

No honest tutor can promise a grade, because the final result depends on the student's own work, the exam and the marking. Be careful with anyone who promises a particular score. What a good tutor can promise is a clear plan, regular feedback, honest reporting of progress and effort on the problems that are costing marks.

### Should the tutor help with my child's maths IA?

A tutor can teach the skills the IA needs, question the student's reasoning and point at the criteria, but cannot write, rewrite or edit it. The IA is the student's own work, and the IB's integrity policy allows teacher feedback on one draft only. A tutor who offers to do the writing puts the student at risk of a malpractice finding.

### Do I need a premium tutor for MYP maths?

Not always. If the issue is a content gap, a competent generalist may be enough. A specialist is more useful when the student understands the maths but loses marks on communication, investigation or application, which are criteria B, C and D. The decision should follow the problem, not the prestige of the tutor, and a trial lesson helps you see which problem you have.

### How can I check a tutor's claims before paying?

Ask for specifics: what they did in the first month with a student like yours (described generally, without names), how they use markschemes, what they set between sessions, and what they would do if progress stalled. Then book a trial lesson and watch. A tutor who can explain their method in plain words and adapt in the moment is usually who you want.

2. Ask two or three tutors the questions listed above, and compare plans for four weeks, not hourly quotes.
3. Book a trial lesson before committing to a package, and review after four to six sessions whether the plan is working.
