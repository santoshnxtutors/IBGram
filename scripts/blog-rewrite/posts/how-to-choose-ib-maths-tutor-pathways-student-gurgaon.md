An IB maths tutor for a Pathways student should be someone who can stay with the student across the biggest jump in IB mathematics: from the Middle Years Programme (MYP) into the Diploma Programme (DP). A tutor who is good at MYP 4 and loses the thread at DP1, or a DP specialist who has never marked a Criterion C write-up, leaves the family changing tutors at exactly the point when the student needs continuity.

This guide is for parents in Gurgaon whose child is in MYP 3, 4 or 5 at Pathways, or at any school where the MYP leads on to the Diploma Programme on the same campus, and who want to hire one maths tutor for the long run. Confirm with the school's MYP and DP coordinators how its own programme runs; this guide describes the general IB structure and does not state what any school offers. The guide covers what changes between the two programmes, how to choose between Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) and Applications and Interpretation (AI) with a tutor's help, what to ask when interviewing, a trial lesson task, and the handover points where families most often lose momentum.

It was written with the approach of Ajay Vatsyayan, IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, in mind. For the maths itself, the flagship [guide to scoring a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/) shows where the pathway ends.

## Why does continuity matter more than the label on the tutor?

Most tutor searches begin with a subject and a year: "MYP 4 maths tutor" or "DP1 maths tutor". That framing treats each year as a separate job. For a student who will stay in the same school and the same programme, it is a poor frame.

What the DP rewards in maths, and what MYP 4 and 5 quietly build, is the same set of habits: reading a problem carefully, choosing a method on purpose, showing the reasoning line by line and checking the answer against the context. A tutor who has watched those habits form over eighteen months knows where a student's algebra is brittle, which topics produce panic and how the student responds to a harder question. A new tutor in DP1 has to rebuild that picture from scratch, and DP1 is not the time to do it.

Continuity does not mean one tutor forever. It means choosing a tutor who can credibly teach the next stage, and agreeing review points at which the plan is checked against the school's progress. If a specialist for the next stage is needed, a planned handover is better than a rescue.

:::steps What continuity looks like across stages
MYP 4 | Foundations, criteria habits, extended pathway
MYP 5 | Final MYP grade, readiness for DP maths
Summer | Bridge: algebra, functions, notation
DP1 | AA or AI content, first unit habits
DP2 | IA, papers, mocks
:::

## What changes between MYP and DP maths

The two programmes assess differently, and a tutor who does not know both will teach the wrong skill at the wrong time.

MYP Mathematics is assessed against four criteria: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating patterns, C Communicating, D Applying mathematics in real-life contexts, each scored 0 to 8. The total out of 32 converts to a final 1 to 7 grade through IB grade boundaries. There is no single national MYP examination in India, and MYP eAssessment in MYP 5 (on-screen examinations plus ePortfolios) is optional, each school deciding whether to offer it. The criteria are explained for parents in [MYP maths criteria A, B, C and D](/blog/ib-criterion-a-b-c-d-explained-myp-mathematics-gurgaon/).

DP Mathematics is graded 1 to 7 per subject and assessed largely by external examination, with an internal assessment. For AA HL, the flagship guide gives the weightings: Paper 1 (no calculator) 30%, Paper 2 (calculator) 30%, Paper 3 20%, and the Internal Assessment 20%.

| Feature | MYP 4 and 5 maths | DP maths (AA or AI) |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment | Criteria A to D, school-marked | External papers plus internal assessment |
| Grade | Criterion total out of 32 to a 1-7 grade | Subject grade 1-7, feeding up to 45 diploma points |
| Question style | Tasks, investigations, tests, real-life contexts | Timed papers, extended problems, an exploration |
| Calculator use | Varies by task | Paper-specific; a graphic display calculator in several papers |
| Pace | Concept-led, project-friendly | Fast, content-heavy, cumulative |
| Feedback loop | Frequent, task by task | Few big tests, mocks, and a long gap to the exam |
| Weakness that hurts most | Poor communication of reasoning | Gaps in algebra and unfamiliar-problem handling |

The practical meaning is that a good MYP tutor teaches students to explain and generalise, and a good DP tutor teaches students to collect marks from timed papers under a heavy syllabus. The best long-run tutor does both and can say where one turns into the other. The bridge between the two is covered in [the concept-first approach to MYP mathematics](/blog/concept-first-ib-myp-mathematics-tutoring-pathways-heritage-gurgaon/), where understanding is shown to pay off later in the DP.

## What should I look for in an IB maths tutor for a Pathways student?

Look for five things in an IB maths tutor for a Pathways student: genuine familiarity with both MYP and DP maths, a written diagnostic, a teaching method that moves from concept to application to communication, willingness to work from the school's own tasks and calendar, and a plan for handover or progression. A tutor who can name the criteria, show how a mark scheme rewards a line of working and describe what a student at your child's stage should be doing next term has the knowledge you need.

Many tutors teach CBSE, ICSE, IGCSE and IB. That is not a problem, but ask how much of their recent work is MYP or DP maths, and ask them to show it in conversation rather than on a profile. The signs of real familiarity are easy to spot. They talk about criteria and command terms, not only chapters. They ask what the school's last task looked like. They distinguish between a student who does not know a method and one who knows it but writes it up badly.

## The three stages a tutor has to handle

### Stage 1: MYP 4, where foundations settle

MYP 4 is the year in which linear and quadratic functions, simultaneous equations, early trigonometry and algebraic fluency settle. Many schools split MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics into standard and extended pathways, and extended covers additional topics that prepare a student for DP Mathematics Analysis and Approaches. A tutor at this stage should know the difference and should be able to say whether the student's work looks like extended or standard.

The two common MYP 4 patterns are strong Criterion A with weak Criteria B and C, and weak Criterion D. The first suggests a student who can follow a method but not generalise or write it up. The second suggests difficulty turning a word problem into a model. Both are fixable, and both predict how the student will handle DP maths. For topic detail, see [MYP 4 and MYP 5 mathematics tutoring in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-myp-4-myp-5-mathematics-tutoring-gurgaon-pathways-heritage/).

### Stage 2: MYP 5, where readiness is built

MYP 5 is when final MYP grades are produced, the Personal Project is completed and the school recommends DP subjects and levels. If the school runs the optional eAssessment, there is also on-screen preparation, described in [MYP 5 mathematics eAssessment preparation](/blog/ib-myp-5-mathematics-eassessment-preparation-pathways-heritage-gurgaon/).

The tutor's job in MYP 5 is two-fold. First, support the grade. Second, make sure the student enters DP1 able to do the things DP1 will assume: manipulate algebra without a calculator, handle function notation, sketch graphs and work with trigonometric identities. A tutor who only chases the MYP grade can leave the second goal untouched. The route from a middling to a top MYP grade is described in [how an MYP maths student can move from a 5 to a 6 or 7](/blog/how-can-an-ib-myp-maths-student-move-from-5-to-6-or-7/).

### Stage 3: DP1, where the style changes

By DP1 the student meets the choice and the content of AA or AI, a heavier workload and papers marked by external examiners. A tutor carrying a student across needs to adjust the method at this point: shorter feedback cycles through weekly past-paper practice, explicit mark-scheme reading, and a first conversation about the Internal Assessment.

The IA is the same task for AA and AI, SL and HL: 20% of the final grade, 12 to 20 pages, marked out of 20 on five criteria: A Presentation (4), B Mathematical communication (4), C Personal engagement (3), D Reflection (3) and E Use of mathematics (6). HL work is judged against a higher standard on criterion E. The student's authorship is absolute. A tutor can teach the underlying skill, question the reasoning and point at a criterion, but cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission.

:::timeline The tutor's focus across the pathway
MYP 4 | Algebra fluency, criterion C habits
MYP 5 | Grade support, DP readiness check
Summer | Bridge work before DP1
DP1 | Content, notation, first paper practice
DP2 | IA, mocks, Papers 1 to 3
:::

## Is it better to keep one tutor from MYP into the Diploma?

Usually yes, if the tutor can credibly teach the DP stage and the student gets on with them. Continuity saves the rebuilding time, keeps the error log alive, and means the tutor already knows how the student reacts under pressure. It is not better if the tutor's strength is clearly in one programme, if the student has outgrown the style, or if the school's DP teaching and the tutor's approach conflict.

A sensible test is to ask the tutor, in the first meeting, to describe in plain language what they would do with your child in DP1 and in DP2. A tutor who answers with specifics, such as "weekly Paper 1 practice, an error log, a first look at the IA topic in the second term", is thinking ahead. A tutor who says "we will see when we get there" may be a good MYP tutor but is not offering continuity.

If a handover is likely, plan it. Agree in advance a review point around the end of MYP 5 where the family and tutor decide together whether to continue, bring in a DP specialist or run both for a term. A planned handover, with the old tutor passing the error log and a short summary to the new one, loses almost nothing. An unplanned one can lose a term.

## AA or AI: how should a tutor help with the decision?

The AA versus AI choice is made by the student, family and school, and the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement. A tutor does not decide, but a good one provides evidence.

DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each available at HL and SL: Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA), which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and Mathematics Applications and Interpretation (AI), which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and the student.

| Evidence from MYP | Points toward AA | Points toward AI |
|---|---|---|
| Extended maths in MYP 4 and 5 | Strong extended results | Standard pathway, comfortable |
| Algebra under time pressure | Fluent and accurate | Slower, but good with context |
| Criterion D (real-life) | Fine, not a favourite | A strength |
| Interest in proof and structure | Enjoys it | Prefers data and modelling |
| Target degree | Engineering, physics, maths | Social science, data, some business |

The tutor's contribution is a short diagnostic in MYP 5 that tests, honestly, how the student does with AA-style problems: a proof-flavoured algebra question, a function transformation, a calculus-style rate problem. The result goes to the coordinator along with the student's school grades. The full comparison is in [IB Maths AA vs AI](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/), and the wider subject combination is covered in [IB DP STEM subject choice](/blog/stem-subject-choice-ib-dp/).

## Questions to ask in the first conversation

A first conversation shows a lot. These questions work for any tutor, and the answers matter more than the delivery.

1. **"Which IB programmes and years do you teach now, and how recently?"** Look for specifics: MYP 4 and 5, DP1 and DP2, AA or AI.
2. **"What would you do in the first two sessions?"** A diagnostic with written output is the right answer.
3. **"How do you explain the four MYP criteria and where my child could gain?"** A tutor should be able to say it simply.
4. **"What changes at DP1 and how would you adapt?"** The answer shows whether they can carry on.
5. **"How do you handle the Internal Assessment?"** The right answer includes the limits on a tutor's role.
6. **"What do I receive after each session?"** A short note on what was covered, what was hard and what to do before next time.
7. **"How will we know it is working?"** Evidence in marked work within four to six sessions.
8. **"If you are not the right fit for DP, what then?"** A professional answer includes a handover.

Ask the tutor to use your child's latest school task as the example. A tutor who can look at a real piece of marked work and say where the marks went, in a few minutes, is worth a trial.

## A trial lesson that tests continuity

A trial session is the best test. Use a task that sits at the boundary between MYP and DP.

**The task.** Give the student an MYP 5-level problem that can be extended: for instance, a pattern of squares or a sequence that leads to a quadratic rule, with a real-life context. Ask the tutor to teach it, then extend it into a DP-flavoured question about the same structure.

**What to watch.** Does the tutor ask the student to explain their thinking first? Do they let the student struggle for a short time before intervening? Do they model the written communication, not just the answer? Do they connect the task to what the student will see in DP1? Does the student leave with a specific habit to change?

**What to be cautious of.** A tutor who solves everything on the page while the student watches. A tutor who talks about their own results more than the student's work. A session that is all practice with no explanation or all explanation with no output from the student.

## Concept, application, expression: the method behind the continuity

Ajay Vatsyayan's approach to IB Mathematics tutoring is centred on three stages: Concept, Application, Expression. The concept stage focuses on understanding. The application stage focuses on using mathematics in different and unfamiliar situations. The expression stage focuses on communicating mathematical reasoning.

This sequence is helpful for continuity because it maps onto both programmes. In MYP, Criterion A rewards the concept, Criteria B and D reward application, and Criterion C rewards expression. In the DP, the same three stages show up as knowing a method, handling an unfamiliar problem on Paper 3 or in the IA, and earning method marks with clean working. A student trained in this sequence over two or three years carries a habit, not just a set of topics.

**Personalised learning.** No two students at the same grade are there for the same reason. The starting point is a diagnosis of the student's own work, task by task.

**An error-correction system.** The focus is on why an error occurred and what habit prevents it, so that recurring errors are treated as trainable.

**Exam and criterion strategy.** Timed practice, mark-scheme reading and the presentation habits that earn credit.

## Handover points where families lose momentum with an IB maths tutor for a Pathways student

Most families who change an IB maths tutor for a Pathways student at the MYP-to-DP boundary do so by accident. The moments to watch are below.

| Moment | What can go wrong | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| End of MYP 5 | Tutor and student take a long break, then restart in DP1 | Plan a summer bridge of a few sessions |
| First DP1 term | Student's schedule fills; sessions drop | Fix a standing weekly slot early |
| First DP1 test | Result is a shock; panic changes | Review the error log before changing anything |
| IA topic choice | Student wants help picking a topic | Teach the criteria; the topic is the student's |
| Mock period | Sessions double suddenly | Plan the intensity at the start of DP2 |

The summer bridge deserves a note. The gap between the last MYP exams or tasks and the start of DP1 is when students lose algebra fluency. A short block of sessions on the foundations DP1 assumes, such as factorising, indices and logarithms, function notation, graph sketching and trigonometric identities, often does more than any amount of DP1 revision later. Whether a student needs it is a matter for the diagnostic.

## A worked example: three possible paths

These are illustrations, not real families.

**Path 1: strong student, steady continuity.** A student finishes MYP 5 with strong extended maths results and wants to study engineering. The same tutor ran a weekly session through MYP 4 and 5. A bridge block in the summer covers algebra and functions, and the student enters DP1 in Mathematics AA HL. The tutor adds weekly paper-style practice from the second term. The review point at the end of DP1 term 1 shows no need to change.

**Path 2: capable student, uncertain choice.** A student has solid but not outstanding extended results and is torn between AA HL and AI HL. The tutor runs a diagnostic in MYP 5 on AA-style problems. The student finds the proof-flavoured algebra hard but handles modelling well. The family takes the evidence to the coordinator and the student chooses AI. The same tutor continues, adjusting the method to statistics and technology.

**Path 3: planned handover.** A student who has had an excellent MYP tutor chooses AA HL, and the tutor says honestly that their own strength is MYP. At the end of MYP 5 the family and tutor agree a handover to a DP specialist. The MYP tutor passes on the error log and a summary. The new tutor starts with the log, and DP1 begins without a lost term.

In each path the decision was made on evidence at a planned review point, not in a panic after a poor test.

## Mistakes parents make when choosing for the long run

**Choosing on a profile line.** "IB maths expert" on a profile means little. Ask for specifics in conversation.

**Choosing for MYP only.** A great MYP 4 tutor can be a poor fit for DP1. Ask the DP question at the start.

**Skipping the diagnostic.** Starting with sessions before anyone has looked at the student's work wastes weeks.

**Treating the summer as holiday.** Algebra fluency fades in a long break. A short bridge is cheap and effective.

**Letting the IA become the tutor's job.** The IA is the student's own work, and a tutor who offers to write or rewrite it is a risk.

**Not agreeing review points.** A long engagement without a planned review drifts. Agree one at the end of MYP 5 and one in the first DP1 term.

**Ignoring the student's view.** A student who dislikes the style will not use the sessions. Ask them what is helping.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Should one tutor cover both MYP and DP maths?

Often yes, if the tutor can credibly teach the DP stage and the student works well with them. Continuity saves the time of rebuilding a picture of the student's strengths and habits. It is not the right choice if the tutor's strength is clearly in one programme. Ask the tutor what they would do in DP1 and DP2, and agree a review point at the end of MYP 5.

### When should we start looking for a DP maths tutor?

Begin the conversation in MYP 5, and decide before the summer. A bridge block between MYP 5 and DP1 is often the most valuable stretch. If the student is already in DP1, start in the first four to eight weeks if there is a clear gap. Waiting for the first test result means the first unit has already gone by.

### How do I know whether my child should take AA or AI?

Use evidence: extended maths results in MYP, how the student handles algebra under time pressure, Criterion D performance, interest and the target degree. The school's DP coordinator makes the final placement. A short tutor diagnostic on AA-style problems adds useful information. Many engineering and physics courses expect AA, but check the course requirements for any degree you are considering.

### Is a tutor who teaches the Pathways school's curriculum necessary?

No, and no tutor needs a relationship with a school. What matters is knowing the IB programme and being willing to work from the student's own school tasks and calendar. Ask the tutor to look at a recent piece of marked work and explain where the marks went. IB Gram is an independent platform and is not affiliated with any school named here.

### Can a tutor help with the Internal Assessment in DP maths?

A tutor can teach the skills: reading the criteria, framing a question, structuring a write-up and checking reasoning. The tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission, and the IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to comment on one draft only. The mathematics exploration is 20% of the grade and marked out of 20 on five criteria, so the student's own authorship is essential.

### What if my child is behind at the start of MYP 4?

Start with a diagnostic and repair the foundations, usually algebra and number fluency, before adding new content. MYP 4 is a good time for this, because the pressure is lower than in MYP 5 and the repair is smaller. Aim for a block of several weeks with a specific goal, then review. A tutor who recommends a long-term plan without a diagnostic should be asked why.

### How often should sessions happen across MYP 4 and MYP 5?

Weekly is a common pattern for a defined aim, with lighter weeks around the Personal Project and heavier ones before school examinations. More sessions are not automatically better, because the student's own practice between sessions matters most. Review after four to six sessions, and again at the end of each term, to confirm that marked work is changing.

### Home or online: which is better for a long engagement?

Neither is better in general. Home suits students who work well side by side on paper and families who like an in-person routine. Online suits packed weeks, a wider choice of tutors and last-minute sessions. A mix across the year is common. What matters is the tutor's whiteboard setup if online, a quiet table if at home, and whether work is done between sessions.

### What should I do if the tutor is not a good fit after a month?

Review the evidence first: is marked work changing, is the student doing the set tasks, and has the tutor explained the plan? If the fit is wrong, say so and ask for a different match. IB Gram can shortlist another tutor, subject to availability. A first mismatch is common and not a reason to abandon tutoring.

### Can a tutor guarantee a 7 or a particular grade?

No. No tutor can guarantee a grade. Progress depends on the starting point, the time available, the student's practice and the exam. A tutor can diagnose, teach, hold a routine and build the habits that earn marks. Be cautious of promises, and judge by whether the student's marked work improves within a few weeks.
