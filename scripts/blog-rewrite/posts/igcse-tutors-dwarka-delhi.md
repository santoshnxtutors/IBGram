## How Are IGCSE Science Papers Built?

Cambridge IGCSE sciences, for syllabuses examined from 2023, combine multiple-choice, theory and a practical component. Core candidates sit Paper 1 (multiple choice) and Paper 3 (theory); Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (multiple choice) and Paper 4 (theory). Practical assessment is through Paper 5 (a practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), and the school chooses which. The weighting is about 30% for multiple choice, 50% for theory and 20% for practical.

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are organised differently. They are untiered, with Paper 1 and Paper 2 for each separate science, and practical skills assessed inside the written papers instead of in a separate paper.

:::bars Cambridge IGCSE science weightings
Theory (Paper 3 or 4) | 50 | 50%
Multiple choice (Paper 1 or 2) | 30 | 30%
Practical (Paper 5 or 6) | 20 | 20%
Check the current syllabus for exact timings and any changes.
:::

Notice what the weightings imply. Practical skills are worth a fifth of the grade, which is as much as a whole component, and students often give them a tenth of their revision. Notice also that the exam series differ: Cambridge runs February/March (offered only in India), May/June and October/November, while Edexcel International GCSE runs in January and May/June. The school decides which series a student sits, so confirm the date with the school.

## What Do Dwarka Students Find Hard About IGCSE Sciences?

Students in Dwarka come to IGCSE from many routes: CBSE or ICSE schools in Delhi, international schools in or near Dwarka, and families returning from abroad. The sciences expose the same handful of difficulties in each case.

**Recall without application.** A student who learned photosynthesis as a paragraph can state it and cannot use it when asked why a plant in a particular experiment stopped producing oxygen.

**Practical skills treated as an afterthought.** In school, practical sessions can be rushed. The exam asks the student to plan, observe, tabulate, plot and evaluate with care.

**Imprecise wording.** "It gets hot" instead of "the thermal energy transferred increases the kinetic energy of the particles".

**Calculation habits.** Chemistry moles, Physics equations rearranged, units dropped, significant figures ignored.

**Weak graph skills.** Axes chosen badly, points plotted carelessly, line of best fit drawn through every point.

For a root-cause view of struggles across all three sciences, see [why students struggle in IGCSE Science](/blog/igcse-science-tutoring-guide/). The rest of this guide focuses on fixes.

## The Practical Paper: Paper 5 and Paper 6

The practical component is the part of IGCSE sciences least like school science in India. Cambridge schools choose either Paper 5, a practical test done in a lab, or Paper 6, the alternative to practical, which is a written paper about experiments. Which one your child sits depends on the school, so ask early.

Whichever it is, the skills assessed are consistent: following a method, recording observations accurately, presenting results in a table, plotting a graph, drawing conclusions, and evaluating the method with suggestions for improvement. Check the current syllabus for the exact skills list, because the wording can change.

### Paper 6 Needs Training Even Without a Lab

Paper 6 is a written paper. That does not make it easy. Students must interpret an experiment they may never have seen, so they need a bank of experimental ideas and the habit of reasoning from variables. A tutor can train this at the dining table: describe a setup, ask which variable is changed and measured, what is kept constant and why, what the table should look like, what shape the graph might take.

### Paper 5 Needs Time in a Lab

If the school uses Paper 5, the student needs real hands-on practice. A home tutor cannot reproduce a lab, so ask the school what practical sessions are available and treat tuition as support for the thinking that surrounds them: planning, tables, graph plotting and evaluating. A student who is nervous about practical work benefits from discussing each practical before and after school sessions.

:::steps The practical-skills loop a tutor should run
Plan | Variables, controls, apparatus
Record | Table with units and decimals
Plot | Axes, scale, points, best fit
Conclude | Pattern, numbers, explanation
Evaluate | Sources of error, one real fix
:::

### Tables, Graphs and Significant Figures

Marks in practical papers are often won by presentation. A table needs headings with units, consistent decimal places and the independent variable in the first column. A graph needs a sensible scale using at least half the grid, labelled axes with units, carefully plotted points and a smooth line or straight line of best fit that follows the trend instead of connecting dots. Answers need the right number of significant figures. None of this is hard, and all of it is routinely lost.

## Biology 0610: Where Marks Go and What to Practise

Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 covers a wide topic list: characteristics and classification of living organisms; organisation of the organism; movement into and out of cells; biological molecules; enzymes; plant nutrition; human nutrition; transport in plants and in animals; diseases and immunity; gas exchange in humans; respiration; excretion in humans; coordination and response; drugs; reproduction; inheritance; variation and selection; organisms and their environment; human influences on ecosystems; and biotechnology and genetic modification. The Pearson Edexcel equivalent is 4BI1.

The syllabus is broad, so Biology rewards a revision system. Most students lose marks on:

- **Data questions.** Describing a graph without quoting numbers.
- **Experimental design.** Missing the control or the variable that must be kept constant.
- **Precise vocabulary.** Osmosis versus diffusion, active transport versus facilitated diffusion, with the correct term for the situation.
- **Linking structure to function.** "The villus has many capillaries" earns a mark only when linked to maintaining a concentration gradient.

A Biology tutor should work with the student's own marked papers and build a glossary of terms that cost marks. ## Chemistry 0620: The Calculation Subject

Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 covers states of matter; atoms, elements and compounds; stoichiometry, including the mole; electrochemistry; chemical energetics; chemical reactions; acids, bases and salts; the Periodic Table; metals; chemistry of the environment; organic chemistry; and experimental techniques and chemical analysis. The Pearson Edexcel equivalent is 4CH1.

Chemistry is where students feel the calculation load. Typical trouble spots:

- **The mole.** Moving between mass, moles and concentration without losing track of units.
- **Balancing equations.** Doing it by guessing, instead of by atom counts.
- **Energetics.** Mixing up exothermic and endothermic, and sign conventions for energy changes.
- **Organic chemistry.** Naming and drawing structures accurately, and explaining why a reaction occurs.
- **Qualitative analysis.** Memorising test results without a system.

### A Worked Habit for Mole Calculations

A tutor can teach a fixed layout so the student never freezes. Write the balanced equation. Convert the given quantity to moles, showing the formula and the substitution. Use the mole ratio from the equation. Convert to the quantity asked, with units. Check that the answer is sensible: if a student gets 4,000 g of product from 5 g of reactant, something went wrong. A student who does this by routine loses far fewer marks than one who attempts each problem fresh.

## Physics 0625: Equations, Units and Graphs

Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 covers motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics. Students must recall and rearrange the equations in the syllabus. The Pearson Edexcel equivalent is 4PH1.

Physics marks are lost through process, not ideas:

- **Rearranging.** A student knows v = d/t and cannot get t on its own.
- **Units.** Using kilometres per hour where metres per second is needed.
- **Graph reading.** Gradient and area under a graph are asked often, and students forget what each represents.
- **Extended reasoning.** Explaining why an object reaches terminal velocity needs a chain of statements about forces.

A tutor should drill short equation-rearrangement sets and run graph-interpretation practice until reading a gradient is automatic.

:::stats Three sciences, three habits
0610 | Biology: precise terms and data
0620 | Chemistry: moles and equations
0625 | Physics: rearranging and graphs
:::

## Data Handling: The Skill That Cuts Across All Three

Every IGCSE science asks students to read, plot and interpret data. A tutor should teach one routine and apply it across subjects.

1. Read the question and underline what is asked: describe, explain, calculate, suggest.
2. Read the axes or column headings, including units.
3. State the trend in one sentence with direction and range.
4. Quote at least two values from the data, with units.
5. Note anything unusual, such as an anomaly or a point where the pattern changes.
6. Give the scientific explanation, using the correct term.
7. Check that the depth of answer matches the command word.

A short drill of three data questions a week, marked with the markscheme, builds the habit in a month.

### Anomalous Results and Repeats

Students often treat an anomalous result as an error to be hidden. The exam wants them to identify it, suggest a cause, and say what to do next, such as repeat the measurement and calculate a mean excluding the anomaly. This is a short answer worth easy marks once the student has seen it a few times.

### Command Words Decide the Depth

State, describe, explain, suggest, calculate and compare ask for different answers. A tutor should train students to underline the command word and to write exactly that kind of response. The most common single error across the three sciences is giving a description when an explanation was asked.

| Command word | The answer must | Typical loss |
|---|---|---|
| State | Give a fact, briefly | Writing a paragraph |
| Describe | Give features or a pattern | Giving reasons instead |
| Explain | Give cause and mechanism | Restating the observation |
| Suggest | Apply knowledge to a new situation | Reciting a memorised answer |
| Calculate | Show working and unit | Answer only, no method |

## What Does an IGCSE Science Session Look Like?

A session with one of the better IGCSE tutors in Dwarka starts from the student's recent work and ends with a task. Here is a typical shape for a ninety-minute science session.

1. The student brings a recent marked test or a past-paper question they got wrong.
2. The tutor asks the student to explain the answer aloud, which shows whether the gap is knowledge, wording or method.
3. They fix the one missing idea, with a diagram, a worked example or a quick demonstration.
4. The student attempts a fresh question of the same type unaided.
5. The tutor marks it against the markscheme logic and shows where the marks were.
6. The student writes the next task: a practical-skills question, a graph, or ten equation rearrangements.

A session that is only the tutor explaining is school again. If a tutor never asks the student to attempt something unaided, the student is not practising.

### Home, Online or Hybrid

The sciences work in all three modes. Home sessions suit students who benefit from a person beside them and diagram-heavy work. Online sessions suit data-question review, because a shared screen and pen tablet make annotating graphs simple, and they widen the choice of specialists. A hybrid plan, for instance home sessions for hard topics and online sessions for quick doubt-solving before tests, often fits a busy student best. ## A Realistic Term Plan for a Grade 10 Science Student

Plans should follow the school calendar, and the school's own dates win. Here is a typical shape for a Grade 10 student preparing for a series in the spring or early summer. Dates are illustrative.

:::timeline A Grade 10 science plan (school dates vary)

After mocks | Error log, targeted repair, timed papers
Final weeks | Light revision, equations and terms, rest

### Early Year: Diagnose

The first tutoring weeks should produce a diagnosis: which topics are red, which skills (graphs, calculations, wording) are weak, and whether the student is on the right tier. A student on Extended who is working at Core level needs an honest conversation with the teacher.

### Mid Year: Repair and Practise

Topic repair with weekly practical-skills practice. Every week includes one data or practical question, so skills do not wither between topics. A mistake log records every lost mark with a reason.

### Mocks and After

The mock tells you what the exam will feel like. After the mock, spend a session on the paper: where did marks go, in knowledge, wording, calculation or time? Then reset the plan. For students who do poorly in a mock, [mock recovery for IGCSE students](/blog/igcse-tutors-sushant-lok-gurgaon/) in Sushant Lok covers what to do in the weeks that follow, and [the six-week IGCSE revision plan](/blog/igcse-revision-six-week-plan/) lays out the final stretch across subjects.

### The Final Weeks

Final weeks are for retrieval and accuracy. Equations, command words, definitions and practical routines are revised in short sessions. New content is rare. The student sleeps.

## Core or Extended, Cambridge or Edexcel?

The tier decision is made with the school, and it shapes a student's science plan. Core students aim at grades up to C and should concentrate on securing the foundational marks. Extended students have access to the top grades and face harder questions requiring more explanation. Edexcel students have no tiers in the sciences, so the paper contains questions of varying difficulty and the student needs to be comfortable with both.

If the school is Cambridge, the practical paper type (Paper 5 or Paper 6) affects how the student should train. If the school is Edexcel, practical skills appear inside the written papers, so the tutor should weave them into every written practice question.

Whichever board it is, ask the school for the syllabus code and use past papers matched to it. Mixing papers from different syllabus versions confuses students, because content and structure change.

## Separate Sciences, Combined or Co-ordinated

Some schools teach separate Biology, Chemistry and Physics; others teach a combined or co-ordinated science course. The difference matters for tutoring because it changes how much time each discipline gets and what appears in the exam. Confirm with the school which course your child is following before choosing a tutor, since a tutor trained in the separate sciences may need to adjust depth for a combined course. For students planning to take IB Diploma sciences later, separate sciences usually give a stronger base, but the school's offer and the DP coordinator's requirements decide that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which IGCSE science subject needs the most tutoring in Dwarka?

There is no single answer, but Chemistry often asks for the most because of mole calculations, while Physics students struggle with rearranging equations and graphs, and Biology students with wording and data. The right subject for tutoring is the one where recent papers show lost marks, not the one that sounds hardest.

### What is the difference between Paper 5 and Paper 6?

Paper 5 is a practical test done in a laboratory, and Paper 6 is a written alternative to practical that tests the same skills on paper. Schools choose which one to use, so ask yours early. Either way, the skills are planning, observing, recording, graph work, conclusions and evaluation.

### Can a tutor help with practical skills at home?

Yes for the thinking around practicals, such as planning, tables, graphs and evaluating, and Paper 6 style questions can be practised fully on paper. Hands-on lab technique needs school lab time. A good tutor works with what the school does and fills the gaps.

### How many sessions a week do IGCSE sciences need?

For most students, one session a week per science that needs support is enough, rising to two in the weeks before mocks and finals. One focused weekly session with marked homework beats two sessions with no follow-up. Tutoring all three sciences at once is rarely necessary.

### Is online tutoring good enough for sciences?

For many students, yes, especially for data questions, calculations and long-answer feedback, where a shared screen and pen tablet work well. Home sessions suit diagram-heavy topics and students who focus better in person. Some families use both. The deciding factor is whether the student actively participates.

### What if my child moved from CBSE to IGCSE?

Expect a gap in command-word answering, data handling and practical skills, not in basic content. A tutor should diagnose with a past paper in the first week and teach the exam style directly. The transition usually takes a term or more of deliberate practice. Be patient with early marks.

### Do Core students need a tutor less than Extended students?

Not necessarily. Core students may need help with foundational understanding and accuracy, while Extended students need help with harder reasoning. The need depends on gaps, not tier. A Core student scoring below target on easy marks usually benefits from a diagnostic and short, regular sessions.

### Should my child take separate sciences or combined science?

That decision belongs to the school and depends on what it offers and what the student plans next. Separate sciences generally give a deeper base, especially for later IB or A Level science. A student unsure about future subjects should ask the teacher and, if relevant, the DP coordinator of the target school.

### What if my child is not improving after a month?

Ask the tutor what the diagnosis was and which patterns of lost marks have shrunk. A month is long enough to see process improvements, such as better graphs or more precise wording, even if marks lag. If the answers are vague, ask for a different match. IB Gram lets families request one.

### Will a tutor promise my child a particular grade?

No, and be wary of any that does. No tutor or platform can promise a grade. Results depend on the student's starting point, effort, school assessment and many other factors. A good tutor shows evidence of progress, not promises.
