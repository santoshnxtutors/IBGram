## How do IB and IGCSE tutoring needs differ in South Delhi?

The two boards ask for different things from a tutor. IB students need help with criterion-marked, inquiry-driven work across the Middle Years Programme (MYP) and the Diploma Programme (DP), including internal assessments. IGCSE students need command of a tight syllabus and relentless past-paper practice for a fixed exam series. A tutor who is excellent at one is not automatically good at the other.

Here is what each looks like in a typical South Delhi household.

**IB.** An MYP student is assessed on criteria, not a single final paper: four criteria, A to D, each scored 0 to 8, with the total out of 32 converted to a 1 to 7 grade. There is no single national MYP examination in India. A DP student studies six subjects over two years, three or four at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL), with Theory of Knowledge (TOK), the Extended Essay (EE) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) alongside. Each subject is graded 1 to 7, and the total including TOK and EE points is up to 45. Tutoring here is partly subject help and partly help with the habits of reasoning, writing and time.

**IGCSE.** This is usually a two-year course across Grades 9 and 10 in Cambridge IGCSE or Pearson Edexcel International GCSE. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November; Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers, where Core tops out at grade C and Extended reaches A*. Tutoring is largely syllabus mastery, paper technique and practice under time.

4 | MYP criteria, A to D
1-7 | IB subject grade scale
2 | Years of a typical IGCSE course
3 | Cambridge IGCSE exam series a year
The February/March Cambridge series exists specifically for India.
:::

## When does home tuition work in South Delhi?

Home tuition works when the tutor can reach the house without losing a large block of the evening, when the student has a quiet place to work, and when the subject benefits from someone sitting next to the student with a pen. It works least well when the student is travelling between school, activities and home with only a short slot left late in the evening.

South Delhi has a particular texture that shapes this. Many homes in the established colonies are independent houses split across floors, or low-rise apartment blocks, rather than the large gated towers of newer cities. Entry is often a doorbell and a staircase rather than a security desk and a visitor log, which makes arrival simpler. Parking on narrow colony lanes can be the real constraint for a tutor who drives, and it is worth telling a tutor where they can leave a vehicle. Evening traffic across the main roads linking the colonies is heavy, and a tutor who arrives from outside the area needs margin.

Practical points families find useful:

- **Fix the slot around the commute home, not around school finish.** A student who gets in at five does better at a lesson from six than at a lesson at half past four.
- **Give the tutor a table, not a sofa.** A desk, a shared whiteboard or a large sheet of paper beats a lap.
- **Tell the tutor about household noise.** A busy lane or a younger sibling affects concentration more than parents expect.
- **Keep one regular slot.** A fixed weekday keeps the tutor's commute predictable, which tends to improve punctuality.

## When does online tutoring win for South Delhi families?

Online wins when the best specialist for the subject is elsewhere, when evenings are packed with activities, when the household is noisy at tutoring time, or when the student needs a few short sessions rather than a long block. It also wins for exam-season top-ups, because a thirty-minute screen session on a tight evening is better than none.

For subjects with heavy diagrams or mathematics, a pen tablet and shared whiteboard make online lessons close to a home session. A tutor writes a derivation or a mechanism live, and the student sees it appear. For an Economics diagram or a Chemistry mechanism, that matters. For a reluctant student who disengages on a screen, a home visit can hold attention better.

Online also widens the choice. In a field where a student needs a Higher Level specialist in a single subject, the pool within a short drive may be small, and the right tutor may be elsewhere in the National Capital Region or abroad. The mechanics are explained in [online IB tutoring in India](/blog/online-ib-tutoring-india/) for IB and [online IGCSE tutoring in India](/blog/online-igcse-tutoring-india/) for IGCSE.

When online is the wrong choice: very young MYP students who tire quickly on screens, students who need a person in the room to stay on task, and subjects that are easier to teach with objects on the table. ## What does a South Delhi evening actually allow?

A typical weekday for a South Delhi student on either board is full. School runs to the late afternoon, then sport, music or a language class, then homework, then, for DP students, internal assessment and CAS commitments. A tutor session sits in whatever gap remains.

Consider a DP1 student who finishes school and a sports practice around five, gets home by six, and has dinner at half past eight. The workable window is perhaps six-fifteen to seven-fifteen, three or four evenings a week at most. A one-hour home lesson fits. A two-hour block does not, because the student is tired by the second hour and little new is learned.

Now consider an IGCSE Grade 10 student in the term before the February/March series. The same student may need a short, frequent rhythm, with several shorter sessions each week focused on past-paper practice. Online sessions of forty-five minutes fit this pattern well.

A few rules of thumb that hold across both boards:

1. Count the real free minutes in a week before choosing a tutor, not the hopeful ones.
2. Put the hardest subject in the freshest slot.
3. Leave at least one weekday evening with no lessons.
4. Use weekends for longer practice blocks, if the student can concentrate, and for a mock paper under timed conditions.

## What should an IB tutor in South Delhi be able to do?

An IB tutor should know the structure of the programme the student is in, the assessment for the subject, and the difference between teaching content and teaching what the IB rewards. For the DP that means paper formats, command terms, and the internal assessment. For the MYP it means the four criteria and how schools write tasks.

Look for four things.

**Knowledge of the assessment.** For DP sciences, the course was redesigned for first assessment in 2025: Paper 1 has two parts, 1A multiple choice and 1B data-based questions; Paper 2 has short-answer and extended-response questions; the internal assessment is the scientific investigation, worth 20% and marked on four criteria of 6 marks each. A tutor who describes the old structure is out of date.

**Respect for academic integrity.** Every IB internal assessment is the student's own work. A teacher may give feedback on one draft; no tutor or parent may write, rewrite or edit the submission. A tutor may teach the underlying skill, question the reasoning and ask the student to reread their paragraph against a criterion.

**Command-term awareness.** Words such as define, describe, explain, evaluate, discuss and to what extent set the depth an answer needs. Many marks are lost by answering a different command from the one asked.

**Evidence of diagnosis.** A tutor should look at the student's own marked work before proposing a plan.

## What should an IGCSE tutor in South Delhi be able to do?

An IGCSE tutor should know the exact syllabus code the student is sitting, the tier, and the paper structure, and should run regular timed practice with mark schemes. IGCSE is a precise exam, and much of the gain comes from knowing how marks are awarded.

Details a good tutor handles without prompting:

- **Syllabus code and tier.** Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 has Core and Extended; Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), while Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher.
- **Series dates.** Which series the school has entered the student for. A student cannot choose a series independently of the school.
- **Practical assessment in sciences.** Cambridge IGCSE sciences have a practical component through Paper 5 or Paper 6, chosen by the school, and multiple choice, theory and practical carry 30%, 50% and 20%.
- **Past papers.** Mark schemes and examiner reports show what the board rewards.
- **Subject-specific traps.** Cambridge Computer Science 0478 and Economics 0455, for instance, have different paper structures and different written-answer expectations.

The Indian academic year runs roughly April to March, and both boards sit on it, though with different peaks. Knowing where the pressure lands helps you book tutoring before the crunch rather than during it.

A few specific patterns:

- **DP2 in the autumn.** Internal assessment deadlines, EE milestones and university applications arrive together. [The IB DP core planning timeline](/blog/ib-dp-core-planning-timeline/) shows how to space them.
- **Grade 10 IGCSE in winter.** The February/March series lets results arrive in time for Indian school admissions to Class 11 and for IB DP or A Level entry in the same academic year, which concentrates revision into a short window.
- **DP2 in spring.** May session papers are the finish line; results are released in early July.
- **MYP 5.** Personal Project work, and in some schools MYP eAssessment, which is optional and decided by each school.

## Is it better to have one tutor for everything or several specialists?

Specialists usually give better results for HL subjects and for exam years, while a single generalist can work for MYP students and for younger IGCSE students. A student with five or six subjects cannot sensibly have six tutors, and no one tutor is expert in all of them.

A workable pattern is to put the most demanding subject, often mathematics or a science at HL, with a specialist, and to cover the rest with the school's own support and the student's own work. Add a second tutor only when a clear gap appears in a different subject. In households with siblings on different boards, one coordinator can plan sessions across both, which reduces the number of evenings taken up by a visit.

## How do you tell whether a tutor is actually good?

A good tutor can explain a hard idea in two ways, spots the error in a student's working quickly, and can say what level of detail a question needs. A trial session shows this more clearly than a profile does.

Use this process for any tutor, whatever the board:

1. **Ask for the tutor's plan for your child.** Not a generic description, but what they would look at first and why.
2. **Give them a piece of real marked work.** Ask what they see. A good tutor names the habit costing marks within minutes.
3. **Book a trial session.** Watch how the tutor treats your child: do they ask questions, or lecture?
4. **Check for current syllabus knowledge.** Ask which syllabus code or subject guide version they are teaching from.
5. **Agree a review date.** After four to six weeks, look at the next school result and decide.
6. **Watch for red flags.** A tutor who guarantees a grade, who offers to write the internal assessment, or who cannot explain how marks are awarded is not worth keeping.

## When might a South Delhi family not need a tutor?

Not every student needs one, and the best IB and IGCSE tutors in South Delhi will say so. A student who attends, completes work, scores consistently and asks good questions at school may need nothing beyond that, and a conversation with the subject teacher is free.

A tutor is worth considering when:

- Marks in a single subject fall while others hold.
- The same error repeats across several tests.
- A student has changed boards or schools and is behind on content.
- A mock result is well below what classwork suggested.
- The student has stopped asking for help and is avoiding a subject.

A tutor will not help much when the real problem is workload across all subjects, a lack of sleep, or anxiety. In those cases the first conversation is with the school's counsellor or coordinator. A tutor can also not replace the student's effort: a student who does not work between sessions gains little from them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I find IB and IGCSE tutors in South Delhi?

Start by writing down the board, year, subject, level, school calendar, your colony and the evenings you can offer. Then send those details to a tutor-matching platform such as IB Gram, or ask your school coordinator for suggestions. Ask for a trial session before committing. The more precise your brief, the better the shortlist, and availability always depends on the tutor.

### Do tutors come to homes in Greater Kailash, Defence Colony and Saket?

Many tutors are willing to visit homes in these areas, subject to their own schedule and travel. Practical points such as parking, evening traffic and your slot affect whether it works. If a home visit is hard to arrange, online lessons with a shared whiteboard are a close alternative. Ask the tutor what they prefer and what they have found works.

### Is online tutoring as good as home tutoring for IB?

For most students, yes. A shared whiteboard and a pen tablet let a tutor write calculations and mechanisms live, and online opens up a wider choice of specialists. Some students, especially younger ones or those who drift on screens, do better with a tutor in the room. Many families use a hybrid arrangement and adjust as the year goes on.

### What is the difference between an IB tutor and an IGCSE tutor?

An IB tutor works with criterion-marked, inquiry-based work and internal assessments across the MYP and DP, while an IGCSE tutor focuses on a fixed syllabus, tiers and past-paper technique for a set exam series. Some tutors teach both, but you should check that they know the specific programme and syllabus code your child is on, not just the subject.

### When should my child start tutoring?

Start when there is a specific problem and enough time to work on it, not just before a test. For DP, many families start in DP1 for demanding HL subjects, and again before the mocks in DP2. For IGCSE, the months before the exam series matter most. Waiting until the last fortnight limits what any tutor can do.

### Can a tutor help with my IB Internal Assessment or Extended Essay?

A tutor can teach the skills behind them and explain the criteria, but cannot write or edit the submission. The IB treats every internal assessment as the student's own work and allows a teacher to comment on one draft only. A responsible tutor asks questions, points at a criterion and asks you to reread your own work against it.

### How many sessions a week does a student need?

It depends on the student and the time of year. One session a week suits a student who needs steady support. Two sessions suit a student who is behind, for a limited period, and short frequent sessions suit the weeks before an exam. Set a review date after four to six weeks and adjust based on the next school result.

### What if we choose a tutor and it does not work out?

Tell the tutor early what is not working, and ask what they will change. If there is no improvement in approach after a few sessions, ask for a different match. With IB Gram, the family can request a different tutor, subject to availability. A trial session before committing reduces the chance of a poor fit.

### Can one tutor cover both IB and IGCSE for siblings?

Sometimes, if the tutor knows both boards well, but check each subject separately. Siblings on different boards often do better with specialists for the demanding subjects. A single coordinator who can plan the household's evenings reduces the number of visits, and online sessions can fill the gaps. Ask the tutor to describe their experience with each board.
