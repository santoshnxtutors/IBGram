If you are working out how to choose an IB maths tutor, the shortest reliable method is to treat it as a four-stage process, not a single decision: build a shortlist of two or three people, interview each with questions that test method rather than reputation, run a trial lesson with a recent piece of your child's work, and review the evidence after the first month. Most families skip stages two and four, which is where bad matches are caught.

I write this as someone who teaches IB mathematics, and who has watched families lose a term to a tutor who was kind, qualified and wrong for the student. This guide is for parents in Gurgaon and Gurugram whose child is in the International Baccalaureate (IB) Middle Years Programme (MYP) extended mathematics course or in the Diploma Programme (DP), in Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) or Applications and Interpretation (AI), at Higher Level (HL) or Standard Level (SL).

## Why Do Families Choose the Wrong IB Maths Tutor?

Families usually choose on proximity, a recommendation or a confident first call, and none of those predicts whether the tutor can change how a particular student handles mathematics. The tutor is judged on personality in the first hour and on results months later, with nothing measured in between.

Three patterns repeat. The first is choosing a school-maths expert who has never taught the IB. Their explanations are sound and their question selection is wrong, because IB papers ask for reasoning, communication and unfamiliar contexts that a conventional chapter drill does not train. The second is choosing a speed coach. Some tutors came up through entrance-exam coaching, and they teach pace and trick shortcuts, which are useful in one setting and harmful in another where a clearly set-out method earns marks that a quick correct answer does not. The third is choosing the nicest person. A student who enjoys the session and learns nothing new is the most expensive outcome of all, because nobody complains.

Anyone asking how to choose an IB maths tutor should start by accepting that first impressions are the weakest evidence available. The antidote is a process with checkpoints that force evidence to appear. The rest of this guide sets it out.

:::steps The four-stage selection process
Shortlist | Two or three candidates, no more
Interview | Questions that test method
Trial | Real work, watched closely
Review | Evidence after four weeks
:::

## Stage One: How Do You Build a Shortlist?

Build the shortlist from four inputs, in this order: your child's exact course, the assessment calendar, the nature of the difficulty, and the practical constraints of mode and location. Two or three candidates is the right number. One gives you nothing to compare; five turns the process into a part-time job.

Start by writing down the course precisely: MYP 4 standard, MYP 5 extended, DP1 AA HL, DP2 AI SL. Ask your child's school, not the tutor, if you are unsure. Next, list the next three dates that matter: the next topic test, the mock window and the Internal Assessment (IA) deadline for DP students. Then describe the difficulty in one sentence in the student's own words if possible. "I understand it in class but lose marks in tests" is a different problem from "I do not understand what the question is asking."

Finally, decide what is workable. A tutor who needs ninety minutes of weekday evening travel will cancel more often than one who is ten minutes from your society, and an online tutor who is a genuine specialist may beat a nearby generalist. Where your tutor is based and how the lesson is delivered is a separate decision, discussed in [IB home tutor vs online tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-home-tutor-vs-online-tutor-gurgaon/).

For programme-specific profiles, the pages for [IB Math AA HL tutors in Gurugram](/ib-tutors/gurugram/math-aa-hl/), [IB Math AI HL tutors in Gurugram](/ib-tutors/gurugram/math-ai-hl/) and [MYP maths tutors in Gurgaon](/ib-myp-maths-tutor-in-gurgaon/) show the profiles currently matching each course.

### What to put in the brief

Send each candidate the same brief so you compare like with like: course and level, school, next assessment dates, the last two marked papers or tests with marks and teacher comments, the topics being taught now and a note on whether the student prefers working at home or online. If a tutor replies with a package price before reading any of this, cross them off.

## Stage Two: What Questions Should You Ask in the Interview?

Ask questions that make the tutor describe a process in specifics. The best interview questions cannot be answered with a slogan: "How do you find out what my child does not understand?" cannot be answered with "I am very patient." Below is the working list, grouped by what each question reveals.

### On the course

1. Which exact mathematics courses have you taught, at which level, and what proportion of your current students are in each?
2. What is the structure of the assessment in this course, and how does that shape what you teach?
3. For DP: how do you handle Paper 3 at HL, and where does the IA fit in the plan?
4. For MYP: how do you teach criterion C, communication, and criterion D, applying mathematics, as distinct from criterion A?

A tutor who has taught the course answers these in specifics. For AA HL, the assessment is papers plus an IA, and the flagship guide to [scoring a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/) sets out the weightings, so you can check what you hear against it. Someone who answers "we cover the syllabus topic by topic" has not described assessment at all.

### On diagnosis

5. What will you do in the first session, and what will I receive afterwards?
6. How would you tell the difference between a student who does not know a topic and one who knows it but loses marks?
7. Can you describe a student with a problem similar to my child's and what you changed?

You are listening for a diagnosis that includes error types: algebraic slips, misread command terms, missing justification, wrong method choice under time pressure, and unfamiliarity with the graphic display calculator (GDC). If the answer is "I will assess their level," ask what that involves.

### On method

8. How is a typical session structured?
9. How much of the time does the student spend writing?
10. What do you do when a student gives a wrong answer?
11. What do you set between sessions, and what do you do with it?

The ideal answer to question nine is "most of it." A session in which the tutor writes and the student watches is a recording of the tutor's thinking, and the student leaves feeling informed and unchanged. The structure should alternate: a short explanation, a student attempt, feedback on the attempt, then another attempt.

### On integrity and boundaries

12. What is your role in the IA?
13. What would you not do?

The correct answer is that the student writes the exploration alone, and that the tutor may teach the skills, explain the criteria and question the student's reasoning. A tutor who is vague here is a risk you do not need.

### On the practicalities

14. How will you tell me whether it is working?
15. What would make you recommend fewer sessions, a pause, or a different tutor?

The second question tests honesty. A tutor with a waiting list and a long list of satisfied students will answer it directly. A tutor who needs your hours will struggle.

:::bars What I listen for in an interview (illustrative weighting)
Specific course and assessment knowledge | 90 | Critical
A diagnostic that names error types | 80 | Critical
Student writes during sessions | 70 | High
Clear stance on IA authorship | 70 | High
Willingness to say what they will not do | 55 | Medium
Years of experience | 25 | Low
These weights are my own judgement from tutoring, not research data.
:::

## Stage Three: How Should You Run the Trial Lesson?

Run the trial with real work, not a demonstration topic the tutor chose. Give them one recent test or piece of homework that your child lost marks on, and a topic coming up in school. Then watch three things: what the tutor asks, how much the student writes, and what happens after the student makes a mistake.

A trial built around the tutor's best material is a performance. A trial built around your child's marked work is an audition for the actual job.

Here is the sequence I recommend.

1. Hand over the marked work in advance and ask for nothing in return. Whether the tutor arrives having looked at it tells you about their preparation.
2. Let the tutor open with questions. In the first five to ten minutes they should be asking the student to explain what they did on a wrong answer, not showing their own solution.
3. Look for a single concept being taught and then tested at once. One idea, one attempt by the student, one round of feedback.
4. Notice the tutor's reaction to a wrong attempt. The better response is a question that lets the student find the slip. The worse response is "no, it is this."
5. See whether the student is asked to write full working, with notation, as they would in an exam or an assessed task.
6. At the end, ask the student to say what they will do before the next session. If the plan is the tutor's and not the student's, the session was lecture.
7. Within a day, expect a short written note: two or three priorities, evidence for each, and a proposal for the next four weeks.

### What a good trial sounds like

In a good trial, the loudest voice is the student's. A typical exchange sounds like this: the tutor asks "tell me what you were thinking when you chose the quadratic formula here," the student explains, and the tutor says "so what does the question give you that could have told you to factorise instead?" That is teaching a decision, not a method. Contrast it with a trial in which the tutor says "you should have factorised, see, it factorises" and moves to the next question. The second tutor may be right about the maths and entirely useless for the student's next unseen problem.

### What the student should say afterwards

Ask your child three things privately: what became clearer, what did you work out yourself, and what do you need to do this week. The second answer is the one that counts. If the student cannot name anything they worked out themselves, the tutor did the thinking.

## How Does the Choice Differ for MYP, AA and AI Students?

The process is the same, but the evidence you look for changes with the course. In MYP you are looking for criterion-aware teaching; in AA, for depth of reasoning and proof under time pressure; in AI, for comfort with modelling, statistics and GDC-heavy work.

| Course | What the tutor must show | A telling trial question |
|---|---|---|
| MYP standard | Clear explanation of criteria A to D, patterns and communication | "How do you teach justification, not just answers?" |
| MYP extended | The above plus preparation for DP AA content | "Which topics here feed AA HL, and how do you introduce them?" |
| DP AA SL | Algebra, functions, calculus, reasoning, calculator discipline | "How do you stop a student losing method marks?" |
| DP AA HL | Proof, complex numbers, vectors, harder calculus, Paper 3 | "Walk me through an unfamiliar Paper 3 problem." |
| DP AI SL and HL | Modelling, statistics, heavy GDC use, the AI exploration | "What do you do when the calculator gives an answer the student cannot interpret?" |

In MYP, the four criteria are Knowing and understanding, Investigating patterns, Communicating, and Applying mathematics in real-life contexts, each scored 0 to 8 and converted to a grade of 1 to 7. A tutor who only trains criterion A will help a student answer familiar questions and leave three quarters of the assessment untouched. The detail of those criteria is in [MYP maths criteria A, B, C and D explained for parents](/blog/ib-myp-maths-criteria-a-b-c-d-explained-for-parents/).

For students deciding between AA and AI, the choice of subject is a school and university question before it is a tutoring question. [IB Maths AA vs AI](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/) lays out the differences. A tutor who pushes a particular choice without asking about the student's intended degree should be treated with caution; the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement.

## What Should You Check About the Tutor's Use of the GDC?

Check that the tutor teaches the calculator as a tool with limits and not as a replacement for thinking. A student who treats the Graphic Display Calculator (GDC) as an answer machine loses method marks and cannot adapt when a question is set so that the calculator does not simply produce the result.

Ask the tutor three things. Which model do they know well? When would they tell a student not to use it? How do they teach a student to record the calculator's contribution in written working, for example stating the equation entered and the solution read from the graph? In AI, where technology runs through the course, the tutor should be fluent in statistics functions, regression, distributions and numerical solving. In AA, the GDC matters most in Paper 2 and Paper 3 and in checking, and where it hurts is when a student reaches for it to avoid algebra that the paper expects shown. The techniques and the failure modes are covered in [the GDC advantage in IB DP Mathematics AA HL](/blog/the-gdc-advantage-in-ib-dp-mathematics-aa-hl/).

## Stage Four: How Do You Run the First-Month Review?

Run the review at the end of week four with a fixed agenda: look at the work, compare with the diagnosis, check independence and decide to continue, adjust or stop. Do not use a test mark as the only measure; one test is noisy, and a single bad paper does not mean the tutoring failed.

Sit down with your child and the tutor's notes, and answer five questions.

1. Did the priorities named after the first session actually get worked on?
2. Is there a pair of attempts, original and corrected, for each priority?
3. Has the same error appeared less often in the student's own homework and school work?
4. Is the student starting problems with less prompting than a month ago?
5. Has the student been writing full working, including justification, without being asked?

If you can answer yes to three or more, continue. If you can answer yes to fewer than three, ask for a reset: a short mini-diagnostic, revised priorities and a date. If the tutor welcomes this, the match can recover. If the tutor is defensive or has no records, switch.

:::timeline What the first month should show
Week 1 | Diagnostic and written priorities
Week 2 | First corrected attempts on those priorities
Week 3 | Mixed questions, fewer prompts
Week 4 | Repeat a comparable task, then review
:::

### An example of a month-one review

Consider a typical DP1 AA HL student whose diagnosis named three priorities: algebraic manipulation with logarithms, justification in "show that" questions and reading command terms. At the review, the parent asks to see the four weeks of work. The folder shows a messy log-law attempt in week one, a clean corrected version in week two, and an unseen question in week three that the student began without help and finished with one slip. On the "show that" question, the student's written working now includes the line that proves the result. Command terms are still being missed, and the tutor says so. That is a month worth continuing: two priorities have moved, one has not, and the tutor knows which.

Contrast it with a review where the folder holds only the tutor's worked solutions, the student has done none of the homework and the tutor says "he is doing well." Nothing in that month is checkable.

## When Should You Switch Tutors?

Switch when two consecutive reviews show no movement in the student's own work, when the tutor cannot show a diagnosis or a plan, or when anything touching academic integrity arises. Do not switch because of a single poor test, one awkward session or the student's mood on a bad week.

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Student dislikes the tutor's manner after three or four sessions | Talk to the student; consider a different match |
| No homework set or reviewed by week three | Ask for a plan; switch if none arrives |
| Tutor writes most of the working | Reset expectations once, then switch |
| Tutor offers to write or tidy IA work | End the arrangement |
| One poor test after a good month | Hold; look at the errors |
| Student improves in class, not yet in tests | Give it two assessment cycles |

Switching is not a failure of the process. It is the process working. The cost of an extra month with the wrong tutor is far larger than the awkwardness of an honest conversation. If you booked through IB Gram, you can ask for a different match, subject to tutor availability, and a good tutor will not take it personally.

## How Do You Check That the Tutor Handles the IA Correctly?

The IA in mathematics, the exploration, is the same task for AA and AI at both levels: 20% of the final grade, 12 to 20 pages, marked out of 20 on five criteria. Presentation is worth 4, mathematical communication 4, personal engagement 3, reflection 3 and use of mathematics 6. The work must be the student's own. Ask the tutor to describe their role in plain terms.

A sound answer sounds like this: help the student find a topic that interests them and that has enough mathematics in it, teach any technique the topic needs, ask questions about the reasoning, point at a criterion and ask the student to reread their own paragraph against it. Anything beyond that, writing paragraphs, supplying data analysis, or tidying the language into a final form, is outside the boundary. The IB's academic integrity policy allows the teacher feedback on one draft and does not allow anyone to write or rewrite the submission. If you want a dedicated IA tutor rather than a general maths tutor, the [IB maths IA tutor in Gurgaon](/ib-maths-ia-tutor-in-gurgaon/) page is the starting point, and the broader guide is [the IB Internal Assessment guide](/blog/ib-internal-assessment-guide/).

## What About Tutors Who Claim IB Examiner Experience?

Take the claim seriously and test it, since marking experience, if real, is valuable. Ask which subject and session, and then ask them to show how they read a script: where they look first, what earns a method mark and what a "follow through" error looks like in practice. A genuine examiner can walk through a marking scheme and explain why a particular line earns or loses a mark.

Treat unverifiable claims, such as "my students all scored 7," as neutral and look for method instead. Results depend far more on the student's starting point and effort than on the tutor, and quoted scores come from students you cannot meet. A tutor who describes the diagnosis and the plan is giving you something you can check against your own child's work.

## How Does a Strong Tutor Teach Mathematical Communication?

A strong tutor teaches students to show their reasoning in the form the mark scheme rewards: a stated method, correct notation, an intermediate result and a conclusion in context. This is a skill taught separately from solving, and it is where many capable students lose a quarter of their marks.

In the trial, watch what happens when the student solves a problem correctly but writes it sloppily. A weak tutor praises the answer. A strong tutor asks the student to rewrite it as if the reader could not see their head. Over a month you should see the written working improve visibly: the equals signs lined up, units and conclusions stated, assumptions named.

For MYP, communication is its own criterion, scored 0 to 8, so this skill has direct value. For DP, it separates marks on paper after paper, because method marks are awarded for working that is shown. The deeper treatment of how problem solving and reasoning are built is in [IB MYP Mathematics reasoning skills](/blog/ib-myp-mathematics-problem-solving-reasoning-skills/).

## What Does a Good Tutor Do for Students Moving Up From IGCSE or MYP?

A good tutor for a transition year starts by mapping the gap between where the student is and where the new course begins, then closes it before the pace picks up. The IGCSE to DP step and the MYP 5 to DP1 step are the two points where otherwise strong students most often stall.

A student coming from Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics, especially the Extended tier, or Additional Mathematics, will meet familiar algebra at a very different depth in AA, and will meet a heavier writing demand in every course. A tutor should be able to say which skills carry over and which do not, and propose a bridge before DP1 begins. The detailed gap analysis is in [from IGCSE mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/), and the two-year calendar for the course itself is in [how to prepare for IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-prepare-for-ib-dp-mathematics-aa-hl/).

## What Is My Approach as a Tutor?

I am Ajay Vatsyayan, IB Gram's IB Mathematics mentor, and I can describe the way I work so that you have something concrete to compare other tutors against. I do not claim it is the only sound approach.

**Diagnosis before lessons.** No two students at the same grade are there for the same reason. One has weak algebraic fluency; another understands everything but writes too little to earn method marks. The starting point is a look at your child's actual scripts: which topics, which error types, which papers.

**Seven-level thinking.** Pattern recognition and multi-step reasoning are trained directly: how to look at an unfamiliar problem and identify the mathematics hiding inside it. It is a teachable skill and it is rarely taught on purpose.

**An error-correction habit.** Mistakes are analysed for why they happened and what habit would stop them happening again. Recurring errors are treated as habits to be trained, not as carelessness to be lamented.

**Exam technique.** Timed drills, marks-to-minutes discipline, paper-specific tactics and presentation habits that keep method marks on the page.

## What Does a Typical Weekly Pattern Look Like Once You Have Chosen?

Once chosen, most students do well on a simple weekly pattern: one focused session, one block of independent practice drawn from the session's errors, and one short review in the following session. The pattern is less important than the discipline of keeping it.

In a typical week, the session opens with ten minutes reviewing the previous week's corrected work. The middle is a new idea with an immediate attempt. The close sets one or two tasks that the student will do alone, and the next session begins by checking them. A DP2 student in mock season may add a second weekly session for timed paper sections, while an MYP student may be better served by a shorter session and a lighter load. For exam-season preparation by paper, see [IB Math AA HL paper preparation](/blog/ib-math-aa-hl-paper-preparation-gurgaon/), and for the topic-by-topic playbook see [IB Maths AA HL topic-by-topic strategy](/blog/how-to-score-a-7-in-ib-maths-aa-hl-step-by-step-strategy/).

## Do You Even Need a Tutor?

Not every student does, and the honest first step in how to choose an IB maths tutor is deciding whether to choose one at all. A student who is working at the grade the school predicts, understands where marks are lost and has a teacher who gives useful feedback may need nothing more than more past papers and a clear error log. Starting a tutor because everyone else has one is the weakest reason.

A tutor earns their place when the student has a specific, diagnosable gap, when school pace and the student's pace are out of step, when the student has stopped attempting questions they do not recognise, or when an IA or mock deadline demands structured help. If none of these apply, wait a term and watch. Waiting costs very little, and starting too early with no clear need creates a dependency that is harder to unwind. The question of timing is considered in [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if an IB maths tutor really knows the IB course?

Ask them to explain how your child's course is assessed, and to do it in specifics. For MYP, they should name the four criteria. For DP, they should describe the papers, the IA and how Paper 3 works at HL in AA. A tutor who only talks about chapters and syllabus coverage has described the content, not the assessment, and that is a reliable warning.

### Should I choose an IB maths tutor who has taught my child's school?

It can help, but it should not decide the question. Familiarity with a school's pace and style saves time, though the school's rules on teachers tutoring their own students vary, and an independent tutor can be more useful. Choose on course knowledge, diagnosis and method. Share the school calendar with whichever tutor you pick so that sessions follow what is being taught.

### How long should a trial lesson be?

A full session, normally the same length as the regular one, is best. Thirty minutes is too short to see how a tutor handles a mistake and what the student does alone. Use real marked work, and hold the trial at the time and in the mode you plan to use, so that you also test the logistics of home travel, gate entry or the online setup.

### What if my child likes the tutor but marks do not improve?

Check the evidence before the marks. Look for corrected attempts, fewer repeated errors and more independent starts. If those are present, give it two assessment cycles because marks lag. If the folder is empty and the sessions are pleasant, the tutor is a friendly presence and not a teacher. Ask for a reset with revised priorities and a review date.

### Is one hour a week enough for DP maths?

For most students outside exam season, yes, provided independent practice follows. The hour is the starting point and the practice between sessions does the learning. Add a second session for mocks, an IA deadline or a short intervention, not as a default. More sessions without more practice rarely help and often tire the student.

### Can a tutor help with both school maths and IB-style questions?

Yes, and a good one will treat them as connected. The tutor should follow the school's current topic so the work is useful immediately, and then extend it with IB-style questions that test reasoning and communication. A tutor who ignores the school topic risks confusing the student, and one who only repeats school homework misses the assessment skills.

### What should I do if the tutor wants to write part of the IA?

End the conversation and find a different tutor. IB rules require the IA to be the student's own work, and the school checks for similarity. A tutor may teach the skills, explain the criteria and question the student's reasoning, and nothing more. Offering to write or tidy a submission puts your child's diploma at risk.

### Do I need a tutor who is also a school teacher?

No. Teaching experience helps with explanation, but what matters is whether the tutor can diagnose and teach to the IB's assessment. Some excellent tutors have never been classroom teachers, and some classroom teachers teach a tutorial class as if it were a lecture hall. Judge on the trial and the first-month evidence, not on the job title.

### How much should I be involved?

Less than you might think. Keep the schedule, protect sleep and ask three questions each week: what did you work on, what was hard, what is next. Do not supervise the maths yourself. Attend the month-one review, look at the evidence together and leave day-to-day teaching to the tutor and the student.

### Can an online tutor be as effective as a home tutor for maths?

Yes, when the tutor uses a pen tablet or shared whiteboard, the student writes during the session and work is saved afterwards. Online suits specialists who are far away and families with tight weekdays. Home suits students who need routine and a physical workspace. Many DP students do best on a mix of both. No mode is automatically better.
