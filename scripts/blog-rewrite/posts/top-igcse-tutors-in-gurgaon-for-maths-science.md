## Why do maths and science need support at the same time?

Maths and science need support together because they share a skill base and a calendar. Science calculations depend on algebra, rearranging formulae, graphs and units, so a gap in maths shows up as lost marks in Physics and Chemistry. Both subjects also reach their heaviest assessment weeks in the same series, so a student weak in both is under double pressure.

Consider a student who scores well in Biology theory but struggles with Physics numericals. The problem is often not Physics at all. It is rearranging a formula, handling powers of ten or reading a graph gradient, which are maths skills. A Physics tutor can patch the symptom for a term. A maths tutor who looks at the algebra fixes the cause. That is why the first session with any tutor should include a diagnostic that asks where the lost marks actually come from.

:::steps How maths gaps leak into the sciences
Algebra | Rearranging formulae and solving for unknowns
Graphs | Gradients, intercepts and proportionality
Units | Conversions, standard form, significant figures
Data | Averages, rates and percentages in practicals
:::

The reverse also happens. Students who understand the physics of motion often find the calculus-free kinematics in Additional Mathematics easier, and chemistry's mole calculations reinforce proportional reasoning. The subjects are not separate silos, and a plan that treats them as such misses the transfer.

## How do IGCSE Maths and the science papers differ in what they test?

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 rewards method, accuracy and reading the question; the sciences reward precise command-word answers, calculations and practical skills. Maths papers are split by calculator use, while science papers are split by question type: multiple choice, theory and practical. The prep for each is different even where the skills overlap.

For Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams), Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), and Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier. Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606 has no tiers, two written papers with a calculator allowed, and is graded A* to E.

For Cambridge IGCSE sciences (syllabuses examined from 2023), Paper 1 is multiple choice for Core, or Paper 2 for Extended; Paper 3 is theory for Core, or Paper 4 for Extended; and practical assessment comes through Paper 5 (practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), chosen by the school. The weighting is multiple choice 30%, theory 50% and practical 20%. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with Paper 1 and Paper 2 for each separate science and practical skills assessed inside the written papers.

| Feature | IGCSE Mathematics | IGCSE Sciences |
|---|---|---|
| Main marks | Method and accuracy | Recall, command words, calculation |
| Split of papers | Calculator versus non-calculator | Multiple choice, theory, practical |
| Tiers | Core and Extended (0580); Foundation and Higher (4MA1) | Core and Extended in Cambridge; untiered in Edexcel |
| Usual lost marks | Skipped working, rounding, misread questions | Wrong depth for the command word, units, planning in practicals |
| Practice that helps | Timed question sets, error log | Retrieval, calculation drills, practical-style questions |

Checking codes matters. Mathematics 0580, Biology 0610, Physics 0625 and Chemistry 0620 are the Cambridge codes most families meet, and Edexcel's equivalents are 4MA1, 4BI1, 4PH1 and 4CH1. Your school's entry sheet states exactly which ones your child sits.

## Should you choose one tutor or two from IGCSE maths and science tutors in Gurgaon?

When comparing IGCSE maths and science tutors in Gurgaon, choose one tutor when only one subject is weak or when the student is in Grade 9 and needs a steady rhythm; choose two when both maths and a science are weak, or when a specialist is needed for each. One tutor covering both rarely has the depth for all three sciences plus maths. A pair of focused specialists usually beats one generalist.

| Situation | One tutor | Two tutors |
|---|---|---|
| Maths is weak, sciences are fine | Fits: one maths specialist | Not needed |
| Sciences are weak because of algebra | Fits: a maths tutor who knows the science link | Optional |
| Maths and one science are both weak | Possible only if the tutor is strong in both | Often better: a specialist each |
| Student aims for A* in Extended maths and separate sciences | Rare to find one tutor with all the depth | Usually better |
| Budget or timetable is tight | A single tutor with a clear priority | Not realistic |
| Student is in Grade 9 and building habits | One tutor, one rhythm | Not needed yet |

Several things tip the choice.

**The syllabus breadth.** IGCSE has three separate sciences and a maths syllabus (and sometimes Additional Mathematics). A tutor who claims equal mastery of all of them should be asked to demonstrate it in a trial, using a real question from each.

**The calendar.** Two tutors means two weekly slots and two sets of homework. For a student with school, activities and six other subjects, that is a real cost in time, not only fees. **The handover.** With two tutors, someone must connect the dots when a physics error is really an algebra error. A short note from each tutor to the family after each fortnight solves it.

If you are unsure, start with one tutor for the weakest subject and review after a month. Adding a second is easy; unwinding two commitments is harder. How to run that selection is covered in [how to choose the right IGCSE tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-igcse-tutor-in-gurgaon/).

## What does a realistic weekly split look like?

A realistic week for a Grade 10 student with maths and one or two sciences in need is two to three tutor sessions of 60 to 90 minutes, plus 3 to 4 hours of independent practice, with each tutor session followed by a task due before the next. More hours do not help if the student cannot finish the work between lessons.

:::bars Illustrative weekly study hours for a student needing maths and science help
Independent practice | 55 | about 4 hours
Maths tutor session | 25 | about 1.5 hours
Science tutor session | 25 | about 1.5 hours
Marking and error log | 20 | about 1 hour
A planning example, not an official figure; scale it to the student's other subjects and the series date.
:::

An illustrative pattern for a Grade 10 student in the term before the series:

1. **Monday:** maths session on one weak topic, with a past-paper section due Thursday.
2. **Wednesday:** science session (the weakest of the three sciences), practical-style or calculation work.
3. **Thursday:** independent maths set, marked with the scheme.
4. **Saturday:** timed past-paper section, alternating maths and science.
5. **Sunday:** error log update and the week's plan, 45 minutes.

In Grade 9, the same shape with lighter loads works: one session per week for the subject that needs help, plus short regular practice. The point is rhythm, not volume. ## What does each tutor do when you use two?

With two tutors, give each a clear brief so they do not duplicate or contradict each other. The maths tutor owns method, accuracy and the non-calculator and calculator paper habits. The science tutor owns command-word answers, calculation structure and the practical-style questions. Both use the same error log and the same past-paper source.

A short written brief for each tutor helps: the board and code, the tier, the series date, the last mock marks by paper, the two largest causes of lost marks, and what the family wants by the next review. Share each tutor's name with the other and agree that a one-paragraph update goes to the family every two weeks. If the tutors disagree on a method, such as how to lay out a calculation, ask which one the mark scheme rewards and follow that. Most disagreements disappear when both look at the same scheme.

## How do you make the maths and science work reinforce each other?

Make them reinforce each other by tagging errors by skill, not by subject, and by giving each tutor the same error log. When a physics error is really a rearrangement error, log it as algebra and ask the maths tutor to drill it. This one habit often removes the most stubborn mid-grade plateau.

A shared error log has four columns: the question, the lost marks, the true cause and the fix. Causes recur across subjects: rearranging, units, significant figures, graph reading, misreading the command word, missing working. A tutor who sees that two of the five physics errors were rearrangement can move one maths session to rearrangement drills.

Three transfer exercises worth building into the plan:

- **Formula rearrangement sets.** Take physics equations from the syllabus and rearrange each for every variable. Do it in a maths session; use it in a physics one.
- **Graph interpretation.** Take a practical-style data table, plot it, find the gradient and say what it means. The same skill appears in coordinate geometry and in Paper 6 style questions.
- **Standard form and units.** Convert and compute with powers of ten across both subjects until it is automatic.

## How do you judge a tutor without relying on a "top tutor" label?

Judge a tutor by their diagnostic, their marking, their independence plan and a trial lesson, not by a ranking, a claimed result or a number of years. A ranked list tells you what someone chose to publish. A trial lesson tells you what the tutor does with your child.

Use this five-point check:

1. **Diagnostic.** In the first session, does the tutor look at your child's own marked work and name two or three specific causes of lost marks? A promise to "cover the syllabus" is not a diagnosis.
2. **Marking.** Can the tutor show how a mark scheme awards a four-mark question and teach your child to write to it? For sciences, can they explain the difference between "state", "describe" and "explain" with examples?
3. **Syllabus precision.** Does the tutor know your code and tier, and the paper structure? A tutor who is vague about whether your child sits Paper 2 or Paper 4 has not prepared.
4. **Independence.** What does your child do between sessions, and does the tutor check it? A student who only works with the tutor is not learning to work alone.
5. **Trial lesson.** Ask for a short lesson on one real problem. Watch who does the thinking.

Red flags include guaranteed grades (no tutor can guarantee a grade), reluctance to show how they teach, a plan that is the same for every student, and homework that is never reviewed. ## When is online better for maths and science?

Online is better when the specialist you want is not nearby, when traffic or weather makes evening visits unreliable, or when the student has a clashing activity. It works well for maths and for science theory and calculation with a digital pen or a camera on paper. Home is better when a student concentrates better in person or needs help with handwriting, diagrams and apparatus drawings.

The honest limit for sciences is hands-on laboratory work, which depends on the school. Online tutors can teach planning, tabulating, graph plotting and evaluation very well, but not replace the school's practical sessions. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need separate tutors for IGCSE maths and science?

Not always. One tutor suits a student whose weakness sits in one subject, or whose science trouble is really algebra. Two specialists usually suit a student weak in both maths and a science, or aiming for A* in Extended maths and separate sciences. Start with the subject that costs the most marks, review after a month and add a second tutor only if the plan needs it.

### Can one tutor teach Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Maths?

It is rare to find one tutor with equal depth in all four. Some tutors are strong in maths and Physics, or in Chemistry and Biology, but claiming equal mastery everywhere should be tested in a trial using real questions from each subject. A specialist for each weak subject is usually safer, and a good tutor will say where their depth ends.

### How many hours a week do maths and science tutoring need?

Two to three sessions of 60 to 90 minutes a week is typical for a Grade 10 student needing help in both, with three to four hours of independent practice. Grade 9 usually needs less, often one session a week. More hours do not help if the student cannot complete the work between sessions, so the independent task is the part to check.

### Why is my child weak in Physics but fine in Biology?

Physics leans on algebra, graphs and unit handling, while Biology leans more on recall and description. A student comfortable with memorising can score well in Biology and still lose marks in Physics calculations. A diagnostic usually shows a maths skill, such as rearranging a formula, behind the Physics gap, which is why a maths tutor can sometimes fix a science problem.

### What should I ask in a trial lesson?

Ask the tutor to teach one real problem from your child's recent paper and watch who does the thinking. Check whether the tutor diagnoses the cause of the error, explains how the mark scheme awards marks, and sets a task for between lessons. A strong trial leaves your child with a clearer view of one specific habit to change.

### Is Cambridge IGCSE different from Edexcel for tutoring?

Yes, in papers, codes and grading. Cambridge grades most syllabuses A* to G, while Pearson Edexcel International GCSE grades 9 to 1. Cambridge sciences have Core and Extended tiers; Edexcel sciences are untiered. A tutor should use past papers and mark schemes from your exact board and code, and know which paper your child sits.

### Can an online tutor handle science practical papers?

Yes for the skills, with a limit. Planning an experiment, drawing tables, plotting graphs and evaluating methods can be taught well online, and these are the marks in Paper 5 and Paper 6 style questions. Hands-on laboratory handling depends on the school's practical sessions. If your child's school offers few of them, ask the tutor how they will cover the skills.

### When is it too late to get help before the series?

It is rarely too late for a focused plan, but the closer the exam, the narrower the target. With three months left a full rebuild is possible. With four to six weeks, work on the single largest cause of lost marks and on timed practice. In the final fortnight, protect sleep and consolidate. A tutor cannot guarantee a grade at any stage.

### Should my child take Additional Mathematics alongside Extended Maths?

Often it suits students heading for IB Mathematics AA HL or A Level Mathematics. Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606 has no tiers, two calculator papers and is usually taken alongside 0580 Extended. It adds workload, so the decision depends on the student's strength and plans. The [IGCSE maths parent guide](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/) explains the trade-offs.

Ask what verification involved and request the tutor's profile, subject and level coverage before the demo.
