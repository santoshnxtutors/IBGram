## Why Is Class 10 the Bridge Year for IGCSE Students in DLF Phase 5?

Class 10 is the bridge year because the IGCSE results and school decisions that follow shape which subjects a student can study next, and the habits formed in this year carry straight into the new course. A student who reaches the DP having practised only recall questions meets a sharp change in the first term. A student who has already been working with unfamiliar problems, written explanations and independent study usually does not.

The IGCSE in India is usually a two-year course across Grades 9 and 10. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November; Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. The February/March Cambridge series lets Grade 10 results arrive in time for admission decisions for Class 11 and for IB DP or A Level entry in the same academic year. Schools decide which series they enter students for. A student cannot choose a series independently of the school, so the first step is to ask the school which one applies.

Because the timeline is tight, the decisions overlap. Subject choices for the next programme are often made while the student is still working on IGCSE content. A tutor who sees both horizons can keep the two from competing.

## What Does the Next Stage Expect From an IGCSE Student?

Each destination expects something different. The IB DP expects independent work, extended writing and unfamiliar problems across six subjects plus Theory of Knowledge (TOK), the Extended Essay (EE) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS). Cambridge AS and A Levels expect depth in fewer subjects, usually three or four, with paper-based exams. CBSE and ISC Class 11 expect a fast change in pace and a heavier content load, with a different assessment style again.

| Destination | Typical structure | What changes for the student | What to build in Class 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| IB Diploma Programme | Two years, six subjects, three or four at Higher Level (HL) and the rest at Standard Level (SL); TOK, EE and CAS | Independence, extended writing, internal assessments, wider reading | Reasoning in writing, unfamiliar problems, research habits |
| Cambridge AS and A Levels | Fewer subjects in more depth, paper-based | Depth and speed; fewer chances to recover | Strong content in the chosen subjects, paper technique |
| CBSE or ISC Class 11 | Board syllabus, content-heavy | Pace, memorised derivations and answer formats | Content fluency, written presentation in board style |

The DP has the most moving parts. It is a two-year course; each subject is graded 1 to 7, TOK and the EE combine for up to 3 more points, so the maximum is 45, and 24 points is the minimum for the diploma, subject to the IB's failing conditions. HL carries 240 recommended teaching hours and SL 150. The DP's May session is the one almost all Indian IB schools use, with results in early July.

For students who will move between systems, the Association of Indian Universities issues equivalence for international qualifications, and requirements differ by university and course. Families should confirm with the institution rather than assume.

## Which IGCSE Maths Topics Become the Foundation for IB Maths?

The IGCSE topics that matter most for DP Mathematics are algebraic manipulation, functions, quadratics, trigonometry, logarithms and exponentials, sequences and series, and basic coordinate geometry. Students who finish Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 Extended, and especially those who add Additional Mathematics 0606, usually find DP Analysis and Approaches (AA) smoother than those who did Core alone.

DP Mathematics comes in two subjects, each at HL and SL: Analysis and Approaches, which is algebraic and proof-heavy, and Applications and Interpretation (AI), which is modelling and statistics-heavy with technology used throughout. Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and on the student, and the school's DP coordinator makes the final placement.

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams): Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator); Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier. Core tops out at grade C and Extended reaches A*, so the tier already tells you something about readiness for AA.

The additional mathematics course 0606 has no tiers, two written papers with a calculator allowed, and is graded A* to E. It covers functions, quadratic functions, factors of polynomials, equations, inequalities and graphs, simultaneous equations, logarithmic and exponential functions, straight-line graphs, coordinate geometry of the circle, circular measure, trigonometry, permutations and combinations, series, vectors in two dimensions, differentiation and integration. It is usually taken alongside 0580 Extended by students heading for IB Mathematics AA HL or A Level Mathematics. Pearson Edexcel's nearest equivalent is International GCSE Further Pure Mathematics (4PM1).

### What the jump looks like in practice

A student who scored well in IGCSE Extended often finds the first weeks of DP1 AA HL comfortable and the next six weeks sharp. The difference is not difficulty of individual topics, it is what the questions ask. A typical IGCSE question tells the student what to do ("Solve", "Find"). A typical DP question asks the student to show, prove, explain or decide, and then offers no obvious method. The student who has never practised starting from nothing freezes.

Bridge work therefore has two strands. The first is closing content gaps: for example, completing the square fluently, working with surds without a calculator, trigonometric identities, and the laws of logarithms. The second is changing the style of practice: attempting a question before looking at any hint, writing a sentence on what the question is asking, and justifying each step. ## Which IGCSE Science Topics Matter Before the DP or A Levels?

For the sciences, what carries over is not a list of facts but a set of skills and a few foundation topics that the next course assumes without teaching again. Students going to DP Physics, Chemistry or Biology, or to A Level, should be fluent in these.

### Physics

Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 covers motion, forces and energy; thermal physics; waves; electricity and magnetism; nuclear physics; and space physics. Students must recall and rearrange the equations in the syllabus. The foundation for the next stage is kinematics and forces, energy, and basic circuits, plus the ability to rearrange an equation symbolically before substituting. Weak rearranging is the commonest quiet problem.

### Chemistry

Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 covers states of matter; atoms, elements and compounds; stoichiometry (including the mole); electrochemistry; chemical energetics; chemical reactions; acids, bases and salts; the Periodic Table; metals; chemistry of the environment; organic chemistry; and experimental techniques and chemical analysis. The foundation is the mole and stoichiometry, which every later chemistry topic uses, plus balancing equations and basic organic naming. A student who leaves IGCSE shaky on the mole will struggle in DP1.

### Biology

Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 covers characteristics and classification of living organisms; organisation of the organism; movement into and out of cells; biological molecules; enzymes; plant and human nutrition; transport in plants and animals; diseases and immunity; gas exchange; respiration; excretion; coordination and response; drugs; reproduction; inheritance; variation and selection; organisms and their environment; human influences on ecosystems; and biotechnology and genetic modification. The foundation is cell biology, enzymes, and genetics, with the habit of writing precise, short explanations of processes.

### The practical skills

Cambridge IGCSE sciences (syllabuses examined from 2023) assess practical work through Paper 5 (practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), chosen by the school. The weighting is multiple choice 30%, theory 50% and practical 20%. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with practical skills assessed inside the written papers. What carries into the next course is the data skill: reading tables and graphs, identifying variables, estimating uncertainty and evaluating a method. These skills are directly useful for the DP's internal assessment, which is the scientific investigation, worth 20%.

## How Do Subject Choices Connect to University Plans?

Subject choices at the end of Class 10 should connect to the broad direction of university study, not to a fixed course decision, because most students are not certain at fifteen. The sensible approach is to keep doors open where the cost is low and to commit only where a requirement is clear.

A short way to think about it is by direction.

| Possible direction | Subjects that keep it open | A note |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering, computer science | Mathematics (AA), Physics, often Chemistry or Computer Science | Check each target university's stated requirements |
| Medicine, life sciences | Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics | Requirements vary widely by country |
| Economics, finance | Mathematics (AA often preferred), Economics | Some courses prefer AA; check |
| Humanities, law, social science | English, History or Global Politics, a language, Economics | Strong writing and reading habits matter most |
| Undecided | A balanced set across groups | The DP is designed for breadth |

The details of combinations are covered in [IB DP STEM subject choice](/blog/stem-subject-choice-ib-dp/), including the regret scenarios. Two principles apply here. First, it is better to take a level you can sustain with strong grades than a heavier level that damages performance. Second, never rely on a rumour about what a university requires: check the university's own published requirements and ask the school counsellor.

Some schools offer IB and IGCSE; some offer IB only after an IGCSE; some limit which students can take HL maths based on their IGCSE tier. Rules differ from school to school, so ask the school's DP coordinator, who makes the final placement, what grade or tier is expected for each subject and level.

## What Should a Class 10 Student in DLF Phase 5 Do Over the Summer?

Use the summer to close specific gaps and start the new course's habits, not to rest entirely or to cram everything. A manageable plan of six to eight weeks, with a few hours a week per subject, is far better than a frantic burst in the final fortnight. This is especially true because the DP and A Levels both begin with a steep first term.

Timing depends on the series. Where the Cambridge February/March series applies, results arrive before the new academic year starts, and the "summer after IGCSE" is closer to a spring-to-summer window. Where the May/June series applies, the break is shorter and more precious. Check the school's own dates.

:::timeline A summer bridge plan for an IGCSE student heading to the DP
Weeks 1 and 2 | Rest, then a short diagnostic in each chosen subject
Weeks 3 and 4 | Close the largest content gap in maths and the main science
Weeks 5 and 6 | Reasoning practice: unfamiliar problems and written explanations
Weeks 7 and 8 | Preview the first DP unit; set up a weekly routine
:::

What to do inside each block:

- **Maths.** Revisit algebra, functions and trigonometry with questions that ask the student to justify, not only compute. Introduce a little proof, such as showing an identity or a divisibility result. Try questions from the first AA unit, like sequences, to feel the style.
- **Sciences.** Rework the mole and stoichiometry, kinematics and forces, and cell biology depending on the subject. Practise data questions with graphs and uncertainty.
- **English and humanities.** Read widely in the area the student will study; practise writing a short analytical paragraph weekly; learn to argue with evidence.
- **Habits.** Build a weekly timetable with study blocks, a place to work and a log of what was done. These habits matter more than any single topic.

The goal at the end of the summer is not mastery. It is a student who walks into the first week of the new course with no major gaps, some practice in the new style of question, and a routine.

## What Should a Good Tutor Do in the Final IGCSE Months and the Bridge?

A good tutor in the final months works on two tracks that share the same weekly sessions: past-paper technique for the series, and bridge skills for the next course. The balance shifts as the exam approaches, and what a session looks like changes with it.

In the term before the exams, sessions are mostly paper practice. The tutor sets a timed section, marks it with the mark scheme, sorts the lost marks into causes, and chooses the next week's work from that list. After the exams, the balance flips, and the tutor spends sessions on bridge topics, reasoning, and the first unit of the next course.

An example week for a Class 10 maths student on a bridge plan, after the exam series:

1. Monday: a short diagnostic on one bridge topic, such as logarithm laws, marked with the student.
2. Tuesday to Thursday: the student does targeted practice alone, with a log of any question that took more than five minutes.
3. Friday: a 60-minute session on the log: the tutor asks "why" about each stuck point.
4. Weekend: one unfamiliar problem to attempt for 30 minutes without help, with notes on what the student tried.

That rhythm is simple and works because the student is doing most of the thinking.

## Core or Extended, Cambridge or Edexcel: Does It Change the Bridge?

Yes, because the tier and board change what the student has already covered. Core candidates sit papers capped at grade C, so a student who did Core and wants DP AA HL has a larger gap to close than one who did Extended. Edexcel Higher tier covers more than Foundation in the same way. The board changes the style of questions and the grading scale, but the bridge topics are broadly the same.

| Situation | What the student likely has | Bridge priority |
|---|---|---|
| Extended or Higher tier, strong grade | Good algebra and trig base | Reasoning, proof, unfamiliar problems |
| Extended or Higher tier, borderline grade | Gaps in algebra or trig | Close gaps first, then reasoning |
| Core or Foundation tier | Limited algebraic depth | Honest conversation with the school about the right DP maths course |
| Additional maths 0606 or Further Pure 4PM1 taken | Calculus and series started | Keep it fresh; preview the first AA unit |
| Switching from CBSE or ICSE earlier | Different notation and style | Adapt to command terms and written reasoning |

If your child did Core and wants AA HL, talk to the school. Schools set their own placement rules, and a bridge programme can help in some cases and not in others. A tutor can help close gaps but cannot change the school's rule.

Edexcel students heading to the DP follow the same steps. Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with practical skills assessed inside the written papers, so the practical habits are built in the written work. [Edexcel IGCSE Mathematics A 4MA1 Higher tier](/blog/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-a-4ma1-higher-tier-tutor-in-gurugram-2026-guide/) covers that paper in detail.

## What About English and Humanities in the Bridge?

English and humanities bridge by skill, not by content: the DP and A Levels expect longer, more argued writing than most IGCSE papers reward. English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses: Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500, English as a Second Language 0510 and 0511, and Literature in English 0475, with Pearson Edexcel offering English Language A (4EA1), B (4EB1) and English as a Second Language (4ES1).

The DP version is different. DP Studies in Language and Literature offers English A Language and Literature, or English A Literature, with Paper 1 as guided textual analysis of unseen passages, Paper 2 as a comparative essay on two studied works, and the Individual Oral. HL students also write the HL essay of 1,200 to 1,500 words. Students whose first language is not English take Language Acquisition: English B, or for genuine beginners, English ab initio.

A student who has done IGCSE First Language English has a head start on analysis, but the DP asks for sustained argument and comparison. Over the summer, a student can practise a weekly analytical paragraph on a short unseen passage, read two or three longer texts, and practise explaining how the writer achieves effects. For economics, DP Economics has four units, with Paper 1 extended response and Paper 2 data response; IGCSE Economics 0455 gives a solid base in the main ideas. See [IGCSE English communication skills](/blog/igcse-english-communication-skills/) for the writing skills that carry over.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need an IGCSE tutor in DLF Phase 5 if my child is already doing well?

Not necessarily, but a light bridge plan can still help. A student with strong grades may need only a short diagnostic and a few sessions on reasoning and the first unit of the next course. The value is in preventing a rough first term, not rescuing a failing one. If your child has a good teacher and steady habits, self-study may be enough.

### When should a Class 10 student start preparing for the IB DP?

Start thinking in the second half of Grade 10 and begin focused work after the exam series, so that the break is used well. Earlier is useful only for specific gaps, such as maths foundations, because the final IGCSE exams need attention. Check your school's calendar and the date results arrive, and avoid leaving all bridge work until the last fortnight.

### Is IGCSE Extended Maths enough to take IB Maths AA HL?

It is a good base, but schools set their own placement rules, so ask the DP coordinator. Extended candidates reach A* and cover most algebra and trigonometry the DP assumes. Many students also take Additional Mathematics 0606 or an equivalent. Whether Extended alone is enough depends on the grade, the school and the student's habits, and a bridge plan can fill remaining gaps.

### Can a tutor help me choose between the IB DP and A Levels?

A tutor can give a view on how each feels at the subject level, but the decision is broader and belongs with the family, the school counsellor and the target universities. Think about the student's range of interests, preferred style of learning and likely university direction. The DP is broader with more coursework; A Levels are deeper in fewer subjects. Confirm requirements directly with universities.

### What do I do if my child did Core maths and wants HL?

Talk to the school first, because placement rules vary and some do not allow it. If the school allows it, plan a longer bridge in algebra, functions and trigonometry and be honest about workload. In some cases, SL or a different maths course is the wiser choice. A tutor can close gaps but cannot change the school's requirements.

### Do tutors in DLF Phase 5 come to the home?

Many do, and it is a common arrangement, with lessons in the evening or at weekends. Gated communities have gate procedures, so tell the tutor which gate and who will approve entry. Online is an alternative if the best-matched tutor lives elsewhere or traffic eats the slot. A hybrid plan is common. Availability is subject to tutor availability.

### How is IGCSE tutoring different from IB tutoring?

IGCSE tutoring is organised around papers, tiers and mark schemes; IB tutoring also includes criteria, internal assessments and extended writing. IGCSE focuses on technique across fixed syllabus content with external exams, while the IB adds coursework and a wider range of skills. A tutor for the bridge year should understand both, because the student needs to finish one well and prepare for the other.

### Should my child take both Cambridge and Edexcel subjects?

That is a school decision, not a family choice, so ask the school. Schools choose a board for each subject and series. Where a student sits papers from both, the tutor needs to know the differences in paper structure and grading. Cambridge grades most syllabuses A* to G, with some 9 to 1 versions; Edexcel International GCSE grades 9 to 1.

### What if my child's IGCSE results are not what we hoped?

Talk to the school about options before reacting. Retakes in a later series may be possible, and subject or programme choices can sometimes be adjusted. A tutor can help identify what went wrong and plan a recovery, but the plan depends on the school's rules and the next programme's entry requirements. Avoid decisions made in a rush in the first week.
