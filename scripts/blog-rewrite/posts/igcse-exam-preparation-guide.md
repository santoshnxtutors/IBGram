IGCSE exam preparation is easiest when you separate three questions that families usually mix up: which exam series your school has entered you for, which paper and tier each subject uses, and how you will revise several subjects in the same weeks. Answer those three and the plan more or less writes itself. Skip them and a student can spend a month revising the wrong topics for the wrong paper.

This is the hub guide for Cambridge IGCSE and Pearson Edexcel International GCSE candidates in Grades 9 and 10, and for parents in Gurgaon, Delhi NCR and abroad who want to understand how the system works before spending money on help. It covers the exam series and entry decisions, how the papers differ by subject, how Core and Extended tiers change the target, a revision system for six or more subjects, how to use past papers, exam-day practicalities and when outside help adds value. Each section hands off to a more specific guide, listed at the end, so this page is the map and the others are the detailed routes.

## What is IGCSE and how does the exam system work?

IGCSE in India means Cambridge IGCSE (Cambridge International Education) or Pearson Edexcel International GCSE, the International General Certificate of Secondary Education, usually a two-year course across Grades 9 and 10. It is often followed by the IB Diploma Programme, Cambridge International AS and A Levels, or a move to CBSE or ISC in Class 11. Students sit external written papers, and some subjects add coursework or a practical component.

Three features set the IGCSE apart from a typical Indian board examination, and they shape how to prepare.

**The papers are externally set and externally marked.** Your school teaches the course, but a board marks your scripts against published mark schemes. That means your teacher's notion of a good answer is only a guide; the mark scheme is the authority.

**Marks reward application and command words, not only recall.** Questions say "describe", "explain", "calculate", "evaluate" or "suggest", and each word sets the depth required. A student who writes everything they know about a topic often loses marks for a definition when the question wanted an explanation.

**Several subjects are examined in the same few weeks.** A typical candidate sits papers in five to eight subjects within one series, so planning across subjects matters as much as mastery of any one.

2 | Years, usually Grades 9 and 10
3 | Cambridge series a year, including one for India
A*-G | Cambridge grading in most syllabuses
9-1 | Edexcel International GCSE grading
:::

## Which exam series will you sit, and who decides?

Your school decides which series to enter you for, and you cannot choose one independently. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. Always confirm your series, subject codes and tiers with your school's examinations officer.

The India-only February/March Cambridge series exists to fit the Indian academic year. It lets Grade 10 results arrive in time for Class 11 admissions and for IB Diploma Programme or A Level entry in the same academic year. A school that enters students in February/March has a very different revision calendar from one that enters in May/June, so the first job in any IGCSE exam preparation plan is a date.

:::timeline Cambridge and Edexcel series, as published
January | Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series
February/March | Cambridge series offered only in India
May/June | Cambridge and Pearson Edexcel main series
October/November | Cambridge series
Schools choose the series; confirm exact dates with your examinations officer.
:::

### Why the series date changes the plan

A February/March candidate has no winter holiday free of revision; the final run-in overlaps with December and January, including pre-board style mocks that many schools hold. A May/June candidate has more time but also a longer stretch in which motivation can dip. An October/November candidate, often a retake or a late entrant, faces a shorter, more focused plan. In every case, count backwards from the first paper date, not from "the exams".

Parents sometimes ask about equivalence for Indian university admission. The Association of Indian Universities issues equivalence for international qualifications, and requirements differ by university and by course, so confirm with the institution rather than rely on general advice.

## How do tiers, codes and paper types work?

Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers: Core tops out at grade C, and Extended reaches A*. Each subject is identified by a syllabus code (for example Mathematics 0580, Biology 0610, Physics 0625, Chemistry 0620), and each syllabus has numbered papers that differ by tier. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher.

Tier is a real decision with a ceiling, not a label. A student entered for Core cannot earn above a C, however well they do. A student entered for Extended who is not ready can fall below what Core would have given. Schools usually decide after assessing the student, and the conversation to have with the teacher is: "What does the evidence say about which tier gives my child the best result, and when is the last date to change?"

| Subject | Cambridge code | How the papers are organised |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | 0580 | Core: Paper 1 (no calculator), Paper 3 (calculator). Extended: Paper 2 (no calculator), Paper 4 (calculator) |
| Additional Mathematics | 0606 | No tiers, two written papers, calculator allowed, graded A* to E |
| Biology | 0610 | Multiple choice, theory and a practical element, tiered Core or Extended |
| Chemistry | 0620 | As above |
| Physics | 0625 | As above |
| Economics | 0455 | Paper 1 multiple choice (30%), Paper 2 structured with data (70%) |
| Business Studies | 0450 | Paper 1 short answer and data response, Paper 2 case study, 50% each |
| Computer Science | 0478 | Paper 1 Computer systems, Paper 2 Algorithms, programming and logic |
| English | 0500, 0510, 0511, 0475 | First Language, English as a Second Language and Literature are different syllabuses |

Check the current syllabus on the board's website: codes, paper numbers and weightings can change between exam years, and some Cambridge syllabuses have 9-1 versions under different codes.

### Reading the paper structure in the sciences

Cambridge IGCSE sciences (syllabuses examined from 2023) use Paper 1 multiple choice for Core or Paper 2 multiple choice for Extended, Paper 3 theory for Core or Paper 4 theory for Extended, and practical assessment through Paper 5 (practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), chosen by the school. The weighting is multiple choice 30%, theory 50% and practical 20%. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with Paper 1 and Paper 2 for each separate science and practical skills assessed inside the written papers. A student who has never seen Paper 6 style questions on planning an experiment often loses marks that have nothing to do with knowing the science.

## How do you start IGCSE exam preparation in Grade 9?

Start with habits, not content: an error log, a retrieval routine and a rule of marking work against the mark scheme. In Grade 9 the syllabus is new and the pressure is low, which makes it the best time to build the routines that carry Grade 10. Revision in Grade 9 should look like short, spaced recall of last month's topics, not a heavy pre-exam sprint.

A workable Grade 9 pattern, for every subject:

1. **After each topic, write a one-page summary from memory**, then check and correct it against the notes. The gaps are the list for the next week.
2. **Do ten exam-style questions per topic**, from the board's past papers where possible, and mark them with the scheme.
3. **Log every lost mark with a cause**: did not know, misread, wrong method, slip, did not show working, did not write enough.
4. **Revisit the topic after a week and again after a month** with five questions each time. Spaced repetition beats a single long review.
5. **Talk to the teacher in the first term** about the tier and what would make a change possible.

Many families begin outside help only in Grade 10. That is often too late for subjects with a long runway, such as mathematics and the sciences, where a gap in Grade 9 algebra or concepts compounds. If there is a gap, a few focused months in Grade 9 are worth much more than a rescue in the final term. The question of when and whether to use a tutor is explored in [personalised IGCSE home tuition](/blog/personalized-home-tuition-igcse-students/) and in the method-first guide to [personalised learning in IGCSE](/blog/personalized-learning-igcse-education/).

## How should you revise several IGCSE subjects at once?

Revise several subjects by giving each a weekly slot, a topic list and a past-paper date, then rotating through retrieval, practice and review. Avoid the common trap of revising one subject for a week at a time: knowledge fades, and the last subject is always under-prepared. Interleaving, where each day has two or three subjects, keeps every subject warm.

The planning grid below gives each subject a *type* of work, not just a time. Different subjects need different training.

| Subject type | Main skill | Typical revision activity |
|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | Method fluency and accuracy | Timed question sets, error log, formula recall |
| Sciences | Concepts, calculations, practical skills | Retrieval cards, calculation drills, Paper 5 or 6 practice |
| English | Writing for purpose and audience | One timed piece a week, marked against criteria |
| Economics, Business | Application and evaluation | Case-based questions, chains of reasoning, definitions |
| Computer Science | Algorithms and logic | Tracing and writing pseudocode by hand and in a language |

### A weekly rhythm for six subjects

1. **Monday to Thursday:** two subjects a day, 45 minutes each, one retrieval block and one practice block.
2. **Friday:** one subject for a longer timed question set.
3. **Saturday:** a past-paper section under timed conditions, rotating subjects.
4. **Sunday:** mark Saturday's work with the scheme, update the error log, plan the week.

The exact proportions depend on the series date. For a week-by-week version of the final six weeks across subjects, use the [six-week IGCSE revision plan](/blog/igcse-revision-six-week-plan/). It covers triage, spaced retrieval, past-paper weeks and the final week, so this hub does not repeat it.

:::steps A weekly revision loop
Recall | Write what you know from memory
Practise | Exam-style questions on that topic
Mark | Use the official mark scheme only
Log | Record the cause of each lost mark
:::

## How do you use past papers properly for IGCSE exam preparation?

Use past papers in stages: topic questions first, then sections, then full timed papers in the last weeks, always marked against the official mark scheme. A past paper is a measuring instrument, not a revision book. Doing many unmarked papers builds the feeling of progress without the substance of it.

The sequence that works:

1. **Early on, use topic questions.** Choose questions from past papers sorted by topic, so you meet the real style while you are still learning content.
2. **In the middle, use sections.** A multiple-choice block in 25 minutes, or one structured question with a strict mark budget.
3. **In the last six to eight weeks, use full timed papers.** Exam conditions, correct calculator, no notes.
4. **Mark with the scheme, not with your intentions.** Award a mark only where the scheme says one is available.
5. **Read the examiner reports** where the board publishes them. They state, in plain words, which mistakes candidates make in each question.

A student who has done four papers properly marked and logged knows far more about their exam than one who has skimmed twelve. The finer points, including when to start and how many to do, are in the guide to [why past papers are key to IGCSE maths success](/blog/why-past-papers-are-key-to-igcse-maths-success/), whose method applies to other subjects too.

## What does each subject's exam actually reward?

Each subject rewards a different habit, and preparation that ignores this wastes effort. Mathematics rewards method and accuracy, the sciences reward precise command-word answers and calculation, English rewards purpose and audience, and Economics and Business reward chains of reasoning and application to context. A student who revises every subject by rereading notes will underperform in all of them.

### Mathematics

Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams) has Core candidates sitting Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), and Extended candidates sitting Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). The marks are in method and accuracy: show working, keep the right number of significant figures, and read the question twice. Many students who are comfortable in class lose marks to calculator habits and problem reading. Root causes and fixes are set out in the guide on [why students struggle in IGCSE Mathematics](/blog/igcse-mathematics-tutoring-guide/), and the parent view of the 0580 and 0606 syllabuses, including the Additional Mathematics decision, is in the [IGCSE maths parent guide](/blog/igcse-maths-parent-guide-0580-0606/).

For students aiming at the top grade, the strategy for Extended is in [how to score an A* in IGCSE Maths 0580](/blog/how-to-score-a-star-in-igcse-maths-0580/). If you want a twelve-week plan for the maths syllabus only, see the [3-month study plan for IGCSE Maths 0580](/blog/3-month-study-plan-for-igcse-maths-0580/), and for the errors that recur on scripts, the [top 10 mistakes in IGCSE maths](/blog/top-10-mistakes-in-igcse-maths/).

### Sciences

Biology, Chemistry and Physics reward precision. A command word such as "state", "describe" or "explain" sets the length and depth, and a one-word answer for a two-mark "explain" question will not score both marks. Calculations need the formula, substitution, units and sensible rounding. Practical papers (Paper 5 or Paper 6) test planning, observation, tables, graphs and evaluation of an experiment. Students who revise only content leave the practical marks on the table. Typical difficulties and subject-by-subject fixes are in the [IGCSE science guide](/blog/igcse-science-tutoring-guide/).

### English

English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses. Cambridge First Language English 0500 has a reading paper, then directed writing and composition or a coursework portfolio. English as a Second Language 0510 and 0511 test reading and writing plus listening, and 0511 counts speaking into the grade while 0510 reports speaking separately. Literature in English is 0475. Pearson Edexcel offers International GCSE English Language A (4EA1), English Language B (4EB1) and English as a Second Language (4ES1). The first step is to confirm which one your school entered. Preparation then rests on writing for a purpose and audience, covered in the guide to [IGCSE English communication skills](/blog/igcse-english-communication-skills/).

### Economics, Business and Computer Science

Economics 0455 has a 30% multiple-choice paper and a 70% structured paper with data across six topics. Business Studies 0450 splits 50-50 between a short-answer and data-response paper and a case-study paper. Computer Science 0478 has Paper 1 on computer systems and Paper 2 on algorithms, programming and logic. For these, the usual lost marks come from not applying a definition to the context in the question, and from writing algorithms that are right in idea but wrong in syntax or trace. Revision should include case-based questions and hand-tracing.

## Core or Extended: how should you decide?

Decide on evidence: recent test results, performance on past-paper sections of both tiers, and a conversation with the subject teacher. Extended opens A* and A, but a student who is not secure on the Core content may do better with Core's smaller ceiling and stronger results. The decision is the school's, so ask for the reasoning and the last date to change.

Three scenarios show how the choice plays out. These are illustrative, not records of real students.

**A student scoring comfortably in class who panics on harder items.** Consider a student who scores well on routine questions but freezes on multi-step problems. Extended may still be right, but the plan should include more unfamiliar, mixed practice, and the family should check at the mock whether the target grade is realistic.

**A student with gaps in Grade 8 algebra who is entered for Extended.** Here the first job is not exam technique. It is rebuilding algebra, because Extended Mathematics assumes it. A few months of focused work before the mock matters more than a hundred past-paper questions.

**A student who wants Core to be "safe".** Core caps the grade at C. If the student wants IB Mathematics AA or A Level Mathematics later, Core will probably close those doors, so the question to ask is what the next stage needs. The links between IGCSE and the next stage are covered in the [bridge from IGCSE maths to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/).

## How do command words and mark schemes change how you write answers?

Command words tell you the depth required, and mark schemes tell you exactly what earns each mark. Learn the common command words and read mark schemes as if they were the syllabus. Many lost marks are not about ignorance but about writing the wrong kind of answer.

| Command word | What it asks for | Typical mark-losing error |
|---|---|---|
| State | A brief answer, no explanation | Writing a paragraph, wasting time |
| Define | A precise meaning | Giving an example instead of a definition |
| Describe | What happens or what you see | Explaining why when only what was asked |
| Explain | Reason with a cause and effect | Describing without giving the reason |
| Calculate | A numerical answer with working | No working, no units or wrong rounding |
| Suggest | Apply knowledge to a new situation | Reciting a memorised answer |
| Evaluate or discuss | Weigh points and reach a judgement | One-sided list with no conclusion |

Use mark schemes actively. For a four-mark "explain" question, look at the scheme and count the points it lists. Then write four distinct, linked points. Over a few weeks you will learn that examiners reward specific vocabulary and specific chains of reasoning.

## How do you handle the practical papers and coursework?

Practical components are separate marks that need separate preparation. In Cambridge sciences, the practical assessment is either Paper 5 (a practical test) or Paper 6 (alternative to practical), chosen by the school, and together practicals account for 20% of the marks. Preparation is about skills: measuring, tabulating, plotting, drawing conclusions and evaluating method.

Practise by doing, and by reading mark schemes for the *planning* and *evaluation* parts. Typical expectations include a table with headings and units, a graph with labelled axes and a line of best fit, and an evaluation that names a specific source of error and a specific improvement. For subjects with coursework, such as some English portfolios, the school's internal deadlines are the real ones, so build the timeline from those. Subjects offering coursework also require authenticity: the work must be your own.

## What does exam week itself look like, and how do you prepare for it?

Exam week is logistics plus pacing: know your timetable, equipment and rules, sleep well, and do light retrieval, not new learning. Candidates who treat exam week as a final cram often arrive tired for the papers that matter. Families should confirm the school's instructions for each paper, including what calculators and instruments are allowed.

A checklist, to be adapted to your school's rules:

1. Write every paper date, time and venue on one page, and check it against the school's timetable.
2. Prepare equipment the evening before: permitted calculator with fresh batteries, geometry set, pens, a clear water bottle if allowed.
3. Do 30 to 45 minutes of retrieval for tomorrow's subject only, then stop.
4. Eat and sleep. A rested student writes more clearly and misreads less.
5. After each paper, do not post-mortem it with friends. Rest, then turn to the next.
6. Plan one short break between papers on the same day.

Note where a practical or a listening component falls, since those have their own arrangements.

## How do you manage stress and workload across the year?

Manage stress by making the workload visible, finite and regular. A written weekly plan, a fixed rest day and a limit on late-night study usually reduce anxiety more than any pep talk. Stress is often about uncertainty: "I do not know where I stand". Marked papers and an error log replace that uncertainty with data.

Practical measures that parents can support without hovering:

- **Ask to see the plan, not the hours.** "What is Tuesday's slot for?" is more useful than "How long did you study?"
- **Protect sleep.** A common error in the final weeks is giving up sleep for extra hours that return less than they cost.
- **Keep one regular non-study activity.** Sport, music or time with friends keeps the study hours sharper.
- **Watch for a stall, not for effort.** If a student works hard for three weeks and the logged errors do not change, the method needs a conversation with a teacher or tutor.
- **Separate subjects in the plan.** An all-or-nothing mood about "the exams" often comes from one weak subject, and naming it makes it manageable.

## When does outside help add value for IGCSE exam preparation?

Outside help adds value when there is a specific, fixable gap: a tier mismatch, a weak foundation, a subject where nobody at home or school can give timely feedback, or a stretch of time when a student needs structure. It adds little when the student has a good plan, good school teaching and steady habits. No tutor can guarantee a grade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should IGCSE exam preparation start?

Start the habits in Grade 9 and the structured revision about six months before your series. Grade 9 is for building an error log, retrieval routine and past-paper habit on current topics. A full multi-subject plan with timed papers belongs in the last two to three months. The exact date depends on whether your school enters you in February/March, May/June or October/November.

### Which exam series will my child sit?

Your school decides, and a student cannot choose independently. Cambridge series run in February/March (India only), May/June and October/November, and Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. Ask the school's examinations officer for the series, subject codes and tiers entered. Write the first paper date on the family calendar and count backwards from it.

### What is the difference between Core and Extended?

Core papers cap the grade at C, while Extended papers reach A*. Many Cambridge subjects have both tiers, and the school enters each student after assessing readiness. Extended suits students who are secure on Core content and can handle harder, multi-step questions. Ask the teacher what evidence supports the choice and when it can still be changed.

### How many past papers should a student do?

Quality matters more than number. A student who marks four papers against the scheme and logs the causes of lost marks learns more than one who skims twelve. Start with topic questions, move to sections, then do full timed papers in the final six to eight weeks, and read examiner reports where available.

### How do I revise six or seven subjects without burning out?

Give every subject a weekly slot and mix two or three subjects each day instead of one subject per week. Alternate retrieval, practice and marking, keep one full rest day, and protect sleep. The loop of recall, practise, mark and log works for any subject and gives a visible sense of progress that lowers anxiety.

### Is a tutor necessary for IGCSE?

No. A student with good teaching, a clear plan and steady habits may not need one. A tutor adds value when there is a specific gap, a tier concern, a subject without timely feedback or a stretch where structure is missing. Check what the tutor will do in the first sessions and ask for a demo before committing. No tutor can guarantee a grade.

### What if my child is behind in Grade 10?

Triage by marks available and time left. Identify the subjects where the gap is largest and the syllabus areas that carry most marks, fix foundations first, then move to timed practice. A realistic plan with fewer targets beats a heroic plan for all subjects. Talk to the school about tier and mock dates, and consider a short, focused block of support.

### Do Cambridge and Edexcel preparation differ?

The study method is the same, but codes, paper structures and grading differ. Cambridge grades most syllabuses A* to G, with some 9-1 versions under different codes, while Pearson Edexcel International GCSE grades 9 to 1. Always use past papers and mark schemes from the exact board and syllabus code your school entered.

### Can IGCSE results be used for Indian university admission?

Possibly, but requirements vary. The Association of Indian Universities issues equivalence for international qualifications, and each university and course sets its own conditions. Families should confirm directly with the institution concerned instead of relying on general advice. Most IGCSE students first move into IB DP, A Levels, CBSE or ISC in Class 11, where the results are the entry evidence.

### What should we do in the final week before the exams?

Reduce, do not expand. Do light retrieval for the next paper only, finish equipment and logistics, sleep properly, and avoid new topics and late-night sessions. Revisit your error log for recurring slips and read a few examiner comments. Do not post-mortem a paper with friends, and rest between papers.

### How do online lessons work for practical subjects?

Online lessons suit most theory and problem-solving work through a shared screen and a digital pen. Practical skills such as drawing apparatus, tabulating data and graph plotting can be taught well online, while hands-on laboratory handling depends on the school's sessions. The honest limits and the format comparison are in the online IGCSE tutoring guide linked above.
