## Which IGCSE English Is Your Child Actually Sitting?

English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses, and the school chooses one. Here is the map.

| Board | Syllabus | Code | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cambridge | First Language English | 0500 | A reading paper, then directed writing and composition, or a coursework portfolio |
| Cambridge | English as a Second Language | 0510 | Reading and writing plus listening; speaking reported separately |
| Cambridge | English as a Second Language | 0511 | Reading and writing plus listening; speaking counts into the grade |
| Cambridge | Literature in English | 0475 | Study of set literary texts |
| Pearson Edexcel | International GCSE English Language A | 4EA1 | First-language style English Language |
| Pearson Edexcel | International GCSE English Language B | 4EB1 | A second English Language option |
| Pearson Edexcel | English as a Second Language | 4ES1 | For students whose first language is not English |

Two practical notes. First, the school decides which syllabus, tier-like options and series a student sits, so the answer comes from the school, not the tutor. Ask for the syllabus code in writing. Second, many students sit **more than one**: for example, First Language English and Literature in English together. Each needs its own preparation.

### How to find out in five minutes

1. Look at the school's subject list or report card for the code (0500, 0510, 0511, 0475, 4EA1, 4EB1, 4ES1).
2. If there is no code, ask the English teacher: "Which syllabus code are we entered for, and is it coursework or exam only?"
3. Note which exam series the school uses. Cambridge runs February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November; Pearson Edexcel runs January and May/June. Schools decide which series they enter.
4. Download the specimen or past papers for that exact code from the board.

Once you have the code, tutoring can be designed.

## What Is the Difference Between First Language and Second Language English?

First Language English is built for students who use English as their main language of study and expects mature, controlled writing, close reading and independent argument. English as a Second Language is built for students for whom English is an additional language, and it tests comprehension, accurate writing and, depending on the syllabus, listening and speaking. They are not "easy" and "hard" versions. They test different things.

A Gurgaon student at an international school who speaks English at home may still be entered for a particular syllabus because of the school's policy, and an Indian student with strong English may find the First Language papers rewarding while a student newer to English will do better on the second-language route. The tutor's role is not to argue about the entry but to prepare the student for the one they have.

| Feature | First Language (0500, 4EA1) | Second Language (0510, 0511, 4ES1) |
|---|---|---|
| Main demand | Control of style, argument and audience | Accuracy, comprehension, clear communication |
| Reading tasks | Close reading and response to unseen texts | Comprehension of shorter, more accessible texts |
| Writing tasks | Directed writing and composition; some options of coursework | Structured writing tasks of defined types |
| Listening | Not a core component in the usual structure | Part of the assessment |
| Speaking | Not a core component in the usual structure | Counts in 0511; reported separately in 0510 |
| Typical weakness | Thin analysis, weak paragraphing | Grammar slips, limited range, misreading tasks |

This is a summary of the structure, not a substitute for the current specification. Syllabuses are revised, so check the board's document for the exam year.

## IGCSE English Home Tuition in Gurgaon: What a Tutor Should Work On

Good English tutoring is diagnostic. A tutor should be able to say, after two sessions, which of the following is the student's real constraint.

- **Reading accuracy.** Does the student understand what the text says, in its own terms, before interpreting it?
- **Reading analysis.** Can the student identify the writer's choices and explain their effect?
- **Planning.** Does the student plan, even briefly, before writing?
- **Paragraph control.** Does each paragraph have one purpose and a link to the question?
- **Sentence control.** Is the student's sentence length varied and the grammar secure?
- **Vocabulary range.** Is the vocabulary precise or repetitive?
- **Task fit.** Does the response match the form, audience and purpose the task demands?
- **Proofreading.** Does the student catch their own errors?

The first session should end with two or three of these named as priorities. The second should begin work on them with real tasks from the right syllabus.

:::steps How a good English tutorial runs
Read | Student reads a fresh text or task
Plan | Two-minute plan before writing
Write | A timed paragraph or short piece
Mark | Tutor and student mark against criteria
Redraft | Student rewrites the weakest part
:::

## First Language English: Reading, Directed Writing and Composition

For Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500, the structure described by the board starts with a reading paper, followed by directed writing and composition or a coursework portfolio, depending on how the school enters the student. The Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Language A (4EA1) and B (4EB1) are separate specifications with their own papers. The tutor must know which.

### Reading: from understanding to analysis

Reading tasks ask the student to understand a passage, select and organise information, and comment on how language is used. The common error is to answer from general impressions. A better habit is to quote briefly, name the technique or word choice, and explain the effect on the reader. Many students lose marks by retelling the passage; the task asks what the writer is doing, not what happens.

A useful drill for reading: take a short unseen paragraph, underline three words or phrases that carry the tone, and write one sentence on each beginning "This word suggests..." and ending with the effect on a reader. After a few weeks, the student's comments move from "it is descriptive" to specific, evidence-based interpretation.

### Directed writing: writing for a purpose and audience

Directed writing asks the student to use a reading text as the basis for a response in a given form, such as a letter, a speech, an article or a report, for a specified audience. The marks depend on selecting relevant material from the text, shaping it for the purpose and writing in an appropriate register.

Common problems seen in student responses:

- Copying chunks from the text rather than transforming the ideas.
- Writing in the wrong register: a speech to peers written like an essay, or a letter to a principal that sounds like a text message.
- Ignoring a bullet in the task.
- No clear opening or close appropriate to the form.

A tutor can teach a simple routine: identify the form, audience and purpose; list the points required by the task; plan a sequence; write with a clear opening and ending; check the register.

### Composition: narrative, descriptive and argumentative writing

Composition tasks give the student a choice of prompts. Narrative writing needs control of structure and pace; descriptive writing needs precise sensory detail and varied sentence rhythm; argumentative writing needs a clear stance, organised points and evidence. The most common reasons for lost marks are over-long openings, no clear structure, repetitive sentence patterns and a rush at the ending.

A practical approach for a short composition:

1. Choose the prompt in two minutes, not ten.
2. Plan a beginning, a turn and an ending (for narrative), or three points with an example each (for argument).
3. Write the opening so that it sets the tone in two or three sentences.
4. Control each paragraph around a single idea.
5. Leave five minutes to proofread.

For students on a coursework route where the school offers it, the portfolio needs steady drafting through the year and the school's own rules about what help is allowed. A tutor can teach the skill of drafting and reviewing; the work must be the student's own, and the school sets the rules for coursework.

## English as a Second Language: Accuracy, Range and the Four Skills

Cambridge IGCSE English as a Second Language comes in two forms: 0510 and 0511. Both test reading and writing plus listening. In 0511 the speaking component counts into the grade; in 0510 speaking is reported separately. Pearson Edexcel has English as a Second Language (4ES1). The student's school will say which version applies.

### What the tutor works on

**Reading.** Understanding the meaning and purpose of short texts, locating information, and answering in the student's own words where asked. The error to watch is lifting phrases from the text when the task wants a paraphrase.

**Writing.** Structured tasks of defined types such as emails, articles and reports. Accuracy of grammar, spelling and punctuation, and range of vocabulary, are rewarded alongside organisation and relevance.

**Listening.** Practice with audio, concentrating on the first listen for gist and the second for detail, and recording answers quickly while listening.

**Speaking.** Practice in extended, organised answers and in responding to follow-up questions. This is where a home tutor in the room is particularly useful: the student speaks, the tutor responds naturally and gives specific feedback on fluency, vocabulary and pronunciation.

### Where the students are, and what they need

Gurgaon has a wide mix of students who sit ESL: those whose home language is Hindi, Punjabi or another language and who study in English; students returning from abroad; and students from families who use English at home but who are entered for the second-language syllabus by their school. Their needs differ. A student who speaks fluently but writes carelessly needs a different plan from a student who reads slowly. The diagnostic matters.

## Literature in English: Reading Set Texts as an Argument

Cambridge Literature in English 0475 involves the study of set literary texts. The exact texts and the structure of the assessment are set by the board and the school, so confirm with the school and the current specification.

Literature work differs from language work. The student is not writing for a purpose and audience; the student is building an argument about a text, supported by evidence. The core skills are:

- Knowing the text well enough to find evidence quickly.
- Reading closely: what a phrase suggests, how a structure works, how a character changes.
- Forming a clear line of argument in response to a question.
- Writing in paragraphs that make a point, give evidence, and explain it.

### A paragraph model that works

A well-built literature paragraph has four moves: a clear point that answers the question, a short quotation or precise reference, an analysis of the writer's methods and their effect, and a link back to the question. Students who skip the analysis write a summary; students who skip the link drift off the question.

A tutor can practise this by giving a short quotation and asking the student to write one paragraph in ten minutes, then marking only for the four moves. Do this weekly and the student's essays tighten quickly.

### Context and personal response

Where the specification rewards context or personal response, the student should treat them as brief, relevant support, not as a separate section that interrupts the argument. A tutor should show how to weave context in a sentence and move on.

## Command Words and Question Reading

Across all syllabuses, many marks are lost not by weak English but by misreading the task. Command words such as "explain", "describe", "compare", "comment on" and "suggest" ask for different things. The same applies to bullet points in directed writing tasks, which are not decoration: each is a mark.

A short habit worth building: before writing, underline the command word, circle the number of marks, and write the number of points or paragraphs the marks imply. Students who do this reduce under-answering and over-writing at once. The general principle is covered across subjects in [IB command terms explained with real examples](/blog/ib-command-terms-explained-with-real-examples/), and a tutor of IGCSE English should adapt it to the specific wording of the board's papers.

## Writing Quality: The Skills That Move a Grade

Marks in English rise when a few writing habits improve. The table below lists the habits that tutors work on repeatedly, with the way each shows up in a script.

| Habit | Weak version | Strong version |
|---|---|---|
| Paragraphing | One block, or a new paragraph every two lines | One idea per paragraph, linked to the next |
| Sentence variety | Every sentence starts the same way and has the same length | Short and long sentences used for effect |
| Vocabulary | Repeats "good", "bad", "very" | Precise words chosen for tone |
| Evidence | General claims | Short, relevant quotations or details |
| Opening | A long preamble | A direct start that sets the task in motion |
| Ending | A restated introduction | A final sentence that lands the point |
| Proofreading | Errors left in | A last read for spelling, punctuation and tense |

None of these is solved by a worksheet. They are solved by writing, receiving specific feedback, and rewriting.

### The redraft habit

:::bars Where an English tutorial hour typically goes (illustrative)
Reading or planning | 25 | 15 min
Timed writing | 33 | 20 min
Marking against criteria | 25 | 15 min
Redrafting | 25 | 15 min
A sketch of a balanced hour, not a fixed rule.
:::

The most effective single practice in English tuition is the redraft. After feedback, the student rewrites the weakest paragraph, not the entire essay. The tutor marks the new version against the same criteria. Over weeks, the student builds a bank of improved paragraphs and absorbs the patterns. A tutor who corrects the student's essay by rewriting it is doing the student's thinking for them; the work should always be the student's.

## What Does a Month of IGCSE English Home Tuition Look Like?

A month of IGCSE English home tuition in Gurgaon should be planned like this. A plan for a student who needs one session a week, adjusted for need. It assumes you have the syllabus code.

:::timeline A first month of IGCSE English tuition
Week 1 | Diagnostic: read, plan, write, and a short conversation
Week 2 | Priority skill one, such as paragraphing or reading accuracy
Week 3 | Priority skill two, with a timed task in the exam's style
Week 4 | Redraft a piece, review progress, set the next targets
:::

### Week 1: diagnostic

The tutor sets a short unseen text and a writing task in the style of the student's actual syllabus. The student reads, plans and writes under light time pressure. The tutor then talks through what happened: what the student understood, where they hesitated, what they would do differently. The result is a short list of priorities.

### Week 2: the first priority

If the priority is reading, the session works on close reading of short passages. If it is writing structure, the session works on planning and paragraphing. If it is accuracy, the session works on the specific grammar patterns that recur in the student's writing, drawn from their own scripts, not from a generic list.

### Week 3: apply under exam conditions

The student attempts a timed task in the exam's format and marks it with the tutor against the criteria. The conversation is about how marks were earned or lost.

### Week 4: redraft and review

The student redrafts one weak piece. The tutor and student compare it with the earlier version, and set two targets for the next month. This is also the time to decide whether to continue weekly, change frequency, or pause.

## Common Mistakes Seen in IGCSE English Scripts

- **Retelling instead of analysing.** The answer summarises the text when the task asks for the writer's methods.
- **Ignoring the audience.** A letter to a head teacher that reads like a message to a friend.
- **Copying from the passage.** Lifting phrases when the task wants the student's own words.
- **Long, slow openings.** Half the time spent before the task begins.
- **Repeated vocabulary.** The same adjectives and connectives throughout.
- **Weak endings.** The writing stops instead of concluding.
- **No planning.** The response meanders and repeats itself.
- **Skipping proofreading.** Easy marks lost on spelling and punctuation.
- **Over-reliance on memorised phrases.** Examiners recognise them; they do not reward them.

Many of these are fixable within a few weeks of focused practice and specific feedback.

## Fitting English Into the Class 10 Year

English is often neglected in the final year because maths and sciences feel more urgent. That is a mistake: English papers reward steady practice, and the improvement curve is gradual. A student who leaves English revision to the last month will find that skills such as paragraph control do not change quickly.

A sensible pattern for Grade 10:

- **Early in the year:** diagnostic, one weekly session if needed, regular reading.
- **Before mocks:** timed papers in the exam's format, with feedback.
- **After mocks:** target the two or three habits that cost most marks.
- **Final weeks:** short timed pieces, reading practice and proofreading.

## Reading Habits That Support the Papers

Tuition alone does not build a reader. A student who reads regularly, in print or online, from varied sources, will find unseen texts less intimidating. Encourage a mix: a news article, a short story, an opinion column, a piece of descriptive travel writing. Ask the student to summarise a piece in two sentences and note three words that carried the tone. Ten minutes a day is more useful than two hours on a Sunday.

For Literature, re-reading the set text with a pencil, marking key moments, and writing a one-line summary of each chapter or scene creates the familiarity that lets a student find evidence quickly in the exam.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between IGCSE First Language and Second Language English?

First Language English is designed for students who use English as their main language of study and rewards controlled style, argument and close reading. English as a Second Language is designed for students for whom English is an additional language and tests comprehension, accurate writing, listening and, depending on the syllabus, speaking. The school decides which syllabus a student sits, and a tutor prepares the student for that entry.

### Does my child need a tutor for IGCSE English?

Not necessarily. A student who reads widely, writes regularly and receives specific feedback from the teacher may do well without one. A tutor helps when writing is unstructured, reading is shallow, marks are flat despite effort, or the student is new to English-medium study. A short diagnostic can tell you whether regular tuition is worth the time.

### How is IGCSE English coursework handled in tuition?

Where the school offers a coursework route, the work must be the student's own, and the school sets the rules about help. A tutor can teach drafting, planning, editing and how to respond to criteria, but must not write or substantially rewrite the portfolio. Ask the school what support is permitted before you start tutoring, and share that with the tutor.

### How many sessions a week does IGCSE English need?

One focused session a week is enough for many students, provided they write between sessions. Two sessions may suit a student with several gaps or a nearby mock. Adding hours without independent writing practice rarely helps. Review after four weeks by comparing a fresh piece of writing with the first diagnostic, not by the number of sessions attended.

### Can online tutoring work for IGCSE English?

Yes, for reading and writing, especially with a shared document that both can annotate. Speaking practice works online with a stable connection and headphones, though some students benefit more from live conversation in the room. A hybrid arrangement is a good option if the student needs both. The decision depends on how the student focuses and where the best tutor is.

### My child speaks fluent English but writes poorly. Why?

Fluent speech does not guarantee structured writing. Writing needs planning, paragraph control, a suitable register and proofreading, which speech does not require. A tutor can diagnose whether the issue is planning, sentence control, vocabulary or timing, and set short, frequent writing tasks with specific feedback. Improvement usually comes within weeks of regular practice and redrafting.

### What should a student read to improve?

A varied diet works best: news articles, opinion pieces, short stories, descriptive travel writing and, for Literature, the set texts themselves. The habit matters more than the choice: ten minutes a day, with a one-line summary and a note of three striking words. Reading with a pencil, and talking about what the writer is doing, turns reading into training for the papers.

### Is Literature in English harder than English Language?

They are different rather than harder. Literature asks for sustained argument about set texts with evidence; Language asks for reading, directed writing and composition. A student who likes close reading and discussion may prefer Literature, while a student who likes writing for varied purposes may prefer Language. The school sets the entry, so prepare for the one assigned.

### How do I know whether the tutor is working?

Look for specific feedback, a short redraft after each piece, a growing bank of improved paragraphs, and a clearer plan from the student before writing. Compare a fresh timed task after four weeks with the first diagnostic. No tutor can guarantee a grade, but a good one can show the student's habits changing and explain the next step.

### When should a Grade 9 student start English tuition?

When a pattern of weak writing or reading appears, often in the first term. Early help is gentler and more effective than a rush in Grade 10, when subjects compete for time. If the student is settling well, a periodic check on a piece of writing is enough. The habits take months to change, so starting early is useful.
