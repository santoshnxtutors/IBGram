IB tutors in Sector 57 Gurgaon are most useful in two windows of the Diploma Programme: the months when Internal Assessments (IAs) are being drafted, and the stretch when the Extended Essay (EE) is moving from a question to a finished essay. Both are coursework, both belong to the student, and both are where families most often ask a tutor to do something the International Baccalaureate (IB) does not allow. A good tutor in this area spends coursework season teaching the skill behind the task, asking hard questions about the student's reasoning, and keeping the student's own voice on the page.

This guide is for Diploma Programme (DP) families living in Sector 57, the neighbouring Sushant Lok pockets and the sectors that feed into them. ## What Is Coursework Season, and Why Does It Hit Families Here So Hard?

Coursework season is the stretch of the DP, usually from the middle of DP1 to the early months of DP2, when internal assessments, the Extended Essay, Theory of Knowledge (TOK) tasks and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) reflections all demand attention at once. It feels heavy because the work is open-ended: nobody can tell a student exactly what to write, and the deadlines are set by the school.

The pressure comes from overlap. A student taking six subjects may have an IA in most of them, an EE with a supervisor who expects regular meetings, a TOK exhibition or essay, and the ongoing weekly load of tests and homework. In a typical Gurgaon DP household, the evenings are already split between school work, a sport or activity, and travel. Coursework needs long, quiet blocks of thinking time, which are the scarcest resource in the week.

For families looking for IB tutors in Sector 57 Gurgaon, the problem is practical as much as academic. School and activity travel eats into weekday evenings, and the quiet two-hour block that an IA needs often exists only on weekends. That is why many of the requests we hear from this area are about timing: when do we start, how often do we meet, and how do we make sure the student is writing, not just attending sessions.

Each school sets its own internal deadlines for drafts and final submissions, and the DP coordinator is the authority on dates. The pattern in this post is a general shape. Confirm the real dates with the school before building a tutoring plan around them.

## The Coursework Map: What a DP Student Actually Has to Produce

Parents hear "IA" and "EE" as one pile. They are separate tasks with different rules, owners and calendars. This table sets out the main components, drawing on the published DP structure.

| Component | What it is | Weight or size | Who assesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics IA (exploration) | An independent exploration, the same task for AA and AI, SL and HL | 20% of the maths grade, 12 to 20 pages, marked out of 20 on five criteria | Teacher, moderated by the IB |
| Science IA | A scientific investigation, reported individually | 20% of the subject grade, maximum 3,000 words, four criteria of 6 marks each | Teacher, moderated by the IB |
| Economics IA | A portfolio of three commentaries on news articles | Each up to 800 words, three different units and key concepts | Teacher, moderated by the IB |
| Language and Literature IA | Includes an Individual Oral of 15 minutes (10 minutes of analysis, then 5 of questions) | Part of the English A assessment | Teacher, moderated |
| Extended Essay | An independent research essay | Up to 4,000 words and roughly 40 hours of work | Externally assessed, graded A to E |
| TOK exhibition and essay | An exhibition on three objects (up to 950 words) and an essay on a prescribed title (up to 1,600 words) | One third and two thirds of the TOK grade | Exhibition by school, essay externally |
| CAS | Creativity, Activity, Service experiences with reflection | Must be completed; not graded | School |

Two grades, the EE and TOK, combine to give up to 3 diploma points, and an E in either is a failing condition, subject to the IB's rules. The IAs, taken together, carry a fifth of each subject grade in most subjects, which is why a rushed one costs more than it appears to.

The syllabuses change. The EE guide was updated for first assessment in 2027, so this post does not quote criterion mark allocations for it. Follow the version your DP coordinator issues.

## A Coursework Calendar for DP1 and DP2 in an Indian School Year

The Indian academic year runs roughly from April to March, and most Indian IB schools enter DP candidates for the May exam session. That means DP2 is a short year that ends in May exams, so coursework is front-loaded. The shape below is typical, not universal.

:::timeline A typical coursework arc (confirm dates with the school)
DP1 Term 1 | Habits, subject foundations, early CAS
DP1 Term 2 | Topic ideas for IAs, EE subject and supervisor chosen
DP1 Term 3 | IA planning, EE research question, first TOK work
DP2 Term 1 | IA drafts and submissions, EE writing
DP2 Term 2 | EE finalised, TOK essay, mocks begin
Before May | Exam papers dominate; coursework is done
:::

What this means for tutoring is simple. Help with coursework is most valuable in the **planning** stage, when choices are still open, and the **draft** stage, when the student can still revise in response to feedback. A tutor who first appears two weeks before a final deadline is limited to damage control.

Many families in the area find that DP1 Term 3, the months before the long summer break, is the quiet window that is easiest to lose. Students feel the pressure is far away, and then DP2 arrives with three IA deadlines in the same fortnight. Doing the topic exploration and early reading in DP1, and using the break for light progress, changes the whole of DP2.

## What May a Tutor Do on an IA or EE, and What Is Off Limits?

A tutor may teach the underlying skill, question the student's reasoning, point to the assessment criteria and ask the student to reread their own work against them. A tutor may not write, rewrite or edit the student's submission. The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher feedback on one draft; it does not allow anyone, tutor or parent, to produce the work.

This is the line every responsible tutor works to, and it is worth being exact about it, because a family that crosses it risks the student's diploma, not just a mark. The school submits the work and runs it through similarity checking, and malpractice findings are handled by the IB.

| Activity | Allowed? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Explaining how a criterion is assessed | Yes | Teaching the skill |
| Asking "why did you choose this method?" | Yes | Questioning reasoning |
| Suggesting the student reread a paragraph against a criterion | Yes | Directing the student back to their own work |
| Teaching a mathematical technique needed for the exploration | Yes | Subject teaching |
| Discussing how to structure a research essay in general | Yes | Skill instruction |
| Choosing the topic or research question for the student | No | The student must own it |
| Writing or rewriting sentences, paragraphs or equations in the submission | No | Academic misconduct |
| Editing the final draft line by line | No | Beyond permitted feedback |
| Supplying data, a bibliography or a conclusion | No | The work must be the student's own |
| Providing a "model" IA to be adapted | Risky | Close adaptation can be flagged as plagiarism |

The practical test a family can use is this: after the session, could the student explain every sentence of what is on the page, and why it is there? If not, something has gone wrong. A related warning sign is a sudden jump in the sophistication of the writing or the mathematics between a student's regular work and their coursework.

### How this changes the shape of a session

A session on an IA is rarely a lesson in the usual sense. It is closer to a structured conversation. The student brings their draft or their plan. The tutor asks questions, points to the criteria, and notes where the reasoning has a gap. The student leaves with a list of things to think about, not a list of changes made. The next version is the student's.

That is slower than being handed a fix. It is also the only approach that produces coursework the student can defend if the school asks about it, and it builds the skills that the exam papers test anyway.

## Mathematics IA: A Scenario of Good Support

The mathematics exploration is an independent piece of work in which the student chooses a topic, investigates it mathematically and reflects on the process. It is marked out of 20 on five criteria: Presentation (4), Mathematical communication (4), Personal engagement (3), Reflection (3) and Use of mathematics (6). HL work is judged against a higher standard on the last of these, so mathematical depth is the main lever.

Consider a typical DP1 student, in a Sector 57 society, who wants to explore something about cricket statistics because it interests them. The first meeting is not about the answer. The tutor asks: what is the mathematical question? What mathematics will you use that goes beyond what you have already been taught? Where might the model fail?

Over two or three sessions, the student refines the idea from "analyse a cricketer's performance" to a question that supports real mathematical work, with a clear aim and a method that has some depth. The tutor does not write the question. The tutor asks why each version is weak, and the student rewrites.

When the draft comes, the tutor reads it against the criteria and responds in questions: "Where in this section do you show your own thinking, as opposed to reporting a result?" "What does this graph tell the reader that the previous one did not?" The student revises. The teacher then gives feedback on one draft, as the IB allows.

## Science IA: The Practical Work Belongs to the School

In the DP sciences, the internal assessment is the scientific investigation, worth 20%, marked on four criteria of 6 marks each: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion and Evaluation. The report has a maximum of 3,000 words. Students may investigate in groups of up to three, but each writes an individual report.

For a tutor, the line is especially clear. The practical work is done in the school laboratory, under the teacher's supervision, and cannot be replicated at a dining table. A tutor can help the student think about a research question, plan variables and uncertainty, interpret data, and read the criteria. A tutor cannot design the investigation for the student, supply data or write the evaluation.

The most useful work usually happens after data exists. A student who has results and does not know what to do with them benefits from questions such as: "What is the largest source of uncertainty here, and how does it affect your conclusion?" or "If you did this again, what single change would improve the reliability most?" The student answers in their own words, in their own report.

## Economics, English and Other Subjects

**Economics** has an IA made up of three commentaries of up to 800 words on published news articles, each drawing on a different unit of the syllabus and a different key concept. The task rewards connecting theory to a live example. Tutoring here is about picking suitable articles, applying diagrams accurately and evaluating with care. Reading the student's draft aloud and asking "what is your evidence for this claim?" is more productive than any rewrite.

**English A** includes an Individual Oral of 15 minutes, with a 10-minute analysis linked to a global issue and then 5 minutes of questions. The preparation is rehearsal: speaking it, timing it, and handling the questions. A tutor can listen and respond, but cannot script the oral.

**Other subjects**, including Business, History, Psychology, Computer Science, Visual Arts and the languages, each have their own internal assessment, with their own criteria. The principle is the same everywhere. The tutor teaches the skill and the subject, reads the criteria with the student and asks questions. The student produces the work.

## Extended Essay Milestones: Where Help Matters and Where It Does Not

The Extended Essay is an independent research essay of up to 4,000 words and roughly 40 hours of work, supervised by a teacher at the student's own school, with reflection sessions recorded along the way. It is written in one DP subject or as an interdisciplinary essay and graded A to E.

The supervisor at school is the primary support. A tutor's role is a supporting one, and it is most useful at specific milestones.

:::steps EE milestones where outside help is useful
Question | Narrow a broad interest to a researchable question
Sources | Judge reliability and plan a reading schedule
Outline | Structure an argument, not a list of facts
Draft | Ask questions about reasoning and clarity
Reflection | Prepare for supervisor conversations
:::

**Choosing the question.** The most common EE failure is a question that is too broad or cannot be answered within 4,000 words. A tutor can help the student test the question by asking what evidence would answer it, and whether that evidence is accessible.

**Research planning.** A student who plans reading across weeks, with a simple log, avoids the last-minute scramble. Citation habits, including keeping a record of every source as it is used, are best set early.

**Argument structure.** Many first drafts are descriptive. A tutor can ask: "What is your argument in one sentence? Where does each section support it?" The student rewrites.

**Not the writing.** Because the EE is externally assessed and similarity-checked, the writing must be the student's. The EE guide was updated for first assessment in 2027, so follow the version the school issues. A detailed walk-through of the process is in [how to write an IB Extended Essay](/blog/how-to-write-a-perfect-ib-extended-essay-ee/).

## TOK and CAS: Quieter Components That Still Need Time

TOK has two assessments. The exhibition uses three objects linked to one of 35 IA prompts, up to 950 words, internally assessed and worth one third of the TOK grade. The essay answers one of six prescribed titles released for each exam session, up to 1,600 words, externally assessed, and worth two thirds. The course covers the core theme (knowledge and the knower), two of five optional themes and five areas of knowledge.

Students often find TOK slippery because there is no content to memorise. Conversation helps most: a tutor who asks "how do you know that?" and "would a historian agree?" makes the abstract concrete. Again, the essay must be the student's own thinking.

CAS must be completed but is not graded, and it is the component families most often leave until late. A short reflection written within a week of an activity is far easier than a batch written in one weekend. A tutor is rarely needed for CAS, but a parent can help by keeping a simple record of dates and activities.

## A Coursework Session: A Numbered Process You Can Run

This is a process that stands alone as a quotable answer to "how should a coursework tutoring session work?" Families can use it as a checklist for what to expect.

1. **Agree the target.** At the start, the student states which component and which stage the session is for: topic, plan, first draft or revision.
2. **Read the criteria together.** The tutor and student read the relevant criteria, and the student says, in their own words, what top-band work looks like.
3. **Student presents.** The student explains their current thinking or draft aloud, in a few minutes, without being interrupted.
4. **Tutor questions.** The tutor asks questions about gaps, assumptions and evidence. Answers go into the student's own notes.
5. **Student decides.** The student chooses what to change, and why.
6. **Set a small task.** One piece of work for the next session, small enough to finish.
7. **Log the session.** A short note of what was discussed and decided, kept by the student.

Step 7 matters more than it looks. If the school ever asks about the development of the work, a student with a dated record of their own decisions is on solid ground.

## How to Choose Between Home and Online During Coursework Season

For most DP students living here, a mix works best: home sessions for the long, thinking-heavy meetings, online for short check-ins, and online whenever traffic or schedules make a home visit unreliable. A home session is better for long reading and annotation of a printed draft. Online suits quick questions and shared-document review.

## Is It Safe to Hire Someone Who Offers to "Handle" the IA?

No. Any person or service that offers to write, complete or substantially edit an IA or Extended Essay for a student is offering academic misconduct, and the consequences fall on the student. The IB treats malpractice seriously, the school submits the work, and similarity checking is routine. A tutor who is honest about the limits is protecting your child.

Watch for these signals during the first conversation.

- **A promise of a specific grade** on the IA. No tutor controls the marking.
- **"We will give you a sample to adapt."** Close adaptation of a model is risky and can be flagged.
- **Reluctance to explain what they will and will not do.** A responsible tutor states it clearly.
- **An offer to write sections, "just to show how it is done".** The student will be tempted to use them.
- **Anything done without the student present.** Coursework support should be visible to the student.

Among IB tutors in Sector 57 Gurgaon, as anywhere, a tutor who tells you what they will not do is worth more than one who agrees to everything. For the broader checklist on judging a tutor, see [how to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/how-to-choose-ib-tutor-gurgaon/).

## What Parents Can Do During Coursework Season

Parents often feel they should either stay out entirely or take over. A middle path serves the student better.

**Know the dates.** Ask the DP coordinator for the full list of internal deadlines across all subjects, and put them on one calendar. Overlaps are visible in advance.

**Protect blocks of time.** Coursework needs unbroken hours, not twenty-minute slots between other tasks. Clear a weekend morning every week during the heavy months.

**Ask about the process, not the product.** "What did you decide this week, and why?" is a better question than "how many pages have you written?"

**Do not edit.** Parents rewriting a sentence feel helpful and can cause serious harm. If something looks wrong, ask the student to look at it again.

**Watch for overload.** A student with three IAs, an EE draft and weekly tests may need something removed from the week, such as an optional activity, for a few weeks. Say so before it becomes a crisis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an IB tutor help with my child's Internal Assessment?

Yes, within limits. A tutor can explain the criteria, question the student's reasoning and teach any subject skill the IA needs. A tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the submission. The school's teacher gives feedback on one draft, and the work must be the student's own. Be wary of anyone who offers to do more.

### What is the right time to start IA support in DP1?

Start when the student is thinking about topics, usually in the second half of DP1, before choices harden. Early help is cheap and effective: narrowing a question or planning a method takes a few sessions. Starting after a draft already exists limits what can change. Confirm the school's own deadlines before fixing a plan.

### How does a tutor help with the Extended Essay without writing it?

By helping at milestones: testing whether the research question is answerable, planning reading, structuring an argument and asking questions about a draft. The student writes and the supervisor at school remains the main guide. The EE guide was updated for first assessment in 2027, so follow the version the school issues.

### Are home lessons practical in Sector 57 societies?

Yes, if arranged properly. Pre-register the tutor at the society gate, agree a weekend or early-evening slot to avoid weekday traffic, and make sure the student has a quiet table. Where travel is unreliable, move sessions online. Many families combine both across the year.

### What if my child's school has strict rules about outside help?

Follow the school's rules, and tell the tutor at the start. Schools are required to follow the IB's academic integrity policy, and some add their own limits on outside support. A responsible tutor will adjust, for example by working only on subject skills and leaving the coursework text to the student.

### How many sessions does a coursework project need?

It depends on the student and the component. As a rough shape, a few sessions for topic and plan, a few for draft questions and one or two for revision, spread over weeks rather than days. More is not better: a student needs time between sessions to think and write. Review after the first month.

### Can online lessons work for IA and EE support?

Yes. Shared documents make it easy to point at a line and discuss it, and a pen tablet works well for mathematics. The student should share their own draft and make the changes themselves. Home sessions are useful for long planning meetings. The practical set-up is covered in the online tutoring guide linked above.

### My child has three IAs due in the same fortnight. What now?

Sort them by effort remaining, not by due date, and talk to the school about extensions where its policy allows. Break each into stages, schedule short daily blocks, and drop one non-essential commitment for two weeks. A tutor can help the student plan and prioritise, but cannot reduce the amount of writing the student must do.

### How do I know the tutor is not writing my child's work?

Ask the student to explain, without notes, why each key choice in the IA was made. Look for a steady voice across drafts, and for sessions where the student does most of the talking. Ask the tutor for a clear statement of what they will do and will not do. If answers are vague, change tutors.
