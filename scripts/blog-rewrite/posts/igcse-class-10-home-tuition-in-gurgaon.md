## What makes Class 10 different from Class 9 in IGCSE?

Class 10 is the exam year. In Class 9 the student learns the syllabus; in Class 10 the student must convert that learning into marks under timed conditions, across every subject, within a fixed series. Mocks begin to count as evidence, school internal tests overlap with revision, and the student's choices about where to spend limited time carry real consequences.

Four shifts mark the change:

- **Content becomes secondary to conversion.** Most syllabus content has been taught by early Class 10, and what remains is applying it to unfamiliar questions.
- **Pace matters.** Every paper is timed, so a student who knows the material but writes slowly loses marks.
- **Subjects compete.** Six or seven subjects share the same weeks, and each has its own past-paper cycle.
- **Decisions have deadlines.** Entry for the series, tier choices and practical paper arrangements are set by the school and cannot be changed late.

A student coming through the Indian system often finds the shift abrupt. In Class 9, a unit test is a snapshot. In Class 10, mocks begin to predict the final result, which means that anxiety rises just when the workload peaks. Home tuition's job is to absorb some of that pressure by giving the student a clear plan and a person who checks it.

## When are the IGCSE exams, and who decides?

The school decides which series a student sits, so the first task is to ask the school for the exact series and dates. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (a series offered only in India), May/June and October/November. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE series run in January and May/June. A student cannot choose a series independently of the school.

:::stats IGCSE series at a glance
3 | Cambridge series: Feb/March, May/June, Oct/Nov
2 | Pearson Edexcel series: January, May/June
1 | Decision-maker: the school, not the family
:::

The February/March Cambridge series exists specifically for India. It allows Grade 10 results to arrive in time for Class 11 admissions and for IB Diploma Programme (DP) or A Level entry in the same academic year. A student sitting in May/June instead will have results later, which changes the timing of subject decisions for the next stage.

| Series | Who it suits | What it means for planning |
|---|---|---|
| Cambridge February/March | Indian schools wanting results before the next admissions cycle | Revision peaks in December to January; mocks earlier in the year |
| Cambridge May/June | Schools following an international calendar | Longer run-up; revision peaks around March to April |
| Cambridge October/November | Retakes, late entries and some schools | Often used for resits; plan separately |
| Edexcel January | Schools running an early series | Compressed final-year timeline |
| Edexcel May/June | The main Edexcel series for many schools | Similar rhythm to Cambridge May/June |

If the school has not yet confirmed dates, ask for the likely series and the expected mock window. The tutor needs these two facts to plan anything useful. ## How should a Class 10 year be planned from August to the exam?

Plan it in four phases: consolidate the syllabus, practise by section, rehearse full papers, then repair and rest. The exact months depend on the series, so the phases below are expressed relative to the exam date. A school running the February/March series compresses everything earlier than one running May/June.

:::timeline The final-year arc, counted back from the exam
6-8 months out | Finish content, diagnose every subject
4-6 months out | Section practice, error logs begin
2-4 months out | Mixed papers, first full timed papers
Mock period | Full papers, honest marking, triage
Final 6 weeks | Short timed work, repair, rest
:::

### Phase one: consolidate and diagnose

In the first phase, the tutor's role is diagnostic. Take the last two or three school tests or the first mock, and sort the lost marks by cause: content gap, method, arithmetic, reading or time. A student who has gaps from Class 9 should repair them now, not in January. A tutor can use short quizzes across the syllabus to locate weak units without making the student feel examined.

### Phase two: section practice and the error log

Here the student works section by section on past-paper questions, marked against the mark scheme, with an error log started in each priority subject. The principle for maths papers is set out in [IGCSE maths past papers](/blog/why-past-papers-are-key-to-igcse-maths-success/), and applies with changes to every subject.

### Phase three: full timed papers

A student should not meet their first full paper in the mock. From around three months out, run one full timed paper a week in the most at-risk subject, and a shorter timed section in others. The aim is to learn pace and stamina, not to chase scores.

### Phase four: repair and rest

Once mocks are done, the work narrows sharply. Fix the three or four biggest error types in each subject, run short timed sections and protect sleep. Final-week cramming is rarely productive.

## How do you triage six or seven IGCSE subjects?

Triage by comparing three things for each subject: how many marks are realistically recoverable, how much time recovery needs, and how much the grade matters for the student's next step. Give the most time to subjects where a moderate effort moves many marks, protect strong subjects with light maintenance, and accept that a weak subject with little upside may need a modest, targeted plan.

A simple scoring table helps. Score each subject from one to three on each column, then discuss the totals with the tutor and the school.

| Subject | Current standing | Recoverable marks | Matters for next step | Time to give |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maths | Slipping on Paper 4 | High | High, especially for IB AA or A Level | Largest share |
| Physics | Solid theory, weak calculations | Medium | Medium to high | Steady weekly |
| Chemistry | Gaps in moles and organic | High | Medium | Targeted blocks |
| Biology | Strong recall | Low | Medium | Maintenance |
| English | Reasonable | Medium | High | Short weekly feedback |
| Economics | Needs structure in answers | Medium | Lower | Light plus papers |

This table is an example of the format, not a ranking of subjects. A different student will fill it in differently. The point is that triage is explicit: families should decide, not drift. If the student plans to go on to the IB DP, maths and the sciences matter more for choice at the next step, and the relevant transition is covered in [from IGCSE mathematics to IB DP](/blog/from-igcse-mathematics-to-ib-dp-building-a-strong-foundation/).

### How much weekly time can a Class 10 student really give?

A Class 10 student has only so many genuinely good study hours per week once school, travel, homework and activities are counted. Plan for the hours that exist, not the ideal. A pattern of one tutored session of about 60 to 90 minutes per priority subject per week, plus short independent tasks, is sustainable for most students; heavier plans tend to collapse within a few weeks.

## What should you do after a weak mock?

After a weak mock, do not add hours immediately. Mark the paper line by line, sort every lost mark into a cause, and look for the two or three patterns that explain most of the loss. Then repair those patterns in a defined fortnight and retest. A weak mock is information, not a verdict, and the panic response of adding more tuition often makes it worse.

Use this numbered process within a week of getting the paper back:

1. **Collect the evidence.** The marked paper, the mark scheme and the school's feedback, if any.
2. **Re-mark for causes.** For each lost mark, record content gap, method, arithmetic, reading, presentation or time.
3. **Count and rank.** Which two or three causes account for most of the lost marks?
4. **Plan a fortnight of repair.** Specific tasks for each cause, with time limits.
5. **Retest.** A short paper or section under timing at the end of the fortnight.
6. **Decide on changes.** Add or drop hours only after retest evidence.

Many students discover that the loss was not where they feared. A student who felt weak in Chemistry may find that most of their lost marks in the mock were from misread command words across subjects, a fix that costs little and helps everywhere. ## What should home tuition actually do in Class 10?

IGCSE Class 10 home tuition in Gurgaon should do four jobs: diagnose, teach what is still missing, mark practice with an examiner's eye and keep the plan honest. It should not just redo school lessons or complete homework. A tutor who spends most sessions on homework help leaves the student with finished worksheets but the same exam weaknesses.

What a good session in the final year looks like:

- **A retest.** Two or three questions that repeat last week's errors, without notes.
- **Targeted teaching.** One topic the student has not mastered, taught through a worked example and a student attempt.
- **Timed practice.** An exam-style question or section, with a clear time limit.
- **Marking together.** Mark scheme open, errors named and logged.
- **A written task.** What to do before next time, with how long it should take.

Home tuition suits Class 10 for a few specific reasons. The tutor can see the student's paper, handwriting and desk habits. A student with a heavy week can have a quieter, more focused hour than a group class gives. And a tutor who comes to the home can help the family set up routines, such as a fixed study space and a shared calendar of tests and mocks. ## Which subjects need what in the final year?

Each subject has a different pressure point in Class 10. The list below sets out the typical ones and the exact papers involved, so that the tutor's time goes to the right place.

**Mathematics.** Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 (from the 2025 exams): Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator); Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is tiered Foundation or Higher, with two calculator papers per tier. Core tops out at grade C; Extended reaches the top grade. In Class 10, the work is timed papers, non-calculator fluency and the habit of showing method. **Sciences.** Cambridge IGCSE sciences (syllabuses examined from 2023) are assessed through multiple choice (30%), theory (50%) and practical assessment (20%), with the practical through Paper 5 or Paper 6, chosen by the school. Pearson Edexcel International GCSE sciences are untiered, with Paper 1 and Paper 2 for each separate science and practical skills assessed within the written papers. Class 10 work is calculations, command words, data handling and practical-skill questions. **English.** English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses: First Language English 0500, English as a Second Language 0510 and 0511, and Literature in English 0475, with Pearson Edexcel offering Language A (4EA1), Language B (4EB1) and English as a Second Language (4ES1). **Economics, Business Studies and Computer Science.** Economics 0455 has a multiple-choice paper (30%) and a structured paper with data (70%); Business Studies 0450 has two papers of 50% each, including a case study; Computer Science 0478 has a computer systems paper and an algorithms, programming and logic paper. The final-year work is structured answer technique, calculations and programming practice.

**Additional Mathematics.** Cambridge IGCSE Additional Mathematics 0606 has no tiers, two written papers with a calculator allowed, and is graded A*-E. It is usually taken alongside 0580 Extended by students heading for IB Mathematics Analysis and Approaches HL or A Level Mathematics. ## One tutor or several in the final year?

Use one tutor for a subject, and keep the total number of tutors small. A student with three or four tutors, each on a different weekday, ends up with an evening full of lessons and no time for the follow-up tasks that make them work. If maths and sciences both need support, consider whether one tutor can cover two related subjects, or alternate weeks. [IGCSE maths and science tutors in Gurgaon](/blog/top-igcse-tutors-in-gurgaon-for-maths-science/) explores how the two interact and when one tutor or two is the right call.

A simple rule: add a subject only if the student can complete the previous subject's follow-up tasks. If tasks are going undone, the schedule is too full, and adding lessons will not help. Check this at the end of the first month.

## How do you protect rest and confidence in Class 10?

Protect a fixed amount of rest, because a tired student marks poorly, reads carelessly and sleeps badly before exams. A realistic plan reserves at least one half-day a week with no study, enough sleep on school nights and a deliberate lighter week after each mock. These are not luxuries; they are part of the study plan.

Signs that the load is too high include:

- Tasks completed late or not at all, with growing excuses.
- Errors becoming careless rather than conceptual.
- Dread around a subject that used to be fine.
- Unusual irritability, headaches or poor sleep.
- A student who stops asking questions and says "fine" to everything.

When these appear, cut the plan to its core: one or two priority subjects, short sessions and no extras, for two weeks. Then rebuild gradually. Students who feel in control of their plan do better than students who simply have more hours. And the same rule applies at home: parents who check in on the plan, not on every question, give the student room to take ownership.

## Mistakes families make in the final year

**Starting too late.** A plan that begins after the mock leaves little time to repair. Starting in Class 10 term one, even lightly, is far less stressful.

**Chasing every subject equally.** Without triage, strong subjects get the same time as weak ones, and the weak ones stay weak.

**Using the tutor as a homework service.** Homework help has a place, but a student who only completes worksheets does not learn to handle unseen questions.

**Doing many papers, marking few.** Papers are only as useful as the marking and repair that follow.

**Ignoring the non-calculator and practical elements.** Both are common sources of lost marks and both are trainable.

**Confusing mocks with the final result.** A mock shows the current state, and the next three months can change it. It is also the best data a family will have.

**Leaving decisions about the next stage to the last minute.** If the student is planning the IB DP or A Levels next, bridge topics and subject choices should be discussed during Class 10. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should we start IGCSE Class 10 home tuition in Gurgaon?

Early in Class 10, or earlier if gaps from Class 9 are visible. A diagnostic in the first term gives time to repair weak units before mocks. Starting after a weak mock is better than not starting, but leaves little time. The aim is a steady plan across several months, not an emergency burst in the final weeks.

### How many hours of home tuition does a Class 10 student need?

Most students do well with one session of 60 to 90 minutes per priority subject per week, plus short independent tasks. More is not automatically better, because the student must also do school work and the follow-up tasks. Increase hours only for a specific reason, such as a hard unit or the weeks around mocks, and review after a month.

### Which exam series will my child sit?

The school decides. Cambridge series run in February/March (offered only in India), May/June and October/November, and Pearson Edexcel runs in January and May/June. Ask the school which series your child is entered for and when mocks are held, and let the tutor work backwards from those dates. A family cannot choose a series independently of the school.

### Should we focus on weak subjects or strong ones?

Give most time to subjects where moderate effort will recover many marks, keep strong subjects on light maintenance and use a modest, targeted plan for weak subjects with little upside. Ask the tutor to score each subject on recoverable marks, importance for the next step and time needed. Triage should be explicit and agreed with the student.

### My child had a bad mock. Should we panic and add tutors?

No. Mark the paper line by line, sort lost marks by cause and identify the two or three patterns that explain most of the loss. Repair those over a fortnight and retest. Adding hours without understanding the cause usually adds stress. A weak mock is information that makes the remaining months more focused.

### Is home tuition better than a coaching class for Class 10?

It depends on the student. Home tuition offers diagnosis, one-to-one marking and flexibility. Coaching classes offer structure and peer pace. Students who need a plan built around their own errors usually benefit more from one-to-one. Students who lack motivation and thrive with peers may prefer a class. Many families combine a class with a short weekly one-to-one session.

### Can online lessons work for Class 10 IGCSE?

Yes, for most subjects. A shared whiteboard and a pen tablet suit maths, calculations and paper marking well. Practical handling needs the school lab, but a tutor can prepare the written side of the practical online. Online also helps in weeks with heavy traffic or illness. Many families use home sessions for long paper blocks and online for short ones.

### What if the tutor and my child do not get along?

Say so within the first two or three sessions. Fit includes pace, clarity and rapport. A family can ask IB Gram for a different match, subject to tutor availability. A trial session before committing helps catch problems early. When asking for a change, say what did not work, so the next match can address it.

### Do we need a tutor for every subject?

Rarely. Most students need help in one to three subjects. A tutor for every subject usually overloads the week without helping. Start with the subject where marks and confidence are most at risk, review after a month, and add only if the student is completing the follow-up work and still needs support elsewhere.

### Will a tutor secure a top grade in Class 10?

No tutor can guarantee a grade, and a promise of one is a reason for caution. A good tutor can improve diagnosis, practice quality and feedback speed. Results depend on the student's effort, the school's teaching, the tier and the exam itself. Judge the tutoring by the quality of the process and the evidence in the student's work.
