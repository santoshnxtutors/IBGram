## What makes IB Physics HL different from other physics tutoring?

IB Physics HL is assessed through written papers and an internal assessment, with command terms deciding how deep each answer must go, and with Higher Level adding Additional Higher Level (AHL) content on top of the Standard Level (SL) core. A tutor who is strong at university physics or at another board's physics can still be weak here, because the marks are won by a particular style of answer.

Three features separate the subject from a standard physics tuition:

- **Command terms control the mark.** "State", "explain", "derive", "evaluate" and "discuss" each ask for a different depth. A student can know the physics and still lose marks by explaining when the question asked for a statement, or stating when it asked for an explanation.
- **Data and graphs are everywhere.** The sciences were first assessed in their current form in 2025, with Paper 1 split into 1A (multiple choice) and 1B (data-based questions), and Paper 2 made of short-answer and extended-response questions. Reading a graph, handling uncertainty and commenting on a trend are marked skills, not extras.
- **The internal assessment is a scientific investigation.** It is worth 20 percent, marked on four criteria of 6 marks each (Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion, Evaluation), with a maximum of 3,000 words. It is the student's own work, and a tutor's role is limited.

Because the syllabus and assessment details are updated periodically, a good tutor works from the current subject guide, not from memory of an older course. This is the first thing to check, and it appears again in the checklist below. ## Does my child need a Physics HL tutor at all?

Your child needs a Physics HL tutor only if there is a specific, repeated problem that school support has not fixed: the same type of question lost across several tests, a gap from IGCSE or MYP that keeps resurfacing, or a mismatch between understanding and marks. A student who is performing near target and acts on teacher feedback usually does not.

Before shortlisting anyone, write one sentence describing the problem. Compare these two:

- "Physics is hard." This cannot be tutored; it is too vague.
- "She follows the lesson, but in the multi-step problems she loses marks in the second step, and her graph analysis answers are descriptive when the question says evaluate." This can be tutored, and it tells you exactly what to test in a trial lesson.

If the sentence you write is the vague one, ask the physics teacher to look at two recent scripts and name the pattern. If the school can name it, the tutor's job becomes clear. ## What qualifications should an IB Physics HL tutor have?

An IB Physics HL tutor should have a strong physics background, direct experience teaching or tutoring the IB Diploma Programme at Higher Level, and familiarity with the current subject guide and assessment. Degrees and experience matter, but the combination of IB-specific knowledge and a track record of explaining physics to students at this level matters more than any one credential.

Ask for the following, and treat each as a question to be answered, not a box to be ticked:

| Check | What to ask | What a good answer sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| Subject depth | Which topics in the Physics HL syllabus are AHL, and where do students struggle? | Names specific topics and the typical errors, without hesitation |
| IB familiarity | How do you use command terms in teaching? | Describes training the student to match answer depth to the verb |
| Assessment knowledge | How do you work with the data-based questions in Paper 1B? | Mentions graph reading, uncertainty, unfamiliar contexts |
| Current syllabus | Which version of the subject guide do you teach from? | Says the current guide and mentions the changes since the older version |
| Experience | Which level and which kinds of students have you taught? | Honest and specific; does not claim to be good at everything |
| IA boundaries | What do you do and not do with the IA? | Describes teaching skills, not writing or editing the report |
| Verification | Can your profile be verified? | Yes, with a clear process |

A tutor who is a strong researcher or engineer may be less practised at the specific demands of an IB paper. A tutor who has taught the course for years but does not read the current guide may carry outdated habits. Neither is disqualifying, but both are worth knowing.

## How can you judge a tutor's explanation quality?

How to choose an IB Physics HL tutor on explanation alone: you can judge it by listening for four things: whether the tutor asks before telling, whether the explanation is anchored to a physical picture, whether the equation is introduced after the idea rather than before it, and whether the tutor checks understanding by asking the student to do something. A tutor who lectures for ten minutes without a question is demonstrating, not teaching.

Here is what each looks like in a live session.

1. **Asks before telling.** "What do you think happens to the speed if the mass doubles?" invites the student's model of the world into the room, and any error in that model becomes visible. A tutor who begins by stating the rule hides every misconception.
2. **Uses a picture or a case.** A free-body diagram, a velocity-time sketch, a field line drawing, an energy bar chart. If every explanation is an algebraic line, the student can follow the symbols without the physics.
3. **Introduces the equation as a summary of an idea.** A good tutor says what each symbol means, when the equation applies, and what it does not cover, before the student plugs numbers in.
4. **Checks by asking for action.** "Try the next one yourself, and say why you chose that equation." The follow-up reveals whether the explanation landed.

A weak explanation has the opposite shape: it starts with a formula, moves quickly through a worked example, asks "Is that clear?" and accepts a nod. The nod is not evidence.

> The test of an explanation is not whether the student nods, but whether they can do the next question without you.

A fast check is to ask the student, after the trial, to explain the idea back in their own words to you. If they can, the tutor taught. If they can only repeat the steps, the tutor demonstrated.

## What should a trial lesson for IB Physics HL include?

A trial lesson for IB Physics HL should include a short diagnostic on a real problem from the student's current unit, one question that tests a prerequisite, a data or graph task, a command-term task, and a few minutes at the end for the tutor to describe the plan. A trial that is only the tutor talking, or only a friendly chat, produces no evidence.

Plan the trial before the tutor arrives. Give the tutor two things in advance: the student's current unit and two recent marked pieces of work. Then watch for these six tasks.

:::steps A useful Physics HL trial lesson
Diagnose | Tutor watches the student work, silently
Prerequisite | One earlier-year idea is tested
Data task | A graph or table with uncertainty
Command term | Same idea, "state" then "evaluate"
Plan | Tutor names three priorities
:::

### Task 1: A diagnostic problem, attempted cold

The tutor sets a problem slightly above the student's comfort level from the current unit and says nothing while the student works. What the tutor does here matters. A good tutor watches where the pen stops, notes which equation the student reaches for first, and asks "what were you thinking there?" afterwards. A weak tutor jumps in at the first hesitation.

### Task 2: A prerequisite probe

Within the session, the tutor drops back to one idea from an earlier year that the current unit depends on, for example vector components before projectile motion, or ratio and proportion before inverse-square relationships. A student's difficulty at HL is often a gap two steps back. A tutor who never probes backwards cannot find it.

### Task 3: A data or graph question

Ask for a question with a graph, a table or a set of measurements, and with an uncertainty. The tutor should ask the student to describe the trend before calculating anything, to say what the gradient or area represents, and to comment on whether the data supports the conclusion. This tests the exact skills that Paper 1B and the internal assessment reward.

### Task 4: One idea, two command terms

Take one fact and ask the student to answer it twice: first as "state", then as "explain", then, if time permits, as "evaluate". The tutor should show how each answer differs in length, structure and justification. If the tutor has no clear view on how the answers differ, the command-term teaching is missing.

### Task 5: A worked example, with the student leading

After the tutor models a solution, the student should do a near-identical problem while the tutor stays quiet. This shows whether the explanation transferred. Pay attention to whether the tutor lets the student make a small mistake and find it, or corrects instantly.

### Task 6: A plan in plain words

At the end, ask the tutor to name three priorities for the student, the order they will take, and how progress will be checked. If the answer is "we will cover the syllabus", there is no plan. If the answer is "your daughter loses marks on unit conversions and on explaining graphs; we will start there, and I will return marked work each week", there is one.

The trial is the cheapest source of evidence you will have, and the most common error is wasting it on pleasantries. If you are comparing home and online for the trial, the guide on [IB home tutor versus online tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/ib-home-tutor-vs-online-tutor-gurgaon/) lists what each needs.

## What are the red flags when choosing an IB Physics HL tutor?

The main red flags are a promised grade, a tutor who solves every problem for the student, no questions about the school's calendar or the student's current work, no marked work returned, and an inability to explain an idea two different ways. Any one is enough to pause and ask a direct question, and two or more are enough to stop.

The list below separates warnings you can catch before the first session from those that only appear during it.

| Red flag | When you see it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Promises a specific grade | Before booking | Nobody controls the student's exam performance |
| Cannot say which topics are AHL | Before booking | Suggests weak familiarity with HL |
| Does not ask about the school, unit or mock dates | First conversation | Plans without the calendar are guesses |
| Offers to write or edit the IA | Any time | Breaches the IB's academic integrity policy |
| Solves problems at the board while the student watches | Trial | Student learns to follow, not to do |
| Only one way of explaining | Trial | Cannot adapt when the first explanation fails |
| No written feedback between sessions | First month | No evidence the plan is working |
| Dismisses the school's teacher | Any time | Tutoring works with the school, not against it |
| Pressure to commit to a bundle before a trial | Before booking | A demo session is a reasonable request |
| Teaches from an old syllabus | Trial | Content and assessment have changed |

Two of these deserve more explanation.

**Solving at the board.** Many tutors default to demonstration because it feels productive and parents see "teaching". But a student who watches a correct solution has seen a correct solution, not built the skill of producing one. The ratio to look for in a session is that the student is writing most of the time.

**The IA boundary.** The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to give feedback on one draft; it does not allow anyone, tutor or parent, to write, rewrite or edit the student's submission. A tutor may teach the underlying skill, question the reasoning, point at a criterion and ask the student to reread their own paragraph against it. A tutor who offers to "fix" the report, or to supply the data analysis, is risking the student's diploma. For the broader rules, see [the IB Internal Assessment guide](/blog/ib-internal-assessment-guide/).

## How should a Physics HL tutor handle the internal assessment?

A Physics HL tutor should teach the skills the internal assessment rewards, such as designing a method that can answer a focused question, handling data and uncertainty, drawing a justified conclusion and evaluating the method, but should not choose the question, collect the data or write the report. The student owns every part of the submitted work.

The IA is the scientific investigation, worth 20 percent, with a maximum of 3,000 words and four criteria of 6 marks each: Research design, Data analysis, Conclusion and Evaluation. Students may investigate in groups of up to three, but each writes an individual report.

What useful help looks like in practice:

- **Question refinement.** The tutor asks "what exactly are you measuring, and what will you change?" until the student's question is precise. The student rewrites it.
- **Feasibility check.** The tutor asks whether the equipment, time and safety make the investigation workable, and what the biggest source of error will be.
- **Uncertainty teaching.** The tutor shows how to propagate uncertainty in a worked example with different numbers, then asks the student to do their own.
- **Reading against the criteria.** The student reads their own paragraph against a criterion descriptor, and the tutor asks what a marker would give it and why.
- **Evaluation practice.** The tutor asks the student to name limitations that are specific, not generic: "the thermometer reads to 1 °C, which is large compared with the temperature change" rather than "human error".

What the tutor must not do is write sentences for the report, rearrange the student's draft, or fill in the analysis. A family should ask directly: "What will you do with my child's IA, and what will you not do?" A clear, firm answer is a good sign.

## How do you test a tutor's grasp of the maths inside Physics HL?

You test a tutor's grasp of the maths by giving a derivation or a rearrangement problem and watching whether the tutor handles the algebra cleanly, links it to the physical meaning, and shows the student how to check the answer by units and limiting cases. Physics HL leans on algebra, trigonometry, logarithms and graphs, and a tutor who is shaky there passes the weakness on.

Many Physics HL students who struggle are not struggling with physics. They are struggling with the mathematics they need to express it. Common examples include rearranging an equation without error, working with exponents and logarithms in decay and growth, handling vector components, and reading a gradient with units. A tutor who sees only "physics difficulty" will teach more physics when the student needs algebra.

A good probe in a trial: ask the student to rearrange a multi-variable equation for a different subject, then check dimensions. A strong tutor will say "let's check the units" automatically, because unit analysis catches errors early and builds the habit examiners reward. If your child is also taking Mathematics Analysis and Approaches (AA) HL, the two subjects reinforce one another, and the structure of marks in the maths paper is described in [how to score a 7 in IB Math AA HL](/blog/how-to-score-7-ib-math-aa-hl/); a tutor who knows how the two subjects overlap can save time.

## Which is better for Physics HL, home or online tutoring?

Both can work. Home tutoring is often better for students who need a person across the table to see their working as it happens, while online tutoring works well when the tutor and student share a screen with a pen tablet and the student is comfortable writing digitally. The decision depends on the student, not on physics.

Physics is a pen-and-paper subject with diagrams. In an online session, the tutor needs to see the student's handwriting in real time, ideally through a shared whiteboard or a camera on the page. A student who sends a photo of finished work and then discusses it is getting a weaker feedback loop than one whose pen is visible as they think.

## What does a good first month with a Physics HL tutor look like?

A good first month with an IB Physics HL tutor has a diagnostic in week one, a short written plan, marked work returned each week, and a review at the end of week four. By then the tutor should be able to name the student's three most common errors and show a change in at least one.

1. **Week 1.** The diagnostic, using the current unit and one earlier prerequisite. The tutor asks for recent marked work from school.
2. **Week 1 or 2.** A written plan of three to five priorities, with the order and the reason.
3. **Weeks 2 and 3.** Each session opens with the previous week's task, works one priority and ends with a set of questions. The student returns the questions before the next session.
4. **Week 4.** A review: an error log is checked, and the tutor reports what is better, what is not, and what changes.

When you are working out how to choose an IB Physics HL tutor from a shortlist, compare them fairly by giving each the same trial task and scoring them on the same criteria, immediately after the session, before the glow of personality fades. ## What if the tutor is good but the student still does not improve?

If a good tutor does not produce improvement within about eight weeks of real work, the cause is usually one of four things: the student is not doing the between-session practice, the real gap is in a subject the tutor is not addressing (often mathematics), the school timeline is working against the plan, or the format is not suiting the student. Diagnose before replacing.

Before replacing a tutor you chose carefully, walk through these in order:

- **Check the between-session work.** If the student returned half of the set questions, the sessions are a lecture. Fix the habit before judging the tutor.
- **Check the maths.** Many Physics HL difficulties are algebra, graph or unit difficulties. Ask the tutor to test this directly.
- **Check the calendar.** A tutor teaching Unit 5 while the school is on Unit 7 can leave the student unable to use the sessions on homework. Share the school's schedule.
- **Check the format.** A student who dozes in an online session at 8 pm might do better at home at 5 pm.

If all four are sound and the student is still not improving, change the tutor. The IB Gram process allows a family to ask for a different match, and no tutor can guarantee a grade, so the evidence of the first two months is the guide.

## Where do IB Physics HL students typically lose marks?

Students typically lose marks in Physics HL by answering at the wrong depth for the command term, by making unit and significant-figure slips, by misreading data or skipping the description of a trend, by failing to link steps in multi-part problems, and by giving generic evaluation in the internal assessment. A tutor should be able to say which of these applies to your child within a few sessions.

Consider a student who scores well in tests but loses marks on external papers. In many such cases, the test questions are narrow and the paper questions are mixed, so the student who recognises "this is the momentum question" in a test cannot choose the method when topics are blended. A tutor who sets mixed-topic questions early is addressing this.

Consider a different student who understands concepts but loses marks on "explain" questions. The answer is correct but incomplete: one link in the chain of reasoning is missing. A tutor who trains the student to write two linked sentences, cause and then effect, fixes this faster than any extra content.

A third pattern is the student who is careful in calculations but gives vague evaluation. "The experiment had errors" is not a mark. "The timing relied on a stopwatch with a reaction time comparable to the measurement, so the percentage uncertainty is large" can be one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the single most important thing when choosing an IB Physics HL tutor?

The single most important thing is whether the tutor can diagnose what your child does not understand and fix it using exam-style questions. Credentials and experience matter only as far as they produce that ability. A trial lesson built around a cold-attempt problem, a prerequisite probe and a data question will show whether the tutor can do it, far better than any profile will.

### Should the tutor have taught IB Physics specifically, or is a strong physics background enough?

IB experience matters because the marks depend on command terms, data-based questions and the internal assessment, which are specific to the programme. A strong physics background is necessary but not sufficient. A tutor who is new to the IB can still be good if they study the current guide, work from past papers and markschemes, and are honest about it; a tutor who dismisses the differences is a risk.

### How many trial lessons should we try before deciding?

One well-planned trial with each shortlisted tutor is usually enough, and two if the first result is close. Give each tutor the same trial tasks and score them straight afterwards. If the trial is only a conversation, it tells you about personality, not teaching. A demo or trial session can be arranged through IB Gram before you commit.

### Can a tutor help with the Physics HL internal assessment?

A tutor can teach the skills but cannot write, rewrite or edit the report. The IB's academic integrity policy allows a teacher to give feedback on one draft; no tutor or parent may edit the submission. Good help includes sharpening the research question, teaching uncertainty handling and asking the student to reread their own work against the criteria. A tutor who offers more than that is a serious risk.

### Is online Physics HL tutoring effective?

Online Physics HL tutoring is effective when the tutor can see the student's handwriting live, using a pen tablet, a shared whiteboard or a camera pointed at the page. Photos of finished work are a weaker feedback loop. Students who focus well on screens and have a quiet space do fine online; those who drift may do better with a tutor in the room.

### How often should a Physics HL student meet the tutor?

Most students do well with one session a week, rising to two in the weeks before mocks or a deadline cluster, and tapering afterwards. The sessions diagnose and the days between consolidate, so what the student does with the set work matters more than the number of meetings. A student who is not doing the between-session practice will not benefit from more sessions.

### What if my child says the tutor explains well but they still do not get it?

That usually means the tutor is explaining, not teaching. Ask the tutor to hand the next problem to the student and stay silent, and to use fewer demonstrations and more questions. If the student still cannot do it, the gap may be a prerequisite from an earlier year, which the tutor should probe. If it persists, change the approach or the tutor.

### How do I know a tutor's profile is genuine?

Ask for specifics you can check: the subjects and levels taught, the syllabus version used, and how they handle the internal assessment. At IB Gram, tutor profiles are verified before they are shortlisted. For any tutor, ask for a demo session and for one example of how they track progress. Vague answers to specific questions are informative.

### Does a more expensive tutor mean a better Physics HL tutor?

No. A higher rate can reflect preparation time, marked feedback between sessions, a specialist's experience or demand, but it can also be only a higher price. Compare what each hour includes: a plan, marked work, a diagnostic, a review. The factors behind fees are set out in the guide on IB tutor fees in Gurgaon, which does not quote amounts because they vary.

### When should we stop and switch tutors?

Switch when, after about eight weeks of real work from the student, the error patterns have not changed and the tutor cannot explain why. Also switch at once if there is a red flag such as a grade promise or an offer to write the internal assessment. Before switching, tell the tutor what is not working, because a small change in pace or format sometimes fixes it.
