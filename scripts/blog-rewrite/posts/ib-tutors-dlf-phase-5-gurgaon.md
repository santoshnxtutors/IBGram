## Why is the MYP 5 to DP1 year so hard for DLF Phase 5 students?

The MYP 5 to DP1 year is hard because three changes arrive together: the assessment style shifts from criteria across eight subject groups to six DP subjects marked on exam papers and internal assessments, the content jumps in pace and abstraction, and the student must make subject choices that shape the next two years. Each change alone is manageable. Together, in the space of a summer, they catch strong students off guard.

Consider a student who finished MYP 5 with comfortable grades. The MYP assesses every subject against four criteria (A to D), each scored 0 to 8, and the total out of 32 converts to a 1 to 7 grade. The student learned to earn marks for investigating, communicating and applying ideas as well as for knowing content. In DP1, the same student meets timed papers where recall and speed carry real weight, subjects where HL means 240 recommended teaching hours against 150 for SL, and a calendar in which the Extended Essay (EE), Theory of Knowledge (TOK) and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS) all begin to ask for time.

### Four shifts a student feels in the first term of DP1

1. **From criteria to papers.** MYP rewards investigation and communication in tasks; DP papers reward accuracy and structure under a clock.
2. **From breadth to depth.** Eight subject groups become six subjects, three or four at HL, each with a heavier content load.
3. **From teacher-led pacing to self-managed deadlines.** Internal assessments, the EE and CAS need steady work with no one chasing weekly.
4. **From general mathematics to a branch.** Maths becomes Analysis and Approaches (AA) or Applications and Interpretation (AI), and the choice carries forward.

None of this means the student is unprepared. It means the preparation has to be deliberate, and a tutor who only knows one side of the boundary will teach the student the old rules or the new ones, not the bridge.

## What changes between MYP 5 and DP1, subject by subject?

The change is not equal across subjects. Some carry over smoothly; others change shape. Knowing which are which saves a family from over-tutoring the wrong subject.

| Subject area | MYP 5 emphasis | DP1 change | Tutoring implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mathematics | Criteria A to D, standard or extended | Branch choice (AA or AI), proof and technology | Early algebra repair; branch-specific skills |
| Sciences | Criteria and investigations | Papers with data-based questions; a 20% internal assessment | Data practice and lab-report habits |
| English and languages | Language and literature or acquisition phases | Paper 1, Paper 2, oral and essays in DP | Timed analysis and structured essays |
| Humanities and economics | Inquiry and criteria | Extended-response papers and commentaries | Essay structure, evaluation, real-world examples |
| Core (EE, TOK, CAS) | Personal Project in MYP 5 | Three new parallel demands | Planning more than content |

Notice that mathematics shows the sharpest break. A student heading for DP Mathematics AA HL moves from a standard or extended MYP course into content that is faster and more abstract. ## How should you choose HL subjects before choosing a tutor?

Choose HL subjects by university direction, genuine strength and workload, in that order of discussion, and make the decision with the school's DP coordinator before booking tutors. The coordinator makes the final placement, and tutoring a subject the student will drop wastes the first term. A DP student takes three or four subjects at HL and the rest at SL.

### A simple frame for the decision

Ask four questions, in this order.

1. **What do the target courses require?** Engineering, economics, medicine and architecture lists often name subjects; check each university's current requirement rather than rely on a general rule.
2. **Where is the student genuinely strong?** Look at MYP 5 criterion profiles, not only the final grade. A student with strong Criterion B and D in mathematics shows different potential from one who scores well on A alone.
3. **What does the workload look like?** Three HL science or maths subjects together are heavy. The student also carries the EE, TOK and CAS.
4. **What does the student enjoy enough to work on at 9 pm in February?** Interest is not a bonus; it is a study strategy.

The decision about STEM combinations is explored in [IB DP STEM subject choice](/blog/stem-subject-choice-ib-dp/), and the choice between the two maths courses in [IB Maths AA vs AI](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/). Students heading for engineering or economics degrees are often advised towards AA, but the right choice depends on the degree and the student.

## What should a tutor carrying a student across MYP and DP be able to do?

A tutor working across MYP and DP must know both assessment systems, diagnose from real work, adjust the style of teaching as the student moves from criteria to papers, and keep a thread of continuity so that habits built in MYP 5 are not thrown away. The point of one tutor across two programmes is not convenience; it is that the tutor already knows how the student thinks.

### Six abilities to look for

- **Reads both marking systems.** They can explain what separates a 6 from a 7 on an MYP criterion and what separates a 6 from a 7 on a DP paper, in specifics.
- **Diagnoses before planning.** They start with the student's latest MYP tasks or DP1 tests, not with a chapter list.
- **Teaches the bridge.** They know which MYP habits help in DP (explaining reasoning, applying to context) and which do not (assuming marks for effort).
- **Builds an error log and uses it.** Lost marks are tagged by cause and retested.
- **Respects the integrity rules.** For internal assessments they teach skills and question reasoning; they do not write or edit the work.
- **Works with the school calendar.** They know when unit tests, mocks and internal deadlines fall and plan around them.

For mathematics, the same questions apply with detail. The selection process is in [how to choose the right IB maths tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/how-to-choose-the-right-ib-maths-tutor-in-gurgaon/), and for a broader set of tests of a good fit see [how to choose an IB tutor in Gurgaon](/blog/how-to-choose-ib-tutor-gurgaon/).

### The question to ask first

Ask the tutor: "If my child's DP1 marks drop in the first term, what will you look at?" A good answer names scripts, error causes and the gap between MYP habits and DP demands. A weak answer is "we will cover more content".

### The summer between MYP 5 and DP1

The summer is where a tutor can add the most for the least cost, because there is no school load. A sensible summer plan has three parts: foundation repair in mathematics (algebra fluency, functions, notation), one subject-specific head start (for example, data handling for a science student), and a light reading habit for subjects such as English A or History. It does not need to be long: three or four short sessions a week and some independent practice is plenty. The goal is to arrive in DP1 already fluent in the basics, not ahead on content.

### The first term of DP1

The first term sets habits. The sensible aims are: a working error log in every quantitative subject, clean notation and working in maths, a note-taking method that supports recall, and a first look at the internal assessments, the EE and CAS so nothing is a surprise. The [IB DP core timeline](/blog/ib-dp-core-planning-timeline/) lays out how these three collide over two years and how to avoid a DP2 crunch.

### The second and third terms

Tutoring shifts from habit-building to problem areas that the first tests expose. This is where a tutor who knew the student in MYP 5 is most valuable: they can say "this is a new problem" or "this is the old habit again" within a session or two.

## How do home sessions work in DLF Phase 5?

Home sessions in DLF Phase 5 work well when the tutor, the study space and the timing are settled in advance. The area has a mix of gated societies and independent homes, and the practical questions are entry procedures, a quiet room, and evening timing relative to traffic. Decide these before the first session so the hour is spent teaching.

### Practical points for families here

Before you book IB tutors in DLF Phase 5, settle these points.

- **Gate and visitor entry.** Societies usually require a visitor registration or pre-approval; tell the tutor which procedure applies and who will approve the entry, so the first ten minutes are not lost at the gate.
- **A fixed study space.** A table with good light, a whiteboard or large sheet of paper for working, and no television in earshot is enough.
- **Evening timing.** Weekday evenings in Gurgaon can be congested on the main roads, and a tutor travelling between homes may arrive late. Slots in the early afternoon after school, on weekends or in the late evening can be steadier; ask the tutor what works for their route.
- **A parent's role.** You need not sit in, but a short message after each session about what was covered and what is set for next time keeps everyone aligned.

### When online is the better choice

Online sessions suit students with packed after-school schedules, families who travel, and subjects where the best-matched tutor is further away. A shared whiteboard or a tablet with a stylus makes maths and science workable online. A hybrid plan, with home sessions for concept-heavy work and short online blocks for exam practice, is often the most practical. ### Sciences

From the first assessment in 2025, DP sciences have Paper 1 in two parts (1A multiple choice and 1B data-based questions) and Paper 2 with short-answer and extended-response questions, plus an internal assessment worth 20% marked on four criteria of 6 marks each. A tutor for DP1 sciences needs to teach data analysis and the investigation habit as much as content. ### Economics, English and humanities

Economics has four units and two or three papers depending on level, with an internal assessment portfolio of three commentaries. English A is analysis and comparison under time. Humanities subjects reward structured argument and evidence. For each, the tutor's job is to teach the writing form and the use of examples, not to supply opinions.

### The core: EE, TOK and CAS

A tutor may help a student plan, question reasoning, and teach research and referencing skills. A tutor may not write, rewrite or edit any part of a submission. The EE is an independent research essay of up to 4,000 words, supervised by a teacher at the student's school. Follow the version of the EE guide your coordinator issues, because the guide has been updated.

## What does a first demo session look like?

A first demo session is a short trial, not a commitment. It should show how the tutor teaches your child, not how well they can present themselves. A typical shape for a student in the MYP 5 to DP1 window is below.

1. The tutor asks to see recent work: an MYP task with feedback, or a DP1 test.
2. They pick one topic, ask the student to attempt a question, and watch the working.
3. They explain, then ask the student to try a similar question alone.
4. They name two or three targets for the next four weeks and say what they would not cover.
5. You and your child compare notes afterwards and decide.

After the demo, ask your child: "Could you do a similar question alone now?" and "Did you feel able to say you did not understand?" If the answers are yes, the fit is promising. If the tutor spent the hour lecturing, or if your child felt talked at, try another match.

## Scenarios from MYP 5 and DP1

These are illustrative situations, not accounts of real students.

### The strong MYP 5 student whose DP1 marks fall

Consider a student with high MYP grades who scores well below that in the first DP1 tests. Often the cause is not ability but the change of assessment: the student earned MYP marks through investigation and explanation, and the DP papers now reward speed and structure. The fix is timed practice with markscheme-based marking, an error log, and a habit of showing every step. A tutor who knew the MYP profile can say which strengths to keep.

### The student unsure between AA and AI

A student who enjoys applications and statistics but fears abstract algebra may drift towards AI; one who likes proof and problem-solving may prefer AA. The decision depends on the target degree and the student's strengths. Speak to the coordinator, read the university requirements and do a short taster of each course's content before committing. See the [AA vs AI guide](/blog/ib-maths-aa-vs-ai-which-one-should-you-choose/).

### The student who wants to start early

Some families ask about starting DP content in the summer. A light head start in the weakest foundation is useful; a rushed march through DP1 content before school begins is not, because the student will meet it again in class and may form wrong habits. Repair and fluency beat early coverage. The question of timing is explored in [when to start IB tutoring](/blog/when-to-start-ib-tutoring-gurgaon/).

When both parents work late, the tutor needs to be able to run a session without a parent present, and the family needs a short written update. ## Do you need a tutor at all in the transition year?

Not always. A student who is coping, has a stable school routine and is learning the new demands without a drop in marks may need only a teacher's feedback and a sound study habit. A tutor makes sense when a student is losing marks to a specific cause the family cannot name, when a gap in foundations is blocking progress, or when the student needs an outside voice for planning the EE, the internal assessments and the exam calendar.

What does not help: a tutor for every subject from the first week, as this can crowd out sleep and independent practice. A better approach is to begin with the subject most at risk, usually mathematics or one of the sciences, run it for four weeks, and review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who are the best IB tutors in DLF Phase 5?

There is no single best, because the right tutor depends on the student's programme, subject, gap and personality. Judge by evidence of method: a diagnosis from real work, a clear session structure, an error log and honest reporting. IB Gram can shortlist verified tutors for a demo session, subject to tutor availability, so you can test fit before committing.

### When should a student start tutoring for the MYP 5 to DP1 transition?

A light start in the summer between MYP 5 and DP1 is usually the best-value moment, with focus on algebra, functions and notation for maths, and one other weak area. Heavy content tutoring before school begins adds little. If marks drop in the first DP1 term, a tutor can still help with a targeted plan; it is never too late to start.

### Is home tutoring practical in DLF Phase 5?

Yes, with preparation. Settle visitor entry at the gate, a quiet study space and an evening slot that avoids the worst of the traffic. If travel between homes makes timing unreliable, an online or hybrid arrangement is a sound alternative, not a downgrade. Choose the format your child will attend consistently.

### How do I choose between HL and SL subjects?

Start with the requirements of the courses your child might apply for, then match to genuine strengths and the total workload with the EE, TOK and CAS. A DP student takes three or four subjects at HL and the rest at SL. The school's coordinator makes the final placement, so discuss the options with them before choosing tutors.

### Can one tutor cover both MYP and DP maths?

Yes, if the tutor knows both assessment systems. MYP maths is criteria-based (A to D, each 0 to 8); DP maths is paper-based with an internal exploration. A tutor who has worked across both can bridge habits and notice when a problem is old or new. Ask them to explain how they would move a student from criterion marking to paper marking.

### Can a tutor help with the IA or the Extended Essay?

Within limits. A tutor can teach research skills, question the student's reasoning and ask them to reread a section against a criterion. A tutor cannot write, rewrite or edit the work, because the IB requires it to be the student's own. If anyone offers to write any part, walk away. The wider rules are in the [IB Internal Assessment guide](/blog/ib-internal-assessment-guide/).

### My child's marks dropped after starting DP1. Is a tutor the answer?

Possibly, but first find the cause. List the lost marks from recent tests and tag each as content, method, execution, reading or time. If most are habits (working, reading, time), a tutor plus an error log can fix them in weeks. If content is the cause, a tutor helps with a specific plan. If workload is the cause, no tutor fixes that; talk to the school.

### What if the tutor and my child do not click?

Say so after the demo or the first couple of sessions, and ask for a different match. Fit matters: a tutor who is excellent but whose manner does not suit your child will not help. IB Gram lets the family request a different match. Give feedback on what was wrong so the next shortlist is better.

### Do you offer online sessions for DLF Phase 5 students?

Yes. Lessons run at home, online or hybrid, and many students use a mix. Online works well with a shared whiteboard or tablet for maths and science. Choose the format by what your child will actually attend, and review it after four weeks. Availability depends on tutor availability.

### How many sessions a week does a student need in the transition year?

One focused session a week in the most at-risk subject is enough for most students, with independent practice between sessions. A second weekly session can help in a heavy term or before a mock, but more rarely adds value. Review after four weeks and adjust to the evidence, not a fixed rule.
