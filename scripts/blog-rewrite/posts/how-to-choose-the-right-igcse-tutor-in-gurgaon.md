## Why Is "IGCSE Tutor" Too Vague a Search?

"IGCSE" names a family of qualifications, and a tutor who is excellent in one corner may be ordinary in another. The board, the subject, the syllabus code, the tier and the series all change what a tutor needs to know, so a vague brief produces a vague match.

IGCSE in India means Cambridge IGCSE (Cambridge International Education) or Pearson Edexcel International GCSE. Cambridge exam series run in February/March (offered only in India), May/June and October/November. Pearson Edexcel series run in January and May/June. Cambridge grades most syllabuses A* to G, with some 9 to 1 versions under different codes; Edexcel International GCSE grades 9 to 1. Many Cambridge subjects have Core and Extended tiers, where Core tops out at grade C and Extended reaches A*.

Those are the facts a tutor must hold without looking them up, and they are the first filter when you work out how to choose an IGCSE tutor in Gurgaon. In maths, Cambridge 0580 Core candidates sit Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator), and Extended candidates sit Paper 2 and Paper 4. Edexcel's Mathematics A (4MA1) is Foundation or Higher with two calculator papers per tier. In Cambridge sciences, multiple choice is 30%, theory 50% and practical 20%, with the practical component assessed by Paper 5 or Paper 6 depending on the school. English at IGCSE is several different syllabuses, which means that "IGCSE English tutor" is barely a description at all.

Before contacting any tutor, write down five items: board, subject and code, tier, series and school. A tutor who asks for these in the first conversation is already behaving correctly.

## What Should You Decide Before You Interview Anyone?

Decide four things first: the exact need, the schedule you can sustain, the mode, and the evidence you will accept as progress. Without these, the interview is a sales conversation and the tutor controls it.

**The need.** Look at the last two marked papers. Is the problem a gap in content, a misunderstanding of what the question asks, poor timing, weak written method, or fear? A parent can usually tell which of these it is by reading the teacher's comments and asking the student to explain two lost marks. If you cannot tell, ask the tutor to find out in a diagnostic session.

**The schedule.** One session a week is the usual starting point. Gurgaon evenings are crowded with school finishing late, sport, activities and traffic that makes arrival times unpredictable. Choose a slot the student can keep for twelve weeks, not the slot that looks best on paper.

**The mode.** Home, online or hybrid. For maths and sciences, the written working matters, so an online tutor needs a pen tablet or shared board. If you are unsure, [online IGCSE tutoring in India](/blog/online-igcse-tutoring-india/) explains how it works well and where it does not, and [online vs home tutor for IGCSE maths](/blog/online-vs-home-tutor-for-igcse-maths/) goes deeper on maths.

**The evidence.** Agree in advance what progress means: corrected attempts, fewer repeated errors, an improved timed paper section. Test marks matter but lag, so do not make a single paper the judge.

## Which Interview Questions Actually Separate Good Tutors From Weak Ones?

The useful questions are the ones that cannot be answered with a slogan. A tutor who has taught IGCSE well answers them in specific detail within a minute; a tutor who has not will drift into general claims about patience, results and experience.

Ask these, in this order.

### Questions about the exam

1. Which exact syllabus code is my child preparing for, and what does each paper look like?
2. How do Core and Extended differ in what you teach, and what happens if my child's tier is wrong?
3. Which series is my child sitting, and how does that change the plan?
4. Have you taught the Edexcel version, the Cambridge version, or both?

The answers show whether the tutor has taught the specification or only the subject. In sciences, a strong answer mentions Papers 5 and 6 and the weighting of practical skills. In English, it distinguishes First Language English (0500), English as a Second Language (0510 and 0511) and Literature in English (0475). A tutor who says "it's all just English" has not taught any of them well.

### Questions about diagnosis

5. What will you do in the first session, and what will I get afterwards?
6. How would you find out whether my child lacks knowledge or lacks exam technique?
7. Tell me about a student with a problem like my child's. What did you change?

### Questions about method

8. How do you structure a session?
9. How much of the hour does the student spend working on their own?
10. What do you do when a student gets something wrong?
11. What do you set between sessions, and what do you do with it afterwards?
12. How do you use past papers, and what happens to a paper once it has been marked?

### Questions about honesty

13. What can tutoring not do?
14. Under what circumstances would you advise fewer sessions, a different tutor or none at all?
15. What is your cancellation and rescheduling policy?

The last group tests character. A tutor who says "my students always improve" has told you nothing credible. A tutor who says "if your child is doing the independent work and still not improving after six weeks, I would want to look again at whether the diagnosis was right" has told you a lot.

:::bars What a strong answer looks like (illustrative weights)
Names the exact syllabus and papers | 90 | Essential
Describes a diagnostic in steps | 85 | Essential
Student works for most of the hour | 75 | High
Explains use of past papers | 70 | High
Says what tutoring cannot do | 60 | Medium
Quotes years of experience | 20 | Low
These weights are a judgement about signal, not a measured statistic.
:::

## How Do You Score a Trial Lesson?

Score the trial lesson against seven criteria, each from 0 to 2, immediately after the session, with your child's view added separately. The rubric below turns a vague impression into a number you can compare across tutors. A total under nine of fourteen usually means the tutor is not ready for your child.

| Criterion | 0 points | 1 point | 2 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Syllabus precision | Speaks about "IGCSE" generally | Names the board or subject | Names code, papers and tier unprompted |
| Preparation | Arrives without reading the work sent | Skimmed it | Quotes a specific pattern from it |
| Diagnosis | Starts teaching a prepared topic | Asks a few questions | Runs short tasks, listens, names two or three gaps |
| Student activity | Tutor talks and writes most of the time | Student writes about half | Student attempts first, tutor responds |
| Handling errors | Corrects instantly | Explains the right method | Asks a question that lets the student find the slip |
| Written method | Ignores presentation | Mentions it | Insists on working that earns method marks |
| Close | No plan for next time | Tutor sets homework | Student states what they will do and why |

Add your child's score separately, from 0 to 2, on one question: "Did you have to think for yourself?" Do not add friendliness to the total; it is real and welcome but separate.

### How to run the trial so the score means something

Send one marked paper and the current school topic beforehand. Hold the trial at the time and in the mode you plan to use, so that you test the logistics: gate entry in a society, parking, a quiet table, the online connection. Sit nearby without hovering. Take brief notes against the rubric during, not after, because memory favours charm.

Afterwards ask the student three questions: what became clearer, what did you work out yourself, and what are you doing before the next session. Within a day, expect a short message from the tutor with named priorities. If it does not arrive, that is information.

### An example of scoring two tutors

Consider a Grade 10 student preparing Cambridge Extended maths who loses marks on algebraic proof and on the last parts of long questions. Tutor A is warm and fast, explains three topics and solves two problems on the board while the student watches. Tutor B asks the student to attempt a proof, listens to the reasoning, points out where the logic skips a step and asks the student to rewrite it. On the rubric, A scores around eight: strong on preparation and close, weak on student activity and handling errors. B scores around twelve. The student liked A more. The student improved with B. That is why the rubric exists.

## Which Red Flags Should End the Conversation?

Most red flags are about tutors substituting confidence for process. A few touch ethics, and those end the conversation at once.

:::bars Red flags, ranked by how fast I would leave (illustrative judgement)
Offers to do or heavily edit assessed coursework | 100 | End at once
Promises a specific grade | 90 | End at once
Package fixed before seeing any work | 65 | Push back
Cannot say which papers the exam has | 60 | Push back
Only quotes other students' results | 45 | Probe
Never asks about the school calendar | 40 | Probe
Rankings are my own judgement, not data. Anything touching authorship ends the talk.
:::

A few notes on the flags.

**Coursework.** Some IGCSE syllabuses, such as certain English options, have coursework or portfolio components, and some schools have school-based assignments. The student must produce assessed work themselves. A tutor may teach technique and explain criteria; writing or rewriting a submission is outside the line, and it can invalidate the work.

**Grade promises.** No tutor can guarantee a grade. Exam results depend on the starting point, practice, the exam itself and a dozen small factors. A tutor who promises an A* is selling, not teaching.

**Fixed packages.** A package built before a diagnostic is built around the tutor's calendar. Ask for a four-week start and a review instead.

**Syllabus blindness.** A tutor who cannot say how many papers the exam has, or which are calculator papers, has not prepared a student for it recently. This is the quickest test of all.

**Results-only credibility.** Quoted student results are unverifiable and mostly describe the students. Ask for method.

**Calendar blindness.** If the tutor never asks about the school's mock dates or the exam series, they cannot plan backwards from the deadlines that matter.

## How Does the Choice Differ by Subject?

The method for how to choose an IGCSE tutor in Gurgaon is the same across subjects; the evidence you look for changes. In maths you watch written method and calculator discipline, in the sciences you watch data handling and practical skills, in English you watch writing structure, and in economics, business and computer science you watch how the tutor handles application.

| Subject | What to test in the trial | Common trap |
|---|---|---|

| Physics, Chemistry, Biology | A calculation or data question plus a practical-skills question | Teaching recall only, ignoring Paper 5 or 6 skills |
| English (First or Second Language) | A short piece of writing marked against the paper's task | Confusing the syllabuses |
| Economics (0455) | A data-response question and one diagram | Memorised definitions with no application |
| Business Studies (0450) | A case-study question with calculation | Generic essay advice |
| Computer Science (0478) | Pseudocode trace and a short program | Theory only, no coding practice |

## One Tutor for Several Subjects or a Specialist for Each?

Use a specialist for any subject where your child is stuck or aiming high, and consider one general tutor only for lighter support in subjects where the student is already steady. A tutor who claims to teach maths, physics, chemistry, biology, English and economics at an equally high level is a claim worth testing hard.

The one real exception is the combination of maths and sciences, where several skills overlap: rearranging equations, unit handling, graph reading. Some tutors teach both well and the overlap helps the student. How that works, and when to split instead, is discussed in [IGCSE maths and science tutors in Gurgaon](/blog/top-igcse-tutors-in-gurgaon-for-maths-science/). The test is simple: ask the tutor to teach one hard topic in each subject during the trial. A genuine generalist will manage; a shallow one will repeat their strongest subject's habits in the other.

For students aiming at the top grades in maths with Additional Mathematics 0606 on the side, a maths specialist is almost always the right call, because the content is heavy and feeds into later study.

## How Do You Judge a Tutor's Use of Past Papers?

A good tutor uses past papers as diagnostic instruments, not as busywork. The question is what happens to a paper after it is marked: whether errors are classified, whether the student redoes lost questions and whether the same type of question appears again a week later.

Ask how many papers they would set in a typical month, whether under timed conditions, how they mark them against the mark scheme, and how they handle examiner reports. In an ordinary term, one timed paper section a week with proper marking is more useful than three full papers marked only for the total. Near the exam series, full papers under real conditions become the main tool. The reasoning for this approach is set out in [why past papers matter in IGCSE maths](/blog/why-past-papers-are-key-to-igcse-maths-success/), and a revision timetable that uses them sensibly is in [the six-week IGCSE revision plan](/blog/igcse-revision-six-week-plan/).

Watch for a tutor who prints papers from a pile and sets them in order without looking at the student's pattern of errors. That is a stock routine, and the student would do as well on their own.

Choose by what the student's week and the tutor's travel can sustain. Home sessions suit students who need routine and a physical workspace, provided the tutor is nearby enough to arrive reliably. ## How Often Should Sessions Happen?

Start with one session a week and review after four weeks. Add a second session only when there is a clear reason, such as mocks, a short repair of a specific gap or a series that is close. More hours without more independent work rarely help.

The reasoning is simple. A session produces understanding; the work between sessions turns it into habit. A student who has two sessions and no time to practise learns less than one who has a single session and does the follow-up. For students in Grade 10, the last eight weeks before an exam series can justify a heavier pattern, with a timed paper each week and a review session afterwards. The Class 10 year in detail is in [IGCSE Class 10 home tuition in Gurgaon](/blog/igcse-class-10-home-tuition-in-gurgaon/).

For a student in Grade 9, patience helps. Foundations built early make the exam year lighter, but a Grade 9 student who is on track does not need intensive tutoring. If you are unsure about the timing, the question to ask is whether the student can already explain, in their own words, where they lose marks.

## What Does a Good First Month Look Like?

A good first month contains a diagnostic, named priorities, corrected attempts, fewer prompts by week three and a review in week four. These are visible things; you should be able to look at them.

:::timeline A sound first month with an IGCSE tutor
Week 1 | Diagnostic; two or three written priorities
Week 2 | Corrected attempts on those priorities
Week 3 | Mixed questions with fewer prompts
Week 4 | Repeat a comparable task; review and decide
:::

At the week-four review, ask five things. Were the priorities worked on? Is there an original and corrected attempt for each? Has the same error appeared less often? Does the student begin tasks with less prompting? Is the written working clearer? If three or more are yes, continue. If fewer, ask for a reset: a short second diagnostic, revised priorities and a new review date.

Consider a typical Grade 10 student preparing Cambridge Chemistry 0620 who is losing marks on mole calculations and on Paper 6 questions. After four weeks, the folder shows a diagnostic that separated the two problems, corrected mole calculations in weeks two and three and a Paper 6 practice question the student completed without prompts. The mark on the latest test has barely moved, but the working has changed. That is a reason to continue. The mark will follow if the habits hold.

## When Should You Switch Tutors?

Switch when two consecutive reviews show no movement in the student's own work, when the tutor cannot produce a diagnosis or plan, or when anything touches academic integrity. Do not switch because of a single poor paper, an awkward session or a bad week.

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| One poor test after a good month | Hold; examine the errors |
| No homework set or reviewed by week three | Ask for a plan; switch if none arrives |
| Tutor does most of the writing | Reset once, then switch |
| Student dreads sessions after three or four weeks | Talk to the student; consider a different match |
| Tutor offers to write or heavily edit assessed work | End the arrangement |
| Tutor misses sessions repeatedly | Agree a firm schedule; switch if it continues |
| Student improving in class, marks lag | Allow two assessment cycles |

Switching is the process doing its job, not a failure. IB Gram lets families ask for a different match if the first does not fit, subject to tutor availability. Tell the tutor honestly and thank them; a professional will understand.

## What Do Parents Often Get Wrong About IGCSE Tuition?

Five misunderstandings recur. First, that every IGCSE tutor can teach every board; they cannot. Second, that more hours mean more progress; progress follows practice. Third, that a tutor's job is to raise marks quickly; the job is to change habits, which then raise marks. Fourth, that the tier is flexible late in the year; the school decides it. Fifth, that the tutor and school are in competition; the best arrangements share the school calendar and work with it.

A sixth, subtler one, and the last thing to remember about how to choose an IGCSE tutor in Gurgaon: treating the first tutor as a permanent decision. Start with a four-week commitment so that either side can adjust. A family that chooses well in week one and reviews honestly in week four rarely regrets the process, even if the first tutor is replaced.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How to choose an IGCSE tutor in Gurgaon if I am new to the board?

Ask each tutor to describe, in plain words, the papers your child will sit, the tier and the series, then check what you hear against the school's information. A tutor who has taught the specification answers precisely. Run a trial on your child's marked work, score it with the rubric above and ask your child what they worked out themselves. Those three steps catch most poor matches.

### Does my child need a Cambridge-specific or Edexcel-specific tutor?

Yes, ideally. The two boards differ in paper structure, tiers and question style, and a tutor who teaches one by adaptation will miss details that cost marks. Ask directly which board and syllabus code the tutor has taught recently. If your child switches boards or the school is unclear, confirm with the school before choosing.

### What should I ask a tutor in the first phone call?

Ask which syllabus code and tier they would teach, what they do in a first session and what you will receive afterwards. Then ask what they would not do. These four questions take five minutes and filter out most tutors who are vague about the exam or who sell packages before they have seen any work.

### How long should a trial lesson last?

A full session, the same length as a regular one, is best. Thirty minutes is too short to see how a tutor handles an error or what the student does alone. Use real marked work and run the trial at the time and in the mode you will use, so that you also test logistics such as gate entry or the online setup.

### Is it fine to switch tutors after a month?

Yes, if the evidence supports it. Review at week four: priorities worked on, corrected attempts, fewer repeated errors and clearer written working. If there is no sign of movement and the tutor cannot offer a reset, switching is reasonable. If one test went badly but the working is improving, hold on and give it two assessment cycles.

### Can a tutor help with school coursework or assignments?

A tutor can teach the skills and explain what the criteria ask, but the student must produce the assessed work. A tutor who offers to write or heavily edit a submission puts the student's result at risk. Ask the question in the interview and listen for a clear boundary. Vague answers on this point are a reason to walk away.

### How many tutors should I trial before deciding?

Two or three is enough. Fewer leaves nothing to compare, and more blurs the comparison and tires the student. Use the same trial format and the same rubric for each, and ask your child which one they would prefer to work with and why. If none of the three feels right, tighten your brief before searching further.

### My child is in Grade 9 and doing fine. Should we hire a tutor now?

Not necessarily. If marks are on track and the student can explain where they lose marks, wait and watch for a term. Start earlier if there are gaps, a high target or a board switch from CBSE or ICSE. A short diagnostic from a tutor can settle the question without a long commitment.

### Is a cheaper tutor a worse one?

Not automatically, and a dearer one is not automatically better. Fees follow experience, subject, travel, session length and preparation. Judge by what each session contains and what the tutor does between sessions. Compare tutors on the rubric first and on price second, especially when two score within a point of each other.

### What if my child likes the tutor but is not improving?

Check the work, not the mood. Look for corrected attempts, fewer repeated errors and more independent starts. If the evidence is there, wait for marks to catch up. If the folder is empty and the sessions are pleasant, ask for a reset with new priorities and a review date, and be ready to switch if nothing changes.
