# Saudi Arabia research pack

Everything a writer needs that is **checkable**, plus the things that will get the page — or the
business — into trouble. Read the cautions in full before writing.

Sourcing rule for this file: anything marked **[verified]** was read from a named source during
research for this project. Anything marked **[unverified]** must not be stated as fact on a
page; you may describe the mechanism without the number.

## 1. Who you are beating

The Saudi IB/IGCSE tutoring SERP is weaker than the UAE one. It is almost entirely vendor
marketing. The pages currently ranking share a profile:

| What rivals do | What you do |
|---|---|
| 900–2,300 words | 4,500+ |
| No tables at all | 1–2 tables minimum on hubs, more where they earn their place |
| Rates hidden behind "contact us", or one number with no context | A SAR band table with who charges what and why |
| Zero coverage of MoE equivalency, Qudurat or Tahsili | A full section — this is the biggest gap in the market |
| Generic "Saudi Arabia" copy with the city name swapped | City- and district-specific school, commute and staffing detail |
| Monday–Friday assumptions inherited from UK/India copy | The Sunday–Thursday week treated as the organising fact |
| 6–8 boilerplate FAQs, identical across their own pages | 14+, every one unique across this whole corpus |
| Self-reported "98% success rate", "4.9/5", "#1 in Riyadh" | No unverifiable claims at all — the gate blocks them |

Named rivals seen in the SERP (all vendor pages, all self-reported — treat their claims as
marketing, never cite them as fact): ACE Education, Velocity Tuition Academy, STO Education,
London Education Centre KSA, Plus Plus Tutors, Unitors, Combo Tutors Academy, Tutopiya,
MyTutorSource, tutoroo.

### The 14 gaps nobody in this SERP fills

1. **Ministry of Education equivalency (mu'adalah)** — the step that turns an IB Diploma into a
   certificate a Saudi university will accept. Almost nobody writes about it.
2. **Qudurat and Tahsili for international-curriculum students** — specifically *why* Tahsili is
   harder for an IB or IGCSE student than for a national-curriculum one.
3. **The Sunday–Thursday week** as a scheduling constraint, not a footnote.
4. **Arabia Standard Time (UTC+3)** and what it means for a tutor in India (+2:30), the UK
   (−3 in their winter), or anywhere else.
5. **Cambridge February/March is India-only** — Saudi candidates sit June and November. Rivals
   copy Indian pages and get this wrong.
6. **What a specific city's schools cannot staff** — the real reason a family needs an outside
   specialist. Dhahran is not Abha.
7. **Compound and camp life** (Aramco, KFUPM, KAUST, ISG) and how it shapes a study week.
8. **Mid-course arrivals** — families relocating into NEOM, AlUla, Tabuk or Riyadh part-way
   through DP1, and what catching up actually involves.
9. **Academic honesty on the IA, EE, TOK essay and Personal Project** — what a tutor may and may
   not do. The essay mills in this SERP will not write this.
10. **Command terms and mark schemes** — the gap between knowing content and being paid for it.
11. **Grade boundaries and predicted grades** — how they work, and why a predicted grade is not a
    promise.
12. **Switching curriculum** — national curriculum to IGCSE, British to IB, American to IB, and
    what transfers.
13. **Three-term vs two-semester calendars** — international schools and MoE schools run
    different years in the same city.
14. **What to ask a prospective tutor**, and what a good answer sounds like.

|---|---|---|

| IGCSE one-to-one online, budget platforms | from ~35 | platform marketing page |

| General Riyadh private tuition, senior-school exam subjects | 200+ | tutor-marketplace guide |

| IB HL / AP premium tracks | ~200–220 | tutoring-company listing |
| International providers quoting USD | ~$60/hour | provider site |

**How to write about this.** Give the band, say who publishes it, say what moves the number
(Higher Level vs Standard Level, examiner experience, subject scarcity, package length,
in-home travel loading). Never present any figure as what IB Gram charges, and never present a
vendor's advertised rate as the market average.

**What drives the number, in order of weight:** subject scarcity (a Higher Level Physics or
Mathematics AA HL specialist costs more than a Mathematics SL one everywhere), examiner or
marking experience, level (HL > SL > MYP > IGCSE), whether the tutor travels, and package
length.

## 3. The school landscape

**[verified]**

- Private and international schools in the Kingdom are licensed by the **Ministry of Education**.
  School evaluation and accreditation runs through the **Education and Training Evaluation
  Commission (ETEC)**; ETEC operates e-services for national school accreditation and for
  approval of international accreditation bodies.
- **Riyadh** IB World Schools named in directories include Al Faris International School
  (PYP, MYP, DP), Advanced Learning Schools, Al Hussan International Academy, Modern Global
  International Schools and SEK International School Riyadh.
- **Jeddah**: the British International School of Jeddah is an IB World School and a Cambridge
  school; Deutsche Internationale Schule Jeddah runs to Grade 12; Jeddah Knowledge International
  School, Alkawthar International Schools and Bright Minds International School also appear in
  IB listings. The American International School of Jeddah and the International Indian School
  Jeddah are long-established international schools.
- **Dhahran / Eastern Province**: the **International Schools Group (ISG)** operates several
  campuses; the IB Diploma is offered at the American School of Dhahran and at ISG Jubail. Not
  all ISG campuses run IB. British School Dhahran and Multinational School Dhahran follow
  British/Cambridge curricula.
- **Thuwal**: The KAUST School serves the King Abdullah University of Science and Technology
  campus community.

**[unverified — do not state]** Any total count of IB World Schools in the Kingdom. Directories
disagree (43 vs 28) and neither is dated. Describe the distribution, or point the reader at the
IB's own school directory. Do not give a number — and do not write "dozens of schools" either:
the gate blocks `dozens|hundreds|thousands of schools/students/tutors/families` as an
unverifiable scale claim. Write "a number of", "a small set of", or name the cities instead.

### Grading scales **[verified — standard published board information]**

Safe to state, and worth stating because it decides a student's ceiling:

- **Cambridge IGCSE** is tiered on many subjects. Core is graded **C–G**; Extended is graded
  **A\*–E**. A student entered for Core cannot be awarded above a C however well they do.
- **Pearson Edexcel International GCSE** grades **9–1**.
- Always tell the family to confirm the tier and the component list with their school in writing.
  Tier entry is a school decision and it is reversible only before the entry deadline.

**How to use school names.** Naming schools as ecosystem context is fine and useful: "students
we support attend schools including…". Presenting any school or group as a partner, client or
affiliate is a hard gate failure and is not true.

## 4. The academic year — NO YEARS ON PAGES

**This is a hard rule and the gate enforces it.** Do not write any month followed by a year
("June 2027"), and do not write an academic year ("2026/27"). A page that names a year is wrong
twelve months later and nobody goes back to fix 2,492 of them. Name the **series** or the
**term**: "the June series", "the November series", "the autumn term", "the spring term".

**[verified]**

- The **school week runs Sunday to Thursday**. Friday and Saturday are the weekend. This is the
  single most important scheduling fact on the page and most rival copy ignores it.
- **MoE public schools** run a two-semester year, starting in late August and ending in late
  June, with an autumn break in November, a midyear break in January and an Eid Al Fitr break.
- **International and British-curriculum schools set their own calendars** and most run three
  terms. Term dates differ school by school within the same city. Always send a family to their
  own school's published calendar rather than quoting one.
- **Cambridge and Pearson Edexcel** both run a **June series** (the main one for Saudi schools)
  and a **November series** (resits and November-entry candidates).
- **Cambridge's February/March series is India-only.** Saudi candidates sit June and November.
  Telling a Saudi family otherwise is a wrong fact they will act on.
- **Ramadan** moves roughly eleven days earlier each year against the Gregorian calendar, so it
  drifts across the academic year. School days shorten during it and evening study patterns
  change. **Never date Ramadan or Eid** — both are confirmed by moon sighting and differ by a day
  between countries.

**[unverified — do not state]** Any specific IB examination or results date. The IB's own site
blocks automated fetching, so nothing has been confirmed here. Write about the **May session**
as the one almost every Saudi IB school sits, and refer families to their school's Diploma
Programme coordinator.

## 5. The university pathway — the biggest content gap

**[verified]**

- A foreign or international school certificate needs an **equivalency (mu'adalah) decision from
  the Ministry of Education** before a Saudi university treats it as equivalent to the Saudi
  secondary certificate. This applies to the IB Diploma, A Levels and an American high school
  diploma alike.
- **Qudurat** (the General Aptitude Test / GAT) is run by ETEC through its National Center for
  Assessment (**Qiyas**). It tests verbal and quantitative reasoning rather than syllabus
  content, and is sat in Arabic or English.
- **Tahsili** (the Achievement Test) covers science-stream subjects — mathematics, physics,
  chemistry, biology and English. **Its content follows the Saudi national curriculum**, which is
  why international-curriculum students frequently find it harder than their own examinations:
  the material overlaps but the framing does not. This is a genuinely useful thing to tell a
  parent and almost nobody does.
- Universities publish **their own admission formulas** combining school grades with Qudurat and
  Tahsili. Weightings differ by university and by programme; competitive engineering, medicine
  and science programmes lean hardest on Tahsili.
- Some universities state a maximum age for the secondary certificate they will accept.

**[unverified — do not state, and the gate blocks it]** Any numeric Qudurat or Tahsili cut-off,
and any specific university's weighting formula. Third-party sites disagree with each other.
Describe the mechanism; send the family to the university's own published criteria.

## 6. Geography and scheduling

**[verified]**

- **Arabia Standard Time, UTC+3, no daylight saving.** The Kingdom does not change its clocks, so
  a slot agreed with a tutor in a country that does will drift twice a year unless it is pinned
  to Saudi local time.
- Riyadh is roughly **an hour across** north to south in traffic. A family in Al Olaya and a
  family in Al Narjis have different school runs to the same school.
- The **Dammam–Khobar–Dhahran conurbation** behaves as one labour and school market; families
  routinely live in one and attend school in another.
- **Al Ahsa** is roughly an hour and a half inland from that cluster.
- The **King Fahd Causeway** links Al Khobar to Bahrain.
- **Jeddah** runs as a long coastal spine from the historic centre north to Obhur.
- **Abha and Khamis Mushait** sit about 25km apart in 'Asir at high altitude and share a
  catchment.
- **Makkah** is roughly 80km east of Jeddah; **Thuwal/KAUST** is north of Jeddah on the coast.

## 7. Cautions — read every one

1. **Never claim MoE, ETEC, Qiyas, IB, Cambridge or Pearson approval, accreditation, licensing or
   partnership.** IB Gram holds none. You may name a regulator and explain what it does.
2. **Never offer to write, complete, draft or produce an IA, Extended Essay, TOK essay, Personal
   Project or any coursework.** A tutor teaches, reviews and gives feedback on the student's own
   work. Say so explicitly — it is a real differentiator against the essay mills in this SERP.
3. **Never present a named school, group or employer as a partner** (Aramco, ISG, KAUST, KFUPM,
   BISJ, AIS-J, GEMS…). "Students we support attend…" is fine.
4. **Never state a Saudi office, centre or branch.** There isn't one.
5. **Never promise a tutor will come to a Saudi address.** Delivery is online — see the writer
   guide's overriding rule.
6. **Never publish a Qudurat or Tahsili threshold**, or a university's weighting formula.
7. **Never guarantee a grade, a score, an improvement, a refund or an admission.**
8. **Never publish a statistic without n, date and method.** No "98% success", no "4.9/5", no
   "500+ tutors".
9. **Never name a year in a calendar context**, and never date Ramadan or Eid.
10. **Never tell a Saudi family they can sit the Cambridge February/March series.**
11. **Never state a count of IB World Schools in the Kingdom** — it is unverified here.
12. **Never state an IB examination or results date.**
13. **Be careful with religious and cultural context.** Mentioning that the week runs Sunday to
    Thursday, that Ramadan changes study patterns, or that Makkah's calendar bends around Hajj
    and Umrah seasons is factual and useful. Commentary on religious practice is not your job.
14. **Do not imply international schools are a workaround for the national system.** Describe
    what each offers; the family's choice is theirs.
15. **Gender-segregated schooling exists in parts of the system.** If a page's query is about a
    female tutor, answer it practically — one-to-one online lessons let a family choose the
    tutor's gender directly. Do not editorialise.

## 8. Tone anchors that work in this market

- The parent is usually an expatriate professional or a Saudi professional with international
  schooling in the family. They are not new to education systems; they are new to *this* one.
- Money is discussed directly in SAR. Vagueness reads as evasion.
- "Near me" queries in a Saudi city are usually about *availability and fit*, not distance:
  the family wants to know whether anyone genuinely knows their child's syllabus. Answer that.
- Distance is the real constraint outside Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province cluster. Say so
  plainly; it is the honest argument for online teaching.
