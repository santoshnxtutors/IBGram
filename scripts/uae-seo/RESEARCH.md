Ten query clusters, sixty-odd competitor pages fetched and dissected (October 2026). Read this
before you write. Everything below was measured, not assumed — where a figure is attributed to
a competitor, that competitor published it.

## The three numbers that matter

1. **The cluster's word-count ceiling is ~2,300 words.** Measured: GetYourTutors 2,294 ·
   Unitors 1,927 · Tutopiya 1,911 · EdFlik 1,685 · UniversityTutor 1,415. A 4,500-word page is
   roughly double the best thing ranking.
2. **Five of six pages have zero tables.** The entire cluster's table inventory is two tables
   and eleven data rows, on one page. Every comparison table you ship is uncontested.
3. **Three of six pages publish no price at all.** The two that do quote USD — a currency UAE
   parents do not transact in.

## Regulatory facts

- Dubai private schools: **KHDA** (Knowledge and Human Development Authority). Abu Dhabi and
  Al Ain: **ADEK** (Department of Education and Knowledge). Sharjah: **SPEA** (Sharjah Private
  Education Authority). Ajman, RAK, Fujairah, UAQ: federal **Ministry of Education**.
- The UAE private-tutoring permit is issued through MoHRE and is **free of charge** — this is
  unanimous across every regulatory source fetched. Do not state a fee.
- **Do not publish fine amounts.** Khaleej Times reported verbatim that the ministry "did not
  specify the fine amounts or detail the penalties". A figure circulating as "up to AED 50,000"
  could not be verified. Leave it out.

## Never do these (two competitors do; both are liabilities)

- Never offer to write, complete or "do" an Internal Assessment, Extended Essay, TOK essay or
  any assessed coursework. A tutor teaches, reviews and gives feedback. ibscholars sells
  completed IAs at AED 3,750; that is an academic-integrity breach and an IB malpractice
  trigger. Our pages say plainly what a tutor may and may not do.
- Never self-assert a star rating in schema. Unitors publishes its own AggregateRating of 4.9;
  self-serving review markup is against Google's structured-data policy and is a manual-action
  risk.

  <https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/education/school-education-k-12/private-schools-in-the-uae>

  <https://u.ae/en/information-and-services/education/school-education-k-12/private-schools-in-the-uae>

# Legal and factual cautions — read every one

These are not style preferences. Each is a thing a competitor does that exposes them,
or a rule a UAE regulator actually enforces.

1. Never claim KHDA, ADEK, SPEA, MoE, MoHRE, IB or Cambridge approval, accreditation, licensing or partnership unless IB Gram actually holds it and the permit/centre number can be shown. A KHDA Educational Services Permit is a specific thing issued to training institutes with premises; do not imply it. 'KHDA-approved tutors' is the single most dangerous phrase available to these pages.
2. Never claim an IB World School, GEMS school, or any named school is a client, partner or endorser. Write 'we support students from' only where true. Several competitors list 25+ schools as 'trusted by' with no relationship; one lists a school that appears not to exist. Every school name must be verified against the regulator's own school listing.
3. No guaranteed outcomes. No 'guaranteed 7', 'guaranteed grade improvement', 'guaranteed 45/45', 'guaranteed university admission', 'score improvement or your money back' unless the exact refund terms are published and honoured on the page. Do not copy competitor patterns like 'top 5%' in one section and '3%' in another.
4. Do not state or imply that private tutoring is unregulated or that a permit is optional. The correct position: individuals providing private lessons need a MoHRE private tuition work permit under Ministerial Resolution No. 710 of 2023, and those operating without one are subject to fines and penalties under the applicable laws.
5. Do not invite or facilitate a school teacher tutoring their own enrolled students. ADEK's professional-ethics rules bar Abu Dhabi school staff from paid private tutoring of students at their own school, and the MoHRE permit carries a code of conduct. Never market 'your child's own teacher, privately'.
6. Do not publish, reproduce or offer to supply IB, Cambridge or Pearson syllabus documents, past papers, mark schemes, grade-boundary tables or subject guides as downloads. Reference them, cite the official URL, and link out. Reproducing them is copyright infringement and, for unreleased material, a malpractice exposure.
7. Do not offer or imply any form of help with work that must be the student's own: IA, Extended Essay, TOK essay and coursework support must be described as teaching, feedback and process coaching, never writing, editing to completion, or 'done-for-you'. Avoid 'we write your IA'. IB academic-honesty rules make this an expulsion risk for the student and a liability for us.
8. Never publish an AggregateRating, review count or star rating that is not backed by real, countable, attributable reviews, and never ship AggregateRating in schema that differs from the visible figure. Competitors show '4.92/5 from 480 reviews' and '4.93/5 from 850 reviews' on the same page; one shows 5.0 from 20 reviews against a claim of 2,500 students. Fabricated review markup is a Google structured-data violation and, as a commercial representation in the UAE, a consumer-protection exposure.
9. Do not publish any statistic without n, date and method on the same page. No '96.7% satisfaction', no 'median tutor IB score 43', no '87% scored A or A*' style claims unless we can produce the underlying data.
10. Do not state the UAE VAT treatment of tuition fees as fact without a Federal Tax Authority citation. Limit the page to whether our own quoted AED prices include VAT, and publish our TRN only if we hold one.
11. Do not publish an IB points threshold for MoE equivalency that has not been read on moe.gov.ae or the emirate attestation authority's own page. The British-track numbers (five GCSE/IGCSE at E/3 excluding Arabic and Islamic Education, plus one A-Level at E or two AS-Levels) are sourced; the IB figure is not. One ranking competitor already publishes a wrong subject count — do not inherit it.
12. Do not present the equivalency or attestation process as something IB Gram performs or guarantees. Describe it, cite the authority, and point parents to the official channel.
13. Do not use non-UAE phone or WhatsApp numbers as the conversion path on a UAE page, and do not state a UAE office address that IB Gram does not occupy. Two ranking competitors route UAE parents to +91 numbers and one shows a Dubai footer address on an Abu Dhabi page — both are trust failures and, for an address, a misrepresentation.
14. Lead forms collect personal data about minors: state what is collected, why, and how to delete it, and keep a privacy link on every page (UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 on Personal Data Protection). Never collect a child's school, grades and photo in a form without a stated purpose.
15. Do not name, compare against or disparage specific competitor brands with claims about their pricing, quality or legality. Comparison tables must compare models (agency vs marketplace vs freelance vs tuition centre) and may quote a competitor's own published price only with the date and source URL stated.
16. Do not use 'free' for anything with a condition attached without stating the condition on the same screen (trial length, eligibility, card requirement).
17. Do not describe tutors as 'IB examiners' or 'Cambridge examiners' for individuals whose current examiner status we cannot evidence, and do not use examiner status to imply access to marking, boundaries or paper content.
18. Do not imply that tutoring is required for MoE equivalency, university admission, or school progression. That is a false-urgency claim about a regulatory process.
